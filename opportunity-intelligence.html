Opportunity Intelligence

What a supplier project-intelligence brief includes

When an evidenced industrial project or development is relevant to your range, the useful question is what it actually establishes. DemandRange’s Opportunity Intelligence capability brings the source record, supplier relevance and unresolved questions into a reviewable brief.

It supports the opportunity-signal component of our wider account-research model. Not every industry-matched account needs a project.

01 / receive

What the brief needs to make clear.

  • Company and facility: which entity the source refers to and how it relates to the target account.
  • Observed change: the specific project or development, with source references and dates.
  • Supplier relevance: why the change may matter to the agreed product or service category.
  • Stage and route: what is known about progress and the organisations involved; what is not established.
  • Unknowns and review: specification gaps, package status, timing and the next verification step.

Outputs, evidence supplied and review cadence are agreed for the brief. This is not a continuous live feed or a promise of complete project coverage. Role-level mapping does not include named contacts by default.

02 / credible

A signal is not an open equipment order.

Observed

State exactly what the source supports. For example, a fictional company statement proposes additional production space.

Inferred

The change may be worth reviewing against the supplier’s range. That is a relevance hypothesis—not proof of required equipment.

Unknown

Whether the project will proceed, which specifications apply, how procurement will run and whether any package is available.

This example is invented to explain the method. In real work, planning, construction, tender and award evidence must keep their actual meanings and dates. An award notice cannot support a claim that the same work remains unawarded.

03 / fit

Use project context where it changes the research question.

This capability is relevant when your brief depends on a facility change or development that can be evidenced. Start with the supplier category and the signal that would be meaningful, not a promise to find an open order.

If the immediate need is a broader set of relevant businesses, industry-matched account research does not require every record to have a current project. Both classes still need fit checks.

04 / Written scope

Scope the signal, evidence and limits.

Agree territory, qualifying signal, source rules, recency, record fields and review cadence in writing. Ireland is the current focus; other territory preferences are assessed before commitment.

The wider programme aims for 90% industry-matched and 10% opportunity-signal leads. That target is not a guaranteed number of projects or a fixed brief package.

Evidence access and customer-use rights must be checked. Named-contact research and outreach are separate scopes. No project signal guarantees buying intent, buyer access, an open package, meetings or sales.

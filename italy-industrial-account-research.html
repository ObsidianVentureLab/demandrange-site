How to find industrial customers in Italy: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Eurostat counted 347,845 manufacturing enterprises in Italy in 2024 (NACE Section C).[1] By our count of the official 2024 EU member-country cells in that series, Italy has the largest manufacturing enterprise count in the EU.[1] Use that headline only to size the market, then build an Italian account list from national sources the country table does not carry: ISTAT statistics, the Registro Imprese (InfoCamere), ATECO 2025 activity codes and ANAC public-contract open data.

For the comparison with other EU countries, see manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country (2024 data). Do not treat ISTAT's wider "Manufacturing (a)" total, which includes mining, energy and water, as if it were the Section C cell.

Key facts

Eurostat 2024 structural business statistics; ISTAT 2024 structural business statistics, published 15 October 2026; ATECO 2025 and the Italian registers; checked 10 November 2026.

The dataset was last updated on 30 September 2026 at 11:00 (UTC+2) and was re-extracted on 10 November 2026. No flag is attached to either cell.[1][2]

02 / how big the italian manufacturing base is

How big the Italian manufacturing base is

Eurostat's 347,845 figure is the official 2024 headline for Italian manufacturing enterprises (NACE Section C). Use it to size the market, then go to national sources for names, codes, sites and buying signals.[1]

ISTAT is the national source of that cell. Its structural business statistics for 2024, published on 15 October 2026, put 347,845 enterprises in "C - Manufacturing" and 4,532,053 active enterprises in the release as a whole. By our count, manufacturing is 7.7% of that ISTAT total. ISTAT describes an enterprise as "an organisational unit producing goods or services which has a certain degree of autonomy in decision-making" and notes that it "may consist out of one or more legal units". Treat these official series as market-sizing figures, not as a named company list.[3][4][5]

ISTAT also maintains the statistical business register, Asia-Imprese (Registro statistico delle imprese attive). The latest Asia table release checked on 10 November 2026 was for 2023, published on 9 July 2025. Asia implements the same EU enterprise definition: an enterprise may be one legal unit, or several legal units under common control that form one organisational unit with a degree of decision-making autonomy. From 2019, ISTAT says the figures are not fully comparable with earlier years in the time series. Asia is a statistical register. It is not a public list of company names.[6]

Eurostat permits reuse of its figures with acknowledgement.[2]

03 / build the list in layers

Build the list in layers

  1. Companies. Confirm each legal entity in the Registro Imprese, the official business register held at the Chambers of Commerce and published online by InfoCamere, which describes itself as "la società delle Camere di Commercio per l'innovazione digitale". A visura camerale is the official information document for a registered enterprise. It can include the legal name, legal form, registered office, activity, officers and, importantly for suppliers, secondary offices and local units (unità locali). Registration for the Telemaco service is free; official documents are paid, at the minimum secretarial fees set by ministerial decree. Check the register site for the current fees and access terms.[10][11][12][13]
  2. Activity. Code the list to ATECO. ATECO 2025 has been in force since 1 January 2025 and in operational use from 1 April 2025, and it is aligned with NACE Rev. 2.1. Older files and some statistics still use earlier ATECO codes (ISTAT publishes a correspondence with ATECO 2022). Keep both codes until your sources have moved, and map them with the official correspondence, as described in our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.[7][8]
  3. Plants. The register shows the registered entity and can list local units, but the registered office is not always the plant. Use site-level evidence such as the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for process industries.[11]
  4. Buying signals. Search official Italian public contracts in ANAC's BDNCP open data and Analytics tools, and EU-threshold notices on TED with CPV codes.[14]

A register match confirms a legal entity. It is not, by itself, a purchasing signal. A tender is a public buying signal at one buyer. It tells you nothing about private plants.

04 / know the italian company landscape

Know the Italian company landscape

  • Legal forms. The visura records the legal form of the registered enterprise, including sole proprietorships and companies. Record the operating entity and, where the group differs, the group.
  • Registered office versus plant. The visura identifies the registered office (sede legale) and can list the operating site, secondary offices and local units. A manufacturer can run several plants under one registered company. Find the plants from the local-unit entries and from public site evidence, not from the registered office alone.[11]
  • Language. Register entries, ISTAT tables, tender notices and many technical pages are in Italian. Plan keyword lists in Italian, for example pompe, valvole, trattamento delle acque and manutenzione. An English-language visura is also available from the register, on the same terms as an ordinary visura.[11]
  • Activity-code vintage. An ATECO 2025 code taken from a recent visura will not always match an older ATECO filter on a statistical table. Do not merge the two unchecked.[7]
05 / ateco 2025 and earlier codes run side by side

ATECO 2025 and earlier codes run side by side

ATECO is Italy's national classification of economic activities. ATECO 2025 entered into force on 1 January 2025 and, ISTAT says, "è coerente con NACE Rev. 2.1" under Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/137, which amends Regulation (EC) No 1893/2006. It was published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale, Serie Generale no. 302 of 27 December 2024 (ISTAT notice 24A06839). Operational adoption for statistical, tax and administrative purposes began on 1 April 2025, under a joint note by ISTAT, the chamber system and the tax authorities.[7][8][9]

ATECO 2025 is a hierarchical classification with alphanumeric codes down to six digits: sections (a capital letter), divisions (two digits), groups (three), classes (four), categories (five) and subcategories (six). Classes are four digits, the same length as NACE classes; the five- and six-digit category and subcategory levels add national detail, consistent with ISTAT's statement that ATECO 2025 "è coerente con NACE Rev. 2.1".[7]

ISTAT also publishes a 2026 update of ATECO 2025. That update, ISTAT says, "non ha modificato la struttura, i livelli o le descrizioni della classificazione", and changed only the correspondence, theoretical and operational, between ATECO 2025 and ATECO 2022. Use the official correspondence tables, including the operational table from ATECO 2022 to ATECO 2025 (2026 update).[7]

For a list, that means:

  • Codes in older datasets, directories and your own records are probably a pre-2025 ATECO version, often ATECO 2022.
  • Keep both the earlier ATECO code and the ATECO 2025 code on each row until your sources have moved, and map them with the official correspondence described in our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.
  • Do not filter one dataset on an earlier ATECO version and another on ATECO 2025 and then merge them unchecked.

The method is the same one used for industrial account research in Germany, where WZ 2025 is that country's NACE Rev. 2.1 implementation.

07 / step by step

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat headline, then define it with ATECO and NACE codes. See our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide for how ATECO 2025 relates to the European classification, and the EU enterprises table for other countries.
  2. Use ISTAT only for market sizing. It tells you how large Section C is, and warns you not to mix that cell with the wider "Manufacturing (a)" total. It does not give you the names.
  3. Resolve each prospect in the Registro Imprese. Confirm the legal entity, the legal form, the registered office and any local units on the visura. Do not treat officer names as a contact list; record the function, not a person.
  4. Find the plants from local-unit entries and from public site evidence, including the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for process industries, because the registered office is not always the plant.
  5. Add a trigger only when you have one: a new visura event, a local-unit change, an ANAC or TED notice, or a new site record.
  6. Log the evidence for each account: legal entity, earlier ATECO and ATECO 2025 codes, site evidence and the reason it fits the brief.

Fictional example: A fictional Irish maker of industrial pumps targets Italian chemical plants. It filters the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for chemical installations in Italy and resolves each operator in the Registro Imprese. It finds that one fictional group runs two plants as local units of a single registered company, and that the visura lists both sites under one legal entity. It records the two plants as separate accounts under one group, each with maintenance as the owning function. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

08 / common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Using only registered-office data. In Italy, as elsewhere in the EU, buying influence often sits at plant level.
  • Treating ISTAT's "Manufacturing (a)" total of 368,044 as if it were the 347,845 Section C manufacturing count.
  • Filtering one source on an earlier ATECO version and another on ATECO 2025 and merging them unchecked.
  • Treating a register match or a tender notice as proof that a plant is buying your product.
09 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in Italy?
Eurostat counted 347,845 manufacturing enterprises in Italy in 2024, for NACE Section C, down from 352,446 in 2023.[1] By our count of the official 2024 EU member-country cells, that is the largest total in the EU.[1] ISTAT's 2024 tables, published on 15 October 2026, show the same 347,845 Section C cell, among 4,532,053 active enterprises in the release coverage.[3]
What is ATECO 2025?
ATECO 2025 is Italy's national classification of economic activities. It entered into force on 1 January 2025 and, ISTAT says, is consistent with NACE Rev. 2.1 under Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/137. It was published in the official gazette on 27 December 2024. Operational adoption for statistical and administrative purposes began on 1 April 2025.[7][8][9]
Should I use earlier ATECO codes or ATECO 2025?
Both, for now. ATECO 2025 has been in force since 1 January 2025 and in operational use from 1 April 2025, but older datasets still use earlier codes, often ATECO 2022. ISTAT publishes correspondence tables, including a 2026 update that changed only those relations, not the structure. Keep both codes on each row until your sources have moved.[7]
How do I look up an Italian company in the business register?
Use the official Registro Imprese, through registroimprese.it or the Telemaco service run with InfoCamere for the Chambers of Commerce. A visura camerale is the official information document: it can show the legal name, form, registered office, activity, officers and local units. Telemaco registration is free; official documents are paid at ministerial fees. Check the register site for current access terms.[10][11][12][13]
What public-contract data does ANAC publish?
ANAC's open-data portal publishes data from the Banca Dati Nazionale dei Contratti Pubblici as open CSV or JSON, updated monthly, and a weekly Analytics dashboard you can search by year or CIG. A notice is a public buying signal, not proof that a named plant will buy from you.[14]
Do Italian public records show who makes buying decisions at a plant?
No. A Registro Imprese visura can show the legal name, form, registered office, activity, officers and local units.[11] ANAC's BDNCP open data cover public contracts, including participating firms and awardees.[14] Neither source shows who makes buying decisions at a plant.

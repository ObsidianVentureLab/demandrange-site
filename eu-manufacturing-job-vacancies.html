Manufacturing job vacancy rates by EU country

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 25 November 2026

In the second quarter of 2026, the job vacancy rate in EU-27 manufacturing was 1.5%, and Ireland's rate was 0.8% (P), not seasonally adjusted. Ireland ranked 21st of the 26 EU countries with a published rate that quarter, by our count. The figures are official cells from Eurostat table jvs_q_r21, for NACE Rev. 2.1 section C (manufacturing), size class total. The dataset was last updated on 19 November 2026 at 10:00 Irish time (GMT, UTC+0), and was checked on 24 November 2026.[1]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page is the country-by-country view of official manufacturing job vacancy rates: a hiring-pressure signal at national level. It does not name employers or list vacancies.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat jvs_q_r21, manufacturing, NACE Rev. 2.1 section C, size class total, not seasonally adjusted; data updated 19 November 2026, 10:00 Irish time (GMT, UTC+0); checked 24 November 2026.

  • EU-27 manufacturing job vacancy rate, second quarter of 2026: 1.5%, with no flag. That was 0.1 percentage points lower than the 1.6% rate in the second quarter of 2025, by our count. The third-quarter 2026 EU-27 rate was also 1.5%, flagged estimated (E).[1]
  • Ireland's manufacturing job vacancy rate, second quarter of 2026: 0.8%, flagged provisional (P). That was 0.1 percentage points higher than the 0.7% rate in the second quarter of 2025, by our count. Ireland's third-quarter 2026 cell had not been published when we checked.[1]
  • Among the 26 EU countries with a published second-quarter 2026 rate, the Netherlands was highest, at 4.1% (P), and Ireland ranked 21st, by our count. Four countries were at 0.5%, the lowest published rates that quarter: Bulgaria (P), Spain (P), Romania and Finland. Portugal had no published cell.[1]
  • In the same quarter, the broader rate for industry, construction and services (NACE Rev. 2.1 sections B to T) was 2.0% in the EU-27 and 1.3% (P) in Ireland, so manufacturing was lower than that broader rate in both, by our count of the official cells.[1]
  • A job vacancy rate is vacant posts as a share of vacant plus occupied posts. It is a national or sector stock figure, not a count of hires and not a list of employers.[1][2]

04 / the nace rev 2 to 2 1 change

The NACE Rev. 2 to 2.1 change

Eurostat now publishes current job vacancy statistics in table jvs_q_r21, "Job vacancy statistics by NACE Rev. 2.1 activity - quarterly data (from 2001 onwards)". The older table, jvs_q_nace2, uses NACE Rev. 2 and ends in the fourth quarter of 2025. This page uses jvs_q_r21 for every quoted rate, including the 2024 and 2025 cells in the time series, so that the figures sit on one classification.[1][3]

The two tables are close, but they are not identical, and they should not be mixed in one comparison without saying which table each cell comes from. In the overlapping quarters we compared (first quarter of 2024 to fourth quarter of 2025), Ireland's manufacturing rates match on both tables; the fourth-quarter 2025 Irish cell is unflagged on jvs_q_r21 and flagged provisional (P) on jvs_q_nace2. The EU-27 manufacturing rate matches from the second quarter of 2024 to the fourth quarter of 2025. In the first quarter of 2024 it is 2.0% on jvs_q_r21 and 1.9% on jvs_q_nace2. Some other country cells also differ. Czechia's first-quarter 2025 cell on jvs_q_r21 is flagged as a break in time series (B), which Eurostat's job vacancy metadata attributes to a methodological change; that break falls before the second-quarter 2025 and 2026 cells used for the year-on-year column above.[1][2][3]

Eurostat's job vacancy metadata still describes the statistics mainly in NACE Rev. 2 terms. The live current table, and every rate quoted here, is NACE Rev. 2.1.[1][2]

Eurostat table jvs_q_r21 has no United Kingdom cells for these recent quarters, so this page does not quote a UK manufacturing vacancy rate and does not rank the UK against the EU-27 or Ireland on this series.[1]

05 / using vacancy rates in account research

Using vacancy rates in account research

A high or rising manufacturing vacancy rate in a country is a sign that manufacturers there, taken as a group, had a larger share of posts vacant on the survey date. That can be useful context when you size a sales territory or judge whether hiring pressure is tighter in one country than another. It is not a reason to approach a particular plant.[1][2]

Eurostat defines a job vacancy as a paid post that is newly created, unoccupied, or about to become vacant, for which the employer is taking active steps to find a suitable candidate from outside the enterprise and intends to fill the post immediately or within a specific period. Because that definition includes posts that exist only because someone is leaving, a vacancy is not, by itself, evidence of expansion, and a national rate is not evidence about any one firm.[2]

Use this page for the official, comparable country rates. When you score accounts, treat a vacancy rate as background, not as a timing score for a named company; see industrial account prioritisation. For the occupational make-up of Irish industry, see Irish industry workforce by occupation.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What is the manufacturing job vacancy rate?
It is Eurostat's official rate: vacant manufacturing posts as a percentage of occupied plus vacant posts, recorded at a point in the quarter. On this page the figures are not seasonally adjusted, cover NACE Rev. 2.1 section C, and use size class total. The rate does not name employers.[1][2]
What was the EU manufacturing job vacancy rate in 2026?
In the second quarter of 2026, the EU-27 manufacturing job vacancy rate was 1.5%, not seasonally adjusted, with no flag. The third-quarter 2026 EU-27 rate was also 1.5%, flagged estimated (E). Both cells are from table jvs_q_r21 (NACE Rev. 2.1).[1]
What was Ireland's manufacturing job vacancy rate in 2026?
In the second quarter of 2026 it was 0.8%, flagged provisional (P), not seasonally adjusted. That ranked 21st of the 26 EU countries with a published rate that quarter, by our count. Ireland's third-quarter 2026 cell had not been published when we checked.[1]
Which EU country had the highest manufacturing job vacancy rate?
In the second quarter of 2026, among the 26 EU countries with a published manufacturing rate, the Netherlands was highest, at 4.1% (P), by our count. Portugal had no published cell that quarter, so it is not in the ranking.[1]
Can I use these rates to find companies that are hiring?
No. The official rate is a country or sector stock figure. It does not list employers or vacancies. To research hiring at a named account, start from public job advertisements and treat them as a signal to investigate, not as proof of expansion or of a purchase.[1][2]
Why are there two Eurostat job vacancy tables?
Table jvs_q_nace2 reports the series on NACE Rev. 2 and ends in the fourth quarter of 2025. Table jvs_q_r21 is the current table, on NACE Rev. 2.1, and is the source of every rate quoted on this page. The overlapping quarters are close but not identical, so the two tables should not be mixed without a label.[1][3]

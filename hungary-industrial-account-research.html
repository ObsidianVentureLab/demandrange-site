How to find industrial customers in Hungary: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

In 2024 Hungary had 55,898 manufacturing enterprises (NACE Rev. 2 Section C) and 769,452 persons employed, or 14 persons employed per enterprise.[1][2] Food products narrowly employed the most people that year; motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers led net turnover and value added, and had led employment in 2023.[1][2] Size the market with Eurostat Structural Business Statistics and with KSH, the Hungarian Central Statistical Office, for more recent production-volume figures, then confirm named enterprises in the official company-register search. Eurostat does not publish a public named list of Hungarian manufacturers.[1][4][5]

For the comparison with other EU countries, see manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country. For the sibling method in nearby markets, see industrial account research in Czechia and industrial account research in Poland.

Key facts

02 / hungarian manufacturing in numbers

Hungarian manufacturing in numbers

Eurostat's 2024 Structural Business Statistics are the comparable stock figures for Hungary and the rest of the EU. They count enterprises, persons employed, net turnover and value added at enterprise level for NACE Rev. 2 manufacturing, not production volume and not a list of named companies. The latest published reference year in this dataset is 2024. The dataset was last updated on 30 September 2026, 11:00 (UTC+2).[1][2]

Enterprise numbers fell from 56,369 in 2023 to 55,898 in 2024, a 0.8% decline by our count, and from 59,552 in 2022. Persons employed fell from 778,611 in 2023 to 769,452 in 2024. 03 / recent production movements from ksh

Recent production movements from KSH

KSH publishes a more recent production-volume picture than the 2024 Eurostat stock figures. Those volume rates and subsection weights are not the same thing as Eurostat's 2024 turnover or employment shares. Do not put a 2024 SBS share and a September 2026 KSH volume rate in one sentence as if they were one measure.[3][4]

In KSH's industry first release for September 2026 (second estimate, published 13 November 2026), the volume of industrial production was +8.2% year on year and +0.9% on August 2026, seasonally and working-day adjusted. Manufacturing, which KSH says represents a 96% weight of industry, was +9.0% year on year in September. From the beginning of 2026, the January–September manufacturing production volume index was 104.4 (corresponding period of the previous year = 100), a 4.4% rise on the same period of 2025, by our count from that official index.[3]

The same release gives annual manufacturing production volume indices, on the same previous-year base, of 95.0 in 2024 and 96.8 in 2025, equivalent to a 5.0% fall in 2024 and a 3.2% fall in 2025, by our count from those official indices.[3]

In September 2026, KSH says manufacture of transport equipment had the largest weight (28% share) within manufacturing output and rose 33% year on year; within that, motor vehicles manufacturing was +62% and parts and accessories +11.9%. Computer, electronic and optical products had a 16% output share and were +11.0%. Food products, beverages and tobacco had a 12% share and were −4.3%.[3] "Manufacture of transport equipment" is KSH's subsection, which is broader than Eurostat's C29 (motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers only). Keep the labels distinct, and do not drop these September 2026 rates into the 2024 Eurostat table.[1][3]

04 / official statistics eurostat and ksh

Official statistics: Eurostat and KSH

Use Eurostat when you need a figure you can set beside other EU countries: the enterprise count, persons employed, net turnover and value added, including the division mix in 2024. The comparison page is manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country.[1][2]

Use KSH when you need a later production-volume reading for Hungary. Start at KSH's English entry point and open the industry first releases. The September 2026 second estimate used above was published on 13 November 2026.[3][4] KSH tables can also describe all industry, not manufacturing alone; read the title before you quote a rate. A STADAT companion table for all industry is listed in the sources, but the manufacturing rates on this page come from the first release.[3][6]

Neither source names customers. Statistics size the segment; a company- or site-level official record is still required before you call a named account a fit.

05 / the company register

The company register

The official free company-register search is e-cégjegyzék, published as Cégszolgálat Ingyenes Céginformáció. Use it to look up a named enterprise and confirm the legal entity.[5] It is a search tool, not a source of an aggregate manufacturing count, and this page does not invent a stock figure from it.

A register match confirms that a legal entity exists and can be identified. It does not, by itself, tell you who buys at a plant, or that the registered entity is the operating site. Record the operating company and, where the group differs, the group, and add site-level public evidence before you treat the row as a qualified account.

06 / how to research industrial accounts in hungary

How to research industrial accounts in Hungary

Build the list in layers

  1. Market size. Take the Eurostat 2024 enterprise and employment counts, and the division mix, to decide whether the segment is worth a named list. Add KSH production-volume figures when you need a more recent direction of travel, without mixing the two measures.[1][2][3]
  2. Companies. Confirm each legal entity in the official company-register search.[5]
  3. Activity. Code the list to NACE. The Eurostat figures on this page use NACE Rev. 2 Section C and its divisions. See our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide for how activity codes are used on account lists.
  4. Plants. A register record identifies the legal entity, not every site. Use site-level official evidence, such as the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for process industries.
  5. Buying signals. Search EU-threshold public-contract notices on TED with CPV codes. A tender notice is a signal, not proof that a plant is buying your product.

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat headline for 2024, then decide which divisions to include. Food and motor vehicles are close on employment and far apart on turnover, so the measure you use will change the shortlist.[1][2]
  2. Check the recent volume picture in the latest KSH industry first release, keeping transport-equipment subsection rates separate from Eurostat C29, and keeping 2026 volume rates separate from 2024 stock shares.[3]
  3. Resolve each prospect in the official company-register search. Confirm the legal entity. Do not treat officer names as a contact list; record the function, not a person.[5]
  4. Find the plants from public site evidence, because the registered entity is not always the buying site.
  5. Qualify the row against your product and the site-level evidence. Demand Range can run this as a named account list, with the official source for each row. Request a written scope if you want that work done on a Hungarian territory.

Common mistakes

  • Describing Hungarian manufacturing as a motor-vehicles-only base because C29 leads turnover and value added. Food products narrowly led employment in 2024.
  • Mixing a 2024 Eurostat employment or turnover share with a September 2026 KSH production-volume rate in one sentence.
  • Treating KSH's "manufacture of transport equipment" subsection as if it were Eurostat division C29 alone.
  • Inventing an aggregate manufacturing count from the company-register search, or treating a register match as proof of a buying plant.
  • "Correcting" the official persons-employed-per-enterprise cells (14 for manufacturing, 191 for motor vehicles, 20 for food products in 2024) from the headcount and enterprise totals.
07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in Hungary?
Eurostat counted 55,898 manufacturing enterprises in Hungary in 2024, for NACE Section C, with 769,452 persons employed, or 14 persons employed per enterprise. There were 56,369 enterprises in 2023 and 59,552 in 2022. Persons employed were 778,611 in 2023. The official employment growth rate was −1.18% in 2024.[1][2]
Which Hungarian manufacturing sectors employ the most people?
In 2024, food products (C10) employed 97,668 people, 12.69% of manufacturing employment, narrowly ahead of motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers (C29) at 96,897, or 12.59%. Fabricated metal products (C25) were third, at 85,969 (11.17%). The food lead was 771 people, by our count. In 2023, motor vehicles led, at 104,814 people (13.46%).[1][2]
Is Hungarian manufacturing mainly motor vehicles?
No. Motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers led net turnover and value added in 2024, at €34,813.67 million (22.5%) and €5,485.45 million (15.74%), but food products narrowly led employment. Motor vehicles is a concentrated division: 506 enterprises and 191 persons employed per enterprise in 2024, against 14 for manufacturing as a whole.[1][2]
Where do I look up a Hungarian company?
The official free company-register search is e-cégjegyzék (Cégszolgálat Ingyenes Céginformáció). Use it to confirm a named enterprise. It is a search tool, not a source of an aggregate manufacturing count, and Eurostat does not publish a public named list of Hungarian manufacturers. A register match confirms a legal entity; it does not, by itself, prove who buys at a plant.[5]
Should I use Eurostat or KSH figures?
Use both, for different jobs. Eurostat Structural Business Statistics give comparable 2024 enterprise, employment, turnover and value-added figures for Hungary and the rest of the EU. KSH, the Hungarian Central Statistical Office, publishes more recent production-volume figures, including the September 2026 industry first release. Do not treat a 2024 SBS share and a 2026 KSH volume rate as one measure.[1][3][4]
How does Demand Range research industrial accounts in Hungary?
Demand Range sizes the market with official Eurostat and KSH figures, then builds a named account list from official sources such as the company register and site-level public records, and qualifies each row at company or site level. Statistics alone do not name customers. Named contacts and outreach are separate scopes, not included by default.[1][5]

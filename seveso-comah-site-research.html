COMAH and Seveso site lists for Ireland and the UK: what they show and how suppliers use them

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Official lists of COMAH (Seveso) establishments are public in Ireland, Great Britain and Northern Ireland, but in different forms: two HSA PDFs, an HSE search tool and a DAERA web table. On 10 November 2026, Ireland's lists, last updated 21 October 2026, named 107 establishments, by our count (51 upper-tier and 56 lower-tier), and Northern Ireland's DAERA table named 28, by our count (10 upper-tier and 18 lower-tier). HSE's establishment list for Great Britain, released under freedom of information on 12 August 2026, names 911 as at August 2026, by our count (342 upper-tier and 569 lower-tier). HSE's covering letter of that date says this information "is subject to frequent change".[9]

A Seveso or COMAH establishment is a site where dangerous substances are present at or above thresholds set under Directive 2012/18/EU, known as Seveso III. It is upper tier or lower tier depending on the quantities involved. For industrial suppliers, the lists confirm that a named site handles hazardous substances in quantity. They say nothing about equipment, budgets or contacts.

The legal basis is Directive 2012/18/EU (Seveso III).[17] Ireland implements it through the Chemicals Act (Control of Major Accident Hazards Involving Dangerous Substances) Regulations 2015 (S.I. No. 209/2015).[15] Great Britain implements it through the Control of Major Accident Hazards Regulations 2015 (S.I. 2015/483).[8] Northern Ireland implements it through the Control of Major Accident Hazards Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2015 (S.R. 2015/325).[18]

This page is the method for using those national lists. The European Industrial Emissions Portal research guide covers the EU portal, including its Industrial Safety subsite. Licence and PRTR evidence that can sit alongside a list entry is explained in Irish environmental register evidence.

The Great Britain row comes from HSE's own establishment list, released under freedom of information and published on WhatDoTheyKnow. It has one row per establishment, each with a duty type of upper or lower tier and a country field.[10] By our count of that field on all 911 rows, England has 640 establishments (254 upper, 386 lower), Scotland 216 (62 upper, 154 lower) and Wales 55 (26 upper, 29 lower). The list covers England, Scotland and Wales only. HSE's covering letter of 12 August 2026 says this information "is subject to frequent change".[9]

03 / getting a full list for great britain

Getting a full list for Great Britain

The HSE public information search works by full postcode, returning establishments within three miles, or by establishment or operator name of at least the first four characters, including any spaces.[4] That suits checking a known site, not building a list from scratch.

There is a second route. HSE has repeatedly released its full COMAH establishment list, upper and lower tier, in answer to freedom of information requests, and the responses are published on WhatDoTheyKnow. The most recent we have was sent on 12 August 2026 as "COMAH Establishment List August 2026". The covering letter says this information "is subject to frequent change" and points to the search tool for current detail.[9] HSE's own guidance also says to contact it, under the Environmental Information Regulations, for more information about inspections at a particular establishment.[3]

If you use a released list:

  • Record the release date and request reference, not the date you downloaded it
  • Check each establishment you keep against the HSE search, which shows the current tier and last planned visit[4]
  • Remember Northern Ireland isn't included; use the DAERA table for it[5]
  • Treat the list as a snapshot of regulatory status, not a list of buyers

There is an EU-wide Seveso database, run by the European Environment Agency. From January 2026 the EEA has been responsible for the EU database of Seveso establishments referred to in Article 21(3) of the Seveso III Directive, taking it over from the Commission's Joint Research Centre, and publishes Seveso information on the Industrial Safety part of its European Industrial Emissions Portal.[7] For establishments in other EU countries, start with the national competent authority's own public information, and use the EEA portal as a cross-check. Our European Industrial Emissions Portal research guide explains the emissions side of the same portal.

04 / upper tier and lower tier

Upper tier and lower tier

The tier depends on the quantity of dangerous substances present, against the thresholds in the regulations.[2][8]

  • Lower-tier operators must prepare a major accident prevention policy (MAPP).[6]
  • Upper-tier operators also prepare the more detailed safety report.[6] The plan for an emergency outside the site, the external emergency plan, is not the operator's: in Great Britain the local authority prepares it, in Northern Ireland HSENI arranges it, and in Ireland the designated local competent authorities prepare it. The operator supplies the information.[13][14][15]

Both tiers are more varied than people expect. Ireland's upper-tier list includes fuel terminals, pharmaceutical plants and distilleries. The lower-tier list includes power stations, dairy plants and distilleries.[1] Don't assume a sector from the tier.

05 / what the lists don t tell you

What the lists don't tell you

  • No contacts. The lists name the operator and site, not people.
  • No equipment or process detail beyond the basics. The GB search gives activities and hazard classes; the Irish PDFs give name and address only.
  • No spending or projects. Being on the list is an ongoing regulatory status, not a buying signal.
  • No full picture of hazardous industry. Plenty of process sites hold substances below the thresholds and never appear.
06 / where comah sites show up in other public records

Where COMAH sites show up in other public records

COMAH status also shapes what happens around a site, which gives you more dated evidence to join to the list.

  • Planning. In Ireland, the HSA's land-use planning pages cover consultation distances around COMAH establishments and requests from planning authorities for technical land-use planning advice.[11] In Great Britain, HSE is consulted on planning applications near major hazard sites.[12] A planning application for new tanks or process buildings at a listed site is often a better timing signal than the listing itself.
  • Environmental licences. Many COMAH establishments also hold industrial emissions or waste licences, which describe the processes on site. See Irish environmental register evidence, including its PRTR returns section, and the EEA's European Industrial Emissions Portal for EU facility data.[16]
  • Company registers. The operator named on the list is a starting point for finding the legal entity and its group.
07 / how suppliers use the lists

How suppliers use the lists

The lists are most useful as a filter and a cross-check, not as a list of target accounts on their own.

  1. Pick the jurisdiction and get the list. For Ireland, take both PDFs and note the "last updated" date. For Northern Ireland, copy the DAERA table. For Great Britain, either search by the names or postcodes of sites you already have, or start from a released full list and check it against the search.
  2. Match each establishment to a legal entity. The operator name on the list may be a trading name or a subsidiary. Check it against the company register.
  3. Join it with other public evidence. Licences describe processes; planning files show change on site.
  4. Record what the list shows, and the date. For example: "Lower tier on the HSA list dated 21 October 2026". Don't add claims the list doesn't make.
  5. Decide fit against your brief. A safety-valve, gas-detection or tank-instrumentation supplier may treat COMAH status as a strong fit criterion. A general MRO supplier may treat it as one signal among several.

Checklist before you use a COMAH entry

  • The list date is recorded
  • The tier is copied from the official list, not inferred
  • The operator is matched to a registered company
  • The site address is matched to the right facility, not the head office
  • Anything beyond "this site is a COMAH establishment of this tier" has its own source

A worked example (fictional)

A fictional supplier of pressure relief valves wants accounts in Ireland. It starts from the 51 upper-tier and 56 lower-tier entries on the HSA list. It removes sites outside its service area and those whose activity it doesn't serve, such as warehousing where it has no product fit. It then checks each remaining site's environmental licence for process detail, and searches recent planning applications at the same addresses. The result is a shortlist of named facilities, each recorded as "COMAH tier X, HSA list dated 21 October 2026, licence reference Y, process Z", with any planning application noted separately and the open question of which valves are installed left for the sales conversation.

08 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Is the COMAH list public?
Yes. Official lists are public in Ireland, Great Britain and Northern Ireland. The Health and Safety Authority publishes Ireland's upper-tier and lower-tier lists as PDFs, HSE provides a public search for Great Britain, and DAERA publishes Northern Ireland's operational establishments as a table.[1][4][5] For other EU countries, the European Environment Agency publishes Seveso information on its European Industrial Emissions Portal.[7]
Who regulates COMAH in Ireland?
The Health and Safety Authority (HSA) regulates COMAH establishments in Ireland. The rules are the Chemicals Act (Control of Major Accident Hazards Involving Dangerous Substances) Regulations 2015 (S.I. No. 209 of 2015), which implement the Seveso III Directive, 2012/18/EU.[2][15][17] The lists the HSA publishes show notified establishments by tier. They do not name buyers or show what equipment is installed.
What is the difference between upper tier and lower tier?
The difference is the quantity of dangerous substances present on the site, measured against the thresholds in the regulations.[2][8] Lower-tier operators must prepare a major accident prevention policy. Upper-tier operators must also produce a safety report.[6] The external emergency plan is prepared by the relevant authority, and the operator supplies the information.[13][14][15]
How many Seveso sites are there in Ireland?
Ireland's HSA lists, last updated 21 October 2026, named 51 upper-tier and 56 lower-tier establishments, 107 in total, by our count of the official PDFs, which we re-checked on 10 November 2026.[1] The figure is a snapshot of notified sites, not a list of buyers, and it changes as establishments are added or removed.
How many COMAH sites are there in Great Britain?
By our count, 911: HSE's list released on 12 August 2026 names 342 upper-tier and 569 lower-tier Great Britain sites.[10] HSE does not publish a running total on its website, and its covering letter says this information "is subject to frequent change".[9] Northern Ireland is separate: the DAERA table named 28, by our count, on 10 November 2026.[5]
How many COMAH sites are there in Northern Ireland?
The DAERA table listed 28 operational establishments when we counted the cells on 10 November 2026: 10 upper-tier and 18 lower-tier, by our count.[5] The page metadata was last modified on 20 November 2025; the table itself has no list-specific date. Reuse the figure only with the date of the count.
Is there a downloadable COMAH list for England, Scotland and Wales?
Not on the HSE website. Its public tool searches by full postcode, returning establishments within three miles, or by establishment or operator name of at least four characters.[4] That suits checking a known site. HSE releases its full list on request. The August 2026 release is published on WhatDoTheyKnow; the covering letter says this information "is subject to frequent change".[9][10]
Does a COMAH listing mean the site is buying equipment?
No. A COMAH or Seveso listing shows that a named site holds dangerous substances at or above the set thresholds. It does not show equipment, budgets, projects or contacts. Treat it as evidence of the type of site, and look for separate, dated evidence of change, such as a licence file or a planning application, before inferring demand.

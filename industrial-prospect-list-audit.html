B2B prospect list audit: an industrial sales checklist

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

A B2B prospect list audit is a one-off check of a list you already have: how many distinct companies meet your written brief, and why does each other row fail or wait? A row total cannot answer that.

Use it to check a bought, inherited or internally built company list before sales time goes into it. The worked example uses Irish companies; the same test applies to lists covering Ireland, the UK and the EU. Demand Range does evidence-backed industrial account research for Ireland, the UK and the EU; named contacts and outreach are separate scopes, not included by default.

An audit tests a list once, at a point in time. To keep an accepted list current afterwards, use the prospect list refresh checklist, which sets the review cadence and change triggers.

01 / Acceptance brief

Agree what counts before inspecting the list

Write down the activity, territory, business role, exclusions, evidence requirements and accepted record unit. Use the same brief for every row. If a criterion changes, version the brief and recheck the affected records.

Fictional brief FIC-BRIEF-01, version 1: an invented component supplier wants company accounts that manufacture complete food-packaging machinery at production facilities in the Republic of Ireland. Resellers without machinery production, businesses with only an Irish sales office, existing accounts and agreed channel conflicts are excluded. The batch, answer key, schema and policy carry that brief version; the teaching ruleset is OG02-HOLD-FIRST-1.

  • Accepted unit: one resolved company. Keep its relevant facilities as supporting detail.
  • Required evidence: company identity, attributable activity and production location, completed exclusion checks, and a resolved source/customer-use posture.
  • Additional signal rule: an invented statement proposing additional production or assembly space qualifies as a signal only after company fit passes.
  • Not required for inclusion: an active project. Equipment compatibility, buying need and procurement authority remain unknown.

This is an analyst-authored exercise policy, not an industry standard. For discovery and list-building, use the Irish manufacturing account-research guide; this page starts from a supplied list.

02 / Decision rules

Give every supplied row exactly one disposition

  1. Preserve the input. Assign a stable row ID. Keep the original batch, supplied label, unit and evidence card; record the resolved company separately. Recheck activity, location and exclusions for any newly resolved company instead of carrying earlier passes across.
  2. Resolve within-batch duplication. Choose one company anchor after reviewing all related rows. Each surplus row points to that anchor with the evidence for the relationship. A second site stays attached to its company. Shared group membership alone is insufficient to merge companies.
  3. Consolidate evidence before classifying. A duplicate may add a site or expose a contradiction. Keep the original cards and record the consolidated assessment separately. If FIC-R03 added an unresolved activity contradiction, FIC-R01 would be held while the two identity-confirmed surplus rows stay duplicates. If identity itself became uncertain, withdraw the duplicate assertion and hold the affected rows.
  4. Apply the hard gates. Check identity, activity, territory, exclusions, intended source use and material consistency. Under this exercise rule, any unresolved material gate means hold. Once uncertainties are resolved, a failed criterion means reject. All gates passing on a company anchor means include.
  5. Keep the policy local. Hold-before-reject is a conservative teaching choice. Real briefs set their own stopping and rejection rules; do not mix policies or brief versions in one count.
  6. Classify only included anchors. Use industry-matched for account fit without a qualifying signal, and opportunity-signal only with the additional signal evidence. A priority score cannot fill missing evidence.
Duplicate
A confirmed surplus reference to a retained company anchor. It adds no account.
Include
A unique company anchor meeting every required account gate in this exercise.
Hold
An unresolved candidate with a concrete next verification question.
Reject
A candidate with a failed brief criterion after material uncertainties are resolved. Keep the reason.

The deduplication boundary here is this supplied batch only. Existing-account exclusions are a separate check against the buyer's agreed records.

03 / Registry checks

Check the facts a list most often gets wrong

When you audit a real list (not the fictional batch below), check the facts that change without notice against the official registers.

  • Irish company status. The Companies Registration Office (CRO) publishes each company's registration status, with its last and next annual return dates, on its CORE search.[3] A dissolved company has no legal existence; a company dissolved after strike-off can be restored within 20 years.[4] Treat a Dissolved or struck-off status as a hold or reject for that row, and record the check date.
  • UK company status. Companies House's free Find and update company information service shows company status, for example live or dissolved, and now includes companies dissolved since January 2010.[5]
  • Licensed industrial sites in Ireland. Where the brief depends on a licensed activity, the EPA's licence and permit search is the starting point.[6] LEAP Online can be searched by licence number, company or facility name and shows the facility location and nature of the activity. Most LEAP records are published 30 calendar days after they are created.[7]
  • Any personal data in the list. If the list holds named contacts, the GDPR accuracy principle applies: personal data must be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date, and every reasonable step must be taken to erase or rectify inaccurate data without delay.[1] The UK GDPR keeps the same wording in Article 5(1)(d).[2]

Record the register, the status shown and the date checked in the worksheet's evidence fields. A register entry confirms identity and status; it does not establish fit with your brief.

Three decisions worth challenging

Why is the second workshop not a second inclusion? FIC-R03 supplies another site of the already counted FIC-C01. The company-level handoff keeps both FIC-S01 and FIC-S02 under FIC-R01. If the buyer needs a facility schedule, add that as a separate view.

Why are B and C not merged? The invented evidence identifies FIC-C02 and FIC-C03 as separate companies. Shared FIC-G01 group membership does not undo that; the unit is the company.

Why hold H rather than reject it? Its evidence neither establishes machinery production nor an excluded role. The next question is whether this company manufactures complete food-packaging machinery at the identified Irish workshop.

The editable answer key gives every row's reason, duplicate pointer, class and next check. Hold questions cover operator identity, production activity, permitted use and conflicting location evidence.

06 / Reconciliation

Balance rows first, then count included companies

Use this identity only when the dispositions are mutually exclusive and cover every supplied row:

Supplied rows = duplicate rows + included company anchors + held rows + rejected rows.

For this fictional batch: 14 = 2 + 4 + 5 + 3. Removing the two surplus references leaves twelve retained candidate rows, some of which still lack a resolved company identity. Check row IDs as well as totals: omitting one row and repeating another can leave the arithmetic balanced but the audit wrong.

The included view contains four distinct company keys: FIC-C01, FIC-C02, FIC-C03 and FIC-C12. Its classes reconcile separately: 4 = 3 industry-matched + 1 opportunity-signal. FIC-R04 has an invented proposed expansion, not a verified purchase requirement. These follow the research-class definitions.

Keep buyer-owned effort separate from acceptance

The blank worksheet leaves review and correction time for you to supply, each marked as measured or estimated. Minutes per included company is total effort divided by the included-company count, when that count is above zero; otherwise report not calculable. To compare sourcing options, weigh licensed platforms, in-house work and analyst research against the same written brief.

07 / Handoff

Accept a bounded result, not an unexplained row total

The fictional result is a candidate included view of four companies plus unresolved work. No minimum batch size, correction allowance or acceptance threshold is assumed; agree those terms separately.

  • Included view: one row per company, source-supported inclusion reasons, project status and open non-gating questions; keep linked sites.
  • Duplicate trail: every surplus row, its anchor and same-company evidence.
  • Hold queue: the missing or conflicting fact, next verification question, internal owner and review trigger. Do not move holds into the included total to reach a quota.
  • Rejected view: the failed rule and supporting evidence.
  • Review record: brief version, input version, reconciled counts, reviewed evidence and source-use boundary, and the release decision.

Balanced arithmetic cannot prove that the evidence is true or permitted for the intended use. Real acceptance still needs the agreed evidence, rights and review checks.

Buyer feedback

Prospect list not useful? Ask for a specific review

If your sales team says a prospect list is not useful, start with the company, the agreed requirement and the question you need answered. “Poor-quality leads” does not separate wrong companies from changed targeting or a different service expectation. Compare each concern with the original brief and the period the evidence describes.

Match the concern to the next question

The company appears outside the agreed criteria
Identify the disputed activity, territory, identity or business role. Ask which evidence supported inclusion and request a correction review.
Sales wanted a different target market
Was the restriction in the agreed brief? If it is new, request a brief-change review and identify the affected accounts.
The operation changed after the research cutoff
Establish when the change occurred and whether monitoring was included. A later change may need a refresh; a disputed earlier fact still needs investigation.
Sales already knows the business or sees a conflict
Identify the company or site and the exclusion instruction that applies. One known subsidiary does not automatically exclude its whole group.
The team expected a project, contact or sales response
Check the commissioned deliverable. Company fit can qualify without an active project; named contacts and outreach are separate scopes. If the missing item was expressly required, investigate it.

Copy this review request into your own working document

Complete one request per issue. Leave unknown dates blank.

  • Reference: delivery/version, company or site ID, issue ID.
  • Agreed requirement: the relevant brief clause and research cutoff.
  • Question: the disputed field, supporting evidence reference and what remains uncertain.
  • Requested response: explain inclusion, investigate a field, assess changed targeting, review a later change or clarify the deliverable.
  • Closure: the evidence needed, internal review role and any agreed review date.
  • Outcome: the response, revised record or brief version, affected accounts and remaining questions.

Keep the original record and acceptance decision, and record the review outcome separately. Use internal references only; keep personal-contact details and confidential lists in your own controlled process.

Returned delivery

Review a corrected list by issue, not just row total

A returned file can have the same number of rows while leaving the original questions unanswered. Keep each issue attached to its original company and version, and check what the response resolves before changing accepted-company counts.

QI-4 — the required evidence is still missing

Count the returned accepted set, not both versions together

Each snapshot contains five rows. v1 has one exercise-accepted company: QL-A. v2 has two: QL-A and QL-B. Q06 duplicates A; Q04/F and Q05/E remain held. Do not pool the versions or accept held companies to fill volume.

If v2 were an incremental patch instead: absence would not mean deletion. Require a known base version and explicit update, withdrawal or addition instructions before merging.

The handoff boundaries above still apply. A genuine later change belongs in the refresh cycle; it must not erase an open delivery dispute.

Irish imports of producer capital goods in 2026

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Ireland imported €24,543.4 million of producer capital goods in January to September 2026, 17.1% more than the €20,965.3 million imported in the same months of 2025, by our sum of the CSO's monthly figures. By our count, that is the highest January-to-September total in the CSO series, which starts in 1975, in current prices, not adjusted for inflation. September 2026 is still provisional.[1][2]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one official series: the CSO's imports by main use, table TSM07, and its "producer capital goods" line. It explains what that line covers, how it differs from the machinery chapter of the trade statistics (SITC 7), and how an equipment supplier can read it. For what manufacturers buy to use up in production, which excludes capital goods, see manufacturing purchases by EU country. For how busy factories are, as background for capex timing, see manufacturing capacity utilisation. For gross fixed capital formation in manufacturing by country, see manufacturing investment by country.

01 / key facts

Key facts

CSO TSM07 and TSM09, updated 18 November 2026; Goods Exports and Imports September 2026; checked 24 November 2026.

  • Producer capital goods imports were €24,543.4m in January to September 2026, against €20,965.3m a year earlier: €3,578.1m or 17.1% more, by our sum of monthly values.[1]
  • September 2026 alone was €3,026.7m, 41.4% above September 2025 (€2,140.6m), by our count. The September figure is provisional.[2][4]
  • Producer capital goods were 19.9% of all goods imports in January to September 2026 (€123,207.0m), by our count. The CSO puts total goods imports for the period at €123.2 billion.[1][2]
  • Imports from the EU-27 fell 3.4% to €9,404.9m. Imports from everywhere outside the EU-27 and the UK rose 38.9% to €13,457.5m, more than the whole increase, by our count.[1]
  • TSM07 does not show which products drove the rise. Over the same months, the CSO reports that imports of office machines and automatic data processing machines doubled to €16.4 billion, a product measure that is not a subset of producer capital goods.[2]

04 / what producer capital goods covers

What "producer capital goods" covers

TSM07 sorts goods imports by main use into three groups: producers' capital goods ready for use, consumption goods ready for use, and materials for further production. The CSO describes these as "a reclassification of imports in terms of national accounts aggregates." "Producer Capital Goods" in TSM07 is the first of those groups.[1][5]

Three points matter when using it:

  • It is the CSO's own main-use grouping. The CSO does not publish which products sit in each group, so the line cannot be matched product by product to other classifications, such as the UN's Broad Economic Categories. This page uses only the CSO grouping.[1][5]
  • It is not SITC 7. SITC section 7, machinery and transport equipment, is a product chapter. It counts goods by what they are, whatever they are used for, so it includes cars, phones and computers bought by households as well as plant. In January to September 2026, SITC divisions 71 to 79 together came to €57,139.4m, by our sum of TSM09, more than twice the producer capital goods total.[3]
  • It is not investment. Imports show equipment arriving in the country. They leave out equipment made in Ireland, and they do not show which sector bought the goods. Materials for further production, the inputs that plants use up, are a separate group: €63,609.1m in January to September 2026 (other than agriculture), 27.7% above 2025, by our sum.[1]

If you sell industrial machinery, the SITC divisions are the closer product measure. Their totals for the same months, from TSM09, all countries, are below. They are by product and are not a subset of the producer capital goods line.[3]

05 / using the trend alongside project signals

Using the trend alongside project signals

A national import total tells you whether the market for equipment is growing. It does not tell you who is buying. Use it as the backdrop, then move to named, dated evidence:

  1. Size the year, not the month. Quote the year-to-date figure and its change. A single month such as May 2026 (+88.3%) can come from a few large deliveries.[1]
  2. Check the product mix before you claim machinery demand. In 2026 the producer capital goods line rose while office and data processing machine imports doubled. Our reading, not a CSO finding, is that part of the rise may be computing equipment rather than process plant, because TSM07 does not show which products are in the line. The SITC divisions above help separate the two.[2][3]
  3. Look at origin. In 2026 the growth came from outside the EU-27 and the UK, by our count. A supplier based in the EU should not read the headline as growth in its own market share.[1]
  4. Pair it with project evidence. For planned sites, construction databases and their limits, see industrial project leads in Ireland and the UK. For announced foreign direct investment, see IDA Ireland announcements as account signals.
  5. Turn signals into accounts. National figures stop at the border. Project-intelligence briefs connect dated project signals to the companies and sites behind them.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Are Irish capital goods imports rising in 2026?
Yes, by value. Producer capital goods imports were €24,543.4m in January to September 2026, 17.1% more than in the same months of 2025, by our sum of CSO TSM07. By our count, that is the highest nine-month total since 1975, in current prices, not adjusted for inflation. September 2026 is provisional and the latest months are revised.[1][4]
What are producer capital goods?
They are one of the CSO's three main-use groups for imports: producers' capital goods ready for use, consumption goods ready for use and materials for further production. The CSO calls them a reclassification of imports in terms of national accounts aggregates. TSM07 does not list the products in each group.[1][5]
Is this the same as SITC 7, machinery and transport equipment?
No. SITC 7 is a product chapter that counts goods by what they are, including cars, phones and computers, whoever buys them. Producer capital goods is a grouping by main use. In January to September 2026, SITC divisions 71 to 79 summed to €57,139.4m, by our count, against €24,543.4m of producer capital goods.[1][3]
Where do Ireland's capital goods imports come from?
In January to September 2026, 38.3% came from the EU-27 (€9,404.9m, down 3.4%) and 6.8% from Great Britain and Northern Ireland (€1,681.0m), by our count of CSO TSM07. The remaining €13,457.5m came from elsewhere and rose 38.9%. TSM07 does not split that remainder by country.[1]
How often is the CSO data updated?
Monthly. The CSO's Goods Exports and Imports release updates TSM07 and TSM09, and the September 2026 data was published on 18 November 2026. Each release revises the previous month and the month six back. SITC 79 figures, which include aircraft, can be revised again when quarterly balance of payments data is added.[2][4]

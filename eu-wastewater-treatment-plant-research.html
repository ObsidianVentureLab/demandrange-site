How to research EU urban wastewater treatment plants

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

If you supply pumps, valves, aeration or instrumentation into urban wastewater plants, start from the official plant-level file, not from a country headline. The European Environment Agency publishes the data that Member States report under the Urban Waste Water Treatment Directive. The file can tell you how many plants were reported in each country, their organic design capacity, the load entering each plant and the treatment types recorded. It does not name a buyer or an equipment order.

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns the EU plant-level reported file and what the recast directive asks of larger plants. Irish programme funding and project evidence stay on our Irish water-investment guide. AMP8 and the English and Welsh companies stay on our UK water industry supplier research. Irish discharge-location datasets stay on our environmental-register guide. For how municipal and process-water roles are scoped before a plant is treated as a prospect, see our pump and flow-control prospecting guide.

01 / key facts

Key facts

EEA Waterbase UWWTD; Directive (EU) 2024/3019; checked 10 November 2026.

  • The dataset used here is "Urban Waste Water Treatment Directive, Waterbase reported under UWWTD data call 2023 - FINAL PUBLIC VERSION, Oct. 2025 (Tabular data)". It is edition 10.00, identifier eea_t_uwwtd-final_p_2023_v10_r00, in the series "Waterbase - UWWTD: Urban Waste Water Treatment Directive – reported data". The series metadata gives a publication date of 3 March 2026. The temporal coverage is 1 January 2022 to 31 December 2023.[1][2][3]
  • The downloaded package also contains Waterbase_UWWTD_13thCall_final.xlsx and Waterbase_UWWTD_13thCall_final.accdb. The CSV tables we counted, including UWWTPS.csv, have file dates of 21 October 2025. We downloaded the public package on 10 November 2026.[3]
  • The UWWTPS table "includes data on individual waste water treatment plants and collecting systems without UWWTP". A row with uwwCollectingSystem = ISCON is a "Collecting system is connected to UWWTP". A row with NOTCON is a "Collecting system is not connected to UWWTP". uwwCapacity is "Organic design capacity (p.e.) of the waste water treatment plant". uwwLoadEnteringUWWTP is "Load entering UWWTP (p.e.)". Status values are 1 (active), 0 (inactive) and 2 (temporary inactive).[4][5][6][7][8]
  • By our count of UWWTPS.csv, the file has 26,064 unique plant or collecting-system records from 30 countries: 23,223 ISCON and 2,841 NOTCON. Of the ISCON records, 22,558 are active. The 27 EU members account for 21,517 of those active plants. The United Kingdom is not in this file. Switzerland, Norway and Iceland reported and are shown separately below; they are not EU members.[3]
  • By our count, those 22,558 active plants have a combined organic design capacity of 729,872,216 p.e. and a combined load entering of 525,266,076 p.e. (every active ISCON row has both fields filled). 22,136 have secondary treatment recorded as Yes, 14,425 have N-removal as Yes, 13,887 have P-removal as Yes, and 15,738 have "More stringent treatment" (uwwOtherTreatment) as Yes.[3][9]
  • For the recast directive, the test is load, not design capacity. By our count, 7,628 active plants (7,307 in the EU-27) have a reported load entering of 10,000 p.e. or more, and 558 (535 in the EU-27) have a load entering of 150,000 p.e. or more. The same file's design-capacity counts at those thresholds are higher (9,651 and 851), so a capacity band is not a substitute for the load test.[3][10]
  • Directive (EU) 2024/3019 of 27 November 2024, concerning urban wastewater treatment (recast), was published in the Official Journal on 12 December 2024 (OJ L, 2024/3019). Member States must transpose Articles 2 to 11 and 14 to 26 and Annexes I, III, V and VI by 31 July 2027. Articles 12 and 13 and Annexes II and IV apply from 1 August 2027.[10][11]
  • Under Article 8, discharges from urban wastewater treatment plants "treating urban wastewater with a load of 150 000 p.e. and above" must meet quaternary treatment requirements by 31 December 2033 for 20% of those plants, 31 December 2039 for 60%, and 31 December 2045 for all of them. Article 11 requires energy audits every four years, the first by 31 December 2028 for plants treating a load of 100,000 p.e. and above, and sets national renewable-energy equivalence targets for plants treating a load of 10,000 p.e. and above, reaching 100% by 31 December 2045.[10]
  • Most countries in this file give a situation date of 31 December 2022. France's reported situation date is 5 December 2023 and Latvia's is 12 July 2024. Do not mix those stocks as if they were the same day.[3]

03 / how to read a plant row

How to read a plant row

Open the tabular package from the EEA datahub series page or the dataset record, and work from UWWTPS.csv (or the same table in the Access or Excel file). Keep the dataset name, edition, identifier and the date you downloaded it in the research note.

  1. Keep ISCON rows when you are counting treatment plants. Keep NOTCON rows only when the brief is about collecting systems that are not connected to a UWWTP. The data dictionary says the table covers both, and "If a collecting system is not connected to a treatment plant, but collects and discharges water, it is considered as active."[4][6][8]
  2. Use uwwState = 1 for plants in operation at the reported situation date. Inactive plants "should be reported one reported period only after the change from ‘active’ to ‘inactive’."[8]
  3. Record both capacity and load. Design capacity sizes the works; load entering is the figure that lines up with the recast thresholds. For Ireland, by our count, 188 active plants have a combined design capacity of 5,646,280 p.e. and a combined load entering of 4,883,790 p.e.; 7 have design capacity of 150,000 p.e. or more, but only 2 have a load entering at that threshold.[3][5][7][10]
  4. Read the treatment flags as recorded technologies, not as a compliance verdict. uwwPrimaryTreatment and uwwSecondaryTreatment are "Primary treatment" and "Secondary treatment". uwwNRemoval and uwwPRemoval are "N-removal" and "P-removal". uwwOtherTreatment is "More stringent treatment". The dictionary says each type available at a plant should be reported, so a plant can have more than one flag set to Yes.[9]
  5. Confirm the operator, the works and any live project from a second source. The file has plant names and coordinates, but a name and a point are not a purchasing record. For Irish programme evidence use the Irish water-investment guide. For UK company programmes use UK water industry supplier research.

The public spatial layer of treatment plants is a narrower extract of the same data call: the metadata says inactive or disconnected plants, and plants without coordinates or with obviously wrong coordinates, were removed. Use the full tabular file when you need a complete reported stock, and the spatial file when you need a map of the active, connected plants with usable coordinates. By our count, all 22,558 active ISCON rows in the tabular file have coordinates filled.[13]

04 / what the recast directive asks of larger plants

What the recast directive asks of larger plants

The figures above are reported under Directive 91/271/EEC, which is the legal basis named in the dataset abstract. Directive (EU) 2024/3019 recasts those rules. "‘quaternary treatment’ means treatment of urban wastewater by a process which reduces a broad spectrum of micropollutants in urban wastewater;" "‘1 population equivalent’ or ‘(1 p.e.)’ means the organic biodegradable load per day, having a five-day biochemical oxygen demand (BOD5) of 60 g of oxygen per day;" "‘load’ means the amount of organic biodegradable matter measured as BOD5 in urban wastewater, expressed in p.e., or of any pollutant or nutrient, expressed in mass units per time;"[2][10]

For plants treating a load of 150,000 p.e. and above, Article 8 requires quaternary treatment of discharges by 31 December 2033 for 20% of those plants, 31 December 2039 for 60%, and 31 December 2045 for all of them. Article 8 also sets later, percentage-based deadlines for agglomerations of 10,000 p.e. and above that discharge into areas listed because micropollutants represent a risk: 10% by 31 December 2033, 30% by 31 December 2036, 60% by 31 December 2039, and 100% by 31 December 2045. The 10,000 p.e. list is not in the 2023 reported file; Member States must establish it by 31 December 2030.[10]

Article 7 sets separate tertiary-treatment (nitrogen or phosphorus, or both) deadlines for plants treating a load of 150,000 p.e. and above, including 31 December 2039 for all such discharges, and a longer ladder for agglomerations of 10,000 p.e. and above discharging to areas sensitive to eutrophication. Article 11 requires energy audits of plants and connected collecting systems every four years, the first by 31 December 2028 for plants treating a load of 100,000 p.e. and above, and by 31 December 2032 for plants treating a load of 10,000 p.e. and above but below 100,000 p.e. At national level, renewable energy generated on-site or off-site by or on behalf of the owners or operators of plants treating a load of 10,000 p.e. and above must be equivalent to at least 20% of the total annual energy used by such plants by 31 December 2030, 40% by 31 December 2035, 70% by 31 December 2040, and 100% by 31 December 2045, with stated derogations. By our count, 920 active plants in this file (886 in the EU-27) have a reported load entering of 100,000 p.e. or more.[10]

Article 9 requires Member States to take measures so that, by 31 December 2028, producers who place products listed in Annex III on the market have extended producer responsibility covering "at least 80 % of the full costs for complying with the requirements set out in Article 8", including investment and operational costs of quaternary treatment. That is a financing rule, not evidence that a named plant has a budgeted upgrade.[10]

None of those deadlines makes a listed plant into a current enquiry. They tell you which official load bands will face new treatment, monitoring and energy requirements, and when. An energy-audit signal for an Irish or EU industrial site is a different legal track; see energy-audit supplier signals.

05 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Which EEA dataset lists EU urban wastewater treatment plants?
The file to use is "Urban Waste Water Treatment Directive, Waterbase reported under UWWTD data call 2023 - FINAL PUBLIC VERSION, Oct. 2025 (Tabular data)", edition 10.00, identifier eea_t_uwwtd-final_p_2023_v10_r00. It sits in the Waterbase UWWTD series, whose metadata gives a publication date of 3 March 2026. The plant table is UWWTPS.csv. We downloaded the public package on 10 November 2026.
Does every row in the EEA table represent a treatment plant?
No. The official table includes individual waste water treatment plants and collecting systems without a UWWTP. Use ISCON rows, "Collecting system is connected to UWWTP", when you are counting plants, and treat NOTCON rows as collecting systems that are not connected to a UWWTP. By our count, 2,841 of the 26,064 records are NOTCON.
How many active urban wastewater treatment plants were reported for the EU?
By our count of active ISCON rows, the 27 EU members reported 21,517 active plants in this file, with a combined load entering of 506,688,519 p.e. Across all 30 reporting countries the active-plant count is 22,558. Most situation dates are 31 December 2022; France and Latvia differ, as noted above.
Is the United Kingdom in the EEA UWWTD reported-data file?
No. The 30 reporters in this final public package are the 27 EU members plus Switzerland, Norway and Iceland. There is no United Kingdom row. For English and Welsh company programmes and AMP8, use our UK water industry supplier research page, which is a different official source.
What does Directive (EU) 2024/3019 require of larger plants?
For plants treating 150,000 p.e. and above, Article 8 requires quaternary treatment by 31 December 2033 for 20% of those plants, by 31 December 2039 for 60%, and by 31 December 2045 for all of them. Article 11 adds energy-audit and renewable-energy duties for plants treating 10,000 p.e. and above. Main articles must be transposed by 31 July 2027.
Does a listed plant mean there is equipment to supply?
No. A reported plant confirms a works, a design capacity, a load and the treatment types recorded at the situation date. It does not show installed equipment, a capital budget, a procurement route or a buyer. Use the file to size the stock, then confirm any live project from a second official source before treating the site as an opportunity.

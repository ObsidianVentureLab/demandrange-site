Are EU factory-gate prices for manufacturing rising or falling?

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Yes. EU-27 factory-gate prices for manufacturing were 7.3% higher in September 2026 than a year earlier, and every EU country had a positive year-on-year rate that month, by our count. The EU-27 index stood at 125.7 (2021 = 100), up 1.2% on August 2026. Ireland was in the middle of the pack, at +7.4%, 12th of the 27 member countries by our count. An early October 2026 cell for Ireland, published before the EU-27 October aggregate, shows a faster rise of 10.2% year-on-year. These figures are for manufacturing (NACE Rev. 2 section C) only. The wider industry index, which includes energy, is higher and is kept off this page's headline.[1][2][3]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one thing: Eurostat's monthly industrial producer price index for manufacturing by EU country (dataset sts_inpp_m, all markets). It is a factory-gate price measure, not a production-volume or sales-value series. For turnover, see EU manufacturing turnover by country. For purchases, see EU manufacturing purchases by country.

Key facts

03 / ireland s recent path

Ireland's recent path

Ireland's September 2026 year-on-year rate of +7.4% sits close to the EU-27 rate of +7.3%, but the Irish index level is lower: 110.8, against 125.7 for the EU-27 (2021 = 100). The early October 2026 cell then moves Ireland to 114.5, +3.3% on the month and +10.2% on a year earlier, still without a published EU-27 comparator for that month.[1][2][4]

The recent rise follows a stretch of year-on-year falls. Irish manufacturing producer prices were lower than a year earlier from February 2025 through March 2026. The low point in that stretch was −6.4% in December 2025. The rate turned positive in April 2026, at +1.4%, and reached +7.4% in September 2026 and +10.2% in October 2026.[1][2][4]

04 / keep energy out of the manufacturing headline

Keep energy out of the manufacturing headline

A wider "industry" producer price index is not a manufacturing figure. Energy is pulling that broader index up, in Ireland and in the EU-27, so this page's headline and country table use NACE section C only.[3][4]

In October 2026, Ireland's electricity, gas, steam and air conditioning prices (NACE D) were 90.6% higher than a year earlier, with an index of 145.6 (2021 = 100). Industry including energy (B–D or B–E36, which published the same cells for Ireland in that month) was +14.5% year-on-year, index 116.7, against +10.2% for manufacturing alone.[4]

In September 2026, the EU-27 industry index for B–E36 was +9.4% year-on-year, index 132.6. The B–D cut was +9.6% (I), index 133.3. MIG energy was +35.1%, index 182.7. Coke and refined petroleum (C19), which sits inside manufacturing, was +72.0%, index 197.5. (I) is Eurostat's published flag on that B–D cell; see Eurostat's metadata for the flag, and do not treat the cell as unflagged.[3][5]

05 / what is rising inside manufacturing

What is rising inside manufacturing

The EU-27 manufacturing rise in September 2026 is not a food-price story. Producer prices for food, beverages and tobacco (C10–C12) were 0.5% lower than a year earlier (I), with an index of 129.5. Intermediate goods rose 7.3%, index 124.0, the same year-on-year rate as manufacturing as a whole. Capital goods rose a more modest 2.5%, index 117.6. Among published divisions, basic metals (C24) were +15.1%, index 126.6, and chemicals (C20) were +10.6%, index 126.1. Rubber and plastic (C22) rose 5.8%, index 120.9. Pharmaceuticals (C21) rose 1.9%, index 106.6.[3]

For Ireland, a narrower set of divisions is published. In October 2026, chemicals (C20) were +16.5% year-on-year, index 138.5, the same index and rate as in September 2026. Other published Irish cells in October 2026 were other non-metallic mineral products (C23) +6.7%, index 135.0; rubber and plastic (C22) +3.4%, index 117.6; food products (C10) +2.5%, index 120.5; and paper and paper products (C17) +1.7%, index 109.8.[4]

Irish cells in this dataset, for January 2025 to October 2026, are confidential (C) for coke and refined petroleum (C19), pharmaceuticals (C21), computer, electronic and optical products (C26), machinery and equipment n.e.c. (C28), and for the MIG groupings. Those gaps are confidential cells, not a reason to borrow a figure from another table.[4]

06 / what the price index can and cannot tell a supplier

What the price index can and cannot tell a supplier

The producer price index shows whether factory-gate prices in manufacturing are rising or falling, and how Ireland compares with other EU countries in the latest complete month. It does not name plants, buyers or budgets, and a rise is not, on its own, evidence that a factory is busier or that a purchasing budget has grown. Pair it with a sales-value measure on EU manufacturing turnover by country and with EU manufacturing purchases by country.[1][2]

Use the figures with three limits. First, keep energy out of the manufacturing comparison: the industry-including-energy index is running higher, in Ireland and in the EU-27, because of energy prices. Second, Ireland's October 2026 print is an early national cell, not a rank against a published EU-27 October figure. Third, several Irish manufacturing divisions are confidential in this dataset, so an Irish sector comparison is only as complete as the published cells.[3][4]

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Are EU factory-gate prices for manufacturing rising or falling?
Yes, they were rising in September 2026. The EU-27 manufacturing producer price index was 7.3% higher than in September 2025 and 1.2% higher than in August 2026, at 125.7 (2021 = 100). By our count, all 27 EU countries had a positive year-on-year rate that month, from +2.2% (P) in Malta to +23.8% in Greece.[1][2]
How do Irish manufacturing producer prices compare with the EU?
Ireland's year-on-year rise was 7.4% in September 2026, close to the EU-27 rate of 7.3%, and 12th of the 27 EU countries by our count. The Irish index was 110.8 (2021 = 100), up 0.5% on the month. An early October 2026 cell, published before the EU-27 October aggregate, shows Ireland at +10.2% year-on-year and +3.3% on the month.[1][2][4]
What does the industrial producer price index measure?
It measures factory-gate prices charged by industrial producers, for all markets (domestic plus non-domestic). This page uses Eurostat's monthly series for manufacturing (NACE Rev. 2 section C), not seasonally adjusted, with 2021 = 100. It is not a consumer price index, a production-volume measure, or a sales-value index, and it is not the domestic-only producer price series.[1][5]
Why keep energy out of the manufacturing headline?
Because energy is pulling the wider industry index up. In October 2026, Ireland's electricity, gas, steam and air conditioning prices (NACE D) were 90.6% higher than a year earlier, and industry including energy was up 14.5%, against 10.2% for manufacturing alone. In September 2026 the EU-27 industry index including energy was up 9.4%, against 7.3% for manufacturing.[3][4]
Is the EU manufacturing price rise a food-price story?
No. EU-27 producer prices for food, beverages and tobacco were 0.5% lower in September 2026 than a year earlier (I). The manufacturing rise was closer to intermediate goods, which were up 7.3%, the same rate as manufacturing as a whole. Chemicals were up 10.6% and basic metals 15.1%, while capital goods rose a more modest 2.5%.[3]
Does this Eurostat table include a UK manufacturing figure?
No. The United Kingdom has no manufacturing cell in this dataset for January 2025 to October 2026, so this page does not publish a UK rate. Eight EU countries, including Ireland, already have an October 2026 cell. The EU-27 October aggregate had not been published when the sources were checked on 24 November 2026.[1][2][4]

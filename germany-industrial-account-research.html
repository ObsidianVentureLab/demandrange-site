How to find industrial customers in Germany: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

Germany has thousands of large manufacturers, so the hard part of finding industrial customers in Germany is choosing the right plants, not finding enough names. Build the list in layers from free public sources: register, activity code, products, plants and buying signals. Then check each account against your brief.

Key facts (Eurostat 2024 data, Destatis and the German registers; checked 28 October 2026)

  • Eurostat counted 198,560 manufacturing enterprises in Germany in 2024, of which 4,415 employed 250 or more people.[8][9]
  • Eurostat counted 220,581 manufacturing local units (sites) in Germany in 2024, about 1.1 per manufacturing enterprise by our calculation.[8][10]
  • Viewing the German Handelsregister and Unternehmensregister has been free since 1 August 2022.[1][2]
  • Destatis introduced WZ 2025, Germany's version of NACE Rev. 2.1, on 1 January 2025; it runs in parallel with WZ 2008 during the switch.[3][16]

01 / how big the german manufacturing base is

How big the German manufacturing base is

Eurostat's structural business statistics give these figures for German manufacturing (NACE Section C) in 2024:[8][9][10]

Germany, manufacturing, 2024 Count
Enterprises 198,560
Enterprises with 50–249 persons employed 14,017
Enterprises with 250 or more persons employed 4,415
Persons employed 7,901,036
Local units (sites) 220,581

An enterprise is the business; a local unit is an enterprise, or part of one, at a geographically identified place.[10] Germany has about 1.1 local units per manufacturing enterprise, so many sites sit behind one entity. Most enterprises are small: 132,433 employ fewer than 10 people.[9] Treat these as market-sizing figures, not as a list. Eurostat permits reuse of these figures with acknowledgement.[15] For the other EU countries, see our EU manufacturing enterprises by country table.

02 / build the list in layers

Build the list in layers

  1. Companies. Viewing the Handelsregister and Unternehmensregister has been free since 1 August 2022.[1][2] One exception: balance sheets that the smallest companies have filed for deposit rather than publication still cost €1.00 per retrieval, plus German sales tax.[2]
  2. Activity. WZ 2025 codes, Germany's version of NACE Rev. 2.1, introduced on 1 January 2025.[3][16]
  3. Products. Export, association and trade-fair directories.[4][11][12]
  4. Plants. thru.de and the EU Industrial Emissions Portal.[5][6]
  5. Buying signals. Tenders on TED.[7]
03 / know the german company landscape

Know the German company landscape

  • Legal forms. GmbH and AG are the usual operating companies. A GmbH & Co. KG is a limited partnership whose general partner is a GmbH. Record the operating entity and the group.
  • Head office vs plant. Many Mittelstand groups run several plants (Werke) under one legal entity. The register shows the registered seat, so you have to find the plants from websites, permits and PRTR data.
  • Language. Register entries, permits and many technical pages are in German. Plan your keyword lists in German, for example Pumpen, Armaturen, Abwasserbehandlung, Instandhaltung.
  • Chamber membership is not a signal. By law, almost every business assessed for trade tax with an establishment in an IHK district belongs to that Chamber of Industry and Commerce (IHK).[14] "IHK member" tells you nothing about fit.
04 / wz 2008 and wz 2025 run side by side

WZ 2008 and WZ 2025 run side by side

Destatis introduced WZ 2025 on 1 January 2025.[3][16] It replaces WZ 2008, but both apply in parallel until the switch is complete.[3] Annual structural statistics move to WZ 2025 from reporting year 2025; short-term statistics move from early 2028.[3] WZ 2025 has 651 classes against 615 in WZ 2008, and a fifth level of 983 subclasses (Unterklassen) below the NACE classes.[3]

For a list, that means:

  • Codes in older datasets, directories and your own CRM are probably WZ 2008.
  • Keep both codes on each row until your sources have moved, and map them with the Eurostat correspondence tables described in our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.
  • Don't filter one dataset on WZ 2008 and another on WZ 2025 and then merge them unchecked.

VDMA members are mostly suppliers of machinery, so the list helps most when your customers are machine builders or you need to know your competitors. Exhibitor lists show companies active in a product area this year, which a register code can't.

06 / step by step

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat figures, then define it with WZ 2025 / NACE Rev. 2.1 codes. See our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide for the changes.
  2. Pull plants from thru.de or the EU portal for process industries, and companies from the export database, the VDMA list and exhibitor lists for discrete manufacturing.
  3. Resolve each one in the register. Confirm the legal entity, seat and group.
  4. Check the website Impressum. German commercial websites must show the provider's name and address and, for companies, the legal form and an authorised representative, plus the commercial register and register number where the business is registered.[13] Match these to the register entry, so the website, the plant and the legal entity are the same business.
  5. Note the plant-level functions that own the decision, such as Werkleiter (plant manager), Leiter Instandhaltung (maintenance), Technischer Einkauf (technical purchasing) and Leiter Technik (engineering). Record the function, not a person.
  6. Add a trigger: a new permit, a hiring wave for maintenance engineers, a TED tender or a published expansion.
  7. Log everything in the free account research template.

Fictional example: A fictional Irish maker of industrial heat exchangers targets German food and drink plants. It filters the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for food and drink installations in two German states and resolves each operator in the Handelsregister. It finds that one fictional dairy group runs three plants under one GmbH, and that the Impressum on the group website shows the same register number. It records the three plants as separate accounts under one group, each with maintenance as the owning function. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

07 / common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Using only head-office data. In Germany, buying influence often sits at plant level.
  • Counting enterprises when you sell to sites. The local-unit count is higher.
  • Ignoring WZ 2008 vs WZ 2025 differences when combining datasets.
  • Translating keywords literally. Use the German technical terms buyers actually use.
08 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Is the German company register free?

Mostly. Viewing register data and documents has been free since 1 August 2022. Balance sheets that the smallest companies filed for deposit cost €1.00 per retrieval, plus sales tax.[2]

How many manufacturing companies are there in Germany?

Eurostat counts 198,560 manufacturing enterprises in Germany in 2024, of which 4,415 employ 250 or more people. There were 220,581 manufacturing local units.[8][9][10]

Should I use WZ 2008 or WZ 2025 codes?

Both, for now. WZ 2025 applies from 1 January 2025, but the two run in parallel during the switch, and short-term statistics stay on WZ 2008 until early 2028.[3][16]

What are WZ codes in Germany?

WZ is Germany's classification of economic activities, published by Destatis. WZ 2025 is Germany's version of NACE Rev. 2.1, with 651 classes and 983 subclasses (Unterklassen). WZ 2025 applies from 1 January 2025 and is replacing WZ 2008; the two apply in parallel during the switch.[3][16]

Do public sources show who buys at each plant?

No. They show companies, sites, activities and some buying signals. Roles and buying plans need separate evidence.

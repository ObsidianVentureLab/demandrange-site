How to read IDA Ireland investment announcements as account signals

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

IDA Ireland approved 323 investments in 2025, up 38% on 2024, associated with the expected creation of 15,319 jobs.[1][2] Each named announcement behind those totals tells a supplier that a company intends to build, expand or research at a site in Ireland. This guide shows how to turn an official announcement into a site-level account record, what fields to capture, what an announcement does not tell you, and where to check the site next.

An announcement is a signal of intended investment. It is not a purchase order, a budget for your product or proof that hiring or construction has started.

01 / key facts

Key facts

IDA Ireland 2025 results and Annual Report & Accounts 2025; checked 10 November 2026.

  • IDA Ireland secured 323 investments in 2025, a 38% increase on the previous year, according to its results press release of 18 December 2025. The 2024 baseline was 234 investments. By our count, (323 − 234) / 234 = 38.0%.[1][3]
  • The 2025 approvals are associated with expected future job creation of 15,319, as stated in IDA's Annual Report & Accounts 2025. The December 2025 press release rounded this to "over 15,300". These are expected jobs, not jobs already in post.[1][2]
  • IDA Ireland reports on-the-ground employment in its client companies of 312,468 in 2025, up 1.5% year on year, in its Annual Report & Accounts 2025 (source: Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment (DETE) Annual Employment Survey 2025). The December 2025 press release had given 312,400.[1][2]
  • 78 of the 323 investments came from companies new to Ireland and 68 were expansions by existing clients. By our count, 78 / 323 = 24.1%, which IDA states as 24%.[1][2]
  • 183 investments, which IDA states as 57%, were won for regional locations outside the capital.[1][2]
  • IDA does not publish a complete public list of the 323 approvals. Its public press-release stream is a selection of named announcements, mixed with other news items.[1][5]
02 / what the 2025 results tell a supplier

What the 2025 results tell a supplier

The 2025 totals describe IDA's whole client base, which spans "a broad mix of sectors": manufacturing, information and communication, and business and financial services. They are not manufacturing-only figures. The annual report lists 1,884 supported companies in 2025.[2]

Within the client base, IDA's 2025 employment table puts modern manufacturing at 114,962 jobs (up 3.5%) and traditional manufacturing at 22,913 (up 1.2%), out of 312,468 across all sectors. Those are employment stock figures for existing clients, not a breakdown of the 2025 approvals by sector, which IDA does not publish in the report.[2]

Two more 2025 figures are useful as context for capital-equipment and site-services suppliers:

  • 80 research, development and innovation investments were associated with a record €2.5bn in client expenditure commitments.[1][2]
  • 31 sustainability projects had €1bn of capital committed by clients.[1][2]

Expenditure figures in the same results, such as total client expenditure of €40.7bn and capital projects of €12.8bn, refer to 2024, the latest year of IDA's Annual Business Survey of Economic Impact, not to 2025.[2]

The employment headline was revised between publications. The December 2025 press release gave 312,400. IDA Ireland's later annual report, with a landing page dated 9 September 2026, reports 312,468 (source: DETE Annual Employment Survey 2025) against a revised 2024 total of 307,742. Use the later official table, and date any figure you quote.[1][2]

03 / where the announcements are published

Where the announcements are published

IDA Ireland publishes named investment announcements in its press-release stream. On 10 November 2026 the newest dated item was from 20 October 2026. The stream also carries trade missions and other news, so filter it for investment items.[5]

The stream is not a register. It covers announcements the company and IDA chose to make public, so many of the 323 approvals will never appear as a named release. Treat the stream as a source of named sites, not as a complete count.[5][2]

04 / the fields to capture from each announcement

The fields to capture from each announcement

Record only what the announcement states. Leave a field blank, and mark it as "not stated", when the release does not give it.

Field What to record Watch for
Company The legal or trading name in the release Group versus local entity; confirm the Irish entity separately
Site The location as the release writes it A town or region name is not a plant address
Activity Manufacturing, research and development, services or a mix A research centre is not a production plant
Capital The stated euro amount and period Often multi-year; not a purchasing budget
Jobs Planned jobs and the target date Construction roles and existing headcount are different figures
Timing Construction start, first output or hiring end date These are plans, not completions
Source Release URL and date Keep it with the record for later checks
05 / three official examples

Three official examples

These are real IDA Ireland announcements, read on 10 November 2026. They show that a release does not always carry every field.

GE HealthCare, 31 January 2025. The company announced a €132 million investment to expand an existing contrast-media fill and finish manufacturing site, with a new 3,000m² facility and first doses expected by the end of 2027. The release names the site and county. It expects over 250 construction roles but gives no permanent operating headcount. Record the capital, the activity and the timing, and leave permanent jobs as "not stated".[6]

Ericsson, 24 April 2025. The company announced a €200 million research, development and innovation investment over three years at an existing facility, supported by IDA Ireland. The release says Ericsson "currently employs more than 1,300 people in the country". That is existing employment, not jobs created by the project, so the jobs field stays "not stated".[7]

Datavant, 31 March 2025. The company announced a global research and development centre, and plans "to initially hire up to 125 people" by the end of 2027. This is a services and research site, not a plant. It carries a jobs figure but no capital amount.[8]

Only the first is a manufacturing site. For process-equipment and plant-services suppliers, that is the kind of record worth taking to the next step.

06 / check the site next

Check the site next

An announcement gives you a named site. The next checks confirm what is being built and when. Each has its own guide, so this page does not repeat them.

  1. Planning. Look for a matching planning application for the new building or extension. See Irish planning applications as construction signals.
  2. Licences. For manufacturing and process sites, check for an EPA industrial emissions or IPC licence application or review, using the EPA licence search and its LEAP portal, which "provides public access to application, compliance and enforcement records". Our Irish environmental register guide explains how to read them.[9]
  3. Sector method. Where the site is a pharmaceutical or life-sciences plant, follow Irish pharmaceutical manufacturing account research for plant-level evidence.
  4. Paid project databases. If you are weighing a paid project-lead database against official sources, see industrial project leads in Ireland and the UK compared.

A matched planning file or licence application makes the signal stronger. It still does not tell you who buys, what they buy or when a tender will run.

07 / common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Treating the 15,319 expected jobs as jobs already created. IDA's own wording is "expected creation" and "future job creation".[2]
  • Counting construction roles or existing employment as new jobs at the site.[6][7]
  • Using DETE's all-agency or foreign-owned employment totals as the IDA figure. DETE's survey covers IDA Ireland, Enterprise Ireland and Údarás na Gaeltachta clients, and its foreign-owned total is a different measure from IDA's 312,468.[4]
  • Reading a research centre or services hub as a production plant.
  • Assuming the press-release stream lists every approval.
08 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many investments did IDA Ireland win in 2025?
IDA Ireland secured 323 investments in 2025, a 38% increase on the 234 it won in 2024, according to its results of 18 December 2025. Of those, 78 came from companies new to Ireland and 68 were expansions by existing clients. The annual report links the 323 approvals with 15,319 expected future jobs.[1][2][3]
Are the 15,319 jobs already in place?
No. IDA's annual report describes 15,319 as jobs associated with the expected creation from the 2025 approvals, and the December 2025 press release says they are expected "in the coming years". IDA Ireland reports actual employment in its client companies of 312,468 in 2025, a separate figure sourced to the annual employment survey, which counts people already in post.[1][2]
Does IDA Ireland publish a list of every investment?
No. IDA publishes totals and type splits in its results and annual report, and a selection of named announcements in its press-release stream. No official list of all 323 approvals for 2025 was found on 10 November 2026. Treat each named release as one signal, and do not assume the stream is complete.[1][2][5]
Is an IDA announcement a buying signal?
It is a signal of intended investment at a named site, not a purchase. A release can state capital, planned jobs and timing, but it does not name a budget for equipment, a buyer or a tender. Confirm the site with planning and licence records, then research who owns the relevant spend at that plant.[2][6]
Why do IDA employment figures differ between sources?
Because figures are revised between publications. The December 2025 press release gave 312,400 jobs in IDA client companies. IDA Ireland's later annual report reports 312,468 (source: DETE Annual Employment Survey 2025) against a revised 2024 total of 307,742. Use the later official figure and quote the publication date alongside it.[1][2]

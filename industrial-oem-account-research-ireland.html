Irish industrial OEM account research: from component to build operation

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

OEM account research in Ireland starts with the build operation that would use your component, not with a list of companies called OEMs. Ireland has about a thousand machinery and electrical equipment makers (1,030 in 2024, our own sum of the CSO counts below), and most are small. This guide gives the CSO counts, the four roles to separate and a worked fictional example.

01 / how many machinery and electrical oems ireland has

How many machinery and electrical OEMs Ireland has

The CSO's business demography table counts active enterprises in Ireland by activity and size. For 2024:[3]

Persons engaged C28 Machinery and equipment n.e.c. C27 Electrical equipment
Under 10 558 244
10 to 19 50 24
20 to 49 58 18
50 to 249 50 15
250 and over 9 4
All active enterprises 725 305
Persons engaged 13,276 5,467

Together that is 1,030 machinery and electrical equipment makers in 2024 (our own sum of the two CSO totals).[3] Both divisions grew slightly from 2023, when the CSO counted 717 machinery and 299 electrical enterprises.[3] Most are micro-businesses: 77% of the machinery and 80% of the electrical enterprises have fewer than 10 persons engaged, so a short list of 117 machinery and 37 electrical enterprises with 20 or more persons engaged is a realistic first cut for a component supplier (our own sums and percentages from the size bands).[3]

The table is built from the CSO's central register of all businesses in Ireland.[4] Builders classified to other divisions, such as metal fabricators or repair and installation businesses, will not appear under C27 or C28, so add them from the brief if they fit.

02 / define the component route first

Define the component route first

Write a route hypothesis: "We supply [component family]. We want businesses doing [build operation] on [equipment family] in [territory]. The question is whether [component responsibility] sits with that business."

For an Irish brief, decide whether the build must happen in the Republic of Ireland, anywhere on the island, or whether selling into Ireland is enough. Keep the build location apart from a sales office or registered address. Use the industrial ICP brief for territory and exclusions.

03 / use four role dimensions

Use four role dimensions

Ask these about one offering and one operation, not the whole company:

  • Equipment brand: whose name is on the equipment? A brand does not show who designs or builds it.
  • Assembler or integrator: what parts or subsystems does the business combine, wire, mount or test, and where?
  • Reseller: does it sell this offering as a finished product? That does not rule out assembly work elsewhere.
  • Contract builder: does it build to another party's design? Then design ownership and supplier choice may sit with that party.

One company can hold several roles for one product and different roles for another. Use the manufacturing discovery guide to resolve company, brand and site links, and the distributor guide when resale is the real question.

04 / fictional example tracing a latch into a labelling machine

Fictional example: tracing a latch into a labelling machine

Fictional example. Every company, product and card here is invented and describes no real business.

A fictional hardware supplier sells enclosure hinges and latches. From the C28 list it picks a fictional builder, Machine Builder A, with 20 to 49 persons engaged. It then works through five invented evidence cards:

  1. A1: A assembles a bench labelling machine from an enclosure and a base at a workshop in the Republic of Ireland. This supports the build and its location.
  2. A2: A sells the finished machine under its own brand. This supports the brand role, not who made the enclosure.
  3. A3: A receives the enclosure complete, with hinges already fitted. So A does not buy hinges for this machine; the enclosure maker might. That maker is not named.
  4. A4: A fits latches to the base access cover. The customer specifies the latch model and A places the order.
  5. A5: A may not change that latch model without customer approval. Who approves is not stated.

The supplier's handover reads: "A builds this machine locally. Hinges arrive in a supplied enclosure, so the hinge route is upstream. A orders the specified base-cover latch. Find out whether A can choose the seller of the approved model, and how the customer handles a proposed change. No open order or technical match is established."

Two other fictional builders show how the route can close. Contract Builder B mounts sensor brackets on conveyor subassemblies, but the customer supplies the bracket kits, so B is not a bracket buyer for that work. Equipment Business C resells a complete dispensing module and builds only the frame around it, so C's frame work says nothing about fittings inside the module.

05 / challenge the attractive conclusion

Challenge the attractive conclusion

  • "It sells equipment in our category, so it builds it." A catalogue can describe a product without naming the builder. Check the build activity.
  • "It designed the machine, so it chooses every part." Ask separately about model choice, approval, supplier selection and ordering.
  • "It built one machine, so it has repeat demand." One build supports that build only.
  • "There is no component detail online, so it is not relevant." Missing detail leaves the question open; it does not close the account.
06 / use the blank component route tool

Use the blank component-route tool

Download the blank OEM component-route CSV from the resource library. It has headers only: no example rows, contacts, formulas or automatic decisions.

Use one row per fact or open question, not one row per company. Give each row its own record_id; reuse company_id and map_id for the same company and component, equipment and operation scope. Many rows are not many accounts. record_context marks working research or labelled fictional practice, and brief_version holds the brief reference.

component_family_question, equipment_family_question and operation_question hold the hypothesis. fact_name is the assertion tested, such as build_activity, build_location, equipment_brand, contract_build, component_provision, model_choice, supplier_choice, approval_constraint or order_placement. fact_status is supported, explicit_negative, unknown, conflict or not_assessed; the next column holds the narrow supported fact in your words, and stays empty when the status is unknown.

Each row has its own evidence_reference, source_location, source_stated_date, checked_date and source_use_status, with the period or build revision in scope_and_period. Finish with research_implication, unknowns_and_conflicts, next_question and stopping_rule. Import as UTF-8 text, switch off formula interpretation, and never paste confidential drawings, bills of materials, customer lists or personal contact data.

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many machinery manufacturers are there in Ireland?

The CSO counted 725 active enterprises in machinery and equipment n.e.c. (NACE C28) in 2024, with 13,276 persons engaged. Nine had 250 or more persons engaged.[3]

How many electrical equipment manufacturers are there in Ireland?

The CSO counted 305 active enterprises in electrical equipment (NACE C27) in 2024, with 5,467 persons engaged. Four had 250 or more persons engaged.[3]

Is every company in those counts an OEM?

No. Being counted under C27 or C28 shows how the CSO classifies an enterprise, not the role it plays for your component. Some build under their own brand, some build to contract, and some mainly resell or service. Check the role for each offering.

What should an OEM research brief include?

The component family, the equipment family and build operation, the Irish territory rule, your exclusions, and the questions to answer: who chooses the model, who chooses the supplier and who places the order.

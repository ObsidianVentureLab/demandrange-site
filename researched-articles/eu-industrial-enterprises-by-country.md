# Manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country and NACE division (2023 data)

**In 2023 the EU-27 had 2,168,169 manufacturing enterprises (NACE Section C), and 16,380 of them employed 250 or more people.** Italy had the most, at 352,446, followed by France, Poland and Germany. Germany had by far the most large manufacturers, with 4,806 enterprises of 250 or more people. Ireland had 19,416 manufacturing enterprises.

The figures come from Eurostat's Structural Business Statistics. The download covers every EU country and 31 industrial activities, from mining through each manufacturing division to energy, water and waste, with counts by size class, employment, turnover and investment in machinery and equipment.

## What the count tells you, and what it doesn't

The headline count is driven by very small firms. Of the EU's 2,168,169 manufacturing enterprises, 335,087 employed ten or more people, which is the sum of the four published size classes from 10–19 upwards.[2] Eurostat's own overview of the sector puts manufacturing at 6.5% of all enterprises in the EU business economy in 2023, close to 2.2 million, employing around 30.2 million persons. That article was extracted in December 2025, so its rounded figures differ slightly from the September 2026 release used here.[4]

Poland and France each have more manufacturing enterprises than Germany, but Germany has more than three times as many with 250 or more people as either.

An **enterprise** is the statistical unit Eurostat counts: broadly, a business producing goods or services with some autonomy over its decisions. It is not a plant. A manufacturer with five factories in one country counts once there. A group with plants in several countries is counted in each country where it has an enterprise.

That matters if you sell equipment or services to sites:

- **The count sizes a market; it doesn't list one.** The dataset has no company names, sites or contacts.
- **Size classes beat totals.** For most industrial suppliers, the 50–249 and 250-plus columns are closer to the number of realistic accounts than the headline figure.
- **Investment is a spending signal, not a budget.** Gross investment in machinery and equipment shows where manufacturers spent on plant in 2023. It doesn't show who will buy next year.

For a site-level list, combine statistics with registers. Our guides to [Irish manufacturer research](irish-manufacturing-account-research.html), [UK manufacturer research](uk-manufacturing-account-research.html) and [European chemical manufacturer research](european-chemical-manufacturer-research.html) show how.

## By NACE division: where the EU's manufacturers are

At EU level, the most numerous manufacturing divisions in 2023 were fabricated metal products (C25, 412,708 enterprises), food products (C10, 272,977) and repair and installation of machinery and equipment (C33, 229,564). Machinery and equipment n.e.c. (C28) had 77,999 enterprises and chemicals (C20) had 31,391.[1]

Many EU division figures are Eurostat estimates, and the rounded ones show it: wearing apparel (C14) and furniture (C31) are each published as 130,000, and other manufacturing (C32) as 180,000. Check the `eurostat_flags` column before quoting any division figure.[1] The EU total for basic metals (C24) is confidential.

## Reading the flags and the gaps

Every row in the CSV has an `eurostat_flags` column naming the cells Eurostat flagged.[1][2]

- **C (confidential).** Eurostat withheld the value, usually because it would reveal data about individual businesses. The cell is empty.
- **e (estimated).** Eurostat published an estimate. The cell has the value.
- **Empty with no flag.** No value was published.

**Ireland has more gaps than most countries.** Its enterprise totals are confidential in eleven activities: beverages (C11), tobacco (C12), leather (C15), coke and refined petroleum (C19), non-metallic minerals (C23), basic metals (C24), electrical equipment (C27), motor vehicles (C29), furniture (C31), water supply (E36) and waste (E38). Turnover and machinery investment are confidential for pharmaceuticals (C21), electronics (C26) and other manufacturing (C32).[1] Ireland's Central Statistics Office publishes its own enterprise counts, which can fill some of these gaps; see our [Irish manufacturing account research](irish-manufacturing-account-research.html) guide.

**The UK is not in the dataset.** Eurostat no longer publishes structural business statistics for the UK. For UK numbers, use national sources such as the Office for National Statistics and Companies House; our [UK manufacturer research](uk-manufacturing-account-research.html) guide covers them.

**One value is negative.** Eurostat published a negative machinery investment figure for Finland's mining and quarrying (B) for 2023: −12.43 (EUR million). The CSV keeps it exactly as published, as a plain number with no formula characters, and the [full table footnote](#fn-fi-b-investment) marks it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many manufacturing companies are there in the EU?

Eurostat counted 2,168,169 manufacturing enterprises in the EU-27 in 2023.[1] Most are small; 335,087 employed ten or more people.[2]

### Which EU country has the most manufacturing enterprises?

Italy, with 352,446 in 2023, followed by France (266,065), Poland (241,254) and Germany (201,177).[1] Germany has the most large ones: 4,806 enterprises with 250 or more persons employed.[2]

### How many manufacturing enterprises does Ireland have?

Eurostat's 2023 figure is 19,416, of which 197 employed 250 or more people.[1][2] Ireland's Central Statistics Office publishes national counts for later years, so use the matching source and year when you compare.

### Why are some cells empty?

Eurostat marks values as confidential when publishing them could identify individual businesses. Ireland, with relatively few large firms in some divisions, has several. The flags column says which cells are affected.[1]

# ZoomInfo, Apollo or researched accounts: what an industrial prospect list needs

<strong>Sales platforms such as ZoomInfo and Apollo.io combine large B2B company and contact databases with prospecting and outreach tools, and they can be the right choice when your market is well described by industry, size and location.</strong> Industrial briefs often depend on facts a database field may not hold: which site performs a process, whether a company makes or only distributes, and whether there is an evidenced project. Test any source on accounts you already know; use researched accounts where those facts decide fit.

This page does not rate any vendor's data quality. Descriptions of third-party products come from their own websites on the date stated in the sources; check current features and terms with each provider.

## What data platforms are built for

<p>ZoomInfo describes itself as a go-to-market platform combining marketing and sales AI with B2B data, covering lead generation, prospecting, market intelligence and account-based marketing.[1] Apollo describes itself as an AI sales platform that brings data, intelligence and execution together for the go-to-market motion.[2]</p>

<p>The common design is <strong>breadth plus speed</strong>: filter a large database, export or sequence contacts, enrich your CRM. That is genuinely useful, and nothing on this page argues against it.</p>

## When a platform is enough

<ul>
<li>Your buyers are defined by sector, headcount, revenue band and city, and those fields match how the platform classifies companies.</li>
<li>You need named contacts at known accounts and will check them before use.</li>
<li>You sell high volume to many similar companies and can tolerate some misfits.</li>
<li>A sample on your own known customers comes back accurate enough for your team.</li>
</ul>

## Where industrial briefs need more

<dl class="research-ledger">
<dt>The site, not the head office</dt><dd>A record often represents a company or group location. Your product is used at a plant. A group with a Dublin head office and a plant in Cork needs the plant.</dd>
<dt>Activity, not industry label</dt><dd>“Chemicals” can mean synthesis, blending, repacking or resale, and they buy different things. The <a href="european-chemical-manufacturer-research.html">chemical manufacturer guide</a> shows why the label is not enough.</dd>
<dt>Register and regulator evidence</dt><dd>Environmental permits, major-hazard registers and company filings tie an operator to a regulated activity at a site. See the <a href="uk-manufacturing-account-research.html">UK</a> and <a href="irish-manufacturing-account-research.html">Irish</a> methods.</dd>
<dt>Local-language sources</dt><dd>Outside Ireland and the UK, the best evidence is often a national register or notice in the local language.</dd>
<dt>Project signals</dt><dd>Planning applications, tenders and award notices point to change at a site. A <a href="ted-eu-tenders-industrial-suppliers.html">TED award notice</a> can name the contractor now buying equipment.</dd>
</dl>

<p>None of these are claims that a given platform lacks the information; they are the questions to test. Some platforms will answer some of them for some markets.</p>

## Test any source on accounts you already know

<p>Before you buy a licence or commission research, give each option the same brief and a sample of 20 to 30 accounts your team already understands: good customers, known misfits and a few near-misses.</p>

<dl class="research-ledger">
<dt>Counting unit</dt><dd>Does each record represent a group, company or site, and is that what you asked for?</dd>
<dt>Activity</dt><dd>Does the record say what the company does at the site in words you can verify?</dd>
<dt>Misfits</dt><dd>Are your known misfits excluded, and is the reason visible?</dd>
<dt>Source and date</dt><dd>Can you see where each fact came from and when it was last checked?</dd>
<dt>Use rights</dt><dd>What may you export, share or reuse, and for how long?</dd>
</dl>

## Contact data comes with rules

<p>In the UK, the PECR rule on marketing by electronic mail does not apply to corporate subscribers such as companies and LLPs, but sole traders and some partnerships are treated as individuals, and UK GDPR applies whenever you process a named business contact's personal data.[3]</p>

<p>In Ireland, Regulation 13 of S.I. No. 336/2011 generally requires consent for unsolicited marketing email to natural persons, with an exception for addresses that reasonably appear to be used mainly in a commercial or official capacity where the message relates solely to that activity; email to non-natural-person subscribers is barred once they have said they do not consent.[4] The Data Protection Commission's guidance explains the general consent rule.[5]</p>

<p>This is a summary, not legal advice. It is one reason Demand Range does not include named contacts by default.</p>

## Frequently asked questions

<dl>
<dt>Is ZoomInfo or Apollo good for industrial prospect lists?</dt><dd>They can be, when your market is well described by industry, size and location and a test on your own known accounts comes back accurate. Where fit depends on what happens at a specific site, test whether the records show the site and activity before relying on them.</dd>
<dt>What is an alternative to ZoomInfo for industrial companies?</dt><dd>The main alternatives are another data platform, an in-house research workflow, or analyst-led account research such as Demand Range, which builds a list against your brief and cites a public source for each account. Many teams combine researched accounts with a platform for contacts.</dd>
<dt>Why do industrial prospect lists need site-level data?</dt><dd>Industrial equipment and services are bought for a plant or process, so the relevant fact is often what happens at a particular site, not where the head office is or which industry label the company carries.</dd>
<dt>How should I compare prospect data providers?</dt><dd>Give each one the same brief and a sample of 20 to 30 accounts you already understand. Check the counting unit, the activity description, whether known misfits are excluded, whether sources and dates are visible, and what the licence lets you do with the data.</dd>
<dt>Can I email business contacts from a bought list in the UK or Ireland?</dt><dd>The rules differ by country and by type of recipient. In the UK the PECR email rule does not apply to companies, but UK GDPR still applies to named contacts. In Ireland, S.I. 336/2011 sets the rules for unsolicited marketing email. Take advice for your case.</dd>
<dt>Does Demand Range sell a database?</dt><dd>No. Demand Range researches account lists against a written brief. It does not license a self-service database or include named contacts by default.</dd>
</dl>

# Find a Tender vs Contracts Finder for industrial suppliers

**Short answer:** for any UK procurement started under the Procurement Act 2023, which took effect on 24 February 2025, the place to look is **Find a Tender**, the central digital platform. Contracts Finder still carries procurements that began under the old regulations, so check both during the transition. Notifiable below-threshold contracts also go on Find a Tender: £12,000 and above (estimated value, including tax) for central government, £30,000 and above for other authorities.

## 1. Which platform for what

The Procurement Act 2023 start date and Find a Tender's role as the central digital platform are set out in GOV.UK's guidance and on Find a Tender itself.[2][3] The below-threshold values, which apply to a contract's estimated value, come from section 87 of the Act.[1]

| Platform | What it now carries | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Find a Tender | All notices for procurements under the Procurement Act 2023, above and notifiable below threshold | Main daily search; saved searches by CPV and buyer |
| Contracts Finder[4] | Legacy procurements started under the old regulations | Checking older frameworks and contract lifecycles until they expire |
| Public Contracts Scotland[5] | Scottish devolved authorities | Scottish councils, Scottish Water and other devolved bodies |
| eTendersNI | Northern Ireland public bodies | NI-specific opportunities |

## 2. The notices that matter most for industrial suppliers

Not every notice is a tender. Under the Act the lifecycle is published in stages,[2] and the early ones are the most useful for account research:

1. **Pipeline notice.** Authorities that expect to spend more than £100m in the coming financial year must list upcoming contracts of £2m or more.[2] This gives you a forward view of capital and maintenance programmes.
2. **Preliminary market engagement notice.** This is your chance to shape a specification before it is fixed.
3. **Tender notice.** The live competition.
4. **Contract award and contract details notices.** These tell you who won and on what terms. That's the account you should now sell into as a subcontractor or component supplier.
5. **Dynamic Markets.** Dynamic Markets replace DPS for new procurements.[2] Joining one keeps you eligible for repeat call-offs.

## 3. Search set-up that works

- Search by **CPV code**, not just keywords. Pumps, valves and instrumentation are spread across several CPV branches. Start from our [CPV code guide for industrial equipment](cpv-codes-industrial-equipment.html).
- Save searches per **buyer type**: water and utilities, NHS estates, local authorities, defence and the nuclear estate.
- Log each relevant notice in the free [tender triage worksheet](industrial-tender-triage-worksheet.csv): buyer, value band, CPV, deadline, incumbent and next action.

## 4. From notice to account list

A contract award notice names the winning contractor. That contractor will buy equipment, services and subcontracted packages for the life of the contract. Build an account row for:

- the **authority** (end owner and future re-procurement);
- the **winning contractor** (immediate buyer of your product);
- the **named roles**: contract manager, procurement lead, design or project engineer.

> **Fictional example:** A fictional valve distributor sets a Find a Tender alert on valve and pump CPV codes. In one month it sees a pipeline notice from a fictional county council listing a wastewater-related works package, and then an award notice to a fictional regional M&E contractor. The distributor adds both to its account list and contacts the contractor's buyer about the package. *This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.*

## 5. Ireland and the EU

If you also sell into Ireland, Irish public contracts appear on eTenders. See our [eTenders guide for industrial suppliers](etenders-industrial-supplier-opportunities.html). Contracts above EU thresholds in EU member states are on TED; see the [TED tenders guide](ted-eu-tenders-industrial-suppliers.html).

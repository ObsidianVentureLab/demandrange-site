Ireland's e-invoicing mandate: phase dates and where manufacturers stand

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Mandatory e-invoicing in Ireland starts on 1 November 2028, when large corporates must issue e-invoices to Irish business customers and every business must be able to receive them. Revenue then extends the domestic duty to businesses in specified EU trading arrangements on 1 November 2029, and EU-wide rules for cross-border trade follow on 1 July 2030.[1][2][11]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one thing: the official phase dates, and how far Irish manufacturers already send machine-readable invoices, from Eurostat's 2023 survey. For public-sector buying, see our guide to Irish eTenders for industrial suppliers and our Irish public tenders data for industrial equipment.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Revenue e-invoicing pages; Eurostat isoc_eb_icsn2; checked 24 November 2026.

  • Phase One, 1 November 2028: in-scope large corporates (about 2,400 businesses in total, according to Revenue) issue e-invoices for taxable domestic business-to-business sales and report a subset of the invoice data to Revenue. All businesses must be able to receive e-invoices from the same date.[1][2][11]
  • Phase Two, 1 November 2029: the domestic duty extends to businesses that trade with other EU businesses under the zero-rate or reverse-charge arrangements. Phase Three, 1 July 2030: EU-wide rules for all cross-border EU business-to-business trade.[1][3][11]
  • A PDF is not an e-invoice. Revenue's definition requires a structured format that meets European Standard EN 16931-1 and excludes PDF and scanned paper.[2]
  • In Eurostat's 2023 survey, 36.23% of Irish manufacturers with 10 or more people sent machine-readable e-invoices, against 43.45% across the EU-27.[6]
  • In the same 2023 survey, 45.42% of Irish manufacturers sent paper invoices, against 67.20% across the EU-27.[6]
02 / the irish e invoicing phases and dates

The Irish e-invoicing phases and dates

Revenue is phasing e-invoicing in to give businesses time to prepare before the EU's cross-border rules apply on 1 July 2030. In every phase, a business must be able to receive e-invoices from any supplier required to issue them.[1][3][11] Revenue's 21 October 2026 paper says the domestic provisions are in Finance Bill (No. 2) 2026 as initiated and remain subject to the legislative process, with further legislation for the cross-border requirements to follow before July 2030. The dates are Revenue's published programme, not yet enacted Irish law.[11]

Phase Start date Who must issue e-invoices and report to Revenue Receiving
Phase One 1 November 2028 Large corporates, for taxable domestic business-to-business sales All businesses in Ireland must be able to receive e-invoices
Phase Two 1 November 2029 The domestic duty extends to businesses trading with other EU businesses under the zero-rate (goods) or reverse-charge (services) arrangements As Phase One
Phase Three 1 July 2030 EU-wide requirements for all cross-border EU business-to-business trade, in every member state As Phase One

Sources: Revenue, Implementation phases (published 20 October 2026) and modernisation timeline (published 20 July 2026), and Revenue's 2026 update paper (published 21 October 2026), checked 24 November 2026.[1][3][11]

Who counts as a large corporate in Phase One

For Phase One, Revenue treats a business as a large corporate if it is tax-registered and makes taxable supplies, its tax affairs are managed by Revenue's Large Corporates Division on 1 January 2027, and it is established, or has a fixed establishment, in Ireland. Revenue says it will engage with the large corporates included in Phase One.[2]

  • Moved out of the division after 1 January 2027: the 1 November 2028 date still applies.[2]
  • Moved into the division after 1 January 2027, and still there on 1 January 2028: the start date is 1 November 2029 instead.[2]
  • Everyone else: must still be able to receive e-invoices from 1 November 2028, even if it does not have to issue them yet.[2][4]

Revenue says the change affects every business in Ireland, with the effect depending on business size, whether it trades with other EU businesses, and whether its supplies are taxable or exempt. Manufacturing is one of the sectors it names.[4]

What the 1 July 2030 date covers

Revenue's EU implementation timeline sets July 2030 as the date e-invoicing and digital reporting requirements apply to business-to-business and business-to-government trade between EU member states, and Revenue's 2026 update paper gives 1 July 2030 for cross-border business-to-business trade.[5][11] The same timeline says that from April 2025 member states have had the option to introduce e-invoicing and digital reporting for domestic business-to-business and business-to-consumer sales.[5]

03 / machine readable invoices against pdf and paper

Machine-readable invoices against PDF and paper

Eurostat's survey of ICT usage in enterprises asks firms with 10 or more persons employed which kinds of invoice they sent. It separates three kinds:[6][7][8]

  • E-invoices suitable for automated processing: a standard structure such as EDI (for example EDIFACT) or XML (for example UBL), excluding PDF files. This is the category closest to Revenue's definition of an e-invoice.[2]
  • Electronic invoices not suitable for automated processing: emails, JPEG or other formats, including PDF files.
  • Paper invoices.

A firm can send more than one kind, so the three shares do not add up to 100%.[8]

2023 survey, manufacturing (NACE C), firms with 10+ persons employed Ireland EU-27
Sent e-invoices suitable for automated processing 36.23% 43.45%
Sent electronic invoices not suitable for automated processing (such as PDF) 73.74% 74.11%
Sent paper invoices 45.42% 67.20%

Source: Eurostat isoc_eb_icsn2, unit "percentage of enterprises", 2023 survey (invoices sent in 2022), data last updated 11 December 2025, checked 24 November 2026.[6]

The survey ran in 2023, but the invoicing question asked about invoices sent during 2022. Eurostat labels the results 2023, and so does this page.[6][8]

By our count, almost two in three Irish manufacturers (63.77%) did not send any machine-readable e-invoices in the 2023 survey. Irish manufacturers used PDF-type invoices about as often as the EU-27 average, and paper much less often.[6]

Ireland against other EU countries

Among the 27 member states, Ireland ranked 11th for the share of manufacturers sending machine-readable e-invoices in the 2023 survey, by our count. By our count, Italy (97.53%) and Finland (91.36%) were highest, and Bulgaria (13.29%) and Poland (14.01%) lowest. Eurostat's tables do not say why the shares differ.[6]

By manufacturing sector in Ireland

The survey also breaks manufacturing into sectors. Irish food, drink and tobacco makers sent machine-readable e-invoices more often than the EU-27 average in the 2023 survey. Irish electrical equipment and machinery makers did so much less often.[6]

The UK runs its own timetable: from 1 April 2029, businesses registered for the tax, and public bodies, will need to issue and receive tax invoices as e-invoices for business-to-business and business-to-government sales, unless an exemption applies.[6][9]

05 / electronic supply chain data sharing

Electronic supply-chain data sharing

E-invoicing is one part of exchanging data with customers and suppliers. Eurostat also asks whether firms share supply-chain information electronically, for example inventory levels, delivery progress or demand forecasts, through websites, apps, EDI systems, sensors or tracking. Emails that cannot be processed automatically do not count.[6][8]

2023 survey, manufacturing, share of firms Ireland EU-27 Ireland's rank (by our count)
Shared supply-chain information electronically with suppliers or customers 27.43% 22.69% 8th of 26

Source: Eurostat isoc_eb_icsn2, reference year 2023, data last updated 11 December 2025, checked 24 November 2026. Hungary's value is not published (flag "U", low reliability). By our count, Slovenia was highest at 38.90%.[6]

So Irish manufacturers were more likely than the EU-27 average to share supply-chain data electronically in the 2023 survey, but less likely to send machine-readable e-invoices. The survey measures whether a firm did each thing, not how much of its trade went through it.[6]

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

When does e-invoicing become mandatory in Ireland?
On 1 November 2028 for large corporates, which must then issue e-invoices for domestic business-to-business sales and report a subset of the invoice data to Revenue. The domestic duty extends to businesses in specified EU trading arrangements on 1 November 2029. EU-wide rules for cross-border business trade follow on 1 July 2030.[1][2][11]
Do Irish manufacturers have to receive e-invoices from 2028?
Yes. From 1 November 2028, every business in Ireland must be able to receive e-invoices from any supplier required to issue them, according to Revenue. That applies even to a manufacturer that does not yet have to issue e-invoices itself, so receiving systems need to be ready by 1 November 2028, whatever date a firm's own issuing duty starts.[1][2][4]
Is a PDF invoice an e-invoice?
No. Revenue defines an e-invoice as one issued, sent and received in a structured electronic format that allows automated processing and meets European Standard EN 16931-1. Unstructured formats such as PDF or scanned paper are excluded. Eurostat's survey also counts PDF invoices as electronic invoices not suitable for automated processing.[2][8]
Who is a large corporate for Phase One?
A tax-registered business making taxable supplies whose tax affairs are managed by Revenue's Large Corporates Division on 1 January 2027, and that is established, or has a fixed establishment, in Ireland. A business assigned to the division after 1 January 2027 that is still there on 1 January 2028 starts on 1 November 2029.[2]
How many Irish manufacturers already send machine-readable e-invoices?
In Eurostat's 2023 survey, 36.23% of Irish manufacturers with 10 or more persons employed sent e-invoices suitable for automated processing, against 43.45% across the EU-27. Ireland ranked 11th of 27 member states, by our count. The survey question covered invoices sent during 2022, so newer adoption is not yet shown.[6][8]
Does the Irish timetable apply in the UK?
No. Revenue's phases cover businesses in Ireland. The UK has its own plan: from 1 April 2029, UK businesses registered for the tax, and public bodies, will need to issue and receive tax invoices as e-invoices, unless an exemption applies, according to HM Revenue & Customs' roadmap. Eurostat has no UK figure for 2023.[6][9]

A practical guide for industrial suppliers

How to build an Irish manufacturing account list that explains every inclusion.

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

Start with the work a company performs, where that work happens and why it matters to your supplier brief. Then check the company behind the activity before counting it. A list of names or matching product words cannot answer those questions on its own.

The result should separate companies you can include under the brief, candidates that need another check and businesses excluded for a stated reason. Keep relevant sites beneath the company record. An active project is not required for an industry-matched account.

This guide teaches the method; it does not supply a verified directory, personal contacts or current purchasing requirements. The worked example below is wholly fictional.

01 / manufacturing market context

How many manufacturing businesses are there in Ireland?

The CSO reports 19,617 active manufacturing enterprises in Ireland for 2024, classified under NACE Rev. 2 section C. The release was published on 6 October 2026; the count describes 2024, not a live 2026 market census.[1][2]

For an industrial supplier, this is a starting point for choosing the scope of a company list—not the number of prospects you can buy. It does not identify individual factories, businesses that match your product range or companies planning to purchase equipment.

How company size changes the research brief

Within that manufacturing total, 17,137 enterprises were in the under-10 persons-engaged band. Combining the other published bands gives 2,480 with 10 or more and 814 with 50 or more.[1]

Eurostat publishes the same 2024 figure for Ireland: 19,617 manufacturing enterprises, 196 of them with 250 or more persons employed.[10] For other EU countries, see manufacturing enterprises by EU country. Quote one of the two; do not add them together.

Use the source's size measure, not an assumed current payroll count. The CSO's employment explanation includes employees, proprietors and family members; these bands should not silently become “verified employee counts” in a sales brief.[3]

Does this headcount meet your site-size condition?

Use a size condition for a commercial reason, not to exclude a specialist workshop merely to make a list look bigger. Ask whether the evidence answers your brief for the chosen factory—not whether a company profile displays an attractive number.

Wholly fictional example: a brief requires 50–199 people at one site on 30 September 2026, inclusive. These arbitrary teaching limits are not statutory definitions, typical sizes or recommended targets. Every case and count is invented; evidence accuracy and permitted source use are stipulated only for this exercise.

In this fictional screen, usable evidence must describe the required site. A group or multi-site company total leaves that site's count unresolved; do not divide the wider total between factories.

  • A multi-site company's 90 people: unresolved. The total does not establish the chosen site's count.
  • A supported site range of 40–80: unresolved. It crosses the lower boundary; taking its midpoint would invent an answer.
  • A supported site range of 60–120: meets this size condition, provided it bounds the actual count at the required site and date. Keep the range; do not invent an exact count or treat an unverified vendor estimate as supported evidence.

The period and measure must also match. A site count of 80 from 30 September 2022 cannot answer the 2026 question; retrieving it later does not update its reference date. An annual average cannot silently replace the requested point-in-time count. Neither mismatch establishes failure. Missing evidence is not zero. A comparable supported count outside the limits fails this condition, not the site's relevance to every supplier.

Passing size alone does not establish process, installed equipment, budget or purchasing authority. Identity, activity, territory, exclusions, source-use rights and any project signal remain separate checks. No contact or outreach permission follows.

Specify in the brief: which operating site, why size matters commercially, the required measure and reference date, inclusive limits, which supported ranges satisfy them, which uncertainties remain unresolved, and the separate account requirements still to assess.

Joining lists that use different NACE versions

A classification match can help you avoid dropping relevant machinery activities when comparing prospect lists. It does not establish a company's actual work or create extra companies.

Wholly fictional example: two supplied rows refer to the same stipulated company. The older row uses NACE Rev. 2 code 28.29 but has no usable activity description. The other uses Rev. 2.1 28.93. These are invented records, not anonymised customers or official company classifications.

In the Eurostat correspondence inspected, Rev. 2 28.29 relates to Rev. 2.1 28.29 and 28.93. The relationship to 28.93 covers machinery for filtering or purifying beverages. Keeping only the unchanged number could therefore miss a relevant activity. But the older fictional row gives no evidence for choosing either candidate.[1]

Keep both source records beneath that company, with the possible codes recorded separately. Two possible codes and two rows do not become additional companies. The newer row does not, by itself, prove when an official recoding happened or that the business changed its activity.

Turn the market context into the right account request

Choose the decision that fits how you sell:

  • Production equipment or process services: define the work that must happen at the customer's operation. Use the relevant statistical group as context, then require evidence of that activity and location. For food-sector work, the maker, brand and packer guide addresses the operating distinction.
  • Components sold to equipment builders: specify the equipment being built and your intended component route. Do not use the whole C28 total as a count of compatible OEMs. The OEM account-research guide owns that next decision.
  • Parts or services sold through maintenance businesses: decide whether you want the operator or a separate service company. The MRO account-research guide separates the service scope from parts responsibility. A C33 count does not establish that a business currently needs new parts or services.

Cork or Limerick: machinery builders or repair businesses?

For 2024, CSO table BRA34 records 71 active enterprises in Cork and 27 in Limerick under Manufacture of Machinery and Equipment n.e.c. (C28), compared with 272 and 100 respectively under Repair and Installation of Machinery and Equipment (C33). These are enterprise counts, not factory counts or a current prospect directory.[8]

Choose the activity before comparing territories. A brief for machinery builders is different from one for repair and installation businesses. C33's place within manufacturing does not make a repair-only business a machinery builder. Neither count establishes component compatibility or purchasing responsibility.

The CSO's county breakdown is approximate and based on Revenue-registered addresses, not necessarily where businesses operate.[3] For an illustrative Limerick operating-site brief, a Cork-registered company could still qualify if its relevant machinery-building operation is in Limerick; a Limerick registration alone does not establish a factory. Both cases need separate company-to-site evidence.

Make that distinction explicit in the ICP brief, for example:

Illustrative instruction: research companies operating machinery-building facilities in Cork or Limerick. Count each company once, retain its qualifying facilities beneath it, and exclude repair-only work. Keep unresolved operating locations outside the accepted count.

Do not divide an operating-site shortlist by 27 and call it Limerick market coverage: the reference year, activity, location basis and counting unit must match first. The statistical count is context, not promised deliverable volume.

02 / fictional brief

Start with a brief you can actually apply.

Use this invented example as a pattern, not a pre-approved market:

We supply production-workstation fixtures and want to research companies making fabricated metal components at production sites in the Republic of Ireland. Count each company once, with its qualifying sites recorded beneath it. Exclude design-only businesses, stockists without production, existing accounts and agreed channel conflicts. Require support for company identity, production activity and production location. A project announcement is not required.

Replace the product range, activity, territory and exclusions before using the pattern. “Ireland” should not silently switch between the Republic, the island, an operating site and a sales territory. If those choices are not settled, use the industrial ICP brief first.

04 / list decisions

Build the list in six decisions.

1. Search for the customer's activity.

Search for the manufacturing work your brief requires, rather than only the product you sell. For the fictional brief, an exploratory query is "metal fabrication" "Ireland" manufacturing. It is a starting prompt, not a tested coverage filter.

Inspect the underlying activity and location pages. Search-result wording can suggest a candidate; it is not sufficient evidence to accept one.

2. Check what the company actually does.

Does the evidence describe production by this company, a product it resells, work performed by a customer, or activity somewhere else in its group? Record the supported activity and its limits. A statement about supplying manufacturers does not establish that the supplier is a manufacturer.

3. Connect the activity to an in-scope site and company.

Identify the operating business behind the production description. A registered address or sales office is not automatically a production location. Do not count a parent, trading label and workshop as three companies, or merge distinct companies merely because they share a group name.

If the relationship remains unresolved, retain the question and hold the affected account decision. The identity-map instructions provide optional detail for more complex cases.

4. Apply the agreed exclusions and evidence rules.

Check the same version of the brief for every candidate. Resolve existing-account and channel restrictions in your controlled workflow. Do not send confidential customer lists through a public enquiry.

Record the source reference, publication date where known, actual check date and the precise fact it supports. Check the source and item's permitted use before retaining or sharing content. A public page does not automatically permit redistribution.

5. Make the decision and keep its reason.

Include only when the required identity, activity, territory, exclusion and evidence-use checks are satisfied. Hold a material gap or conflict with a specific next question. Exclude a supported mismatch against the brief; missing information is not the same as a negative finding.

Keep included, held and rejected records separate. Reconcile included company counts after deduplication; give each hold one next verification question. Stop at the agreed research boundary and disclose unresolved coverage rather than filling a quota with weaker matches.

A group is a relationship container here, not a claim that a holding company has been researched. If a holding company is itself relevant, it requires its own company identity and fit evidence. A facility is a distinct physical operating site, not each building, department or postal spelling. Entity fields
  • fiction_label: standalone fiction warning on every worked row. Do not remove it or insert real prospect records into this exercise.
  • entity_id, entity_type, display_name: unique ID, one of group/company/brand/facility/project, and an unmistakably fictional label. A name does not establish identity.
  • identity_status: supported, unknown or conflict; the last two do not support account inclusion.
  • production_status: pass/fail/unknown/conflict/not_applicable. On a company, asks whether the required production is attributable to that company; on a facility, whether that production occurs there. fail needs a positive contradictory fact, not missing evidence.
  • territory_status: same vocabulary. On a company, tests attributable qualifying production in the Republic of Ireland. On a facility, tests its physical location. A sales office can pass facility territory and fail facility production: that does not settle the company's production elsewhere.
  • exclusions_status: pass/fail/unknown/conflict/not_applicable. Company-level brief exclusions must be checked separately; a group link alone is not clearance.
  • source_use_status: in this dataset only, stipulated_pass; it is an invented teaching assumption, never real rights clearance. For authorised work, use separately reviewed states appropriate to the governing workflow, not automatic interpretation of these teaching fields.
  • consistency_status: supported or conflict; no material contradiction may be hidden by counting only the favourable observation.
  • account_decision: include/hold/reject for companies; always not_an_account for all other entity types. The worked hold-first convention is local to this exercise, not a universal stopping rule.
  • evidence_id: foreign key to a supporting invented card. The compact teaching schema uses one consolidated card per entity; retain distinct original observations in any real evidence system rather than overwriting them.
  • unknowns, next_question: record the unresolved fact and its next bounded verification question. A non-company entity can remain unresolved without being falsely counted as a held company.

Work the cases, then reconcile

A — Multi-site, same company. FIM-F01 and FIM-F02 link to FIM-C01. The trading label FIM-B01 also links to C01. Count one company and two qualifying sites, not four accounts.

B — Same group, different companies. FIM-C01 and FIM-C02 both link to FIM-G01. Keep the two distinct companies; C02 adds FIM-F03 as another qualifying site. Shared membership is not a duplicate rule.

C — Brand versus operator. FIM-B02 and FIM-F05 have unresolved company links. Do not join them by similar wording, and do not assert that the two unresolved references are two distinct companies. They contribute zero included accounts and zero qualifying attributed sites.

D — Distribution versus Irish production. FIM-C03 is resolved as a company, but supplying Irish customers and operating sales office FIM-F04 leave relevant production unresolved. Hold C03. The sales office is not a qualifying manufacturing facility. Do not silently recast this as the earlier guide's positive resale-only rejection case.

E — Project, not extra account. FIM-P01 points to company C01 and existing site F02. This gives one project linked to an already counted company, not another account or another operational facility. Its proposed status proves no live purchasing requirement.

Count conventions
  • Included companies: distinct company IDs with account_decision=include, all required gates passed, and at least one supported operator link from a qualifying facility. The worked result is 2.
  • Qualifying facilities under included companies: distinct facility IDs with passing production and physical territory, supported identity and operator link to an included company. The worked result is 3. Do not count a sales office, unresolved site or proposed extension as qualifying production.
  • Represented groups: distinct group targets of supported membership links from the included companies: 1, reported separately. Unknown group membership is not an independent group.
  • Projects linked to included companies: distinct project IDs with supported company links: 1, reported separately, not added to the company count. No project is required for either included company.
  • Held resolved companies: 1 (C03). Unresolved brand/site references stay in the relationship queue and must not inflate this denominator.
  • Unresolved mapping assertions: 2 (L04 and L09). These are work items, not two additional companies.

Never add the company, group, brand, facility and project counts together and label the result “accounts”. A resolved-company inventory is also not the same as an included-company view. These are within-exercise counts, not global CRM deduplication or coverage claims.

Pharma account research: test a description before assigning an operating segment

Original fictional exercise for Irish pharma and biopharma account research. This is a supplement to the manufacturing guide, not a sector directory or an interpretation of pharmaceutical regulation. Every company, site, description and decision below is invented. None is an anonymised prospect.

Set the physical-work rule without defining a legal category

For this exercise only, the brief seeks companies themselves physically making an invented medicine at a site in the Republic of Ireland. Packing already-made goods, testing supplied samples, storing goods and office administration alone do not meet this particular brief. These are invented commercial screening rules, not definitions of manufacture or conclusions about authorisations. A different supplier may deliberately choose one of the excluded activities.

Count one company, preserving its separate activity records. No project is required for this industry-matched, or Look Alike, exercise. Identity, exclusions and source-use checks for real account acceptance remain outside the fictional activity screen.

Work the invented cards

Use the fictional cards before opening the answer key. Each card is a teaching stipulation, not a source quotation. The exercise includes a role-only description, an office, distribution-only work, laboratory-only work, packing-only work, a mixed company, overseas production with an Irish office, and conflicting descriptions.

For Fictional Pharma F, one card stipulates physical making at an invented Republic of Ireland site, while another describes its separate office. The production card meets the activity/place screen; the office adds no company. For Fictional Pharma A, a role-only card leaves physical work unresolved. Keep A held rather than calling it either a producer or a non-producer.

For Fictional Pharma H, the invented descriptions disagree about physical making at the same site. Do not select the favourable sentence. Preserve the conflict and ask which description applies to the present scope. No regulatory determination follows from that conflict.

Complete the worksheet

The blank worksheet has headers only. Give each card a unique ID and each company a stable ID. Record the description’s purpose, the permitted observation, the activity and location it supports, its limit and the next evidence question. Leave unknown fields blank rather than inserting a guessed operator or date. Store evidence references separately from your screening judgement.

Choose match only for supported in-scope physical making; out_of_scope only for positive facts contradicting the activity or territory rule; otherwise choose hold. A conflicting required fact also means hold. At company level, a matching operation may support the screen, but a material contradiction affecting it must be resolved first. An unrelated office record neither cancels a valid production record nor creates another account.

For live work, check each source and item’s intended-use conditions before retaining observations. Where reuse is unresolved, keep the content out of the worksheet and retain only the permitted review reference. Changing the wording is not a substitute for permission. Do not use this exercise to interpret registers, certificates, inspections or authorisation scope.

Hand over the supported activity segment and the next question—not a claim of installed equipment, technical suitability, regulatory compliance, purchasing authority or an open package. A worksheet result does not establish demand or permission to contact anyone. Use the existing company/site identity map and ICP workflow for the broader account checks.

For the broader source-record and facility-fit questions, use the Irish pharmaceutical manufacturing account-research guide. This companion rehearses fictional decisions; it does not replace that guide or clear source-use rights.

Business R&D in Irish manufacturing: what the official data shows

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Irish manufacturing industries spent €3,594.983 million on research and development (R&D) in 2023, or 51.3% of all Irish business R&D, according to the Central Statistics Office (CSO). That was up from €1,502.419 million in 2021, the previous actual survey year. All Irish business R&D, not only manufacturing, was €7,003.745 million (€7.0 billion) in 2023, 81% higher than in 2021. Eurostat's manufacturing series, which we use to compare EU countries, puts Ireland at €3,587.6 million in 2023. By our count, the two official Irish manufacturing totals differ by about €7 million; they are not mixed in the tables below. There is not yet an agreed 2024 total.[1][4][9]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one question: how much Irish manufacturers spend on R&D, who spends it, how Ireland compares with other EU countries, and which official 2024 figure to trust. 01 / key facts

Key facts

CSO Business Expenditure on Research and Development 2023 (Final) – 2024 (Preliminary), and Eurostat rd_e_berdindr2 and rd_e_gerdtot; checked 24 November 2026.

  • 2023, CSO, actual: manufacturing industries (NACE 10–33) spent €3,594.983 million on R&D, or 51.3% of all Irish business R&D, up from €1,502.419 million in 2021.[1][4]
  • 2023, CSO and Eurostat, actual: all Irish business R&D was €7,003.745 million (€7.0 billion), 81% higher than €3,879.007 million in 2021. Both sources agree on this 2023 total to the euro.[1][2][9]
  • 2023, CSO, all business R&D: foreign-owned enterprises accounted for 83.8% of spend (€5,871.498 million) and 27.4% of R&D-active enterprises; large enterprises (250 or more persons engaged) accounted for 77.1% of spend; the top 10 enterprises accounted for 57.8%.[1][2][3][8]
  • 2023, Eurostat, manufacturing (NACE C): Ireland €3,587.6 million, 10th among the 26 EU countries that publish a 2023 cell, by our count. Portugal's cell is confidential. The EU27 manufacturing total is confidential.[9]
  • Do not use a single 2024 Irish business R&D total. The CSO estimates €6,589.565 million; Eurostat estimates €7,763.567 million. They do not agree on whether spend rose or fell against 2023.[1][2][9][10]
02 / irish manufacturing r d 2021 and 2023

Irish manufacturing R&D, 2021 and 2023

The CSO's Business Expenditure on Research and Development (BERD) survey runs every two years. Enterprises report actual spend for the survey year and an estimate for the following year, so 2021 and 2023 are actual figures and 2022 and 2024 are estimates. In 2023, the CSO's manufacturing industries grouping (NACE Rev. 2 codes 10–33) accounted for more than half of all Irish business R&D.[1][4]

The 2021 share is our division of the official cells. Values are converted from the CSO's €'000 cells. Totals may differ due to rounding.[1][4]

By our count, CSO manufacturing R&D rose 139% between 2021 and 2023. The rise in all business R&D was not only in day-to-day costs: capital expenditure went from €440.053 million in 2021 to €2,411.792 million in 2023, while current expenditure went from €3,438.954 million to €4,591.952 million.[1][2][4]

Eurostat publishes a separate manufacturing series under NACE Rev. 2 section C, which is also codes 10–33, and is the series that lets us place Ireland beside other EU countries. It is close to, but not the same as, the CSO manufacturing industries total. We found no official explanation of the difference in the CSO release or Eurostat's Irish metadata, so we do not offer one.

Source: Eurostat rd_e_berdindr2, million euro, updated 29 September 2026; checked 24 November 2026. Eurostat does not publish an Irish manufacturing (C) figure for 2022 or 2024. By our count, this series rose 138% between 2021 and 2023. By our count, the 2023 manufacturing totals differ by €7.383 million and the 2021 totals by €6.486 million. Tables on this page keep the two series apart.[9]

In 2023, 744 manufacturing enterprises were engaged in R&D, out of 2,351 R&D-active enterprises in Ireland, or 31.6% by our count. Of those manufacturing enterprises, 320 (43.0%) spent €0–€99,999, and 529 (71.1%, by our count) spent less than €500,000. Manufacturing therefore accounts for about half the money and under a third of the R&D-active enterprises.[1][7]

A published slice of the Eurostat manufacturing total is pharmaceuticals (C21): €369.492 million in 2023, down from €437.948 million in 2021. Chemicals (C20) were €185.168 million and machinery and equipment n.e.c. (C28) €57.673 million in 2023. Computer, electronic and optical products (C26) is published for 2021 (€411.613 million) but is confidential for 2023. We do not infer which divisions account for the rest of manufacturing R&D. For pharmaceutical manufacturing as an account list, see Irish pharmaceutical manufacturing companies.[9]

03 / who spends it ownership and size

Who spends it: ownership and size

The ownership and size figures in this section are for all Irish business R&D, not manufacturing only. The CSO does not publish an ownership or size split inside manufacturing in this release. They still matter for account research, because manufacturing was 51.3% of the same 2023 total.[1][4]

Foreign-owned enterprises spent most of the money. Irish-owned enterprises were most of the R&D-active count.

Of the 1,706 Irish-owned R&D-active enterprises, 1,309 (76.7%) spent less than €500,000 on R&D in 2023. Of the 645 foreign-owned enterprises, 246 (38.1%) spent less than €500,000.[1][6]

Spend is also concentrated in a very short list of enterprises. In 2023 the top 10 enterprises accounted for 57.8%, or €4,046.679 million, of all business R&D, up from 37.4%, or €1,452.664 million, in 2021.[1][8]

For account research, being "R&D-active" says little about how much an enterprise spends. Most R&D-active enterprises, and most Irish-owned ones, spent under €500,000 in 2023, while a small number of large, mostly foreign-owned enterprises accounted for most of the money. Use firm-level evidence, such as IDA Ireland announcements as account signals, and treat the national totals as context when you score industrial accounts.[1][3][5][8]

04 / ireland against other eu countries

Ireland against other EU countries

To compare countries, we use Eurostat's manufacturing (NACE C) series in million euro for 2023, the latest year with an Irish manufacturing cell. Ireland spent €3,587.6 million, which ranks 10th among the 26 EU countries with a published 2023 value, by our count, between Denmark and Finland. Germany is first, at €69,763.675 million, with a break-in-series flag. Portugal's 2023 manufacturing cell is confidential. Eurostat's EU27 manufacturing total is confidential, so we do not state a European manufacturing share or an EU average level.[9]

Ireland looks different depending on the measure. On business R&D per person in 2023, Ireland ranked second in the EU, at €1,329 per capita, behind Sweden (€1,375) and ahead of Denmark (€1,203). The EU27 average was €580, according to the CSO; Eurostat flags that average as estimated and Sweden's value as a break in series.[10][12]

As a share of GDP, Ireland is mid-table. In 2023, Irish business R&D was 1.34% of GDP, 10th of the 27 EU countries by our count, below the EU27 figure of 1.5% (estimated), according to Eurostat. Sweden was first, at 2.7%, with a break-in-series flag. We use the 2023 share, not 2024, because the 2024 Irish percentage (1.38%) is flagged as estimated and rests on the 2024 spend estimate covered next.[10]

05 / why the 2024 cso and eurostat figures differ

Why the 2024 CSO and Eurostat figures differ

For every year from 2019 to 2023, the CSO and Eurostat all-business-R&D totals for Ireland match to the euro. Only 2024 differs. The CSO's 2024 figure is €6,589.565 million, labelled estimated. Eurostat's is €7,763.567 million, flagged as estimated. By our count, Eurostat's figure is €1,174.002 million, or 17.8%, above the CSO's. Against the 2023 actual total of €7,003.745 million, by our count, the CSO estimate implies a 5.9% fall in 2024 and the Eurostat estimate a 10.8% rise. They do not agree on the direction.[1][2][9][10]

The official metadata explains how the two kinds of figure are compiled, but it does not say which compilation method produced Eurostat's 2024 Irish estimate, so we do not assign it.

  • The Irish BERD survey is carried out every two years. Enterprises provide final data for the survey year and estimates for the following year. That is the basis for the CSO's published 2022 and 2024 estimates.[1][11]
  • Ireland also sends Eurostat a preliminary figure at T+10 months after the end of the reference period. There is no national release of that preliminary data. Final data go to Eurostat at T+18 months, and the national final release is at T+15 months.[11]
  • Preliminary data "is derived from survey responses as available and from previous responses where current year data is not available."[11]
  • The last time this preliminary figure can be compared with a final one, for 2023, it was too high on spend. The T+10 preliminary total was €8,072.043 million; the final figure was €7,003.745 million, €1,068.298 million lower. By our count, the preliminary figure was 15.3% above the final one.[11]

Until both sources publish a final 2024 figure, use the 2023 actual total of €7,003.745 million, which they already agree on. If a 2024 number has to appear, quote both official estimates and label them as estimates. Do not treat 2021 to 2022 as like-for-like growth: Eurostat flags Ireland's 2022 total as both a break in series and an estimate, and the CSO footnotes 2022 as estimated and revised from preliminary to final.[1][9][11]

The latest survey covers 2023 (actual) and 2024 (estimated). According to the official metadata, final even-year data also include imputed values for R&D expenditure taken from the Community Innovation Survey where available.[1][11]

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How much do Irish manufacturers spend on R&D?
€3,594.983 million in 2023, according to the CSO, was spent by manufacturing industries, or 51.3% of all Irish business R&D, up from €1,502.419 million in 2021. Both years are actual survey figures. Eurostat's manufacturing series, used for the country comparison, puts Ireland at €3,587.6 million in 2023. By our count, the two official totals differ by €7.383 million.[1][4][9]
How much did Irish businesses spend on R&D in 2023?
€7,003.745 million, or €7.0 billion, according to the CSO, which was 81% higher than €3,879.007 million in 2021. Eurostat publishes the same 2023 total, to the euro. The survey runs every two years, so 2023 is an actual survey figure. Manufacturing industries accounted for 51.3% of that total, according to the CSO.[1][2][4][9]
How much did Irish businesses spend on R&D in 2024?
There is no agreed actual figure. The CSO estimates €6,589.565 million; Eurostat estimates €7,763.567 million. By our count they differ by €1,174.002 million, and they do not agree on whether spend rose or fell against 2023. Use the 2023 actual total of €7,003.745 million, which both sources already agree on, until a final 2024 figure is published.[1][2][9][10]
Who spends most of Ireland's business R&D?
In 2023, foreign-owned enterprises accounted for 83.8% of all business R&D spend but only 27.4% of R&D-active enterprises, according to the CSO. Large enterprises, with 250 or more persons engaged, accounted for 77.1% of spend, and the top 10 enterprises for 57.8%. These figures cover all business R&D, not manufacturing alone.[1][2][3][6][8]
Is Ireland a high-R&D country?
On spend per person, yes: in 2023 Ireland ranked second in the EU at €1,329 per capita, behind Sweden (€1,375) and ahead of Denmark (€1,203). The EU27 average was €580, according to the CSO. As a share of GDP, Ireland was 10th of 27 by our count, at 1.34%, below the EU27 estimated 1.5%, according to Eurostat.[10][12]

Electrical equipment manufacturing in Ireland: what the official data shows

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Turnover in Irish electrical equipment manufacturing rose 56.2% in 2025, the fastest growth among the 17 EU countries with a published value, by our count. That is Eurostat's short-term business statistics for NACE Rev. 2 division C27, which covers switchgear, motors, transformers, cables, batteries, lighting and similar equipment. Of all manufacturing (C), machinery (C28) and electrical equipment (C27), it is the only turnover series of the three that Eurostat publishes for Ireland.[1]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one thing: the official numbers for Irish electrical equipment makers, home and export, against the EU, plus what Prodcom shows for switchgear and motors. For how Prodcom works in general, see our Prodcom guide. For the activity codes behind C27, see Irish manufacturing NACE codes.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat sts_intv_a, sts_intvd_a, sts_intvnd_a and sts_intv_m; Prodcom DS-059367; checked 24 November 2026.

  • Irish C27 turnover rose 56.2% in 2025, after 18.6% in 2024. The EU-27 rose 2.4% in 2025, after falling 6.3% in 2024.[1]
  • The 2025 turnover index (2021 = 100) was 254.6 for Ireland and 117.7 for the EU-27.[1]
  • The rise came on both markets in 2025: domestic turnover +57.3% and non-domestic turnover +55.2%.[2][3]
  • In 2025 Ireland exported €1,077.3 million and imported €618.6 million of electricity distribution and control apparatus (switchgear), by our count of Prodcom's 23 headings.[5]
  • Eurostat publishes no Irish turnover figure for all manufacturing (NACE C) or machinery (C28). Those cells are confidential.[1]

Five of the nine Irish months were below the same month of 2025, and four were above, by our count. The monthly index stayed well above its 2021 base in every month. Eurostat's tables do not say why the Irish series moves, and neither do we.[4]

03 / home market and export market

Home market and export market

Eurostat splits turnover into the domestic market (customers in the same country) and the non-domestic market (customers in other countries). For euro-area members it also splits the non-domestic market into euro-area and non-euro-area customers.[2][3][9]

2025, NACE C27 Ireland, change on 2024 Ireland, index (2021 = 100) EU-27, change on 2024 EU-27, index (2021 = 100)
Domestic market +57.3% 218.4 +1.8% 119.9
Non-domestic market +55.2% 296.4 +3.0% 115.9

Sources: Eurostat sts_intvd_a and sts_intvnd_a, data last updated 19 November 2026, checked 24 November 2026.[2][3]

In 2024 the two Irish markets grew at different speeds: domestic +13.5% and non-domestic +23.4%. In 2025 they grew at almost the same rate. Over 2021 to 2025 the non-domestic index rose further than the domestic one, 296.4 against 218.4.[2][3]

Within non-domestic sales, Irish C27 turnover to euro-area customers rose 51.4% in 2025 and turnover to customers outside the euro area rose 56.8%. The UK is outside the euro area, but the table does not show the UK on its own.[3]

05 / switchgear motors and what prodcom shows

Switchgear, motors and what Prodcom shows

Prodcom is Eurostat's product statistic. For each product heading it gives sold production and, alongside it, exports and imports taken from external trade statistics.[5][7][8] We looked at two NACE classes inside C27 for Ireland in 2025.

Ireland, 2025 (by our count of Prodcom headings) Exports Imports Balance Published sold production Production headings confidential
27.12 Electricity distribution and control apparatus (switchgear), 23 headings €1,077.3m €618.6m +€458.7m €121.9m 14 of 23
27.11 Electric motors, generators and transformers, 51 headings €253.0m €427.7m −€174.7m €71.4m 24 of 51

Source: Eurostat Prodcom DS-059367, reference year 2025, data last updated 10 November 2026, checked 24 November 2026.[5]

  • Switchgear: a trade surplus. By our count, Ireland exported €458.7 million more switchgear and control apparatus than it imported in 2025.[5]
  • Motors, generators and transformers: a trade deficit. By our count, Ireland imported €174.7 million more than it exported.[5]
  • Exports are not Irish-made output. Prodcom's trade fields come from external trade statistics, which record a product each time it crosses a border. A product can be counted as a trade more than once, but it is only produced once.[8]
  • Published production is a floor. The €121.9 million for switchgear adds only the 9 headings with a published value. The 14 confidential headings are left out, so the real Irish total is higher by an unknown amount.[5]

At EU-27 level, sold production of switchgear and control apparatus was about €60.18 billion in 2025, up from about €55.79 billion in 2024, a rise of about 7.9% at current prices, by our count. The 23 headings are the same in both years. EU-27 exports were €33.30 billion and imports €17.17 billion, counting only trade with countries outside the EU.[5][6][8]

We give no growth rate for motors, generators and transformers. The class had 46 Prodcom headings in 2024 and 51 in 2025, with only 37 in common, so a simple comparison would not be like for like.[5][6]

06 / gaps confidential cells and no all manufacturing total

Gaps: confidential cells and no all-manufacturing total

  • No Irish all-manufacturing turnover. Eurostat publishes no Irish value for all manufacturing (NACE C) or for machinery (C28) in its turnover tables, annual or monthly, total, domestic or non-domestic. The cells are marked confidential. C27 is the only one of the three published for Ireland, so it cannot stand in for Irish manufacturing as a whole.[1][2][3][4]
  • Confidential Prodcom headings. Reporting countries mark some Prodcom cells confidential, and Eurostat is legally bound to suppress them.[8] In 2025, 14 of 23 Irish switchgear production headings and 24 of 51 motors headings were confidential, by our count.[5]
  • Provisional months. September 2026 is provisional and can be revised. October 2026 is not yet in the table.[4]
  • Value, not volume. Turnover is at current prices. Eurostat's volume-of-sales measure deflates turnover, and these tables do not.[9]
  • No cause in the data. The tables show how much turnover changed, not why. They do not show which firms or products drove the change.
  • The UK. Eurostat keeps UK short-term business statistics only up to 2020, and Prodcom does not cover the UK. For UK product data, the Office for National Statistics publishes UK manufacturers' sales by product.[8][9][10]

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How fast did Irish electrical equipment sales grow in 2025?
Irish electrical equipment turnover rose 56.2% in 2025, after 18.6% in 2024, according to Eurostat. That was the fastest growth among the 17 EU countries with a published value, by our count. The EU-27 rose 2.4%. Turnover is at current prices, so the figure includes price changes as well as volume.[1]
Did Irish electrical equipment makers sell more at home or abroad?
Both grew at almost the same rate in 2025. Domestic turnover rose 57.3% and non-domestic turnover rose 55.2%, according to Eurostat. Since 2021, non-domestic sales have grown further: their index reached 296.4 in 2025, against 218.4 for the domestic market, with 2021 set at 100 for both.[2][3]
Is Ireland a net exporter of switchgear?
Yes. In 2025 Ireland exported €1,077.3 million and imported €618.6 million of electricity distribution and control apparatus, a surplus of €458.7 million, by our count of Eurostat Prodcom headings. Exports are trade figures and can count the same goods more than once, so they are not a measure of Irish-made output.[5][8]
How is Irish electrical equipment turnover doing in 2026?
It has been uneven. Irish turnover in January to September 2026 swung between 25.4% below and 27.1% above the same month of 2025, according to Eurostat. The latest month, September, was 25.8% higher and is provisional. The EU-27 rose 8.0% in August, its latest published month.[4]
Why is there no Irish manufacturing turnover figure?
Eurostat marks the Irish all-manufacturing (NACE C) and machinery (C28) turnover cells as confidential, so it publishes no value for them. Electrical equipment (C27) is published. That means no official Irish manufacturing-wide turnover growth rate exists in these tables, and C27 should not be read as a proxy for all Irish manufacturing.[1]
Does the data cover the UK?
No. Eurostat keeps UK short-term business statistics only up to 2020, without updates, and its Prodcom tables do not include the UK. For UK figures, the Office for National Statistics publishes UK manufacturers' sales by product. Check its product codes and latest edition before comparing them with the Eurostat tables on this page.[8][9][10]

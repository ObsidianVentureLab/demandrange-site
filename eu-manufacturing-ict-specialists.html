Which EU manufacturers employ their own ICT specialists

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

About one in five EU manufacturers employs its own ICT specialists, and in Ireland it is closer to two in five. Eurostat's survey of ICT usage in enterprises asks firms with 10 or more persons employed whether they employ ICT specialists. The answer, by country and by manufacturing sector, is a public proxy for in-house technical capability.

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one thing: the share of manufacturers employing ICT specialists in 2024, by EU country and by manufacturing sector, with Ireland's position. For how firm counts differ by country, see EU industrial enterprises by country.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat isoc_ske_itspen2, enterprises with 10 or more persons employed; data last updated 15 June 2026; extracted 24 November 2026.

  • 20.62% of EU-27 manufacturers employed ICT specialists in 2024.[1]
  • Ireland had the highest share in the EU at 38.11%, about 1.8 times the EU-27 share by our count. Belgium (35.31%) and Finland (33.73%) came next.[1]
  • 13 of the 27 member states were above the EU-27 share, by our count. Romania was lowest at 10.01%.[1]
  • Irish manufacturers were more likely than Irish businesses overall to employ ICT specialists: 38.11% against 30.28% across all activities covered by the survey, without the financial sector.[1][2]

The EU-27 share has barely moved in a decade: 20.17% in 2014, 21.45% in 2022 and 20.62% in 2024. Ireland's share was 36.91% in 2022 and 38.11% in 2024, but it has swung more, from 33.48% in 2020 to a high of 45.78% in 2017. Eurostat's survey runs every year, but this indicator has no value for 2021 or 2023.[1][3]

Country shares reflect sector mix as well as capability. A country with many pharmaceutical or electronics plants tends to score higher than one dominated by food, textiles or metal fabrication, so read the country table together with the sector table below.

Food and drink stands out. In the EU, it is one of the sectors least likely to employ ICT specialists (13.52%), but in Ireland the share was 38.50%, about 2.8 times the EU-27 share by our count. Pharmaceuticals had the highest share in both Ireland and the EU.[1]

Read the sector rows with care. Irish sector groups hold far fewer firms than the EU totals, so a handful of firms can move a share by several points between survey years. Eurostat flags none of the Irish 2024 cells in this table. We leave out coke and refined petroleum (C19), where Ireland's published value is 0.[1]

04 / what counts as an ict specialist

What counts as an ICT specialist

For this survey, Eurostat defines ICT specialists as "workers who have the ability to develop, operate and maintain ICT systems, and for whom ICT constitute the main part of their job".[2] In practice, by our reading, that means in-house roles such as systems and network administrators, developers and data or infrastructure engineers, not staff who simply use computers in their work.

Four points on scope:

  • Firms with 10 or more persons employed only. The survey covers enterprises with at least 10 employees and self-employed persons. Micro firms are optional and are not in these figures.[1][3]
  • A yes-or-no measure. The figure is the share of enterprises that employ at least one ICT specialist. It says nothing about how many specialists a firm has.[1]
  • Reference year. Data are filed by survey year. Eurostat states that data on enterprises that employed ICT specialists refer to calendar year 2024.[2]
  • No UK figures. Eurostat's table has no United Kingdom value after 2019, so this page compares EU member states only.[1]
05 / using it as a buyer capability signal

Using it as a buyer-capability signal

In our view, a manufacturer with its own ICT specialists can usually evaluate, integrate and support software, automation and connected equipment in-house. One without them is more likely to rely on the supplier, an integrator or a managed-service partner. Both are buyers, but they buy differently.

How suppliers can use the figures:

  • Set expectations by market. In Ireland, nearly two in five manufacturers employ ICT specialists; in Italy or Spain, fewer than one in seven do.[1] Where the share is low, plan for more implementation support and simpler integration in the offer.
  • Weight the sector, not just the country. Of the sectors in the table above, Irish pharmaceutical, transport-equipment and electronics makers were the most likely to employ ICT specialists in 2024, and metal products, wood and paper, and textiles makers the least likely. Across the EU, pharmaceuticals and electronics led, and textiles and food were lowest.[1]
  • Treat it as a prior, not an account fact. The statistic is a share of firms. Whether one named site has an ICT team has to be checked account by account, from job adverts, published roles and project evidence.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What share of EU manufacturers employ ICT specialists?
About one in five. In 2024, 20.62% of EU-27 manufacturing enterprises with 10 or more persons employed had at least one ICT specialist on staff, according to Eurostat. The share has stayed close to that level for a decade, at 20.17% in 2014 and 21.45% in 2022.[1]
Which EU country has the most manufacturers employing ICT specialists?
Ireland. In 2024, 38.11% of Irish manufacturers with 10 or more persons employed employed ICT specialists, the highest share in the EU and about 1.8 times the EU-27 share of 20.62%, by our count. Belgium (35.31%) and Finland (33.73%) followed. Romania was lowest at 10.01%.[1]
Which Irish manufacturing sectors are most likely to employ ICT specialists?
Pharmaceuticals, at 69.27% of Irish pharmaceutical manufacturers in 2024, then motor vehicles and other transport equipment (57.27%) and computer, electronic and optical products (54.55%). Irish food and drink manufacturers were at 38.50%, against 13.52% across the EU. Sector groups are small, so shares can move between survey years.[1]
What is an ICT specialist in Eurostat's data?
Eurostat defines ICT specialists as workers able to develop, operate and maintain ICT systems, for whom ICT is the main part of their job. The survey records only whether an enterprise employs at least one, not how many. It covers enterprises with 10 or more persons employed.[1][2]
Does the data include the UK?
No. Eurostat's table has no United Kingdom value after 2019, so the 2024 comparison covers EU member states only. UK figures on firms employing ICT specialists would need a separate UK official source, and its definitions should be checked before comparing them with the EU table.[1]

UK manufacturing output by industry

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 25 November 2026

In September 2026, UK manufacturing output rose 0.7% on the month and 4.4% on the year, in the Office for National Statistics (ONS) Index of Production — a seasonally adjusted chained volume index with 2024 = 100. Seven of the 13 manufacturing subsectors rose on the month. The largest positive contribution came from food products, beverages and tobacco, up 2.9%. The largest negative contribution came from basic metals and metal products, down 2.1%. The figures are from the Index of Production bulletin and the Output of the production industries dataset (IOP5), both released on 12 November 2026.[1][2]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns the UK manufacturing volume series by industry: the 13 subsectors in SIC 2007 section C, so that suppliers can see which parts of UK manufacturing are expanding or contracting. It does not count sites or enterprises. For that, see UK manufacturing sites by nation. For how we build a UK manufacturer list, see how to build a list of UK manufacturers. The section codes used here are UK SIC codes for manufacturing.

01 / key facts

Key facts

ONS Index of Production and IOP5, seasonally adjusted chained volume indices, 2024 = 100; checked 24 November 2026.

  • Manufacturing output rose 0.7% in September 2026 on August, and 4.4% on September 2025. The September 2026 index was 102.4. That was the third consecutive monthly rise, by our count of the official month-on-month rates (0.8% in July, 0.7% in August and 0.7% in September).[1][2]
  • In Quarter 3 (July to September) 2026, manufacturing output rose 0.9% on Quarter 2. Eight of the 13 subsectors increased. The largest positive contribution came from computer, electronic and optical products, up 4.6%. The largest negative contribution came from rubber and plastic products and non-metallic mineral products, down 2.0%.[1][2]
  • In 2025 as a whole, the manufacturing index was 99.4, 0.6% below 2024.[2]
  • Food products, beverages and tobacco has the largest official weight of the 13 subsectors, at 117.8 parts per thousand of the production industries (sections B to E). That is 16.9% of manufacturing's official weight of 697.4, by our count, which is why a 2.9% monthly rise can be the largest contribution even when a smaller industry rises faster.[2]
  • Transport equipment output was 15.9% higher in September 2026 than a year earlier, the largest official year-on-year rate among the 13. That comparison is with a weak September 2025, when the index was 85.4 after a 12.4% fall on August 2025. On the month, transport equipment rose 0.6% in September 2026.[2]
02 / manufacturing output in september 2026

Manufacturing output in September 2026

The latest month is useful for timing, but a single month can jump around, especially in smaller-weight industries. The table below shows the official September 2026 index for each manufacturing subsector, with the official month-on-month and year-on-year percentage changes.

The month-on-month rises, besides food, were coke and refined petroleum products (12.2%), electrical equipment (3.8%), other manufacturing and repair (3.7%), machinery and equipment not elsewhere classified (1.6%), chemicals and chemical products (1.3%) and transport equipment (0.6%). The six falls were textiles, wearing apparel and leather products (−2.3%), basic metals and metal products (−2.1%), computer, electronic and optical products (−2.1%), wood and paper products and printing (−2.0%), basic pharmaceutical products and preparations (−0.8%) and rubber and plastic products and non-metallic mineral products (−0.2%).[2]

Coke and refined petroleum products had the largest official monthly percentage rise, at 12.2%, but the smallest official weight of the 13 subsectors (8.6). A large percentage change in a small-weight industry is not the same as a large contribution to manufacturing as a whole. The bulletin's largest positive contribution in September was food, up 2.9%, not petroleum.[1][2]

By our count of the official September 2026 index numbers, basic pharmaceutical products and preparations were the farthest above their 2024 baseline, at 115.5, and basic metals and metal products were the farthest below, at 89.5.[2]

03 / output in the third quarter of 2026

Output in the third quarter of 2026

Quarterly changes smooth some of the monthly noise and are often the better guide when you are deciding where to put sales time in the next quarter. In Quarter 3 2026, manufacturing output rose 0.9% on Quarter 2, and 3.0% on Quarter 3 2025. The Quarter 3 2026 index was 101.7, after 100.8 in Quarter 2 and 100.3 in Quarter 1.[1][2]

Eight of the 13 subsectors rose on the quarter. The official quarter-on-quarter rates were: electrical equipment 5.6%; computer, electronic and optical products 4.6%; machinery and equipment not elsewhere classified 3.0%; basic pharmaceutical products and preparations 2.1%; other manufacturing and repair 2.1%; coke and refined petroleum products 1.3%; food products, beverages and tobacco 0.4%; and transport equipment 0.4%. The five falls were: textiles, wearing apparel and leather products (−3.7%); rubber and plastic products and non-metallic mineral products (−2.0%); basic metals and metal products (−0.7%); wood and paper products and printing (−0.4%); and chemicals and chemical products (−0.1%).[1][2]

The bulletin's largest positive contribution in the quarter was computer, electronic and optical products, up 4.6%, not electrical equipment, even though electrical equipment had the larger official percentage rise. Electrical equipment's official weight is 19.5, against 44.9 for computer, electronic and optical products.[1][2]

04 / the 2025 annual picture

The 2025 annual picture

Monthly and quarterly rises in 2026 sit on top of a soft year in 2025. Manufacturing output was 0.6% lower in 2025 than in 2024, with an annual index of 99.4 against the 2024 baseline of 100.0.[2]

The official annual changes for 2025 were mixed. By our count of those official rates, the sharpest fall among the 13 subsectors was basic metals and metal products, down 8.4% in 2025. Transport equipment fell 3.4%, before the year-on-year bounce in September 2026 described above. Food products, beverages and tobacco, and textiles, wearing apparel and leather products, each fell 2.4%. Rubber and plastic products and non-metallic mineral products fell 2.0%, and chemicals and chemical products fell 1.5%.[2]

The official annual rises in 2025 were: coke and refined petroleum products 13.1%; machinery and equipment not elsewhere classified 5.8%; electrical equipment 4.9%; basic pharmaceutical products and preparations 4.7%; computer, electronic and optical products 3.2%; wood and paper products and printing 0.8%; and other manufacturing and repair 0.2%.[2]

Some of those 2025 moves continue earlier shifts. Chemicals and chemical products fell 20.6% in 2022 and 20.8% in 2023, then rose 5.8% in 2024, before the 1.5% fall in 2025. The 2025 annual index for chemicals was 98.5, and the Quarter 3 2026 index was 94.9, still below the 2024 baseline. Basic metals and metal products rose 3.8% in 2022 and 2.4% in 2023, then fell 1.4% in 2024 and 8.4% in 2025.[2]

05 / how to read the figures for uk territory planning

How to read the figures for UK territory planning

The Index of Production measures the volume of output, not the cash value of sales. The ONS constructs it mainly by taking turnover and removing the effect of price changes, or by using direct volume estimates. A rise therefore means more output, not only higher prices.[1]

The official "latest weight" is in parts per thousand of the UK production industries (mining and quarrying, manufacturing, electricity and gas, and water and waste), which sum to 1,000.0. Manufacturing's weight is 697.4. When you are comparing one manufacturing industry with another, use those official weights, not the percentage change alone. Food can move the manufacturing total more than petroleum because its weight is much larger.[2]

Use the year-on-year rate with care when the year-ago month was unusually weak or strong. Transport equipment in September 2026 is the clear case: a 15.9% rise on the year against a 0.6% rise on the month. Look at the index level and the quarter-on-quarter rate as well as the year-on-year percentage.[2]

This release gave September 2026 data for the first time and revised July 2026 and August 2026. Later monthly releases can revise recent months again. The IOP5 tables mark the earliest revised period in the table; they also note that apparent inconsistencies between rounded index numbers and the published percentage changes are due to rounding.[1][2]

The 13 section codes (CA to CM) are the manufacturing subsections in UK SIC 2007. They are the same groupings we use when we search for manufacturers by SIC code. The series does not tell you how many firms or sites sit in each group; for site counts, use UK manufacturing sites by nation. If you want this output series read against a manufacturer list for a UK territory, request a quote.

06 / how this series differs from the eu production index

How this series differs from the EU production index

Our page on the EU industrial production index by country uses Eurostat's industrial production index, which has a 2021 = 100 base and is calendar adjusted. The ONS series on this page is a seasonally adjusted chained volume measure with 2024 = 100, and it is published for UK manufacturing industries rather than as a country league table. The two series are not on the same basis, so this page does not rank the UK against EU countries, and it does not place UK and EU index levels in the same table.

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Which UK manufacturing industries grew in September 2026?
Seven of the 13 manufacturing subsectors rose on the month in September 2026. The largest positive contribution was food products, beverages and tobacco, up 2.9%. The largest negative contribution was basic metals and metal products, down 2.1%. A large percentage change in a small-weight industry is not the same as a large contribution to manufacturing as a whole.[1][2]
What is the ONS Index of Production?
It is the official monthly measure of the volume of output in the UK production industries, including manufacturing. The figures on this page are seasonally adjusted chained volume indices with 2024 = 100, from the Output of the production industries dataset. They remove price changes, so a rise means more volume, not higher prices. The ONS publishes the series monthly.[2]
Why did transport equipment rise so sharply on the year?
Transport equipment output was 15.9% higher in September 2026 than in September 2025. That year-on-year rate compares with a weak year-ago month: the September 2025 index was 85.4, after a 12.4% fall on August 2025. On the month, transport equipment rose only 0.6% in September 2026, and in Quarter 3 it was up 0.4% on Quarter 2.[2]
Can I compare these figures with the EU industrial production index?
Not as a ranking. This page uses the ONS Index of Production for UK manufacturing, which is a seasonally adjusted chained volume measure with 2024 = 100. The EU industrial production index uses Eurostat's series, which has a 2021 = 100 base and calendar adjustment. The bases do not match, so we do not rank the UK against EU countries.[2]
What happened to UK manufacturing output in 2025?
In 2025 as a whole, the manufacturing index was 99.4, 0.6% below 2024. The sharpest official annual fall among the 13 subsectors was basic metals and metal products, down 8.4%. Coke and refined petroleum products rose 13.1%, the largest official annual increase, but that industry has the smallest official weight of the 13.[2]

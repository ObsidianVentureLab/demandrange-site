How Irish manufacturers use their websites

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

88.8% of Irish manufacturers with 10 or more employees and self-employed persons had a website in 2025, against 82.59% across the EU27. But only 16.17% offered online ordering, and only 9.31% had website content in two or more languages, against 41.59% EU-wide. These are Eurostat figures from the 2025 survey on ICT usage and e-commerce in enterprises, table isoc_ciwebn2.[1]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page sets out what the official data says about Irish manufacturers' websites, how that compares with the EU, and what a manufacturer's website can and cannot tell you when you research an account. It does not name companies, and the figures are shares of enterprises, not of sales or spend.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat isoc_ciwebn2 and isoc_cismtn2, manufacturing (NACE Rev. 2 section C), enterprises with 10 or more employees and self-employed persons, % of enterprises; checked 24 November 2026.

  • 88.8% of Irish manufacturers had a website in 2025, against 82.59% in the EU27.[1]
  • 74.72% had a website describing goods or services or giving price lists in 2025 (EU27: 66.55%).[1]
  • 16.17% had a website offering online ordering, reservation or booking in 2025 (EU27: 15.22%).[1]
  • 4.11% offered online order tracking in 2025 (EU27: 7.54%).[1]
  • 9.31% had website content in at least two languages in 2025, against 41.59% EU-wide.[1]
  • 67.73% used any social media in 2025 (EU27: 59.58%).[2]
02 / irish manufacturers websites in 2025

Irish manufacturers' websites in 2025

The figures come from Eurostat's annual survey on ICT usage and e-commerce in enterprises. National statistical institutes collect the data each year using Eurostat's model questionnaire. The statistical unit is the enterprise, and the survey population is enterprises with 10 or more employees and self-employed persons, so the smallest manufacturers are not in these figures. In general, the data refer to the situation during the survey period. Every figure on this page is a percentage of all manufacturing enterprises in that population, not a percentage of those with a website. The EU figure is Eurostat's EU27 (from 2020) aggregate. Eurostat last updated both tables on 27 February 2026, and no value used on this page carries a flag.[1][2][3]

The eight listed functions are: descriptions of goods or services or price lists; online ordering, reservation or booking; customising or designing goods or services online; online order tracking; personalised content for repeat visitors; a chat service for customer support; job advertisements or online job applications; and content in at least two languages.[1]

Irish manufacturers are more likely than the EU average to have a website, but their websites do fewer things. In 2025, 38.6% of Irish manufacturers had a website with at least two of the eight listed functions, against 52.74% across the EU27, a gap of 14.14 percentage points by our count. The Irish website share has edged down in each survey since 2021, from 93.0% to 89.99% in 2023 and 88.8% in 2025, though it was 87.09% in 2020.[1]

03 / product information ordering and tracking

Product information, ordering and tracking

Most Irish manufacturers' websites are a product catalogue, not a shop. Three in four (74.72%) described their goods or services or gave price lists in 2025. About one in six (16.17%) offered online ordering, reservation or booking, such as a shopping cart. Few offered order tracking (4.11%), personalised content for repeat visitors (5.28%) or online customisation of goods or services (5.37%).[1]

Online ordering is slightly more common among Irish manufacturers than across the EU27 (16.17% against 15.22% in 2025), but it fell from 19.19% in 2023. Order tracking moved the other way from the EU: 4.11% of Irish manufacturers offered it in 2025, against 7.54% EU-wide. The share describing goods or price lists rose from 64.84% in 2023 to 74.72% in 2025. In 2021 it was 84.95%, so the Irish series can move by as much as 20 percentage points between surveys (by our count) and is best read as a level, not a trend.[1]

These shares count enterprises, not orders. A manufacturer with a web shop may still take most of its business through sales staff, quotations and framework contracts. The table does not measure the value of orders placed through websites.[1][3]

04 / languages and social media use

Languages and social media use

The widest gap with the EU is language. In 2025, 9.31% of Irish manufacturers had website content in at least two languages, against 41.59% across the EU27, a gap of 32.28 percentage points by our count. The 2023 survey showed the same pattern (Ireland 9.71%, EU27 40.24%).[1]

That gap should be read with care. By our reading, English already reaches many export markets, so a single-language site is not the same weakness for an Irish manufacturer as for one writing only in a smaller EU language. Even so, about two in five EU manufacturers (41.59%) had website content in at least two languages in 2025, against fewer than one in ten Irish manufacturers (9.31%).[1]

Irish manufacturers use social media more than the EU average. In 2025, 67.73% used any social media, against 59.58% across the EU27. The Irish share was 56.83% in 2019, 63.11% in 2021 and 68.09% in 2023, so it has been flat since 2023. In 2023, the latest year with a breakdown by type, 66.46% of Irish manufacturers used social networks (EU27 2023: 53.01%).[2]

Paid online advertising is also more common among Irish manufacturers. In 2024, 39.03% paid to advertise on the internet, up from 29.29% in 2023, against 27.2% across the EU27 in 2024.[2]

05 / what a website tells you during account research

What a website tells you during account research

The official figures describe the whole population. For one account, the website is often the first source you open. By our reading of the figures above, here is what it can and cannot tell you.

  1. Product fit, usually. Three in four Irish manufacturers describe their goods or services or give price lists on their website (74.72% in 2025). That makes the website a reasonable first check of product range and fit, before you score the account. Our industrial account prioritisation page sets out how to weigh that evidence.
  2. How they buy, rarely. Only 16.17% of Irish manufacturers offered online ordering in 2025, and 4.11% offered order tracking. The absence of a web shop does not mean an account is small or inactive. Most industrial buying still happens outside the website.
  3. Export reach, partly. With only 9.31% of Irish manufacturers publishing in two or more languages, a multilingual site is a distinctive, though not conclusive, signal of selling into non-English-speaking markets. A single-language site tells you little either way.
  4. Hiring, sometimes. 23.55% of Irish manufacturers advertised jobs or took job applications on their website in 2025. A careers page with current roles is a dated signal of activity at that enterprise; an empty one is not evidence of decline.
  5. Not size, sites or decision-makers. A website rarely states employee numbers, plant output or who signs off on purchases, and 11.2% of Irish manufacturers had no website at all in 2025, by our count (100% minus 88.8%). Use official registers, plant data and dated evidence alongside the website. Our sample account research shows what that looks like for one account, and the prospect list audit shows how to check a bought list against the same evidence.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many Irish manufacturers have a website?
88.8% of Irish manufacturers with 10 or more employees and self-employed persons had a website in 2025, according to Eurostat table isoc_ciwebn2. That is above the EU27 figure of 82.59%. The Irish share was 93.0% in 2021 and 89.99% in 2023, so it has edged down slightly in each survey since 2021.[1]
Do Irish manufacturers sell online through their own websites?
Few offer it. In 2025, 16.17% of Irish manufacturers with 10 or more employees and self-employed persons had a website offering online ordering, reservation or booking, according to Eurostat isoc_ciwebn2. The EU27 figure was 15.22%. Three in four (74.72%) described their goods or services or gave price lists online instead.[1]
Why are so few Irish manufacturers' websites multilingual?
Eurostat does not give a reason. It reports that 9.31% of Irish manufacturers had website content in at least two languages in 2025, against 41.59% across the EU27. English reaches many export markets already, so Irish firms may see less need to translate. The figure covers enterprises with 10 or more employees and self-employed persons.[1]
Do Irish manufacturers use social media more than the EU average?
Yes. In 2025, 67.73% of Irish manufacturers with 10 or more employees and self-employed persons used any social media, against 59.58% across the EU27, according to Eurostat table isoc_cismtn2. The Irish share was 68.09% in 2023, so use has been flat since then. The table has no 2025 breakdown by type of social media.[2]
Can a manufacturer's website be used to judge it as an account?
Partly. Most Irish manufacturers' websites describe products or price lists, so a website is a useful check of product fit. Few show ordering, order tracking or other languages, and a website rarely states size, plant output or who buys. Treat it as one dated source alongside official registers and site evidence.[1]

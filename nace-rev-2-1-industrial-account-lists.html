NACE Rev. 2.1 manufacturing codes: what changed and how to fix your industrial account lists

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

NACE Rev. 2.1 manufacturing codes mostly match the old ones, but the classes that moved can quietly break an industrial account list. NACE Rev. 2.1 is the EU's updated activity classification, phased into official statistics from 2025 to 2031. Map old codes with Eurostat's correspondence tables, then review the changed classes by hand.

Key facts (EU Regulation 2023/137 and Eurostat code lists; checked 28 October 2026)

  • NACE Rev. 2.1 is set by Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/137, which phases it into official statistics from 1 January 2025 through to 2031.[1]
  • In Eurostat's published code lists, manufacturing (Section C) has 24 divisions, 92 groups and 232 classes in NACE Rev. 2.1, against 24 divisions, 95 groups and 230 classes in NACE Rev. 2.[6][7]
  • Class 25.61 was treatment and coating of metals in NACE Rev. 2 and is manufacture of cutlery in NACE Rev. 2.1, while new class 28.97 covers manufacture of additive manufacturing machinery.[6][7]

01 / 1 how section c is structured now

1. How Section C is structured now

In Eurostat's published code lists, manufacturing (Section C) keeps 24 divisions. It has 92 groups and 232 classes in NACE Rev. 2.1, against 95 groups and 230 classes in NACE Rev. 2.[6][7] The counts barely move, but codes inside them change meaning. Class 25.61, for example, was treatment and coating of metals in Rev. 2 and is cutlery in Rev. 2.1.[6][7] Across the whole classification, by our count of the codes at each level in Eurostat's code lists (NACE_R2_1 version 13.2 against NACE_R2), NACE Rev. 2.1 has 22 sections, 87 divisions, 287 groups and 651 classes, against 21, 88, 272 and 615 in NACE Rev. 2.[6][7] Germany's WZ 2025 has the same four totals.[4]

02 / 2 section c changes that matter for industrial suppliers

2. Section C changes that matter for industrial suppliers

Regulation (EU) 2023/137 sets NACE Rev. 2.1 and its 22 sections, and the phase-in dates from 2025 to 2031.[1] Eurostat's correspondence tables map old codes to new ones.[3]

Eurostat has published a 2026 edition of the NACE Rev. 2.1 manual (KS-01-26-047). It adds the latest explanatory notes, reconciled with CPA Ver. 2.2, and a new annex on national versions of NACE.[9] The manual gives class-level correspondence types for correspondence table version 1.06, across all sections: 354 NACE Rev. 2 classes are 1-to-1, 25 are merged (n-to-1), 12 are split (1-to-m) and 224 are n-to-m.[9]

Under Article 2 of Regulation (EU) 2023/137, the default is data for reference periods from 1 January 2025. Some domains switch later: structure of earnings, waste statistics, job vacancies and the labour force survey from 2026; the labour cost index, energy statistics and accidents at work from 2027; labour costs, agricultural accounts and the air-emissions and energy-flow accounts modules from 2028; national accounts transmissions from September 2029; vocational training and the consumption and time-use domains from 2030; and the health domain from 2031.[1] Eurostat says parallel data sets by NACE Rev. 2.1 will be published for many domains, using backcasting, and that for some statistics countries will report under both versions for a period. For bank reporting, the Joint Banking Reporting Committee recommends using NACE Rev. 2.1 from 1 January 2026.[11]

03 / 3 a safe recoding process

3. A safe recoding process

  1. Export your account list with the current NACE Rev. 2 or UK SIC 2007 code on each row.
  2. Join it to Eurostat's NACE Rev. 2 → Rev. 2.1 correspondence table.[3]
  3. Flag one-to-many rows, where one old class maps to several new ones. These need a human decision, usually from the company's website or its register filing.
  4. Keep both codes during the transition. National registers and statistics move at different speeds; Germany, for example, runs WZ 2025 alongside WZ 2008.[4]
  5. Re-run your ideal customer profile filters against the new codes, and compare account counts before and after.

How many rows need that decision? By our count of Eurostat's current NACE Rev. 2 → Rev. 2.1 correspondence table (ShowVoc download dated 31 August 2026, which matches the version 1.06 totals in the 2026 NACE manual), 55 of the 230 NACE Rev. 2 manufacturing classes (24%) map to more than one NACE Rev. 2.1 class.[9][10] We counted distinct Rev. 2.1 codes for each class-level row. The table's own "type of correspondence" cell, a different measure, gives 137 of the 230 as 1-to-1, 4 as n-to-1, 3 as 1-to-m and 86 as n-to-m. Two classes partly leave manufacturing: 33.11 (repair of fabricated metal products) maps partly to 03.30 (support activities for fishing and aquaculture), and 33.19 (repair of other equipment) partly to 91.30 (conservation and restoration).[10] The file itself carries no version number, so check each changed class against the table you download.

Germany shows how long the overlap lasts. WZ 2025 has 983 subclasses, against 839 in WZ 2008. Destatis says the statistical offices were to recode the statistical business register to WZ 2025 by the end of 2025, and WZ certificates showing an enterprise's WZ 2025 code are issued from 1 January 2026. Until WZ 2008 is fully replaced, both classifications apply in parallel.[4][18] Structural business statistics move from reference year 2025, with first WZ 2025 publications in summer 2027. Short-term statistics move from January 2028 and are published by WZ 2008 until the end of 2027. The production, orders and turnover indices start with January 2028 data, first published in March 2028 and backcast to January 1991. The annual plant report and the investment survey start with reference year 2028.[19]

Fictional example: A fictional coatings supplier targets NACE Rev. 2 class 25.61 (treatment and coating of metals). Under NACE Rev. 2.1, Eurostat's current correspondence table maps old 25.61 to 25.51 (coating of metals), 25.52 (heat treatment of metals) and 25.53 (machining), and machining also moves from 25.62 to 25.53.[2][10] In Rev. 2.1, 25.61 and 25.62 mean cutlery and locks and hinges, so a filter left on the old codes would pull in the wrong companies. The supplier reviews each one by hand, keeps both codes in its CRM, and updates its ICP filter so no accounts are silently dropped. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

04 / 4 uk and irish lists

4. UK and Irish lists

  • UK: Companies House records use UK SIC 2007,[5] which is derived from NACE Rev. 2.[20] The ONS has published UK SIC 2026, effective from 14 April 2026 and based on NACE Rev. 2.1 down to the four-digit level. It has 22 sections, 87 divisions, 287 groups, 668 classes and 410 subclasses: 17 more classes than NACE Rev. 2.1, and 410 subclasses against 191 in SIC 2007. The ONS says it is not yet in operational use across ONS systems, and the earliest planned use is the 2031 Blue Book.[15][16] Companies House has not switched. In a 10 June 2026 reply to a comment on its blog (a blog reply, not formal guidance), it said: "A future SIC code framework is under active discussion between Companies House and ONS, but no final framework has yet been agreed or implemented. Any changes to the SIC code framework would require legislative change and careful consideration of the impact before implementation."[17] UK SIC 2026 also departs from NACE Rev. 2.1 in Section C. NACE 29.10 becomes UK 29.11 (motor vehicle engines) and 29.12 (motor vehicles), and NACE 30.31 and 30.32 become UK 30.31 civilian aircraft, 30.32 military aircraft and 30.33 spacecraft. So UK 30.31 and 30.32 do not mean the same as NACE Rev. 2.1 30.31 and 30.32.[16] Don't assume a UK code matches a NACE Rev. 2.1 code with the same number. The ONS has also published a UK SIC 2007 to SIC 2026 correspondence table, dated 3 August 2026, on its UK SIC 2026 page,[21] and our UK SIC codes for manufacturing page joins the UK Section C codes to it.
  • Ireland: the CSO says its Business Register "completed work on updating the coding of enterprises to NACE Rev. 2.1" during 2025, and that from January 2026 its outputs move to NACE Rev. 2.1 gradually, starting with business and labour force statistics.[12] The Labour Force Survey moved to Rev. 2.1 from Q1 2026, backcast to 2019, with Rev. 2 estimates still published for reference year 2026.[13] Earnings and Labour Costs moved from Q1 2026, backcast to 2018.[14] Check which NACE version each dataset uses. For the codes that matter in Irish lists, see our Irish manufacturing NACE codes page; for the Irish manufacturing base, see our Irish manufacturing account research guide.
05 / 5 codes are a starting point not a list

5. Codes are a starting point, not a list

Activity codes are self-declared and often out of date. Use them to build a first cut. Then confirm each account with evidence: products on the website, permits (see the EU Industrial Emissions Portal guide), tenders and hiring. Our industrial ideal customer profile and prospect list maintenance guides cover that step.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

When does NACE Rev. 2.1 apply?

It is being phased into official statistics from 1 January 2025 through to 2031.[1] National versions such as Germany's WZ 2025 run alongside the old codes during the switch.[4]

Do all manufacturing codes change?

No. Section C keeps 24 divisions, and most classes are unchanged. A small number were split, merged or renumbered, which is why one-to-many mappings need a manual check.[3][6][7]

Is there an official mapping from NACE Rev. 2 to Rev. 2.1?

Yes. Eurostat publishes correspondence tables between the two versions.[3]

Do UK SIC codes follow NACE Rev. 2.1?

Not yet in practice. UK SIC 2026 is based on NACE Rev. 2.1 to four digits, but its earliest planned ONS use is the 2031 Blue Book. Companies House uses UK SIC 2007, derived from NACE Rev. 2; its June 2026 blog reply said changes would need legislation. Map UK codes with the ONS SIC 2007 to SIC 2026 table.[5][15][16][17][20][21]

How many sections, divisions, groups and classes does NACE Rev. 2.1 have?

NACE Rev. 2.1 has 22 sections, 87 divisions, 287 groups and 651 classes, by our count of the codes at each level in Eurostat's code lists. NACE Rev. 2 had 21, 88, 272 and 615. Germany's WZ 2025 has the same totals, and manufacturing (Section C) has 24 divisions, 92 groups and 232 classes.[4][6][7]

Is there a 2026 version of the NACE code list?

Yes, as a 2026 edition of the NACE Rev. 2.1 manual, not a new revision. Eurostat's 2026 edition (KS-01-26-047) adds the latest explanatory notes, reconciled with CPA Ver. 2.2, and a new annex on national versions of NACE. It also gives the correspondence types for correspondence table version 1.06.[9]

Which statistics switched to NACE Rev. 2.1 in 2025, and which switch later?

By default, EU statistics moved to NACE Rev. 2.1 for reference periods from 1 January 2025. Regulation (EU) 2023/137 sets later dates for some: the labour force survey and job vacancies from 2026, energy statistics from 2027, labour costs from 2028, national accounts from September 2029 and health from 2031. Eurostat plans backcast parallel data sets for many domains.[1][11]

How many manufacturing classes map to more than one new code?

By our count, 55 of the 230 NACE Rev. 2 manufacturing classes (24%) map to more than one NACE Rev. 2.1 class in Eurostat's current correspondence table, dated 31 August 2026. We counted distinct new codes for each class-level row. The file has no version label, but its totals match the version 1.06 figures in the 2026 NACE manual.[9][10]

Do UK SIC 2026 aircraft codes mean the same as NACE Rev. 2.1?

No. UK SIC 2026 turns NACE Rev. 2.1's 30.31 and 30.32 (civilian and military air and spacecraft) into UK 30.31 civilian aircraft, 30.32 military aircraft and 30.33 spacecraft. So UK 30.31 and 30.32 cover narrower activities than the NACE classes with the same numbers. UK SIC 2026 also splits NACE 29.10 into 29.11 and 29.12.[16]

When will German production and orders statistics use WZ 2025?

From January 2028 data. Destatis's timetable switches the manufacturing production, orders and turnover indices to WZ 2025 with January 2028, first published in March 2028 and backcast to January 1991. Short-term statistics are published by WZ 2008 until the end of 2027. Structural business statistics move earlier, from reference year 2025.[19]

Has the CSO moved Irish statistics to NACE Rev. 2.1?

Yes, in stages. The CSO Business Register finished recoding enterprises to NACE Rev. 2.1 during 2025, and from January 2026 CSO outputs began moving to Rev. 2.1 gradually, starting with business and labour force statistics. The Labour Force Survey switched from Q1 2026, backcast to 2019, and Rev. 2 estimates are still published for 2026.[12][13]

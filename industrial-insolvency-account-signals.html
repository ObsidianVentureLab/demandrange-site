How to spot insolvency signals in an industrial account list

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Check the official public notices, then the company register. In Ireland, insolvency and winding-up notices appear in Iris Oifigiúil, and the Companies Registration Office records liquidations, receiverships and strike-offs. In the UK, statutory insolvency notices appear in The Gazette, and Companies House records the company's status and insolvency information. Those sources are the public record to consult before you treat a firm as a live buyer.

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one thing: how to use official insolvency notices to risk-check and clean an industrial account list. It is not a country-by-country count of bankruptcies, and it is not a substitute for legal advice on any one case. For a scheduled review of the wider list, see industrial prospect list maintenance. To find UK firms in the first place, see Companies House industrial prospecting.

Key facts

  • In October 2026, 1,885 UK companies entered a formal insolvency procedure, by our count of the Insolvency Service's three official, not-seasonally-adjusted tables, published on 17 November 2026. Compulsory liquidation figures in the main series are marked provisional.[1][2]

  • The Companies Registration Office registered 2,665 Irish company liquidations in 2025. Of those, 1,988 were members' voluntary liquidations and 677 were creditors' voluntary or court liquidations, by our count. At the end of 2025, 11,768 companies were in the course of liquidation.[3]
  • Iris Oifigiúil, the official Irish State gazette, is published on Tuesdays and Fridays. The Companies Registration Office Gazette is published each Wednesday.[4][5]
  • Where an Irish company has passed a resolution for voluntary winding up, the Companies Act 2014 requires notice of that resolution to be advertised in Iris Oifigiúil within 14 days.[6][7]
02 / why insolvency notices matter for industrial account lists

Why insolvency notices matter for industrial account lists

An account list goes stale when companies stop trading, enter a formal insolvency procedure, or leave the register. National bankruptcy or enterprise-death figures tell you about a market. They do not tell you whether a named buyer is still a company you can sell to. Official notices and the company register do.

The check is worth doing even when headline counts are falling. The October 2026 UK total above was lower than a year earlier in the Insolvency Service's published commentary, but 1,885 new procedures in a single month is still a large number of legal entities leaving, or about to leave, the set of live accounts.[1][2] In Ireland, most liquidations in 2025 were members' voluntary, which are solvent windings-up, not failed businesses. They still take the company off a live prospect list.[3]

A notice can also arrive earlier than a change of status on a downloaded list. In the UK, a petition to wind up a company is published in The Gazette before a winding-up order is made. In Ireland, the winding-up resolution must be advertised in Iris Oifigiúil within 14 days, alongside the filings made at the Companies Registration Office.[6][7][8][9]

03 / official irish sources

Official Irish sources

Use three official Irish sources together. They do not contain the same information.

Iris Oifigiúil is the official Irish State gazette and is published twice a week, on Tuesdays and Fridays. It is the place where company officers must publish many insolvency-related notices, including notice of a resolution to wind up voluntarily. The Companies Registration Office also directs readers to Iris Oifigiúil for liquidations, receiverships and examinership information that the company or its officers must publish. On 24 November 2026, the current-issues list showed issue 94 of 2026 as the latest edition, published that day.[4][5][6][7]

The Companies Registration Office Gazette is a separate weekly publication. The Companies Act 2014 provides for the Registrar to publish it online. It is published each Wednesday and includes lists of liquidations, strike-offs, restorations, new companies and other registered documents.[5]

The company record on the Companies Registration Office register shows whether a named company has filings for a liquidation, receivership or examinership. Treat the register and the two gazettes as complementary: a notice may appear in Iris Oifigiúil before, or apart from, a weekly gazette list, and the register shows the filings on that company.[3][5]

By our count, members' voluntary liquidations were about three in four of the 2025 total (1,988 of 2,665). A members' voluntary winding up is used when the company can pay its debts. A creditors' voluntary winding up is used when it cannot, by reason of its liabilities, continue its business. Court liquidations follow a court order. All three should be flagged on a live account list, but they do not all mean the same thing about the company's finances.[3][6][7]

Other formal procedures also leave a public trace. In 2025, a receiver was appointed over the property of 97 companies, and the court appointed an examiner in 15 cases, with 10 examinerships in force at year end. Notices of receiver appointments must also be published in Iris Oifigiúil.[3][10]

Strike-off is not insolvency, but it also removes a company from the live list. In 2025 the Companies Registration Office recorded 7,239 voluntary strike-offs and 506 companies struck off for having no director. Involuntary strike-off for failure to file annual returns restarted on 11 August 2025. Those lists appear in the weekly gazette.[3][5]

04 / official uk sources

Official UK sources

Use The Gazette and Companies House together. They are not substitutes for each other.

The Gazette is the UK's official public record. Official notices about insolvency and companies being wound up are published there. The insolvency section includes statutory corporate notices, which The Gazette groups by notice code. Codes that often matter when you are risk-checking a list include petitions to wind up a company (2450), winding-up orders (2452), resolutions and liquidator appointments in members' and creditors' voluntary windings up (2431 to 2443), and the appointment of administrators (2410). A petition is an application to the court, not a finding that the company has been wound up.[8][9][11]

Companies House provides free company information that includes insolvency information, as well as the company's status, filing history and charges. You can also set up free email alerts when a company updates its details. GOV.UK's guidance on searching for companies in insolvency proceedings points to the Companies House service and to The Gazette. For how to build a UK industrial list from the register in the first place, see our Companies House industrial prospecting guide.[8][12]

The Insolvency Service publishes official monthly counts of companies that have entered a formal insolvency procedure. Those counts are a backdrop, not a substitute for checking named accounts. In October 2026, by our count of the three not-seasonally-adjusted official tables, 1,885 UK companies entered a procedure. In the 12 months to October 2026, manufacturing accounted for 1,771 insolvencies in the main industry table, 8% of cases with an industry recorded. Industry is taken from the first recorded Standard Industrial Classification code on the Companies House register, which the company reports itself, so treat the sector figure as a classification, not a site-level fact.[1][2]

Members' voluntary liquidations are not included in those Insolvency Service company-insolvency totals, which cover companies that are unable to pay their debts, or that enter administration or another company rescue process. A solvent UK winding up can still appear as a Gazette notice and as a change of status at Companies House, and it should still come off a live prospect list.[1][11]

05 / how to run the check

How to run the check

Keep a unique official company number on every row of the list. Match notices and register updates on that number and the legal company name, not on a trading style or a website name alone.

  1. Watch the official notices. In Ireland, read each new issue of Iris Oifigiúil (Tuesdays and Fridays) and the Companies Registration Office Gazette (Wednesdays). In the UK, search The Gazette's insolvency notices, including petitions, winding-up orders, liquidator and administrator appointments. Record the notice type, the publication date and a link to the notice.[4][5][9]
  2. Confirm on the register. Open the company record at the Companies Registration Office or at Companies House. Check the status and any insolvency filings or insolvency information. A notice and a register update can arrive on different days, so do both.[5][12]
  3. Classify the signal, then decide. A petition or a meeting notice is a warning to pause outreach and re-check. A winding-up order, a liquidator or administrator appointment, or a registered liquidation is a reason to remove the company from a live prospect list, or to hold it pending a confirmed outcome. A members' voluntary liquidation still ends the company as a trading account, even if it is solvent.[6][7][9]
  4. Look for related entities. A group may have one company in a procedure and a sister company still trading. Do not assume the whole group has failed, and do not assume the trading site has closed, until you have checked the other legal entities and the site evidence.
  5. Log the check. Keep the source, the check date and the decision (retain, hold, correct or remove). Re-run the notice and register checks when you refresh the list. Our industrial prospect list maintenance guide sets a cadence for that wider review, and the industrial prospect list audit covers a one-off check of a supplied list.

For UK companies you intend to keep, Companies House email alerts can tell you when a record changes, so you are not waiting until the next manual pass.[12]

06 / what a notice does and does not mean

What a notice does and does not mean

Official notices are strong legal-entity signals. They have limits:

  • A petition is not an order. In the UK, a petition to wind up a company can be dismissed. Do not treat the first notice as the final status.[9][11]
  • A solvent winding up is still a winding up. Members' voluntary liquidations dominated the Irish 2025 counts. The company is leaving the register, but the notice is not, on its own, evidence that it failed or that unpaid suppliers will go unpaid.[3][6]
  • Status is about the legal company, not the plant. Registers and gazettes record the company. They do not reliably tell you where it traded, whether a factory has closed, or which site a supplier should stop calling on. The Insolvency Service itself notes that the address on the register at insolvency is often that of an insolvency practitioner or a head office, not the place the company traded.[1]
  • Strike-off is a different process. A company can leave the register without entering a formal insolvency procedure. Check the strike-off lists as well as the insolvency notices, and do not label every dissolved company as insolvent.[3][5]
  • A national rate is not an account fact. Manufacturing's share of insolvencies, or a monthly national total, can inform territory planning. Whether one named manufacturer is in a procedure has to be read from its notice and its register record.[1][2]

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Where do official insolvency notices appear in Ireland?
In Iris Oifigiúil, the official Irish State gazette, published on Tuesdays and Fridays, and in the Companies Registration Office Gazette, published each Wednesday. Iris Oifigiúil carries the notices that company officers must advertise, including a voluntary winding-up resolution, which must be advertised within 14 days. Also confirm the company's status on the Companies Registration Office register.
Where do official insolvency notices appear in the UK?
In The Gazette, the UK's official public record, and on the company's Companies House record, which includes insolvency information. The Gazette is often where a petition to wind up a company first appears. Companies House also offers free email alerts when a company's details change. Use both sources, and match on the company number.
Does a winding-up petition mean the company has already failed?
No. A petition is an application to the court to wind the company up, not a finding that it has been wound up. The petition can be dismissed. Treat it as a reason to pause and re-check the later notices and the register status. A winding-up order, or the appointment of a liquidator, is a later and stronger signal.
What is the difference between a members' voluntary liquidation and a creditors' voluntary liquidation?
A members' voluntary liquidation is a solvent winding up: the company can pay its debts. A creditors' voluntary liquidation is used when the company cannot continue its business because of its liabilities. In Ireland in 2025, three in four liquidations were members' voluntary. Both should come off a live prospect list, but they do not signal the same distress.
Is a company strike-off the same as insolvency?
No. Strike-off removes a company from the register, often after a voluntary application or for a filing or officer failure, and is listed in the official gazette or at Companies House. It is not a formal insolvency procedure such as liquidation or administration. The company is no longer a live account, but it should not automatically be labelled insolvent.
How often should I recheck an industrial account list for insolvency?
At least as often as the official notices are published, and whenever you refresh the list. In Ireland that means each issue of Iris Oifigiúil and the weekly Companies Registration Office Gazette. In the UK, Gazette notices and Companies House alerts can be checked as they appear. Log the source, the check date and the decision on each affected account.

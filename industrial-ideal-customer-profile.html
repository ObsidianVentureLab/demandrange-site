Industrial ideal customer profile: a manufacturing ICP template

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

An industrial ideal customer profile is a written set of rules for which companies and sites to research. It names the activity code, the activity at the site, the size band, the territory, the regulator evidence that confirms the site and the exclusions. Each field gets a weight, so every candidate account is scored the same way.

01 / the six fields of an industrial icp

The six fields of an industrial ICP

Write each field as a rule that a researcher can check, not as a description of a market. "Food companies" is a market. "Sites that process milk in Ireland, run by a company with 50 or more persons engaged" is a rule.

Field What to write Example rule Public evidence that can confirm it
Activity code The NACE or SIC classes that are a first filter NACE C10.51 (dairies and cheese making) Company filings and business registers; official statistics by code
Site activity The process that must happen at the site Milk is received and processed at the site Licence, permit or approval that names the site and its activity
Size band The unit (company or site) and the band Company has 50 or more persons engaged Company filings; official counts by size class for context
Territory Where the activity must happen The processing site is in the Republic of Ireland Address of the licensed or approved site, not only the head office
Regulator evidence Which register must list the site EPA industrial emissions licence or DAFM approval The register entry itself, with its reference and date
Exclusions Who must not be researched, and how far the rule reaches Existing customers and their sites; distributors Your own controlled records, checked before scoring

Keep the activity code and the site activity as separate fields. A code is a statistical label attached to a company. It does not prove that a particular site does the work your product serves.

02 / weight the fields before you search

Weight the fields before you search

Agree the weights before anyone looks at a candidate, so the scores cannot drift towards accounts the team already likes. The weights below are an illustrative starting point for a process-equipment supplier, not a Demand Range standard. Change them to suit your offer.

Field Weight Score 2 Score 1 Score 0
Exclusions Gate Not excluded: score the account — Excluded: stop, do not score
Activity code 20 Code matches the rule Related code, needs a check Code is outside the rule
Site activity 30 Named source confirms the activity at the site Activity is likely but not confirmed No evidence, or evidence contradicts it
Size band 15 Inside the band Within one band of it Outside, or the wrong unit
Territory 15 Site is inside the territory Only the head office is inside it No site inside it
Regulator evidence 20 Register lists the site Register lists the company, not the site Not listed

Weighted points are the weight multiplied by the score, divided by 2, so the maximum is 100. A sensible decision rule is: 75 or more, accept for research; 50 to 74, hold and check the weakest field; under 50, reject. Record "not checked" separately from a score of 0. An unchecked field means the account is held, not rejected.

The scoring worksheet has one row per field with the weights, the meaning of each score and blank columns for the candidate's score, the evidence reference and the date checked. The longer brief worksheet covers the rest of the profile, including customer roles, hold rules and ownership of the brief.

03 / worked example three fictional accounts scored

Worked example: three fictional accounts scored

Fictional example. A fictional maker of hygienic valves for dairy plants writes the rules in the first table and the weights above. Three fictional companies are scored. None is a real account.

Fictional company Exclusions Activity code Site activity Size band Territory Regulator evidence Points Decision
Company A: dairy ingredients maker Not excluded 2 (20) 2 (30) 2 (15) 2 (15) 2 (20) 100 Accept
Company B: chilled-food distributor Not excluded 0 (0) 1 (15) 2 (15) 2 (15) 1 (10) 55 Hold
Company C: infant-formula plant Group company of an existing customer — — — — — — Excluded

Company A has a milk-processing site in Co. Cork with an industrial emissions licence for milk treatment, so every field is confirmed. Company B is classified under wholesale, not manufacturing. Its warehouse holds a food approval, but the approval covers cold storage, not processing, so the researcher holds it and checks whether any processing happens on site. Company C would score highly, but the exclusion rule covers the existing customer's group companies, so it is never scored. These companies and figures are fictional and only illustrate the method.

04 / classification codes nace rev 2 1 uk sic 2026 and the cso

Classification codes: NACE Rev. 2.1, UK SIC 2026 and the CSO

Use codes to narrow the universe, then confirm the activity from site evidence.

  • EU and Ireland: NACE Rev. 2.1 is used for European statistics from reference year 2025 onwards. Statistics for 2008 to 2024 still use NACE Rev. 2.[3] So a 2024 table, such as the CSO's BRA31, is still coded to NACE Rev. 2.[2] See our guide to NACE Rev. 2.1 changes for industrial account lists and the NACE codes for Irish manufacturing.
  • UK: the ONS published UK SIC 2026 in 2026 with its structure, indexes and a SIC 2007 to SIC 2026 correspondence table.[4] It is being introduced in stages, and the earliest planned use in the UK National Accounts is the 2031 Blue Book.[5] Companies House still asks companies to file a code from its condensed SIC 2007 list, so a Companies House search by code uses SIC 2007 today.[6] See UK SIC codes for manufacturing.

Size bands narrow a universe quickly, but official counts are counts of enterprises, not sites. Eurostat's structural business statistics show 2,180,638 manufacturing enterprises in the EU-27 in 2024, of which 15,975 had 250 or more persons employed.[1] In Ireland, the CSO counted 196 manufacturing enterprises with 250 or more persons engaged in 2024.[2] A size rule in the brief must say whether it applies to the company or to the site.

05 / icp fields that public data can verify

ICP fields that public data can verify

Some ICP fields can be checked against public registers and official statistics before anyone contacts the business. Others, such as current need, budget or buying authority, cannot. Record which source confirmed each field and when.

ICP field Public evidence that can support it What it does not tell you Guide
Activity code NACE Rev. 2 or Rev. 2.1 codes in Ireland and the EU; SIC codes in the UK Whether a site does the specific activity your range serves UK SIC codes for manufacturing
Site activity Industrial emissions licences, pollutant release registers and other permits that name a facility and its activity Installed equipment, or who buys it Irish environmental registers; PRTR returns; EU Industrial Emissions Portal
Hazard tier Official Seveso and COMAH establishment lists, by upper or lower tier Process detail beyond what the operator has published Use the competent authority's current list
Size band Official enterprise counts by size class; company filings for individual firms The headcount of a single site EU manufacturing enterprises by country
Territory The address of the permitted, approved or listed site Whether the site or the head office makes the buying decision See the territory test below
Recent change at the site Public tenders, planning applications and licence reviews that name the site or operator, with dates Whether you will be invited to quote Irish public tenders; Opportunity intelligence

A public record supports a field. On its own it does not qualify the account.

06 / territory and exclusion checks

Territory and exclusion checks

Two fields cause most disagreements later: what "in the territory" means, and how far an exclusion reaches. Test both on fictional cases before the brief is agreed.

Test the same company under different territory rules

Before approving the brief, test what would change if “Ireland” meant a different relationship. These are alternative instructions, not interchangeable evidence or universal Demand Range requirements. Keep the activity and counting unit fixed while testing the territory choice.

Wholly fictional exercise: every company below makes control panels somewhere; count one company, not its sites. All facts are invented, not researched accounts. Each alternative uses the Republic of Ireland:

  • O — operation: the company itself operates a panel-assembly site there.
  • S — customers supplied: the company supplies its panels to customers there; local assembly is not required.
  • I — incorporation: the company is incorporated there; this is an optional brief condition, not a legal definition or a proxy for local activity.
Fictional Company T-A: all assembly sites and incorporation outside the Republic; supplies customers in the Republic.
Operation (O): Out of scope. Customers supplied (S): Match. Incorporation (I): Out of scope.
Fictional Company T-B: operates assembly in the Republic and is incorporated there; explicitly supplies only customers outside it.
Operation (O): Match. Customers supplied (S): Out of scope. Incorporation (I): Match.
Fictional Company T-C: supplies customers in the Republic; assembly-site location and incorporation territory unresolved.
Operation (O): Hold. Customers supplied (S): Match. Incorporation (I): Hold.

Choose the authorised rule before sourcing. If several conditions are required, write the combination explicitly: T-A matches S alone but fails O AND S. For O AND S, an explicit no in either required condition means out of scope, even if the other is unknown. Otherwise, an unresolved required condition means hold; both yes means match. A missing fact is not a negative finding. A sales office, planned facility or registered address must not silently substitute for the chosen test.

The CSV leaves the overall brief unselected. Its full_account_decision_before_rule_selection holds are pre-selection states, not final O AND S decisions. If O AND S is the authorised brief, T-A and T-B are out of scope; do not keep a supported mandatory failure as a possible match because another check remains open.

No account is accepted by this exercise. Identity attribution, required activity evidence, exclusions, source-use rights, conflicts and duplicate checks remain separate gates. The supplier's ability or permission to serve the account, purchasing responsibility, technical suitability and buying intent remain unassessed. Customer coverage is not supplier reachability or an export permission. No outreach is authorised; an industry-matched brief need not require a project.

Test the exclusion boundary before accepting the account

Use this proposed manual check when related companies, sites or trading labels collide. An identity map answers “what is linked?”; the authorised instruction answers “which of those things is excluded?”. Neither substitutes for the other. Compare one candidate with one versioned instruction, then record the bounded outcome—not a whole-account pass.

Wholly fictional counterfactuals: Company X-A operates Sites X-1 and X-2; Trading Label X-T belongs to X-A. Separate Company X-B and X-A are confirmed members of Group X-G. Company X-C has similar wording in its name, but its membership is unresolved. These invented relationships stay fixed while the alternative instructions change:

Entity only: “Exclude Company X-A, including its trading labels and operations; not other group companies.”
X-T is a match to the excluded company, not a route around it. X-1 and X-2 fall within that company boundary. X-B is not excluded by this instruction: confirmed group membership does not widen entity-only authority.
Site only: “Exclude Site X-1; other sites are outside this rule.”
Exclude X-1, not automatically X-2 or all of X-A. X-T identifies the company but not the relevant site: hold a site-specific decision until the operation is identified. If the handover counts whole companies, ask the authorised role how X-A may be represented with X-1 excluded; do not accept an unrestricted X-A record or silently reject every site.
Explicit group scope: “Exclude confirmed member companies of X-G and their operations.”
X-A and X-B match; retain their separate identities while excluding both under this rule. X-C stays held for membership resolution. Similar naming proves neither membership nor non-membership. If X-C is confirmed outside X-G, the result becomes “not this rule”, not account acceptance.
Ambiguous instruction: “Exclude X.”
Hold the affected decision even where relationships are confirmed. Ask which exact entity, site or group the instruction identifies and whether it extends to related operations. Evidence of a relationship cannot supply missing commercial authority. Do not choose the broadest interpretation “to be safe”.

For a hold, record the exact missing instruction or identity link and the internal role authorised to resolve it. Keep the candidate outside accepted counts until that answer is recorded and checked against the same identity. A missing exclusion reference is not an empty list. Recheck every applicable instruction before acceptance; one non-match does not cancel another exclusion. Preserve prior instructions, source references and decisions when a version changes.

Use the blank boundary-review worksheet

Get the blank exclusion-boundary worksheet from the resource library. It has headers only, no data rows or automated decisions. One row is one candidate-to-instruction comparison at one review event—not one accepted company. Start a new row for another instruction or a later review; keep earlier rows.

Import as UTF-8 with IDs as text. Use manual reference IDs into your controlled records, not raw client lists, names, contacts or confidential instructions. An ID is a pointer, not evidence. Document every column as follows:

review_id; prior_review_id
Unique event ID; earlier event being revisited, or blank for the first review.
candidate_ref; candidate_unit
Stable candidate ID; company, site or trading_label. Do not silently change the unit after matching.
brief_version
The account brief/version under which this comparison is made.
instruction_ref; instruction_version
Controlled exclusion instruction ID and its exact version. Leave missing values blank and hold.
scope_kind; scope_target_ref
entity, site, group or unresolved; the exact instructed target ID, blank while unresolved. Do not infer the boundary from the candidate type.
identity_evidence_refs
Semicolon-separated references supporting or disputing this exact match, including operator, label or membership links where needed.
boundary_outcome; reason
exclude for a supported match within the authorised scope; not_this_rule for a supported non-match; otherwise hold. Explain the comparison without copying restricted material. None means accepted.
resolution_question; authorised_role
Exact open question and internal role responsible for the instruction or resolution. Leave the question blank only when resolved.
decision_ref; reviewed_at
Reference to the recorded decision/clarification and actual review date. Do not supply a planned date as a completed check.

This is company-level commercial exclusion research, not personal-data suppression or legal clearance. It neither replaces separate outreach, privacy and suppression controls nor authorises contact. Fit and other acceptance checks remain separate; excluded records leave the current candidate view, not the retained source history. The method and cases are original teaching material, not a universal exclusion policy or evidence of service performance.

08 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What should an ideal customer profile for manufacturers include?

The activity code, the activity at the site, the size band and its unit, the territory, the regulator evidence that confirms the site, and the exclusions. Add a weight to each field so every account is scored the same way.

Is there a manufacturing ICP template I can download?

Yes. The ICP scoring worksheet on this page lists the six fields with illustrative weights and score meanings, and the brief worksheet covers the rest of the profile. Both are blank CSV files with no account data.

How is an ICP different from a buyer persona?

An ICP describes the businesses and sites to research. A buyer persona describes people inside them. This page covers the ICP only.

Can I use NACE or SIC codes as my ICP?

Only as a first filter. A code is a statistical label attached to a company, so confirm the activity at the site from evidence before you accept the account.

Should I build the ICP from my existing customers?

Start there. List the accounts you serve well and write down what they share: activity, site type, size and territory. Then keep only the traits you can check in public records, because a trait you cannot check cannot be used to find new accounts.

How many large manufacturing enterprises are there in the EU?

Eurostat counted 15,975 EU-27 manufacturing enterprises with 250 or more persons employed in 2024.[1]

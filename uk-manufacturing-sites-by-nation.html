UK manufacturing sites and enterprises by nation, March 2026

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

If you are setting field-sales territories for UK manufacturers, start from where the sites are, not only from where the enterprise is counted. A dated ONS table can tell you how large the manufacturing base is in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, and how many of those sites are large. It cannot name the plants. Use it to size a territory, then confirm each operating site from a second source.

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page covers the four UK nations only: site and enterprise counts, size bands, and the manufacturing activities that stand out in each nation. How to build a named UK manufacturer list is on our UK manufacturing account-research guide. The Section C code list, and the national comparison of company and business counts, is on our UK SIC codes for manufacturing page.

01 / key facts

Key facts

ONS UK business: activity, size and location 2026; checked 10 November 2026.

  • The figures come from a snapshot of the Inter-Departmental Business Register (IDBR) that records the position of businesses on 13 March 2026. The dataset and bulletin were released on 24 September 2026. Counts are control-rounded to base 5.[1][2][3]
  • A local unit is "an individual site (for example a factory or shop) within an enterprise."[1] Where an enterprise has several local units, "the location of the enterprise is generally the main operating site or the head office."[3]
  • By our count, summing the 24 UK SIC 2007 manufacturing divisions (10 to 33) in Tables 3 and 18, the UK had 128,125 manufacturing enterprises and 138,955 manufacturing local units in March 2026, or about 1.08 sites per enterprise. Of those local units, 8,640 had 50 or more employees and 1,335 had 250 or more.[3]
  • By our count, England had 118,370 manufacturing sites (85.2% of the UK total), Scotland 9,670 (7.0%), Wales 5,955 (4.3%) and Northern Ireland 4,960 (3.6%).[3]
  • By our count, Wales had the highest share of manufacturing sites with 50 or more employees: 505 of 5,955, or 8.5%, against 6.2% for the UK as a whole.[3]
  • The official broad-industry Manufacturing line in Table 28 is 128,095 enterprises in 2026, down from 130,185 in 2025. It can differ slightly from the division sum. ONS notes: "Totals across tables may differ by minor amounts due to the disclosure control methods used."[3]
  • The all-industry UK total was 3.2 million local units in March 2026. The tables below are manufacturing only.[1]

By our count, sites with 0 to 4 employees make up 62.0% of UK manufacturing local units. The share is highest in Northern Ireland (64.2%) and lowest in Wales (60.6%). A territory sized on the headline site count is mostly sized on very small workplaces, so use the 50-or-more or 250-or-more columns if your offer only fits larger plants.

04 / why a site count is the right base for a territory

Why a site count is the right base for a territory

An enterprise is "an organisational unit producing goods or services, which has a certain degree of autonomy in decision-making."[1] For this release, ONS uses "business" to mean an enterprise.[1] A local unit is the site. If you plan travel, warehouse cover or a field-sales patch, the question is how many manufacturing sites sit in that patch, not how many enterprises are counted there.

The two counts diverge when a firm has more than one site. ONS generally locates the enterprise at the main operating site or the head office, while each factory, workshop or other site is counted as a local unit in the nation where it sits.[3] By our count, every UK nation has more manufacturing sites than manufacturing enterprises, but the gap is not even. Scotland has about 1.13 sites per enterprise, the highest ratio of the four nations; Northern Ireland has about 1.06, the lowest. A territory drawn only from enterprise counts would under-weight nations where more plants belong to firms counted elsewhere.

05 / which manufacturing activities stand out in each nation

Which manufacturing activities stand out in each nation

By our count, from the Table 18 division cells, fabricated metal products (division 25) has the most manufacturing local units in every UK nation, followed by repair and installation of machinery and equipment (division 33). The differences show up further down the list. Each comparison below sets a nation's share of the UK's local units in one division against that nation's share of all UK manufacturing sites.[3]

  • Scotland: 510 of the UK's 2,865 beverage-manufacturing local units (division 11), or 17.8%, and 1,125 of 10,490 wood and wood-product local units (division 16), or 10.7%. Both are well above Scotland's 7.0% share of all manufacturing sites.
  • Wales: 300 of 5,095 local units making other non-metallic mineral products (division 23), or 5.9%, and 295 of 5,670 rubber and plastic product local units (division 22), or 5.2%, against a 4.3% share overall.
  • Northern Ireland: 340 of 6,680 furniture-manufacturing local units (division 31), or 5.1%, and 560 of 11,035 food-product local units (division 10), also 5.1%, against a 3.6% share overall.
  • England: 8,670 of 9,645 printing and reproduction local units (division 18), or 89.9%, and 3,000 of 3,320 wearing-apparel local units (division 14), or 90.4%, against an 85.2% share overall.

These are counts of sites in a division, not of buyers. They tell you where a sector-specific patch is likely to be deeper or thinner, not which sites buy your product.

06 / what the counts include and what they do not

What the counts include, and what they do not

The 2026 workbook is compiled from a 13 March 2026 extract of the IDBR, which ONS builds from HMRC records, Companies House, Dun and Bradstreet and ONS business surveys.[1][3] Manufacturing, in the workbook's industry definition, is Section C, divisions 10 to 33, under UK SIC 2007.[3] We summed those 24 published division cells for each nation; we did not use a single pre-totalled manufacturing row in Tables 3 and 18, because those sheets are organised by division.

The counts are not a prospect list. They do not name sites, give addresses, or say what equipment is installed. A local unit can be a factory, but it can also be a very small workplace: by our count, 82,895 of the 128,125 UK manufacturing enterprises (64.7%) had 0 to 4 employees.[3]

The IDBR count is also not a Companies House count. Our UK SIC codes for manufacturing page explains how those two official sources differ, and keeps the answer to its national question, "How many manufacturing companies are there in the UK?" This page does not repeat that comparison, and it does not reprint the all-industry 3.2 million local-unit figure as a manufacturing total.[1]

This page stays at UK nation level. We have not used the workbook's smaller geographies.

07 / how to use the tables in a live territory plan

How to use the tables in a live territory plan

  1. Pick the counting unit that matches the job. Use local units for a site-based patch; use the 50-or-more or 250-or-more local-unit columns if you only call on larger plants; use enterprises only when you are sizing registered businesses rather than places to visit.
  2. Record the date and the rounding. These cells are a 13 March 2026 stock, control-rounded to base 5, published on 24 September 2026. If a later edition appears, replace the tables rather than splicing years.
  3. Build the named list from other official sources. Search Companies House for candidates, then confirm the operating site and activity from a second source, as set out in the UK manufacturing account-research guide. The nation tables only tell you whether the resulting list is roughly the right size for the territory.
  4. Filter by activity when you need a sector patch. Table 18 of the same workbook counts local units by SIC 2007 division and nation, and Table 3 does the same for enterprises. Use the Section C list on UK SIC codes for manufacturing to choose the divisions.

A public tender notice can also show that a public body in a nation is buying equipment, but it will not size the installed manufacturing base. For UK notices, see UK public tenders for industrial suppliers. For the wider EU enterprise counts, which use a different official source and are not UK IDBR figures, see EU industrial enterprises by country.

08 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How are UK manufacturing sites split between England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?
By our count, England had 118,370 manufacturing local units in March 2026, 85.2% of the UK total. Scotland had 9,670, Wales 5,955 and Northern Ireland 4,960. The figures sum SIC 2007 divisions 10 to 33 in the ONS 2026 workbook and are control-rounded to base 5.
Why use sites rather than enterprises for territory planning?
Use the local-unit count when you plan field visits, because a local unit is an individual site. ONS generally locates an enterprise at its main operating site or head office, which can be in a different nation from other plants. By our count, the UK had 138,955 manufacturing sites and 128,125 enterprises in March 2026, about 1.08 sites per enterprise.
Which UK nation has the largest share of bigger manufacturing sites?
Wales does, by our count. In March 2026, 505 of its 5,955 manufacturing local units, or 8.5%, had 50 or more employees, against 6.2% across the UK. Northern Ireland was at 7.0%, Scotland 6.7% and England 6.0%. These are register counts of sites, not a list of named plants.
Why does the Table 28 Manufacturing line differ from this table's UK total?
The official Table 28 Manufacturing line is 128,095 enterprises in March 2026; this table's UK row is 128,125, the sum of SIC 2007 divisions 10 to 33. ONS notes that totals across tables may differ by minor amounts because of disclosure control. Both are IDBR stocks, control-rounded to base 5, not a Companies House count.
What does ONS mean by a local unit?
A local unit, in this ONS release, is "an individual site (for example a factory or shop) within an enterprise." An enterprise is "an organisational unit producing goods or services, which has a certain degree of autonomy in decision-making." The 2026 figures record the position of businesses on 13 March 2026. The counts are not a list of named plants.
Can I break the nation counts down by industry?
Yes. Table 18 of the same ONS workbook counts local units by SIC 2007 division, employment size band and nation, and Table 3 does the same for enterprises. By our count, Scotland holds 17.8% of UK beverage-manufacturing sites. Our UK SIC codes for manufacturing page lists the Section C divisions to use as filters.

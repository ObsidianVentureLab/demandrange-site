Manufacturing employment by EU country, 2024

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

EU-27 manufacturing enterprises employed 29,962,568 people in 2024, down from 30,291,604 in 2023, a 1.09% fall by our count.[1][2] Germany had the most, at 7,901,036, or 26.37% of the EU total by our count. Ireland had 278,377, 20th of the 27 member countries by our count, and was one of the countries that added people while the EU total fell.[1][2] The figures are Eurostat Structural Business Statistics, indicator "Persons employed - number" (EMP_NR), for enterprises in NACE Rev. 2 Section C, all size classes. The latest published reference year is 2024. The dataset was last updated on 30 September 2026, 11:00 (UTC+2), and was checked on 24 November 2026.[1][2][6]

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat sbs_ovw_act and sbs_sc_ovw, both data updated 30 September 2026, 11:00 (UTC+2); checked 24 November 2026.

  • EU-27 manufacturing persons employed, 2024: 29,962,568, down 1.09% from 30,291,604 in 2023, by our count. No flag on either EU total.[1][2]
  • Germany leads, at 7,901,036 people in 2024 (26.37% of the EU-27, by our count). Italy, France, Poland and Spain follow; those five countries held 67.18% of the EU total, by our count.[1][2]
  • Ireland: 278,377 persons employed in 2024, 20th of 27 by our count, up 2.18% from 272,438 in 2023, by our count. Croatia is just ahead, at 278,470.[1][2]
  • Persons employed per manufacturing enterprise, 2024: Ireland 14, EU-27 14. Germany 40; Italy, which has the most manufacturing enterprises, 11.[1][2]
  • Enterprises with 250 or more persons employed held 49.34% of EU-27 manufacturing employment in 2024 and 52.73% of Ireland's, by our count, but only 0.73% and 1.00% of enterprises respectively, by our count.[4][5]

Three points stand out:

  • Employment is concentrated. By our count, Germany, Italy, France, Poland and Spain together accounted for 20,127,580 people, 67.18% of EU-27 manufacturing employment in 2024. Italy has the most manufacturing enterprises (347,845 in 2024), but Germany has far more people in them: 40 persons employed per enterprise in Germany, against 11 in Italy and 14 in the EU-27.[1][2]
  • The 2024 fall was uneven. The EU-27 total fell by 329,036 people from 2023, or 1.09%, by our count. Germany's fall of 260,729 people was 79.24% of that EU decline, by our count. Greece (+4.68%), Malta (+2.58%), Ireland (+2.18%) and Spain (+2.02%) were among the countries that added people.[1][2]
  • Ireland and Croatia are almost level. In 2024 Croatia had 278,470 persons employed in manufacturing and Ireland 278,377, 93 people apart by our count, for 19th and 20th place. Both countries' share of the EU-27 total rounds to 0.93%.[1][2]
03 / ireland against the eu

Ireland against the EU

Irish manufacturing employment rose in 2024 as the EU-27 total fell, so Ireland's share of the bloc edged up, by our count, even though it remains under 1%. Persons employed per enterprise matched the EU-27 figure of 14. A slightly higher share of Irish manufacturing employment sat in enterprises with 250 or more persons employed than in the EU as a whole.[1][2][4][5]

The country totals and the size-class cells quoted here carry no flag. For Ireland, enterprises with fewer than ten persons employed numbered 17,137 in 2024, which is 87.36% of enterprises by our count, but they employed 31,264 people, 11.23% of manufacturing employment, by our count.[2][5]

These are enterprise figures, not persons employed in local units, which Eurostat publishes as a separate indicator. Do not mix the two. "Persons employed" is Eurostat's published label for EMP_NR; it is a labour-input count for the enterprise, not a list of buying contacts.[1][6]

04 / employment by nace division 2024

Employment by NACE division, 2024

Food products (C10) was the largest published manufacturing division in the EU-27 in 2024, with 4,367,211 persons employed, and it was also the largest published division in Ireland, at 55,587.[3] After food, EU employment is heavy in fabricated metal products (C25, 3,661,572) and machinery and equipment n.e.c. (C28, 3,085,098). Motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers (C29) follow, at 2,400,000 (E).[3]

Ireland's next largest published 2024 divisions were other manufacturing (C32, 45,790), computer, electronic and optical products (C26, 21,700) and fabricated metal products (C25, 20,195).[3] Several Irish division cells are confidential, including basic pharmaceutical products (C21) in 2024. Eurostat's published Irish figure for that division in 2023 was 28,184 persons employed.[3] Confidential cells are suppressed so that individual businesses cannot be identified; they should not be treated as zero.[3][6]

In the table, "(E)" means Eurostat flagged the cell as estimated. "Not published (confidential)" means the cell carries Eurostat's confidential flag and no value. Estimated EU totals are often rounded more coarsely than unflagged cells.[3]

05 / using employment figures to size a sales territory

Using employment figures to size a sales territory

Persons employed show where the manufacturing workforce sits. Enterprise counts show how many businesses there are. The two can point to different places. Germany has fewer manufacturing enterprises than Italy, France or Poland, but far more people in them, so a territory sized only by company count understates Germany and overstates countries with many small firms.[1][2] Pair this page with the EU industrial enterprise counts by country.[1][2][4][5]

A small number of large enterprises hold about half of manufacturing employment. In 2024, enterprises with 250 or more persons employed were 0.73% of EU-27 manufacturing enterprises and 49.34% of persons employed, by our count. In Ireland the same size class was 1.00% of enterprises and 52.73% of persons employed, by our count. If you sell into plants rather than micro-firms, the size-class split matters as much as the country total.[4][5]

Use the figures with three caveats. First, some division cells are confidential, especially in smaller countries, so a sector ranking is only as complete as the published cells. Second, the counts describe enterprises classified in NACE Rev. 2 manufacturing, which may not match every local plant or buying site. Third, persons employed is a labour-input total, not a list of people who award contracts. 06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many people work in EU manufacturing?
Eurostat counted 29,962,568 persons employed in EU-27 manufacturing enterprises in 2024, down from 30,291,604 in 2023, a 1.09% fall by our count. The figures come from Structural Business Statistics, cover all size classes, and use NACE Rev. 2 Section C. Germany's fall of 260,729 people made up 79.24% of the EU decline, by our count.[1][2]
How many people work in Irish manufacturing?
Eurostat counted 278,377 persons employed in Irish manufacturing enterprises in 2024, up from 272,438 in 2023, a 2.18% rise by our count. That is 20th of the 27 EU countries, just behind Croatia at 278,470, and 0.93% of the EU-27 total, by our count. Ireland added people in 2024 while the EU total fell.[1][2]
Which EU country has the most manufacturing employment?
Germany, with 7,901,036 persons employed in manufacturing enterprises in 2024, or 26.37% of the EU-27 total by our count. Italy (3,943,190), France (3,265,897), Poland (2,829,435) and Spain (2,188,022) follow. Those five countries held 67.18% of EU manufacturing employment in 2024, by our count. Germany's total was down 3.19% from 8,161,765 in 2023, by our count.[1][2]
Which Irish manufacturing sectors employ the most people?
Among the NACE divisions Eurostat published for Ireland in 2024, food products (C10) employed 55,587 people and other manufacturing (C32) 45,790. Computer, electronic and optical products (C26) employed 21,700 and fabricated metal products (C25) 20,195. Ireland's 2024 figure for basic pharmaceutical products (C21) is confidential; the published 2023 figure was 28,184 persons employed.[3]
Do the country totals include small firms?
Yes. The Structural Business Statistics country totals cover manufacturing enterprises in every published size class, including those with fewer than ten persons employed. In 2024, enterprises with 250 or more persons employed held 49.34% of EU-27 manufacturing employment and 52.73% of Ireland's, by our count, but only 0.73% and 1.00% of enterprises respectively, by our count.[4][5]
How should I use employment figures to size a sales territory?
Use persons employed to see where the manufacturing workforce sits, and enterprise counts to see how many accounts there are. A country can have many small enterprises and relatively few people, or the reverse. Allow for confidential division cells, and do not treat the headcount as buying contacts. Demand Range can turn the official counts into a named account list.[1][2][4]

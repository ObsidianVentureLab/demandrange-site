ICT security incidents in EU manufacturing, by country

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

About one in five EU manufacturers reported an ICT security incident in 2023 (Eurostat's 2024 survey), and far more had ICT services go down through hardware or software failures than through attacks. The survey asks firms with 10 or more persons employed whether an incident made their ICT services unavailable, destroyed or corrupted data, or disclosed confidential data, and why.[1][5]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one thing: the share of manufacturers reporting ICT security incidents in 2023 (2024 survey), by EU country and by cause, with Ireland's position. For how firm counts differ by country, see EU industrial enterprises by country.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat isoc_cisce_icn2, manufacturing enterprises with 10 or more persons employed; 2024 survey, incidents in calendar year 2023; data last updated 15 June 2026; extracted 24 November 2026.

  • 21.58% of EU-27 manufacturers reported at least one ICT security incident with a consequence in 2023 (2024 survey).[1][4]
  • In 2023 (2024 survey), 18.28% reported ICT services going down because of hardware or software failures. 2.83% reported them going down in 2023 because of an attack from outside, such as ransomware or denial of service.[1]
  • In 2023 (2024 survey), Finland had the highest share at 47.30%. Bulgaria was lowest at 11.29% in 2023, by our count.[1]
  • In 2023 (2024 survey), Ireland was below the EU at 17.23%, 15th of 27 by our count, and had the lowest share of attack-caused outages in the EU (1.74%), by our count.[1]

The 2024 survey asks about the previous calendar year, so these shares describe incidents in 2023.[4] Eleven countries were above the EU-27 share of 21.58% in 2023, by our count. Finland's 2023 share, 47.30%, was about 2.2 times the EU-27 share by our count.[1]

Read the country gaps with care. A high share can mean more firms had problems, but it can also mean firms detect and record problems more thoroughly. In our view, the table is best used to compare the mix of causes within a country, not to name the least secure market.

03 / failures against attacks

Failures against attacks

In our view, the common picture of an industrial cyber incident is ransomware. The Eurostat data point the other way. Across the EU-27 in 2023 (2024 survey), 18.28% of manufacturers had ICT services become unavailable because of hardware or software failures, against 2.83% because of an attack from outside, about 6.5 times as many by our count.[1]

The pattern holds for destruction or corruption of data too, but not for disclosure of confidential data, the rarest consequence, where attacks and intentional actions came slightly ahead:[1]

Unavailability of ICT services 18.28 (hardware or software failures) 2.83 (attack from outside)
Destruction or corruption of data 4.00 (hardware or software failures) 1.81 (malicious software or unauthorised intrusion)
Disclosure of confidential data 0.96 (unintentional actions by own employees) 1.45 (intrusion, pharming, phishing or intentional actions by own employees)

Source: Eurostat isoc_cisce_icn2, NACE C, 10 or more persons employed, incidents in 2023 (2024 survey). Labels shortened from Eurostat's indicator names. A firm can report more than one consequence, so the rows do not add up.[1]

Because every firm with a failure-caused outage also counts as a firm with an incident, about 85% of the manufacturers that reported any incident had an outage caused by hardware or software failures, by our count (18.28 of 21.58, both 2023).[1] Failure-caused outages were above attack-caused outages in all 27 member states in 2023, by our count.[1] Eurostat's own summary for all businesses makes the same point for 2023: enterprises more often reported damage caused by non-malicious incidents.[3]

Attacks are not negligible. In 2023 (2024 survey), in Latvia (8.02%), Finland (7.49%) and Greece (6.04%), the share of manufacturers with an attack-caused outage was more than twice the EU-27 share, by our count.[1]

04 / ireland against the eu

Ireland against the EU

Irish manufacturers were less likely than EU-27 manufacturers to report each of the three measures in the country table in 2023 (2024 survey):[1]

  • Any incident, 2023: 17.23% in Ireland against 21.58% in the EU-27, 4.35 points lower by our count.
  • Outage from hardware or software failures, 2023: 11.73% against 18.28%.
  • Outage from an attack from outside, 2023: 1.74% against 2.83%. This was the lowest share in the EU, by our count.

Manufacturing stands out within Ireland. Irish manufacturers were more likely than Irish businesses overall to report an incident in 2023 (2024 survey): 17.23% against 13.32% across all activities covered by the survey, without the financial sector. Across the EU, the two shares were almost the same (21.58% and 21.54% in 2023).[1][2]

Failure-caused outages lead in Ireland too. About 68% of Irish manufacturers that reported any incident had a failure-caused outage, by our count (11.73 of 17.23, both 2023), and Irish manufacturers were about 6.7 times as likely to report a failure-caused outage as an attack-caused one.[1]

05 / what it means for suppliers of ot and it services

What it means for suppliers of OT and IT services

In our view, the data change the sales conversation more than the size of the market. If far more manufacturers lose ICT services to failures than to attacks, a manufacturer's problem is often resilience: ageing hardware, unpatched or unsupported software, weak backups and thin maintenance cover. Security tools are only part of the answer.

How suppliers can use the figures:

  • Lead with uptime, not only threat. Across the EU-27 in 2023 (2024 survey), a manufacturer was about 6.5 times as likely to report losing ICT services to a failure as to an outside attack, by our count.[1] Offers built around monitoring, redundancy, backup and recovery, and support for plant systems speak to the more common problem.
  • Expect higher incident shares at larger firms. In 2023 (2024 survey), across all businesses covered by the survey, 38.29% of EU-27 enterprises with 250 or more persons employed reported an incident, against 19.85% of those with 10 to 49, about 1.9 times as many by our count.[2] Eurostat's incident tables do not split manufacturing by size class.
  • Use the country table as a prior, not an account fact. The statistic is a share of firms. Whether one named plant has had an outage, or runs unsupported systems, has to be checked account by account, from published incidents, job adverts, tenders and project evidence.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many EU manufacturers had an ICT security incident?
About one in five. In 2023 (Eurostat's 2024 survey), 21.58% of EU-27 manufacturers with 10 or more persons employed reported an ICT security incident that made ICT services unavailable, destroyed or corrupted data, or disclosed confidential data. Across all businesses covered by the survey, the share was almost the same, at 21.54% in 2023.[1][2][4]
Are cyber attacks the main cause of ICT outages at EU manufacturers?
No. Failures were far more common. In 2023 (2024 survey), 18.28% of EU-27 manufacturers had ICT services go down because of hardware or software failures, against 2.83% because of an attack from outside, such as ransomware or denial of service. By our count, about 85% of manufacturers reporting any incident in 2023 had a failure-caused outage.[1]
Which EU country had the highest share of manufacturers with ICT security incidents?
Finland. In 2023 (2024 survey), 47.30% of Finnish manufacturers reported an ICT security incident, about 2.2 times the EU-27 share of 21.58%, by our count. Malta (34.64%) and Poland (32.69%) came next in 2023, and Bulgaria (11.29%) was lowest. High shares can reflect thorough detection and recording as well as more problems.[1]
How do Irish manufacturers compare with the EU?
Below the EU-27 share. In 2023 (2024 survey), 17.23% of Irish manufacturers reported an ICT security incident, against 21.58% across the EU-27, ranking 15th of 27 by our count. Only 1.74% had an outside-attack outage in 2023, the lowest share in the EU, by our count. Irish businesses overall were lower still, at 13.32% in 2023.[1][2]
Does the data include the UK?
No. Eurostat's 2024 survey tables on ICT security incidents (2023 reference year) have no United Kingdom value, so this page compares EU member states only. UK figures on business cyber incidents would need a separate UK official source, and its questions and definitions should be checked before comparing them with the Eurostat shares.[1][2]

How to find industrial customers in Romania: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

In 2024 Romania had 76,284 manufacturing enterprises (NACE Rev. 2 Section C) and 1,072,454 persons employed, or 14 persons employed per enterprise.[1][2] Food products employed the most people that year; among published manufacturing divisions, motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers led net turnover and value added.[1][2] Size the market with Eurostat Structural Business Statistics, and use INS, the National Institute of Statistics, for more recent production-volume figures, then confirm named enterprises in the official trade-register portal. Eurostat does not publish a public named list of Romanian manufacturers.[1][3][4]

For the comparison with other EU countries, see manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country. For the sibling method in nearby markets, see industrial account research in Poland and industrial account research in Czechia.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat sbs_ovw_act, data updated 30 September 2026, 11:00 (UTC+2); checked 24 November 2026.

  • Manufacturing enterprises in Romania: 76,284 in 2024, 78,194 in 2023 and 76,099 in 2022. The 2023–24 change is −2.4%, by our count.[1][2]
  • Persons employed in manufacturing: 1,072,454 in 2024 and 1,108,269 in 2023. The official employment growth rate was −3.23% in 2024 and −1.02% in 2023. Persons employed per enterprise in 2024: 14 (official cell). There were 1,119,714 persons employed in 2022.[1][2]
  • Manufacturing value added: €32,257.49 million in 2024, up from €30,144.44 million in 2023 (+7.0%, by our count). Net turnover: €134,777.14 million in 2024, up from €132,939.17 million in 2023 (+1.4%, by our count). In 2022, value added was €31,147.74 million and net turnover was €140,752.59 million.[1][2]
  • Romania's 2024 share of the EU-27, by our count: 3.50% of manufacturing enterprises, 3.58% of persons employed and 1.39% of net turnover.[1][2]
  • Largest divisions by persons employed in 2024, official shares: food products (C10) 174,704 (16.29%), then motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers (C29) 156,101 (14.56%), then fabricated metal products (C25) 89,298 (8.33%). The food lead is 18,603 people, by our count. Food also led employment in 2023, at 168,832 (15.23%).[1][2]
  • Largest divisions by net turnover in 2024, official shares: C29 €29,260.38 million (21.71%), then C10 €17,035.94 million (12.64%), then coke and refined petroleum (C19) €12,966.82 million (9.62%). By value added in 2024, among published manufacturing divisions: C29 €5,690.67 million (17.64%), then C10 €3,290.51 million (10.2%), then C25 €2,319.92 million (7.19%). Value added for C19 and C12 is confidential (C).[1][2]
  • Motor vehicles is a concentrated division: 520 enterprises in 2024, with 300 persons employed per enterprise, against 14 for manufacturing as a whole and 13,414 food-product enterprises with 13 persons employed per enterprise. Use this as a research-scope signal, not as a named-account claim.[1][2]
02 / romanian manufacturing in numbers

Romanian manufacturing in numbers

Eurostat's 2024 Structural Business Statistics are the comparable stock figures for Romania and the rest of the EU. They count enterprises, persons employed, net turnover and value added at enterprise level for NACE Rev. 2 manufacturing, not production volume and not a list of named companies. The latest published reference year is 2024. The dataset was last updated on 30 September 2026, 11:00 (UTC+2).[1][2]

The sector mix depends on the measure. In 2024, food products (C10) led persons employed, 18,603 people ahead of motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers (C29), by our count, and had also led employment in 2023. On net turnover, and on value added among published manufacturing divisions, C29 was clearly first in 2024. Wearing apparel (C14) was still fifth on employment, at 67,681 people (6.31%), but only 1.35% of net turnover. Do not describe Romania as a motor-vehicles-only manufacturing base.[1][2]

03 / official statistics eurostat and ins

Official statistics: Eurostat and INS

Use Eurostat when you need a figure you can set beside other EU countries: the enterprise count, persons employed, net turnover and value added, including the division mix in 2024. The comparison page is manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country.[1][2]

Use INS, the National Institute of Statistics (Institutul Național de Statistică), when you need a later production-volume reading for Romania. Start at the INS entry point and open the industrial production index releases. This page does not quote an INS production figure. Read the title of any later release before you quote a rate, and do not drop a 2026 volume rate into the 2024 Eurostat table.[3]

Neither source names customers. Statistics size the segment; a company- or site-level official record is still required before you call a named account a fit.

04 / the company register

The company register

The official trade register is kept by the Oficiul Național al Registrului Comerțului (ONRC, the National Trade Register Office). Use the official ONRC portal to look up a named enterprise and confirm the legal entity.[4][5] It is a search tool, not a source of an aggregate manufacturing count, and this page does not invent a stock figure from it.

A register match confirms that a legal entity exists and can be identified. It does not, by itself, tell you who buys at a plant, or that the registered entity is the operating site. Record the operating company and, where the group differs, the group, and add site-level public evidence before you treat the row as a qualified account. If a company name contains a place, use a neutral label and do not repeat the place.

05 / how to research industrial accounts in romania

How to research industrial accounts in Romania

Build the list in layers

  1. Market size. Take the Eurostat 2024 enterprise and employment counts, and the division mix, to decide whether the segment is worth a named list. Add INS production-volume figures when you need a more recent direction of travel, without mixing the two measures.[1][2][3]
  2. Companies. Confirm each legal entity in the official trade-register portal.[4][5]
  3. Activity. Code the list to NACE. The Eurostat figures on this page use NACE Rev. 2 Section C and its divisions. See our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide for how activity codes are used on account lists.
  4. Plants. A register record identifies the legal entity, not every site. Use site-level official evidence, such as the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for process industries.
  5. Buying signals. Search EU-threshold public-contract notices on TED with CPV codes. A tender notice is a signal, not proof that a plant is buying your product.

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat headline for 2024, then decide which divisions to include. Food and motor vehicles are far apart on turnover and close enough on employment that the measure you use will change the shortlist.[1][2]
  2. Check the recent volume picture in the latest INS industrial production release when the site is available, keeping 2026 volume rates separate from 2024 stock shares.[3]
  3. Resolve each prospect in the official trade-register portal. Confirm the legal entity. Do not treat officer names as a contact list; record the function, not a person.[4][5]
  4. Find the plants from public site evidence, because the registered entity is not always the buying site.
  5. Qualify the row against your product and the site-level evidence. Demand Range can run this as a named account list, with the official source for each row. Request a written scope if you want that work done on a Romanian territory.

Common mistakes

  • Describing Romanian manufacturing as a motor-vehicles-only base because C29 leads turnover and, among published manufacturing divisions, value added. Food products led employment in 2024 and in 2023.
  • Mixing a 2024 Eurostat employment or turnover share with a later INS production-volume rate in one sentence.
  • Sizing a territory only by the enterprise count. Romania's share of EU-27 manufacturing enterprises is larger than its share of net turnover, by our count.
  • Inventing an aggregate manufacturing count from the trade-register portal, or treating a register match as proof of a buying plant.
  • "Correcting" the official persons-employed-per-enterprise cells (14 for manufacturing, 300 for motor vehicles, 13 for food products in 2024) from the headcount and enterprise totals.
  • Reconstructing confidential value-added or investment cells, including those for coke and refined petroleum (C19) and tobacco products (C12).
06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in Romania?
Eurostat counted 76,284 manufacturing enterprises in Romania in 2024, for NACE Section C, with 1,072,454 persons employed, or 14 persons employed per enterprise. There were 78,194 enterprises in 2023 and 76,099 in 2022. Persons employed were 1,108,269 in 2023. The official employment growth rate was −3.23% in 2024.[1][2]
Which Romanian manufacturing sectors employ the most people?
In 2024, food products (C10) employed 174,704 people, 16.29% of manufacturing employment, ahead of motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers (C29) at 156,101, or 14.56%. Fabricated metal products (C25) were third, at 89,298 (8.33%). The food lead was 18,603 people, by our count. Food also led in 2023, at 168,832 people (15.23%).[1][2]
Is Romanian manufacturing mainly motor vehicles?
No. Among published manufacturing divisions, motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers led net turnover and value added in 2024, at €29,260.38 million (21.71%) and €5,690.67 million (17.64%), but food products led employment in 2024 and in 2023. Motor vehicles is a concentrated division: 520 enterprises and 300 persons employed per enterprise in 2024, against 14 for manufacturing as a whole.[1][2]
Where do I look up a Romanian company?
The official register is the Oficiul Național al Registrului Comerțului (ONRC). Use the ONRC portal to confirm a named enterprise. It is a search tool, not a source of an aggregate manufacturing count, and Eurostat does not publish a named list of Romanian manufacturers. A register match confirms a legal entity, not who buys at a plant.[4][5]
Should I use Eurostat or INS figures?
Use both, for different jobs. Eurostat Structural Business Statistics give comparable 2024 enterprise, employment, turnover and value-added figures for Romania and the rest of the EU. INS, the National Institute of Statistics, publishes more recent production-volume figures. Do not treat a 2024 SBS share and a later INS volume rate as one measure.[1][3]
How does Demand Range research industrial accounts in Romania?
Demand Range sizes the market with official Eurostat figures, and with INS production-volume figures when they are available, then builds a named account list from the trade register and site-level public records, and qualifies each row at company or site level. Statistics alone do not name customers. Named contacts and outreach are separate scopes, not included by default.[1][4]

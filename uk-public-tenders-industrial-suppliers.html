UK public tenders for industrial suppliers

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

Short answer: for procurements started from 24 February 2025 under the Procurement Act 2023, look on Find a Tender, the UK's central digital platform. It carries above- and below-threshold notices, except below-threshold contracts in Scotland. Contracts Finder and the devolved portals still matter for older procurements and Scotland. Searching is free; you register only to bid.

01 / find a tender or contracts finder

Find a Tender or Contracts Finder?

Find a Tender's own service notice sets out the split.[3] From 24 February 2025, both above- and below-threshold notices for new UK procurements are published on Find a Tender, except below-threshold notices in Scotland. For procurements that started before that date, Find a Tender holds only above-threshold notices, usually over £139,688 including tax. Information on older below-threshold contracts sits on Contracts Finder for England and other non-devolved territories, and on the devolved portals.[3][4]

The below-threshold figures come from section 87 of the Act. A contract is notifiable at £12,000 for a central government authority other than a devolved Welsh authority, and at £30,000 for a devolved Welsh central government authority or any other authority. These are estimated contract values.[1] Find a Tender replaced TED for UK notices on 31 December 2020.[3]

03 / the notices that matter most for industrial suppliers

The notices that matter most for industrial suppliers

The Act's notices run through the whole procurement and into contract management, and anyone can view them free of charge. There are 17 notice types; not every procurement uses all of them.[7] GOV.UK's guidance collection covers each stage in more detail.[2] The early ones are the most useful for account research:

  1. Pipeline notice (UK1). It is mandatory for a contracting authority that spends more than £100 million under relevant contracts in a financial year, and it must list each intended requirement over £2 million.[7] This gives you a forward view of capital and maintenance programmes.
  2. Preliminary market engagement notice (UK2). It is published before the procurement starts, so it gives early notice of a requirement.[10]
  3. Tender notice (UK4). The live competition.[7]
  4. Contract award (UK6) and contract details (UK7) notices. These tell you who won and on what terms. That winner is the account you sell into as a subcontractor or component supplier.[7][10]
  5. Dynamic market notices (UK13 to UK16). These announce, set up, modify and close dynamic markets, which buyers use for repeat call-offs.[10]
04 / set up a search you can repeat

Set up a search you can repeat

  • Filter by CPV code, not just keywords. Find a Tender's search has a CPV filter as well as notice type, procurement stage, location and an SME suitability filter.[7][10] Pumps, valves and instrumentation are spread across several CPV branches; start from our CPV codes for pumps, valves and equipment.
  • Save the search. Select "save search", name it and choose how often you want the email.[7]
  • Split by buyer type: water and utilities, NHS estates, local authorities, defence and the nuclear estate.
  • Log what you find in the free tender triage worksheet: buyer, value band, CPV, deadline, incumbent and next action.

Contracts Finder has its own keyword, location and procurement-stage search for older below-threshold contracts.[4]

05 / register on the central digital platform before you bid

Register on the Central Digital Platform before you bid

The enhanced Find a Tender service launched on 24 February 2025 as the central digital platform. It is free for everyone, and you should not pay anyone to view public sector opportunities.[8] There is no need to register to search; suppliers only need to register when they want to bid.[8] GOV.UK describes three steps:[9]

  1. Register. Sign in with, or create, a GOV.UK One Login account. Then register your organisation as a supplier, with its Companies House number or an equivalent and its registered address. If none applies, the service generates a unique identifier for you. A non-UK address can be entered.
  2. Enter your details. The administrator inputs or uploads Basic Information, Connected Persons, Qualifications, Trade Assurances, Exclusions and Financial Information.
  3. Declare and share. Confirm the information is accurate, then share it with a buyer either as a share code entered in the buyer's tendering portal or as a downloaded file.

You still complete each bid in the form the buyer requires. Many buyers publish through a third-party eSender system, so the share code or file goes wherever the tender notice says.[8]

06 / from notice to account list

From notice to account list

A contract award notice names the winning contractor. That contractor will buy equipment, services and subcontracted packages for the life of the contract. Build an account row for:

  • the authority (end owner and future re-procurement);
  • the winning contractor (immediate buyer of your product);
  • the roles involved (contract manager, procurement lead, design or project engineer), recorded at role level.

Fictional example: A fictional valve distributor sets a Find a Tender alert on valve and pump CPV codes. In one month it sees a pipeline notice from a fictional county council listing a wastewater-related works package, and then an award notice to a fictional regional M&E contractor. The distributor adds both to its account list and prioritises the contractor for account research ahead of the package. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

07 / ireland and the eu

Ireland and the EU

If you also sell into Ireland, Irish public contracts appear on eTenders; see our guide to Irish eTenders for industrial suppliers. Contracts above EU thresholds in EU member states are on TED; see TED EU tenders for industrial suppliers.

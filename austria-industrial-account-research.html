How to find industrial customers in Austria: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Eurostat counted 34,351 manufacturing enterprises in Austria in 2024 (NACE Section C), of which 517 had 250 or more persons employed.[1][2] Austria's manufacturing base is small enough to research plant by plant, so the work is choosing the right accounts and proving each one. Build the list in layers from official Austrian sources the EU country table does not carry: Statistik Austria, the Firmenbuch (the company register), ÖNACE 2025 activity codes and the public-tender search of the Unternehmensserviceportal (USP, the business service portal), with TED for notices above EU thresholds.

For the comparison with other EU countries, see manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country (2024 data). For the sibling method in the neighbouring German-speaking market, see industrial account research in Germany.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat 2024 structural business statistics; Statistik Austria; the Austrian registers and procurement rules; checked 24 November 2026.

  • Eurostat table sbs_ovw_act counts 34,351 manufacturing enterprises in Austria in 2024 (NACE Section C, indicator "Enterprises - number"). Eurostat table sbs_sc_ovw counts 517 of them with 250 or more persons employed. Both datasets were last updated on 30 September 2026, and neither cell carries a flag.[1][2]
  • By our count, those 517 large enterprises are about 1.5% of Austria's 34,351 manufacturing enterprises.[1][2]
  • Statistik Austria's own structural business statistics for 2024 show the same Section C cells: 34,351 statistical enterprises, 517 with 250 or more employed. Those tables are coded to ÖNACE 2008 and, on Statistik Austria's results page, are attributed as created on 30 June 2026.[4][5][6]
  • ÖNACE 2025 is Austria's national version of NACE Rev. 2.1. It came into force on 1 January 2025 and took over from ÖNACE 2008.[8][10]
  • Basic company data from the Firmenbuch, published as EU high-value datasets, has been available free of charge through a JustizOnline interface since January 2025.[13]
  • The USP's tender search lets anyone search published Austrian public-contract notices and award notices free of charge, without registering.[16]
02 / austrian manufacturing in numbers

Austrian manufacturing in numbers

Eurostat's 34,351 figure is the official 2024 headline for Austrian manufacturing enterprises (NACE Section C), and 517 is the 2024 count of those with 250 or more persons employed.[1][2] Division, size-band, employment, turnover and investment rows for Austria sit on the EU manufacturing enterprises by country page; this page does not repeat them.

Treat the Eurostat cells as market-sizing figures, not as a list of company names. Eurostat permits reuse of its figures with acknowledgement.[3] Statistik Austria's own tables, including a provisional 2025 national update, are covered in the next section.

03 / official statistics statistik austria

Official statistics: Statistik Austria

Statistik Austria, the national statistics office, produces those structural business statistics and publishes its own tables. An English overview of its industry, construction, trade and services statistics is on its website; the detailed 2024 and provisional 2025 tables used here are the German-language files linked from its structural business statistics page.[4][4a][5][6][7]

Its 2024 main results by ÖNACE 2008 show the same Section C cells as Eurostat: 34,351 statistical enterprises, 517 with 250 or more employed. Statistik Austria's page attributes those 2024 results as created on 30 June 2026.[4][5][6] On 30 October 2026 it also published provisional 2025 results, set out by ÖNACE 2025 groups, which count 34,140 statistical enterprises in Section C, 507 of them with 250 or more employed. The structural business statistics page was last updated on 30 October 2026.[4][7] Those 2025 figures are provisional, and Statistik Austria's implementation table says its 2025 structural statistics are its first reference year on ÖNACE 2025, with provisional results at the end of October 2026 and final results at the end of June 2027.[9] Use the 2024 cells for comparison with other EU countries and treat the 2025 cells as a provisional national update. Do not subtract one from the other to claim a change.

04 / the company register and the business service portal

The company register and the business service portal

The Firmenbuch is Austria's public company register, kept by the courts. Each registered entity has a Firmenbuch number made up of digits and a check letter.[11] Online extracts and documents come through JustizOnline or the ministry's authorised billing agents (Verrechnungsstellen).[12][14]

The Firmenbuch registers, among others, sole traders, general and limited partnerships, limited liability companies (GmbH), stock corporations (AG), flexible companies, cooperatives and European companies (SE).[11] Record the operating entity and, where the group differs, the group.

A current extract costs €4.89 and a document €1.52 online, per the fee table dated 1 August 2026.[12] Basic company data and accounts documents published as EU high-value datasets have been free through a JustizOnline interface since January 2025, after sign-in with ID Austria or another EU eID.[13]

A manufacturer can run several plants under one registered entity. The register shows the registered seat, not every site. Find the plants from public site evidence, not from the registered seat alone. Site-level evidence such as the EU Industrial Emissions Portal can help for process industries.

Register entries, tender notices, ÖNACE titles and many technical pages are in German. Plan keyword lists in German, for example Pumpen, Armaturen, Abwasserbehandlung and Instandhaltung.

The Unternehmensserviceportal (USP) is Austria's business service portal. Statistik Austria classifies each statistical unit by its main economic activity and tells the business its ÖNACE code in a free classification notice, sent electronically to its USP mailbox. Statistik Austria scheduled electronic notices of the ÖNACE 2025 code for almost 700,000 businesses in the first half of 2025.[15] The notice goes to the business itself; it is not a public search of other companies. The USP is also the official search point for Austrian public-contract notices, covered below.

Build the list in layers

  1. Companies. Confirm each legal entity in the Firmenbuch.[11][12][14]
  2. Activity. Code the list to ÖNACE. ÖNACE 2025 is identical to NACE Rev. 2.1 down to the four-digit class, and adds a fifth digit for national subclasses.[8][10] Map older ÖNACE 2008 codes with the official correspondence, as described in our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.
  3. Plants. The register shows the registered seat, not every site. Use site-level evidence such as the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for process industries.
  4. Buying signals. Search Austrian public-contract notices in the USP tender search, and EU-threshold notices on TED with CPV codes.[16][22]

A register match confirms a legal entity. It is not, by itself, a purchasing signal.

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat headline, then define it with ÖNACE 2025 / NACE Rev. 2.1 codes. See our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide for how the codes map, and the EU enterprises table for other countries.
  2. Use Statistik Austria only for market sizing. Its tables tell you how many enterprises sit in each activity and size class. They do not give you names.
  3. Resolve each prospect in the Firmenbuch. Confirm the legal entity, Firmenbuch number, registered seat and filed accounts. Do not treat officer names as a contact list; record the function, not a person.
  4. Find the plants from public site evidence, including the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for process industries, because the registered seat is not always the plant.
  5. Add a trigger only when you have one: a new Firmenbuch entry or filing, a USP or TED notice, an award notice or a new site record.
  6. Log the evidence for each account: legal entity, Firmenbuch number, ÖNACE 2008 and ÖNACE 2025 codes, site evidence and the reason it fits the brief.

Fictional example: A fictional Irish maker of industrial heat exchangers targets Austrian food and drink plants. It filters the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for food and drink installations in Austria and resolves each operator in the Firmenbuch. It finds that one fictional dairy group runs three plants under one GmbH, and that the filed accounts belong to the same Firmenbuch number. It records the three plants as separate accounts under one group, each with maintenance as the owning function. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

Common mistakes

  • Using only registered-seat data. In Austria, as elsewhere in the EU, buying influence often sits at plant level.
  • Treating the provisional 2025 Statistik Austria count and the 2024 Eurostat count as one series, or subtracting one from the other.
  • Filtering one source on ÖNACE 2008 and another on ÖNACE 2025 and merging them unchecked.
  • Searching only TED. Notices below EU thresholds are published nationally and appear in the USP tender search.
  • Treating a register match or a tender notice as proof that a plant is buying your product.
05 / nace codes and nace rev 2 1

ÖNACE codes and NACE Rev. 2.1

ÖNACE is Austria's classification of economic activities, maintained by Statistik Austria. From 1 January 2025 the EU applies NACE Rev. 2.1, and ÖNACE 2025 is Austria's national version of it. Eurostat approved the ÖNACE 2025 structure on 29 February 2024. It came into force on 1 January 2025 and took over from ÖNACE 2008, which had been the national version of NACE Rev. 2 until 31 December 2024.[8][10]

ÖNACE 2025 has 651 classes (615 in ÖNACE 2008) and 711 national subclasses (701 in ÖNACE 2008).[8] Statistik Austria recoded every unit in its business register in December 2024 and keeps all units coded to both ÖNACE 2008 and ÖNACE 2025.[8]

Statistics move over on different dates. Statistik Austria's implementation table, dated 9 October 2026, lists structural business statistics with reference year 2025 on ÖNACE 2025, provisional results at the end of October 2026 and final results at the end of June 2027, with 2025 reported on both classifications. The 2024 structural tables checked on 24 November 2026 were still coded to ÖNACE 2008.[5][9]

For a list, that means:

  • Codes in older datasets, directories and your own records are probably ÖNACE 2008.
  • Keep both ÖNACE 2008 and ÖNACE 2025 on each row until your sources have moved, and map them with the official correspondence described in our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.
  • Do not filter one dataset on ÖNACE 2008 and another on ÖNACE 2025 and then merge them unchecked.

The method is the same one used for industrial account research in Germany, where WZ 2025 is that country's NACE Rev. 2.1 implementation. For the neighbouring Czech system, see industrial account research in Czechia.

06 / public tenders in austria

Public tenders in Austria

Under the Austrian federal procurement act (BVergG 2018), contracting authorities publish notices by providing metadata on data.gv.at that points to a machine-readable core-data source, and they also publish award notices that way.[20][18] The act requires the USP to offer those notices in a readable, searchable form, free of charge.[20] Since 1 October 2026 the core data has to be supplied in the EU eForms format, under the 2026 amendment to the procurement act that came into force on 1 March 2026.[19]

In practice:

  • USP tender search (Ausschreibungssuche). Lets you search all notices and award notices published under BVergG 2018 in one place, without registering. It is refreshed twice a day and can be filtered by notice type, publication period, CPV category and place of performance, with full-text search. Taking part in a tender happens on the platform named in each result.[16][17]
  • data.gv.at. The national open-data portal where contracting authorities' procurement metadata is published.[18][21] It suits teams that want to read the core-data sources directly, not through the USP search.
  • TED. Contracts above EU thresholds are also sent to the EU Publications Office and appear on TED. Austrian award notices above thresholds go there within 30 days of award.[20][22] Use our TED guide and CPV codes.

A tender or award notice tells you that a public buyer is spending in your category. It does not tell you that a plant outside the public sector will buy from you.

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in Austria?
Eurostat counted 34,351 manufacturing enterprises in Austria in 2024, for NACE Section C, and 517 of them had 250 or more persons employed. Statistik Austria's own 2024 tables show the same figures. Its provisional 2025 results, on the new ÖNACE 2025 codes, count 34,140, but they are provisional and not a like-for-like series.[1][2][5][6][7]
What is ÖNACE 2025?
ÖNACE 2025 is Austria's national version of the EU activity classification NACE Rev. 2.1, maintained by Statistik Austria. It came into force on 1 January 2025 and took over from ÖNACE 2008. It matches NACE Rev. 2.1 down to the four-digit class and adds national subclasses, 711 in total, as a fifth digit.[8][10]
Should I use ÖNACE 2008 or ÖNACE 2025 codes?
Both, for now. ÖNACE 2025 has applied since 1 January 2025, but Statistik Austria's 2024 structural tables are still coded to ÖNACE 2008, and its business register keeps both codes for every unit. Keep both codes on each row and map them with the official correspondence until all your sources have moved.[5][8]
Is the Austrian company register free?
Partly. Basic company data and accounts documents published as EU high-value datasets have been free through a JustizOnline interface since January 2025, after sign-in with ID Austria or another EU eID. Standard online extracts are charged: a current Firmenbuch extract costs €4.89 and each document €1.52, per the fee table dated 1 August 2026.[12][13]
Where are Austrian public tenders published?
Austrian contracting authorities publish notices as metadata on data.gv.at that points to their own machine-readable notice data. The Unternehmensserviceportal's tender search gathers those notices and award notices in one place, free and without registering, and refreshes twice a day. Contracts above EU thresholds also appear on TED, the EU's tenders journal.[16][17][18][20][22]
Can the Firmenbuch tell me who buys at an Austrian plant?
No. The Firmenbuch confirms the legal entity, its Firmenbuch number, registered seat and filed documents, and USP or TED notices show which public buyers are spending in a category. None of them shows who decides purchases at a company's own plant. Roles and buying plans need separate evidence, and Demand Range leaves named contacts out of its default scope.[11][12][16][22]

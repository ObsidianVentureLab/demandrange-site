Using the EU Industrial Emissions Portal to find industrial facilities

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Short answer: the European Environment Agency's Industrial Emissions Portal is a free, public list of Europe's largest industrial installations. It is built from reporting under the Industrial Emissions Directive (2010/75/EU) and the E-PRTR Regulation (166/2006). Download the Industrial Reporting dataset (v16, February 2026, covering 2007–2024). Filter by country and activity, then add the operator company, the functions likely to own the decision and a recent trigger to each facility. That gives an industrial supplier an evidence-backed account list for process industries across the EU.

This guide is for EU-wide and multi-country lists: comparing installations across member states from one harmonised dataset. If your brief is a single Irish site and you need its EPA licence file, PRTR returns or discharge records, use our guide to Irish environmental register evidence instead.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Dataset and legal facts behind the method on this page.

  • The latest Industrial Reporting dataset in the European Environment Agency's catalogue is version 16.0, showing the database as of 20 February 2026; its release and emissions data for E-PRTR facilities cover 2007 to 2024.[2][9]
  • The dataset is built from reporting under the Industrial Emissions Directive 2010/75/EU and the E-PRTR Regulation (EC) No 166/2006.[2]
  • The European Commission says the Industrial Emissions Portal Regulation replaces the E-PRTR Regulation, and the first data reported under it, describing 2027, will be published in 2028.[6]
  • Regulation (EU) 2024/1244 "shall apply from 1 January 2028", when the E-PRTR Regulation is repealed. Its reporting requirements apply from reporting year 2027, the portal must include all data reported for at least the previous 10 reporting years, and data must be made public free of charge within one month of Member States submitting their reports.[5]

Key facts checked against the sources below on 10 November 2026.

02 / 1 what s in the data

1. What's in the data

The portal and its Industrial Reporting downloads, including the v16 dataset, are published by the European Environment Agency.[1][2] The reporting behind them comes from the E-PRTR Regulation and the Industrial Emissions Directive.[3][4]

  • Facility identity: name, address, coordinates, operator (parent company where reported) and activity code.
  • Activity: the E-PRTR or IED activity the installation is regulated for, for example chemicals, energy, metals, minerals, paper, food and drink, waste and intensive livestock.
  • Releases and transfers: pollutant releases to air and water, off-site transfers of waste and of pollutants in wastewater.
  • Large combustion plants: fuel input and emissions for LCPs.

The installation and site identities come from the EU Registry on Industrial Sites, which links the reporting entities together.[2][6]

The EEA says the portal "covers over 60 000 industrial sites from 65 economic activities" across Europe, and that it "replaced the E-PRTR website in 2021".[7] The Commission says "around 60,000 large industrial facilities are operating in the EU".[6] By our count, the registry holds 58,714 sites in EU countries for reporting year 2024, with 53,037 E-PRTR facilities, 60,390 IED installations and 3,654 large combustion plants. For 2023 it holds 61,952 sites.[8] Much of the drop is a reporting gap, not closures: Czechia (3,783 sites in 2023) and Malta have no 2024 rows, and Slovakia has none after 2022. Across all reporting countries, 19,728 of the 59,033 sites listed for 2024 (33.4%) have pollutant release data for that year.[8]

By our count for 2024 (2023 in brackets), Germany has 10,513 sites, France 9,133 (9,018), Italy 7,702 (7,540), Spain 7,591 (7,502), the Netherlands 4,577 (4,499) and Poland 4,143 (4,058). Denmark has 2,565, Belgium 2,283, Sweden 1,484 and Ireland 1,114. The listed E-PRTR facilities, IED installations and large combustion plants are 7,502, 7,275 and 276 in France; 5,650, 7,048 and 437 in Italy; 7,303, 7,203 and 302 in Spain; 4,207, 4,472 and 312 in the Netherlands; and 4,691, 5,424 and 343 in Poland.[8]

Counts marked "by our count" on this page are Demand Range queries of the EEA's IED_SiteMap map service (dataset v16.0), run on 9 and 10 November 2026: sites counted by country for reporting year 2024 (and 2023 where shown). EU totals exclude Serbia, Montenegro, the UK, Norway, Iceland, Switzerland and Liechtenstein. Sector counts use the map's E-PRTR sector field.[8]

03 / 2 step by step from download to account list

2. Step by step: from download to account list

  1. Download the latest Industrial Reporting dataset from the EEA download page.[2] Use the facility table as your master list.
  2. Filter by country and activity. For example, you might keep only mainland Europe's food and drink installations or the chemical installations in Benelux. Food plants below the IED and E-PRTR capacity thresholds are not in the portal; for plants handling products of animal origin, national approved food establishment lists are another public source.
  3. Decide what a buying signal looks like for your product. A wastewater transfer suggests on-site treatment, and that means pumps, dosing and instruments. A large combustion plant points to boilers, burners and heat recovery.
  4. Resolve the operator to a legal entity in the national company register, and record the parent group.
  5. Note the functions involved: site or plant management, engineering, EHS and procurement. Record roles, not people.
  6. Add one current trigger per account, such as a permit review, an expansion, a tender or a published investment.
  7. Track your work in the free account research template.

Sector counts depend on how you filter. The map's E-PRTR sector field is a single text value; where a site has more than one entry they are joined with commas, and the same sector can repeat (for example "CHEMICALS,CHEMICALS"). The field has 374 distinct values. An exact match counts only sites whose field is that one sector name; a "contains" (LIKE) match counts every site whose field includes it, alone, repeated or with other sectors. By our count for 2024 EU-country sites (58,714), food and beverage has 3,282 sites on an exact match and 3,537 on a LIKE match, and 676 of the exact-match sites (20.6%) have release data. Chemicals has 2,830 exact and 3,435 LIKE, with 855 exact-match sites reporting releases. Other exact-match counts are intensive livestock 19,795, waste and wastewater 9,073, metals 4,157, minerals 3,347, energy 1,925, other 1,373, and paper and wood 800.[8] By country (LIKE / exact), food and beverage: France 670 / 661, Germany 604 / 562, Spain 516 / 516, Poland 386 / 337, Italy 294 / 259, the Netherlands 207 / 195, Belgium 145 / 112, Denmark 104 / 96, Ireland 100 / 89. Chemicals: Germany 780 / 576, Spain 468 / 468, France 414 / 403, Italy 399 / 315, Belgium 192 / 99, Poland 183 / 155, the Netherlands 178 / 167, Ireland 96 / 88.[8] Either way, a sector filter misses sites with no sector value (see Limits).

Fictional example: A fictional maker of membrane filtration skids filters the dataset for food and drink installations in two countries that report transfers of pollutants in wastewater. It ends up with a shortlist of large dairies and breweries, resolves each to its operating company, and notes whether each site shows its own engineering function. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

04 / 3 limits to plan for

3. Limits to plan for

  • Coverage: only installations above the annex capacity thresholds. Use national registers and NACE-based company lists for smaller manufacturers. Enterprise counts by country, in the EU manufacturing enterprises by country table, complement the portal's site data.
  • Release data: a listing shows that a site is registered, not that it reports releases. By our count for 2024, the share of sites with pollutant release data is 62.7% in Spain (4,756 of 7,591), 32.9% in Italy, 32.3% in France, 26.1% in Poland, 25.9% in Ireland, 23.0% in Germany and 11.9% in the Netherlands (543 of 4,577).[8]
  • UK: the EEA says that from reporting year 2020 "only Northern Ireland shall report information on industrial sites, facilities, installations, LCPs and waste incinerators".[7] The map service lists 7 UK sites for 2024 (4 for 2023), so the portal is not a source for sites in Great Britain.[8]
  • Missing sectors: by our count, 8,942 of the 58,714 EU-country sites for 2024 (15.2%) have no E-PRTR sector value, so a sector filter alone misses them. They cluster in France (3,111, about a third of French sites), Italy (2,175), Germany (1,275), the Netherlands (512), Spain (327) and Ireland (244, the sites with IED installations but no E-PRTR facility). Filter on the IED activity as well.[8]
  • Don't use the map service's has_seveso flag as a filter. In the 2024 rows it is set only for sites in 14 reporting countries, and it is 0 for every site in France and the Netherlands.[8]
  • Time lag: the February 2026 release runs to 2024,[2] so confirm the site is still operating.
  • Operator names may be a site company, not the group that buys. Always resolve the group.
  • Regulatory change: Regulation (EU) 2024/1244 replaces E-PRTR, with new reporting rules from reporting year 2027.[5][6] Fields and coverage will change, so recheck your filters after each release.
05 / 4 country registers that add detail

4. Country registers that add detail

National registers often carry permit documents and inspection records that the EU portal doesn't. In Ireland, the EPA publishes licence files and PRTR returns. See the PRTR returns section of our guide to Irish environmental register evidence. Chemical producers across the EU are covered in our European chemical manufacturer research guide. Installations with verified emissions in the EU and UK ETS registries are covered in our EU ETS installations for account research guide.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Does the Industrial Emissions Portal include smaller manufacturers?
Usually not. The portal covers installations above the capacity thresholds in the annexes of the reporting legislation, so many smaller manufacturers never appear in it. For those companies, use national company and environmental registers and NACE-based company lists, then add the facility evidence from the portal where a site qualifies.
Is the operator named in the dataset the company that buys?
Not necessarily. The reported operator may be a site company rather than the group that makes purchasing decisions. Resolve each operator to a legal entity in the national company register, record the parent group, and note the functions likely to own the decision before treating the facility as an account.
How current is the facility data?
The February 2026 release covers reporting years up to 2024, so a facility record can describe a site as it was more than a year earlier. Confirm that the site is still operating, and that the operator has not changed, before adding the facility to a current account list.
Will the data change when the new regulation applies?
Yes. The Industrial Emissions Portal Regulation replaces the E-PRTR Regulation, and the first data under it, describing 2027, will be published in 2028. Fields and coverage will change, so recheck your country and activity filters after each new release instead of reusing an old extract unchanged.
How many industrial sites does the portal list in France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands or Poland?
For reporting year 2024, by our count of the EEA map service, France has 9,133 sites, Italy 7,702, Spain 7,591, the Netherlands 4,577 and Poland 4,143: 33,146 in total. Germany has 10,513. These are sites in the EU Registry on Industrial Sites, dataset v16.0, counted by country on 10 November 2026.[8]
How many food and drink or chemical sites are in the EU Industrial Emissions Portal?
For 2024, by our count, 3,282 EU sites have food and beverage as the single entry in their E-PRTR sector field, and 3,537 have it anywhere in the field, including multi-sector sites. Chemicals has 2,830 and 3,435 on the same filters. Sites with no sector value are missed either way, so check the IED activity too.[8]
Why do sector filters miss sites?
Because many sites have no sector value. By our count, 8,942 of the 58,714 EU-country sites listed for 2024 (15.2%) have no E-PRTR sector, mostly in France (3,111), Italy (2,175) and Germany (1,275). A sector filter alone misses them, so filter on the IED activity as well.[8]
Does the portal include UK sites?
Only from Northern Ireland, since reporting year 2020. The EEA says that from then "only Northern Ireland shall report information on industrial sites". The map service lists 7 UK sites for 2024, so the portal is not a source for industrial sites in England, Scotland or Wales.[7][8]
When does the Industrial Emissions Portal Regulation apply, and how many years of data will the portal keep?
It applies from 1 January 2028, when the E-PRTR Regulation is repealed, and its reporting requirements apply from reporting year 2027. The portal must include all data reported for at least the previous 10 reporting years, published free of charge within one month of Member States submitting their reports.[5]
Is the 60,000 figure sites, facilities or installations?
It depends on the source. The EEA says the portal covers over 60,000 industrial sites, while the Commission says around 60,000 large industrial facilities operate in the EU. By our count, the 2024 registry holds 58,714 sites in EU countries, with 53,037 E-PRTR facilities and 60,390 IED installations.[6][7][8]

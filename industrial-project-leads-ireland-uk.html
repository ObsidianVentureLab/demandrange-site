Industrial project leads in Ireland and the UK: construction databases vs account research

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Industrial project leads tell you which plants are about to be built, extended or upgraded; account research tells you which sites fit your offer whether or not a project is public. Equipment suppliers covering Ireland, the UK and the EU usually need both. Construction databases and capex trackers find projects, public tenders show what a public body is buying, and account research covers the installed base.

This guide compares those source types neutrally. Each commercial provider is described only from its own public pages, as we opened them on 10 November 2026. We do not rate providers or claim that one outperforms another, because the right choice depends on what you sell and when in a project you can win the work. This is a research guide, not a recommendation to buy or drop any service, and it is not legal advice.

02 / project leads and account leads two different things

Project leads and account leads: two different things

An industrial project lead is a planned or active capital project (a new plant, an expansion or an upgrade) that will buy equipment. An account lead is a site or company that fits the supplier's profile whether or not a project is public.

The difference matters because most equipment is not bought only through new-build projects. A pump, valve or instrumentation supplier also sells into running plants through maintenance, upgrades and small changes that never appear in a construction database. A project lead is time-limited: once the equipment is specified, the window for that package usually closes. An account lead lasts as long as the site runs and still fits.

So the useful question is not "which provider is best?" but "which question am I trying to answer?"

04 / what each source says about itself

What each source says about itself

Barbour ABI

Barbour ABI's industrial sector page offers industrial construction project leads across the UK, with "key project updates, contact information, and more". It lists project types including utilities, data centres, battery storage, industrial units and warehouses, and it also describes factory-type projects such as mills, refineries, chemical plants, manufacturing facilities and pharmaceutical factories. The page says you can filter by project type, planning stages, location and value, then save, share and export the list. It also shows a top 10 of major UK industrial projects ranked by total value, with a start date six months back and six months forward. We have not reproduced that table here.[1]

The same page includes a regional contract-award table from Barbour ABI's sector review, and that table has a Republic of Ireland line. The page's own offer, in the opening text, is for industrial project leads across the UK. Do not treat a line in that awards table as a coverage claim for Ireland until the provider says so in its product description.[1]

Glenigan

Glenigan describes its offer as construction sales leads in the UK and the Republic of Ireland. When we opened its homepage on 10 November 2026, the counter showed more than 600,000 live projects. Visible search filters included location (including Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland), stage (from early planning to contract awarded), sector (including industrial, infrastructure and utilities) and project value. Glenigan says it draws on planning data from the Planning Portal and describes this as "Up to 3 Weeks Earlier Insight". It also states that it has 160 dedicated researchers and 30,000 real-time information updates each day. Those are Glenigan's claims, not our measures of completeness or accuracy.[2]

Hubexo's UK and Ireland site lists Glenigan among its products, and Glenigan's homepage identifies the operating company as a Hubexo entity. That is a corporate fact from those pages, not a reason to treat the two databases as the same product.[2][4]

CIS Ireland (Hubexo)

Hubexo describes CIS as its construction intelligence platform for Ireland and Northern Ireland, tracking projects "from pre-planning through to on-site execution", with "detailed insights across all sectors—from early planning to on-site activity".[4]

As seen on 10 November 2026, the CIS Ireland homepage (cisireland.com) showed counters of 158,800 projects and 62,700 companies. Those counters were not present on the Hubexo CIS product page. The CIS Ireland page also says suppliers use CIS to get the contact details of the main contractor or architect so they can submit a supply tender, and that contractors can get notice of new projects at pre-planning and plans-submitted stages. The homepage counters change, so record the date if you rely on them.[3][4]

Protel Projects

Protel says it specialises in seeking out and tracking projects at a very early stage, and that it provides capital expenditure project sales leads covering process manufacturing in the UK, Ireland, France, Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany. It says subscribers receive access to thousands of qualified project bulletin reports. Each report, it says, gives an overview of the proposed capital expenditure and includes the company's address and website, project location, budget and status, key contacts, project milestones, a snapshot of the site's main activity, "Full project notes & equipment requirements", and the scope of supply and product keywords. Directly under that list, the page states: "The above details are for illustrative purposes only. To see the information available to full subscribers, request a live example bulletin." Ask for a sample in your own sector before you treat those fields as guaranteed.[5]

Industrial Info Resources

Industrial Info Resources (IIR) says it delivers industrial market intelligence built on primary research, continuously updated and verified by a network of human researchers. It offers searchable project, plant, company and contact intelligence across industrial markets, covering capital and maintenance project activity, and plants, assets and owners. The homepage also states "400K+ Companies Researched Globally", coverage of 12 industries and 180+ countries, and "Data Updated - Oct 2026". The same page has labels for active projects, total investment value, operational plants and contact count, but the numbers themselves were not in the page text we could read, so we have not cited them.[6]

Public tenders

Public buyers publish tenders and contract award notices on procurement portals. They show what a public body is buying, such as water, energy or infrastructure works, but not private plant investment. For Irish competitions, see eTenders opportunities for industrial suppliers. For UK notices, see UK public tenders for industrial suppliers. For EU above-threshold notices, see TED tenders for industrial suppliers.

05 / where project leads fall short for equipment suppliers

Where project leads fall short for equipment suppliers

  • Timing. By the time a project is public, the designer or EPC contractor may already be setting the equipment list. See EPC and EPCM equipment supplier research for who decides what.
  • Coverage of running plants. Construction databases are built around projects. Maintenance, small upgrades and spares at an existing plant rarely appear as projects.
  • Buyer roles. A project record may name the client, architect or main contractor. The person who specifies a valve or pump may sit elsewhere, so you still need to work out the buyer route.
  • Fit. A project in your sector is not always a fit. Location, process, scale and existing suppliers still decide whether it is worth pursuing.
06 / where account research falls short

Where account research falls short

  • No live project feed. A researched account list does not, by itself, alert you when a new plant is announced. You still need planning checks, news, tenders and project data for that.
  • Effort. Each account takes time to evidence. A list of a few hundred well-researched sites is usually more useful than thousands of thin records, but it is slower to build.
  • Decay. Sites close, change hands or change process. Lists need regular maintenance.

Public planning files and environmental licences can later confirm that a site exists and what it does; they do not, on their own, prove that a package is open. See Irish environmental register evidence for how a register match is coverage and identity evidence, not a purchasing signal.

07 / how to combine them a method

How to combine them: a method

  1. Write down the questions. For example: "Which UK food plants are expanding in the next 18 months?" or "Which Irish pharmaceutical sites run the process we serve?"
  2. Match each question to a source type using the table above. Do not buy a project database to answer an installed-base question, or the reverse.
  3. Build the account base first. Research the sites that fit your offer, with evidence for each.
  4. Layer project signals onto accounts. When a project lead appears, check whether the site is already on your list. If it is, you know the context. If it is not, ask why.
  5. Record stage and buyer route. For each live project, note the stage, the likely buyer of your package and the evidence date.
  6. Review on a fixed cadence. Remove dead projects, add new ones and refresh account evidence.

08 / a worked example fictional

A worked example (fictional)

This example is fictional. The supplier, the plants and the projects are invented.

A supplier of hygienic valves wants more work in dairy and beverage plants in Great Britain.

  • Project question: Which dairy plants plan extensions in the next year? A construction database or capex tracker can answer this. The supplier finds two fictional projects: an evaporator extension at "Dairy Plant A" and a new warehouse at "Drinks Plant B".
  • Account question: Which dairy plants run processes that use hygienic valves? Account research answers this. The supplier's list already holds 140 fictional sites, including Dairy Plant A.
  • Combined view: The Dairy Plant A extension is a strong lead, because the plant is a known fit and the project adds process equipment. The Drinks Plant B warehouse is a weak lead for valves: it is storage, not process. The other 139 accounts still need regular contact, because their maintenance and upgrade work will never appear as a project.
09 / questions to ask any project lead provider

Questions to ask any project-lead provider

  • Which countries, sectors and project stages are covered?
  • How early does a project typically appear, and from what source?
  • Does a record name the client, the designer, the contractor or all three?
  • Are equipment requirements recorded, or only building type and value?
  • How often are records updated, and how are dead projects removed?
  • Can you export records, and on what licence terms?

Ask for a sample in your own sector and territory, and check a few records against public planning files or tender notices yourself.

10 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What is an industrial project lead?
An industrial project lead is a planned or active capital project, such as a new plant, expansion or upgrade, that will buy equipment. It is time-limited: once that equipment is specified, the window usually closes. It is not an account lead, which is a site or company that fits your offer whether or not a project is public.
What is the difference between a project lead and an account lead?
A project lead is tied to one capital project and usually closes once the equipment is chosen. An account lead is a site or company that fits your offer whether or not a project is public. Suppliers often need both, because maintenance and small upgrades at running plants rarely appear as construction projects.
Do construction databases cover Ireland?
Yes, some do. Hubexo describes CIS as its construction intelligence platform for Ireland and Northern Ireland, and the CIS Ireland homepage showed project and company counters when we opened it on 10 November 2026. Glenigan says it covers construction sales leads in the UK and the Republic of Ireland. Check each provider's stated coverage on the day you compare.[2][3][4]
Which source shows equipment requirements?
Protel says each of its project reports includes "Full project notes & equipment requirements", and it also says those details are for illustrative purposes only until you see a live example bulletin. The construction-database pages we opened emphasise project type, stage, value and contacts more than an equipment list. Ask any provider for a sample in your own sector.[1][5]
Do I still need account research if I buy project data?
Usually, yes. A project database answers which plants are planned, extended or upgraded. Account research answers which sites fit your offer even when no project is public. Maintenance, small upgrades and spares at running plants rarely appear as construction projects, so a project feed alone will miss much of the installed base.
How should I compare project-lead providers?
Ask which countries, sectors and stages are covered, how early a project typically appears, and whether a record names the client, designer or contractor. Ask whether equipment requirements are recorded, how often records are updated, and on what terms you can export them. Then check a sample in your own sector against public planning or tender files.

How to find industrial customers in Slovakia: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 25 November 2026

In 2024 Slovakia had 89,886 manufacturing enterprises (NACE Rev. 2 Section C) and 477,988 persons employed.[1][2] That is 4.12% of EU-27 manufacturing enterprises but only 1.60% of persons employed and 1.08% of net turnover, by our count.[1][2] Among published manufacturing divisions, fabricated metal products narrowly employed the most people; motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers were a close second on employment and led net turnover and gross investment in machinery and equipment.[1][2] Size the market with Eurostat Structural Business Statistics, then confirm named enterprises in the official Slovak business register (ORSR). Eurostat does not publish a public named list of Slovak manufacturers.[1][3]

For the comparison with other EU countries, see manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country. For the sibling method in nearby markets, see industrial account research in Czechia and industrial account research in Austria.

Key facts

Eurostat sbs_ovw_act, data updated 30 September 2026, 11:00 (UTC+2); checked 24 November 2026.

  • Manufacturing enterprises in Slovakia: 89,886 in 2024 and 89,803 in 2023, a 0.1% rise by our count. That is 4.12% of the EU-27 total of 2,180,638, by our count.[1][2]
  • Persons employed in manufacturing: 477,988 in 2024, down 2.7% from 491,418 in 2023, by our count. That is 1.60% of the EU-27 total of 29,962,568, by our count.[1][2]
  • Manufacturing net turnover: €105,166.1 million in 2024, down 2.0% from €107,283.6 million in 2023, by our count, and 1.08% of the EU-27 total of €9,706,327.5 million, by our count.[1][2]
  • Gross investment in machinery and equipment: €4,089.7 million in 2024, up 29.9% from €3,148.2 million in 2023, by our count, against an EU-27 total of €255,657.6 million.[1][2]

(C28) 44,401 (9.3%). The metal-products lead is 285 people, by our count.[1][2]

02 / slovak manufacturing in numbers

Slovak manufacturing in numbers

Eurostat's 2024 Structural Business Statistics are the comparable stock figures for Slovakia and the rest of the EU. They count enterprises, persons employed, net turnover and gross investment in machinery and equipment at enterprise level for NACE Rev. 2 manufacturing, not production volume and not a list of named companies. The latest published reference year is 2024. The dataset was last updated on 30 September 2026, 11:00 (UTC+2).[1][2]

Do not mix these enterprise statistics with headcounts from the business register; they are different sources.[1][3]

Slovakia's share of EU-27 manufacturing enterprises is more than twice its share of persons employed, by our count, so a territory sized only by company count will overstate the workforce.[1][2] Employment and net turnover both fell from 2023, by our count, while gross investment in machinery and equipment rose sharply.[1][2]

The sector mix depends on the measure. Among published manufacturing divisions, fabricated metal products (C25) were narrowly first on persons employed in 2024, 285 people ahead of motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers (C29), by our count. On net turnover and on gross investment in machinery and equipment, C29 was clearly first. Do not describe Slovakia as a motor-vehicles-only manufacturing base.[1][2]

Motor vehicles is much more concentrated than fabricated metal products. In 2024, C29 had 1,517 enterprises and almost the same employment as C25, which had 33,505 enterprises, but C29 accounted for €39,508.2 million of net turnover, 37.6% of the manufacturing total by our count, and €1,265 million of gross investment in machinery and equipment, 30.9% by our count. That is a signal for how you scope research, not a claim about any named account.[1][2]

For scale, persons employed in manufacturing in 2024 were 1,248,982 in Czechia, 769,452 in Hungary, 727,738 in Austria, 2,829,435 in Poland and 7,901,036 in Germany.[1][2]

03 / official statistics eurostat

Official statistics: Eurostat

Use Eurostat when you need a figure you can set beside other EU countries: the enterprise count, persons employed, net turnover and gross investment in machinery and equipment, including the division mix in 2024. The comparison page is manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country.[1][2]

The figures on this page are Structural Business Statistics for enterprises classified in NACE Rev. 2 manufacturing (Section C). Do not call a broader industry total manufacturing, and name the division when you use C29 or any other division cell.[1][2]

Eurostat does not name customers. Statistics size the segment; a company- or site-level official record is still required before you call a named account a fit.

04 / the company register

The company register

The official Slovak business register is ORSR. Use it to look up a named enterprise and confirm the legal entity.[3] It is a register, not a statistical table, and this page does not invent a stock figure from it or treat a register headcount as if it were the Eurostat enterprise count.

A register match confirms that a legal entity exists and can be identified. It does not, by itself, tell you who buys at a plant, or that the registered entity is the operating site. Record the operating company and, where the group differs, the group, and add site-level public evidence before you treat the row as a qualified account. If a company name contains a place, use a neutral label and do not repeat the place.

05 / how to research industrial accounts in slovakia

How to research industrial accounts in Slovakia

Build the list in layers

  1. Market size. Take the Eurostat 2024 enterprise and employment counts, and the division mix, to decide whether the segment is worth a named list. Employment and turnover will not always point to the same divisions.[1][2]
  2. Companies. Confirm each legal entity in the official business register.[3]
  3. Activity. Code the list to NACE. The Eurostat figures on this page use NACE Rev. 2 Section C and its divisions. See our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide for how activity codes are used on account lists.
  4. Plants. A register record identifies the legal entity, not every site. Use site-level official evidence, such as the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for process industries.
  5. Buying signals. Search EU-threshold public-contract notices on TED with CPV codes. A tender notice is a signal, not proof that a plant is buying your product.

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat headline for 2024, then decide which divisions to include. Fabricated metal products and motor vehicles are close on employment and far apart on turnover, so the measure you use will change the shortlist.[1][2]
  2. Resolve each prospect in the official business register. Confirm the legal entity. Do not treat officer names as a contact list; record the function, not a person.[3]
  3. Find the plants from public site evidence, because the registered entity is not always the buying site.
  4. Qualify the row against your product and the site-level evidence. Demand Range can run this as a named account list, with the official source for each row. Request a written scope if you want that work done on a Slovak territory.

Common mistakes

  • Describing Slovak manufacturing as a motor-vehicles-only base because C29 leads turnover and machinery investment. Among published manufacturing divisions, fabricated metal products narrowly led employment in 2024.
  • Sizing a territory only by the enterprise count. Slovakia's share of EU-27 manufacturing enterprises is much larger than its share of persons employed, by our count.
  • Mixing a Eurostat Structural Business Statistics count with a headcount from the business register as if they were one series.
  • Inventing an aggregate manufacturing count from the register, or treating a register match as proof of a buying plant.
  • Calling a broader industry total manufacturing, or treating C29 as a label for the whole manufacturing base.
06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in Slovakia?
Eurostat counted 89,886 manufacturing enterprises in Slovakia in 2024, for NACE Section C, with 477,988 persons employed. There were 89,803 enterprises in 2023, a 0.1% rise by our count. Slovakia had 4.12% of EU-27 manufacturing enterprises in 2024, by our count, but only 1.60% of persons employed.[1][2]
Which Slovak manufacturing sectors employ the most people?
In 2024, among published manufacturing divisions, fabricated metal products (C25) employed 75,790 people, 15.9% of manufacturing employment, narrowly ahead of motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers (C29) at 75,505, or 15.8%. Machinery and equipment n.e.c. (C28) were third, at 44,401 (9.3%). The metal-products lead was 285 people, by our count.[1][2]
Is Slovak manufacturing mainly motor vehicles?
No. Among published manufacturing divisions, motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers led net turnover and gross investment in machinery and equipment in 2024, at €39,508.2 million and €1,265 million, but fabricated metal products narrowly led employment. Motor vehicles is a concentrated division: 1,517 enterprises in 2024, against 33,505 fabricated-metal enterprises.[1][2]
Where do I look up a Slovak company?
The official Slovak business register is ORSR. Use it to confirm a named enterprise. It is a register, not a source of an aggregate manufacturing count, and Eurostat does not publish a public named list of Slovak manufacturers. A register match confirms a legal entity; it does not, by itself, prove who buys at a plant.[3]
How does Slovak manufacturing compare with the EU?
In 2024 Slovakia accounted for 4.12% of EU-27 manufacturing enterprises, 1.60% of persons employed and 1.08% of net turnover, by our count. Persons employed were 477,988, against 1,248,982 in Czechia, 769,452 in Hungary and 727,738 in Austria. Net turnover was €105,166.1 million, 2.0% below the 2023 figure, by our count.[1][2]
How does Demand Range research industrial accounts in Slovakia?
Demand Range sizes the market with official Eurostat figures, then builds a named account list from official sources such as the business register and site-level public records, and qualifies each row at company or site level. Statistics alone do not name customers. Named contacts and outreach are separate scopes, not included by default.[1][3]

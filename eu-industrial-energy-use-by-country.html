Which EU industries use the most energy, by country

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

If you sell energy-efficiency, heat or electrification equipment into industry, start from how much energy each sector and country actually uses, not from how many enterprises there are. Eurostat's complete energy balances give a dated, citable measure of that physical use, in thousand tonnes of oil equivalent (ktoe).

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns only the physical energy-use figures from Eurostat dataset nrg_bal_c. It does not cover energy purchases in euro, the Irish energy-audit obligation, or data-centre electricity.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat complete energy balances, nrg_bal_c; extracted 10 November 2026.

  • EU-27 final energy consumption in the industry sector, energy use, was 215,501.322 ktoe in 2024, after 215,070.029 ktoe in 2023. The 27 official member-country cells sum to those EU-27 totals. Eurostat last updated the data on 2 June 2026.[1][2]
  • Chemical and petrochemical was the largest EU-27 industry energy-use subsector in 2024, at 45,479.276 ktoe, about 21.1% of the official industry total by our count. Non-metallic minerals, food, beverages and tobacco, paper, pulp and printing, and iron and steel were the next four.[1]
  • Germany used the most industrial energy in 2024, at 48,368.387 ktoe, about 22.4% of the EU-27 total by our count. France, Italy, Spain and Poland followed. By our count, those five countries together accounted for 59.8% of the official EU-27 total.[1]
  • These figures are physical energy use. They are not energy purchases in euro, and they are not a list of sites.
02 / eu 27 industry energy use by subsector 2023 and 2024

EU-27 industry energy use by subsector, 2023 and 2024

The indicator is Eurostat's "Final consumption - industry sector - energy use" (code FC_IND_E), for all energy products (SIEC TOTAL), in thousand tonnes of oil equivalent. The official subsectors below are also energy-use items. Non-energy use of energy products, such as chemical feedstocks, is a separate balance item (FC_IND_NE) and is not included in the total or in the shares.[1][2]

The industry total is broader than manufacturing. Official subsectors include mining and quarrying and construction, as well as manufacturing branches such as chemicals, iron and steel, food, and paper.[1]

04 / the mix is not the same in every country

The mix is not the same in every country

A country with a smaller industry total can still be a concentrated market for one process. Two official examples, with shares by our count of each country's 2024 FC_IND_E total:[1]

  • In Finland, paper, pulp and printing used 5,167.794 ktoe in 2024, 55.0% of Finland's industry energy use.
  • In Ireland, food, beverages and tobacco used 507.367 ktoe (23.3%), non-ferrous metals 439.497 ktoe (20.2%) and non-metallic minerals 378.354 ktoe (17.4%). Ireland's official iron and steel cell was 0.000 ktoe in 2023 and 2024.

Use the country table to size the market, then open the same dataset for that country's subsectors before you plan coverage.

05 / what the figures include and what they do not

What the figures include, and what they do not

  • They measure final energy consumption in the industry sector, for energy use, in ktoe. Definitions for energy statistics follow Annex A of Regulation (EC) No 1099/2008.[2]
  • They include mining, quarrying and construction, not only NACE Section C manufacturing.
  • They are national totals, not plant-level figures, and not a prospect list.
  • They are not energy purchases in euro, not an audit finding, and not evidence that a plant will buy equipment.
  • No observation-status flags were present in the API extract we used. 2024 is the latest period in the dataset.

If you already work with Irish energy-audit or published Action Plan signals, see energy-audit signals for industrial suppliers. That page owns the Irish audit obligation and LIEN. This page adds the EU-wide physical energy-use picture.

06 / how to use the tables in a supplier search

How to use the tables in a supplier search

  1. Pick the subsectors that fit your offer, using the official labels above, not a looser "heavy industry" shorthand.
  2. Read the country totals and the country mix. A large national total is not always the best first territory for your process.
  3. Move from energy use to sites. An energy-balance cell does not name plants. For large installations in those sectors, use the EU Industrial Emissions Portal. Check any country reporting gaps on that guide before you treat a year-on-year fall in site counts as closures.
  4. Keep the evidence types apart. Energy use sizes the installed base. Audits, tenders and published projects are separate signals.
07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Which EU industries use the most energy?
Chemical and petrochemical was the largest EU-27 industry energy-use subsector in 2024, at 45,479.276 ktoe, about 21.1% of the official industry total by our count. Non-metallic minerals, food, beverages and tobacco, paper, pulp and printing, and iron and steel were next. Shares use official nrg_bal_c cells extracted on 10 November 2026.[1]
Which EU countries use the most industrial energy?
Germany used the most in 2024, at 48,368.387 ktoe, about 22.4% of the official EU-27 total by our count. France, Italy, Spain and Poland were next. By our count, those five countries together accounted for 59.8% of the official EU-27 total. The 27 official member-country cells sum to that total.[1]
Does "industry" here mean manufacturing only?
No. In Eurostat's complete energy balances, "Final consumption - industry sector - energy use" is broader than manufacturing. The official subsectors include mining and quarrying and construction, as well as manufacturing branches such as chemicals, iron and steel, and food. The unit is thousand tonnes of oil equivalent, not euro.[1][2]
Is this the same as industrial energy spending?
No. This page uses physical energy, in thousand tonnes of oil equivalent (ktoe), from Eurostat dataset nrg_bal_c. It does not use energy purchases in euro. Non-energy use of energy products, such as chemical feedstocks, is a separate balance item and is not part of the industry energy-use total.[1][2]
Do the figures include Ireland and the UK?
Ireland is included. In 2024 it used 2,174.005 ktoe of industrial energy, about 1.0% of the EU-27 total by our count. The United Kingdom's last published cell in this dataset is 21,118.796 ktoe in 2019; Eurostat has not published UK figures for 2020 to 2024 in nrg_bal_c.[1]
How do I find the plants in a high-energy sector?
Start from the subsectors and countries with the highest energy use, then look up large installations in those activities on the EU Industrial Emissions Portal. An energy-balance total is not a site list, and it is not an audit or buying signal. Use the portal with the method in our emissions-portal guide, and keep any country reporting gaps in view.

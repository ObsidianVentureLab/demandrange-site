Foreign-owned industry in Ireland: what the CSO data shows

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

In 2022, 1,149 of Ireland's 21,451 industry enterprises were foreign-owned, or 5.4%, yet they produced 88.0% of industry turnover and 87.3% of gross value added (GVA), according to the Central Statistics Office (CSO). Industry here means NACE Rev. 2 sections B to E: mining and quarrying, manufacturing, electricity and gas supply, and water supply and waste. The CSO does not publish these ownership figures for manufacturing on its own, so none of the figures below is a manufacturing-only count.[1][2]

Key facts

CSO Business in Ireland 2022: Insights on Multinationals and PxStat table IPA05; checked 24 November 2026.

  • 2022: 1,149 foreign-owned and 20,302 Irish-owned enterprises in industry, out of 21,451.[1][2]
  • 2022: foreign-owned enterprises produced 88.0% of industry turnover and 87.3% of industry GVA.[1]
  • 2022: they employed 48.5% of the 290,505 persons employed in industry; Irish-owned enterprises employed 51.5%, by our count.[1][2]
  • 2022: enterprises owned outside the EU were 3.7% of industry enterprises but produced 83.5% of industry turnover, by our count.[2]
  • 2022 is the latest year published. The CSO has not yet released a later edition with figures.[2][7]
02 / foreign owned and irish owned industry the counts

Foreign-owned and Irish-owned industry: the counts

The CSO classifies each enterprise by the country of its ultimate controlling institution: the unit at the top of the chain of control. Control means holding, directly or indirectly, more than half of the voting power or more than half of the shares. An enterprise controlled from outside Ireland is foreign-owned, even if it was set up and operates in Ireland.[4]

The counts add up exactly: 20,302 Irish-owned plus 1,149 foreign-owned make 21,451, and 352 EU-owned plus 797 non-EU-owned make 1,149, by our count. In the CSO's PxStat tables the same units are labelled "affiliates".[2]

Foreign-owned and Irish-owned enterprises employed roughly the same number of people in 2022. The difference is in money: Irish-owned enterprises had 51.5% of the people but 12.0% of turnover, by our count. The CSO notes that the bulk of the foreign-owned share came from enterprises owned outside the EU.[1][2]

EU-owned enterprises are a much smaller group. In 2022 the 352 enterprises owned by EU multinationals employed 24,345 persons, 8.4% of industry employment, and produced 4.5% of industry turnover, by our count.[2]

04 / why averages mislead

Why averages mislead

An average across all of Irish industry describes almost nobody. By our count, the average industry enterprise in 2022 had about 14 persons employed and about €20.0 million in turnover. Split by ownership, the two groups sit far either side of that figure.[2]

Industry (NACE B to E), 2022, by our count Average persons employed per enterprise Average turnover per enterprise
Irish-owned about 7 about €2.5 million
Foreign-owned about 123 about €329 million
of which EU-owned about 69 about €55 million
of which non-EU-owned about 146 about €450 million
All industry enterprises about 14 about €20.0 million

Source: our division of CSO PxStat table IPA05 cells (persons employed and turnover divided by enterprises), reference year 2022; checked 24 November 2026.[2]

  • Means, not typical firms. These are averages. A small number of very large enterprises can pull an average up a long way, and these CSO tables publish no medians or size bands by ownership. The typical foreign-owned enterprise may be much smaller than €329 million in turnover.[2]
  • Sector totals hide the split. Industry's 2022 totals, 290,505 persons employed and €430.1 billion turnover, mix two very different groups: 5.4% of enterprises with 88.0% of the turnover, and 94.6% with 12.0%, by our count.[1][2]
  • Turnover is not a buying budget. Turnover and GVA come from structural business statistics for each enterprise. They do not say how much any one site spends on equipment, services or suppliers.
05 / using ownership in account research

Using ownership in account research

Ownership changes who you are selling to. A foreign-owned site in Ireland may buy through a group or regional function outside Ireland, while an Irish-owned enterprise may decide locally. Before you score an account, check who sits at the top of its chain of control, not only the name of the Irish entity.

  • Expect two very different lists. On the 2022 figures, an Irish industry list is mostly small, Irish-owned enterprises, about 20,300 of them, averaging about 7 persons employed each, by our count. The foreign-owned list is short, 1,149 enterprises, but holds most of the sector's turnover.[1][2]
  • Look past the largest names. Irish-owned industry still employed 149,742 persons in 2022, more than the foreign-owned group. For original equipment makers and Irish-owned suppliers, see industrial OEM account research in Ireland.[2]
  • Use whole-economy country figures with care. Across the whole business economy in 2022, not only industry, the United Kingdom was the most common single country of ownership (3,812 UK-owned enterprises), while United States-owned enterprises (2,379) produced 73.8% of foreign-owned turnover. The CSO tables give no country-of-ownership split inside industry.[1][3]
  • A newer, different measure. A CSO Frontier Series release for 2023 covers Industry and Construction (NACE B to F) and counts employments, not persons employed. In 2023, 60.9% of employments in that grouping were in Irish-owned enterprises, 19.0% in US-owned, 7.3% in "EU27 excl Ireland 2020", 3.8% in UK-owned and 9.0% in "Other Countries (4)". The CSO says particular care is needed with Frontier Series outputs. It is not an update of the 2022 industry figure of 48.5%.[5][6]

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How much of Irish industry is foreign-owned?
In 2022, 1,149 of Ireland's 21,451 industry enterprises were foreign-owned, or 5.4%, according to the CSO. Those enterprises produced 88.0% of industry turnover and 87.3% of gross value added, and employed 48.5% of persons employed. Industry here covers NACE sections B to E, including manufacturing, energy and water.[1][2]
How many foreign-owned manufacturers are there in Ireland?
The CSO does not publish a separate count for manufacturing. Its ownership tables cover industry as a whole, NACE sections B to E, which adds mining, energy, water and waste to manufacturing. In 2022, 1,149 industry enterprises were foreign-owned: 352 owned by EU multinationals and 797 by multinationals outside the EU.[2]
Do foreign-owned enterprises employ most people in Irish industry?
No, but nearly half. In 2022 foreign-owned enterprises employed 140,763 of the 290,505 persons employed in industry, or 48.5%, according to the CSO. Irish-owned enterprises employed 149,742, or 51.5%, by our count. The gap is in money: Irish-owned enterprises produced only 12.0% of industry turnover.[1][2]
Which countries own the most foreign-owned businesses in Ireland?
Across the whole business economy in 2022, the United Kingdom was the most common single country of ownership, with 3,812 UK-owned enterprises, according to the CSO. United States-owned enterprises numbered 2,379 but produced 73.8% of foreign-owned turnover. The CSO publishes these country figures for the whole business economy, not for industry alone.[1][3]
Is there newer data than 2022?
Not for these measures. The CSO's latest Insights on Multinationals edition with figures covers 2022, released in October 2024. A separate 2023 Frontier Series release counts employments in Industry and Construction, NACE B to F, by ownership, but it uses a different unit and sector grouping, so it does not update 2022.[5][7]

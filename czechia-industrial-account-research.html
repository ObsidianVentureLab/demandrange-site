How to find industrial customers in Czechia: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Eurostat counted 193,224 manufacturing enterprises in Czechia in 2024 (NACE Section C).[1] By our count of the official 2024 EU member-country cells in that series, Czechia ranks fifth in the EU, after Italy, France, Poland and Germany, and ahead of Spain.[1] Use that headline only to size the market. Then build a Czech account list from national sources the country table does not carry: the Czech Statistical Office (CZSO) business register, ARES, the public register, CZ-NACE 2025 activity codes and the NEN and ISVZ public-contract systems.

For the comparison with other EU countries, see manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country (2024 data). This page does not repeat that table.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat 2024 structural business statistics; CZSO; the Czech registers and procurement systems; EEA Industrial Emissions Portal; checked 10 November 2026.

  • Eurostat table sbs_ovw_act counts 193,224 manufacturing enterprises in Czechia in 2024 (NACE Section C, indicator "Enterprises - number"). The dataset was last updated on 30 September 2026 and was re-extracted on 10 November 2026. No flag is attached to that cell.[1][2]
  • By our count, that is the fifth-largest manufacturing enterprise count among EU member countries in 2024, and about 8.9% of the EU total of 2,180,638 in the same extract.[1]
  • CZSO's own industry results (published 15 April 2026) count 193,224 active manufacturing enterprises (CZ-NACE Section C) in 2024, with 1,249 thousand persons employed, of whom an average 1,074 thousand were registered employees.[21]
  • CZ-NACE 2025 is the Czech national version of NACE Rev. 2.1. It was introduced by CZSO Communication No. 400/2024 Coll. of 10 December 2024, with effect from 1 January 2025.[3][4][5]
  • CZSO says it reclassified the roughly three million economic subjects in its business register (RES) to the updated NACE by November 2025, changing the activity code of more than 1.1 million of them. The new codes went public on 20 January 2026, labelled NACE2025.[6][7]
  • In the EEA map service for the industrial site registry (database version 16, dated 20 February 2026), Czechia has no rows for reporting year 2024. By our count of that service on 10 November 2026, it had 3,783 sites for 2023.[18][19]
02 / how big the czech industrial base is

How big the Czech industrial base is

Eurostat's 193,224 figure is the official 2024 headline for Czech manufacturing enterprises (NACE Section C). Division, size-class, employment and investment rows for Czechia sit on the EU manufacturing enterprises by country page; this page does not repeat them.[1]

By our count of the 2024 cells for EU member countries in the same extract, only Italy, France, Poland and Germany have more manufacturing enterprises, and Spain is next after Czechia.[1] A large count does not mean large firms: an enterprise count includes many very small businesses, so use the size-class rows on the comparison page before you plan territory or headcount.

What CZSO's own statistics add

CZSO's annual industry results for 2012–2024 (published 15 April 2026, Table 3, Section C manufacturing) give the national view of the same base:[21]

Czechia, manufacturing (CZ-NACE Section C), CZSO Count
Active enterprises, 2024 193,224
Persons employed, 2024 (thousands) 1,249
of whom average registered employees (thousands) 1,074
Average monthly gross wage, 2024 (CZK) 44,195

CZSO's 2024 count of active manufacturing enterprises is the same number as the Eurostat cell above; CZSO puts it at 1.98% more than in 2023 (index 101.98).[21][1]

CZSO's business register statistics count something different: registered economic subjects, whether or not they are active. As at 30 September 2026, RES held 2,950,895 registered economic subjects, of which 326,440 had manufacturing as their prevailing CZ-NACE activity (326,443 when coded to CZ-NACE 2025).[22] At the end of 2025, CZSO counted 2,893,480 economic subjects in RES, of which 1,780,970 had ascertained activity.[23] So do not compare the registered figure with the active-enterprise count, and do not use it as a market size.

Treat all these figures as market sizing, not as a list. Eurostat permits reuse of its figures with acknowledgement.[2]

03 / build the list in layers

Build the list in layers

  1. Companies. Look up each prospect in ARES, the Ministry of Finance's search across Czech public registers, then confirm the legal entity in the public register at justice.cz.[8][9][10]
  2. Activity. Code the list to CZ-NACE. CZSO's register now carries both the older CZ-NACE code and the CZ-NACE 2025 code (labelled NACE2025) for each economic subject.[6][7]
  3. Plants. The registered office is not always the plant. Use site evidence such as the EU Industrial Emissions Portal, and note the 2024 gap described below.[18][19][20]
  4. Buying signals. Search Czech public contracts in NEN and the public-contracts journal in ISVZ, and EU-threshold notices on TED with CPV codes.[11][14][16][17]

A register match confirms a legal entity. It is not, by itself, a purchasing signal.

04 / the czech registers

The Czech registers

ARES. The Administrative Register of Economic Subjects is a Ministry of Finance information system for searching economic subjects registered in Czechia. It displays data held in source registers, including the public register kept by the registry courts, the trade licence register and CZSO's business register (RES).[8] The ministry states that ARES data are for information only and cannot be used as evidence in court proceedings. ARES also has a public API with query limits, and the ministry may restrict or block access for users who send more than 500 queries a minute.[8]

The public register at justice.cz. The Ministry of Justice runs the "Veřejný rejstřík a Sbírka listin" portal (public register and collection of documents). It offers a search by entity and links to the commercial register, the insolvency register, trust funds and beneficial owners.[10] Use it to confirm the legal entity and read its filed documents.

CZSO's business register (RES). RES holds selected information on all economic subjects with a seat in Czechia, and on some foreign subjects linked to Czech companies. Its web application searches by company identification number (IČO) or name, and shows the registered address, legal form, activity classification and employee-count category.[7] CZSO notes that RES data cannot serve as proof in administrative or other proceedings.[7]

05 / cz nace and cz nace 2025 run side by side

CZ-NACE and CZ-NACE 2025 run side by side

CZ-NACE is Czechia's national classification of economic activities, first introduced with effect from 1 January 2008.[5] NACE Rev. 2.1 took effect on 1 January 2025 under Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/137, and CZSO built CZ-NACE 2025 on it.[3][4] CZSO Communication No. 400/2024 Coll. of 10 December 2024 brought CZ-NACE 2025 into effect from 1 January 2025.[5]

  • Structure. Down to four-digit codes, CZ-NACE 2025 follows the European classification. It adds a national fifth level of 99 items for more detailed national breakdowns.[4]
  • Statistics and the register. CZSO's CZ-NACE 2025 page states that it is implemented in RES and CZSO surveys from 1 January 2026.[3] RES exports in CZ-NACE 2025 started on 1 January 2026, and dual coding in RES is scheduled to end on 31 December 2028.[7]
  • Automatic reclassification. CZSO reassigned codes automatically, using algorithms and past survey results, without contacting each subject. It warns that some NACE2025 codes may not match what a business actually does.[6]
  • Name corrections. In December 2025, CZSO corrected the Czech names of 10 CZ-NACE 2025 items, with no change to the codes.[3]

For a list, that means:

  • Keep both the older CZ-NACE code and the NACE2025 code on each row until your sources have moved.
  • Check automatically assigned codes against the company's own products and site evidence.
  • Map the codes with the official correspondence described in our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.

The method is the same one used for industrial account research in Germany, where WZ 2025 is that country's NACE Rev. 2.1 implementation.

06 / czech public contracts nen and isvz

Czech public contracts: NEN and ISVZ

NEN (Národní elektronický nástroj, the national electronic tool) is the state system for administering and awarding public contracts. Under Government Resolution No. 467 of 21 June 2017, central government bodies and their subordinate organisations that are contracting authorities have had to use NEN from 1 July 2018, with an exception procedure.[11][12][13] On 10 November 2026, the NEN home page displayed 26,550 contracting authorities, 43,873 suppliers, 276,467 contracts carried out in NEN and a contract value of CZK 2,212 billion.[11]

ISVZ (Informační systém o veřejných zakázkách, the public procurement information system) is defined in section 224 of Act No. 134/2016 Coll. on public procurement.[15] Its functions include the public-contracts journal (Věstník veřejných zakázek), the list of qualified suppliers, procurement statistics, code lists and open data.[14][15][16]

Use NEN for live procedures run there, the journal for published notices, and TED for notices above EU thresholds.[11][16][17]

07 / czechia s 2024 gap in the eu industrial site registry

Czechia's 2024 gap in the EU industrial site registry

The EEA's Industrial Reporting database (version 16, as of 20 February 2026) covers E-PRTR facilities from 2007 to 2024 and IED installations from 2017 to 2024.[19] Czechia has no site rows for reporting year 2024 in the EEA map service behind the EU Industrial Emissions Portal: by our count on 10 November 2026, the latest Czech reporting year is 2023, with 3,783 sites.[18][20]

By our count in the same service, Slovakia (latest year 2022) and Malta (latest year 2023) have no 2024 rows either.[18] So a 2024 filter in the portal returns no Czech results, because Czechia's 2024 reporting year has no rows yet. That is a reporting gap, not a sign that plants closed. Use 2023 site records for Czechia until the 2024 rows appear, and record the reporting year on each account.

09 / step by step

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat headline, then define it with CZ-NACE / NACE codes. See our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide and the EU enterprises table.
  2. Find candidate companies in ARES and RES by activity code, and keep both code vintages on each row.
  3. Confirm each legal entity in the public register at justice.cz and read its filed documents. Do not treat officer names as a contact list; record the function, not a person.
  4. Find the plants from public site evidence, including the EU Industrial Emissions Portal, using 2023 records for Czechia.
  5. Add a trigger only when you have one: a NEN procedure, a journal or TED notice, a register change or a new site record.
  6. Log the evidence for each account: legal entity, IČO, both activity codes, site evidence with its reporting year, and the reason it fits the brief.

Fictional example: A fictional Irish maker of industrial filtration units targets Czech metal-finishing plants. It filters the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for Czech surface-treatment installations in reporting year 2023 and resolves each operator in ARES and the public register. It finds that one fictional group runs two plants under one company, and records them as two accounts under one group. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

10 / common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Using only registered-office data. In Czechia, as elsewhere in the EU, buying influence often sits at plant level.
  • Treating an ARES or RES entry as legal proof. Both sources say their data are informative only.
  • Trusting an automatically assigned NACE2025 code without checking what the company makes.
  • Filtering the emissions portal on 2024 and concluding that Czech sites have closed.
  • Treating a register match or a tender notice as proof that a plant is buying your product.
11 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in Czechia?
Eurostat counted 193,224 manufacturing enterprises in Czechia in 2024, for NACE Section C. By our count of the official 2024 cells for EU member countries, that is the fifth-largest count in the EU, after Italy, France, Poland and Germany, and ahead of Spain. It sizes the market; it is not a company list.[1]
What is CZ-NACE 2025?
CZ-NACE 2025 is the Czech national version of NACE Rev. 2.1. CZSO introduced it by Communication No. 400/2024 Coll. of 10 December 2024, with effect from 1 January 2025. It follows the European codes to four digits and adds a national fifth level of 99 items. CZSO's register carries both the old and new codes until 2028.[3][4][5][7]
What is ARES?
ARES is the Ministry of Finance's Administrative Register of Economic Subjects. It searches economic subjects registered in Czechia and displays data from source registers, including the public register, the trade licence register and CZSO's business register. Its data are for information only and cannot be used as evidence in court. It also offers a public API with query limits.[8][9]
Where are Czech public tenders published?
Look in two national systems. NEN is the state system for running public-contract procedures, and central government bodies have had to use it since 1 July 2018. ISVZ, defined in the public procurement act, includes the public-contracts journal, statistics and open data. Notices above EU thresholds also appear on TED, so search there too.[11][12][14][15][17]
Why does the EU emissions portal have no Czech rows for 2024?
Czechia has no site rows for reporting year 2024 in the EEA data behind the portal, although the database covers 2024 for other countries. By our count on 10 November 2026, the latest Czech year is 2023, with 3,783 sites. Treat the gap as missing reporting, not closures, and use the 2023 records.[18][19]
Can Czech registers tell me who buys at a plant?
No. ARES, the public register and CZSO's business register show legal entities, registered addresses, legal forms, activity codes and employee-count categories, and ARES data are for information only. NEN and the public-contracts journal show public buying, not a private plant's suppliers. Roles and buying plans need separate evidence; Demand Range does not include named contacts by default.[7][8][10][11][16]

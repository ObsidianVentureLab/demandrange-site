UK food and drink manufacturer research

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 25 November 2026

The UK had 12,085 food and drink manufacturing enterprises in the 13 March 2026 snapshot of the Inter-Departmental Business Register (IDBR), by our count of the official Office for National Statistics (ONS) totals: 9,485 in food products (SIC 10) and 2,600 in beverages (SIC 11). Those figures were published on 24 September 2026 in the ONS dataset UK business: activity, size and location. They count enterprises, not named plants. Of the 12,085, 1,075 had 50 or more employment, by our count of the official size-band cells. The official lists that name sites are the approved food-establishment registers, which cover premises handling products of animal origin and are not a count of every SIC 10 and 11 business.[1][3]

For plants in EU member states, see EU food and beverage plant research, which excludes UK registers. For the wider UK method, see how to build a list of UK manufacturers. The codes used here are UK SIC codes for manufacturing.

01 / key facts

Key facts

ONS UK business: activity, size and location, 2026 edition, IDBR snapshot 13 March 2026, published 24 September 2026; approved-establishment files checked 24 November 2026.

  • Food-product manufacturing (SIC 10) had an official UK total of 9,485 enterprises and 11,035 local units. Beverage manufacturing (SIC 11) had an official UK total of 2,600 enterprises and 2,865 local units. Combined, that is 12,085 enterprises and 13,900 local units, by our count. The two official enterprise totals do not include tobacco (SIC 12).[1]
  • By our count of the official UK employment-size cells, 8,590 of those enterprises had fewer than 10 employment, 3,495 had 10 or more, and 1,075 had 50 or more, including 295 with 250 or more.[1]
  • Food and drink manufacturing was 9.4% of UK manufacturing enterprises, by our count of the official UK division totals for SIC 10 to 33, which sum to 128,125. The official UK total for all IDBR enterprises was 2,786,985.[1]
  • The largest official UK class total in food and drink manufacturing was manufacture of bread, fresh pastry goods and cakes (SIC 1071), at 2,905 enterprises.   • By our count of unique approval numbers in the latest official UK approved-establishment files available on 24 November 2026, there were 6,390 approved establishments in the UK, after excluding entries outside the UK. The Food Standards Agency files are dated 1 November 2026 and the Food Standards Scotland file is dated 29 October 2026. This is a count of approved premises, not of SIC 10 and 11 enterprises, and the two snapshot dates are not the same.[3][4][5][6]
02 / how many food and drink manufacturers the uk has

How many food and drink manufacturers the UK has

ONS counts IDBR enterprises and local units by UK Standard Industrial Classification (SIC 2007). An enterprise is the organisational unit that ONS treats as a business. A local unit is an individual site, such as a factory, belonging to that enterprise. The 2026 dataset is a snapshot of the IDBR taken on 13 March 2026 and published on 24 September 2026. Counts are control-rounded to base 5 to avoid disclosure, and totals can differ by small amounts across tables for that reason. This page uses official UK totals only.[1]

SIC 10 is manufacture of food products and SIC 11 is manufacture of beverages. Combined figures below are by our count of those official cells. They are a measure of the size of the universe, not a named account list.

04 / official lists that name sites

Official lists that name sites

ONS does not publish the names of the enterprises or local units in these tables. To name a site, use official registers and then match the site back to a company.

The Food Standards Agency and Food Standards Scotland publish lists of establishments approved to handle, prepare or produce products of animal origin under Regulation (EC) No 853/2004. The lists name the premises and the approved activities, which is what a supplier needs when the question is “which plant does this work?” They are not a list of every UK food and drink manufacturer.[3][4][5][6]

By our count of unique approval numbers in the latest official UK files available on 24 November 2026, there were 6,390 approved establishments in the UK, after excluding entries outside the UK. The Food Standards Agency files are dated 1 November 2026 and the Food Standards Scotland file is dated 29 October 2026, so the figure combines snapshots that are a few days apart. Read it as the size of the named approved-premises lists, not as a second SIC 10 and 11 total.[3][4][5][6]

Use the lists with those limits in mind:

  • They cover products of animal origin, such as meat, fish, dairy, egg and similar approved activities. A large share of bakeries, confectionery makers, soft-drinks plants and other non-animal-origin manufacturers will not appear.
  • They also include sites that are not manufacturers, such as cold stores, packing centres and some vessels. An approved establishment is a premises with an approval, not automatically a SIC 10 or 11 enterprise.
  • Approvals are at site level. One enterprise can have more than one approved site, and a site can hold more than one approved activity.
  • The lists are updated monthly and should be re-counted from the latest files before they are used in a live list.

For a full UK manufacturer list that is not limited to approved animal-origin premises, start from the ONS size of the SIC 10 and 11 universe, then build names from official and other public sources and qualify each row. That method is set out in UK manufacturing account research.

05 / how to research uk food and drink accounts

How to research UK food and drink accounts

Start from your product, not from the food category. A supplier of process equipment, a supplier of refrigeration and a supplier of packaging lines may all sell to “dairy”, but they need different plants.

For each company, record three things separately: the product (what it makes or sells), the work (what process happens) and the site (where that work is done). A company that sells cheese may make it at its own plant, buy it in and pack it, or have it made under contract. Only the first runs a cheese-making process.

Separate three kinds of business that look alike in search results: manufacturers that run their own plants, brand owners that use contract manufacturers, and distributors or packers. Record which one each company is before you count it.

A practical sequence is:

  1. Size the universe with the official ONS enterprise and local-unit totals for SIC 10 and 11, and with the class mix above, so you know how large the sector is and which activities dominate.
  2. Name the sites from the official approved-establishment lists where the process involves products of animal origin, and from other official or public sources for activities those lists do not cover.
  3. Confirm the unit you are counting: an enterprise, a site, or a brand. Match multi-site businesses so that you do not treat two sites as two customers, or a contract brand as a second plant.
  4. Qualify the row against your product. Keep “not found” separate from “checked, does not apply”.

Fictional example. A fictional supplier of clean-in-place systems for dairy plants wants UK sites that process milk on site. It uses the official dairy class total (390 enterprises, 445 local units) as the size of that part of the universe, then takes named dairy sites from the approved-establishment lists and qualifies each one for on-site milk processing. A cheese brand whose product is made under contract is recorded under the plant that makes it, not as a separate process site. The companies and decisions are fictional and only illustrate the method.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many food and drink manufacturers are there in the UK?
The UK had 12,085 food and drink manufacturing enterprises in the 13 March 2026 IDBR snapshot, by our count of the official ONS totals: 9,485 in food products (SIC 10) and 2,600 in beverages (SIC 11). Of these, 1,075 had 50 or more employment, by our count. These are enterprises, not a named list of plants.[1]
Where can I find a list of UK food and drink manufacturers?
There is no single official named list of every UK food and drink manufacturer. ONS counts enterprises and local units by SIC, but does not name them. The Food Standards Agency and Food Standards Scotland lists name approved sites for products of animal origin, and do not cover every SIC 10 and 11 business.[1][3]
What is the difference between an enterprise and a local unit?
An enterprise is the organisational unit that ONS counts as a business. A local unit is an individual site, such as a factory, belonging to that enterprise. In the same snapshot, food and drink manufacturing had 12,085 enterprises and 13,900 local units, by our count of the official UK division totals, so some businesses operate more than one site.[1]
Does the approved establishments list include every food manufacturer?
No. The official approved-establishment lists name premises approved to handle, prepare or produce products of animal origin. Many food and drink manufacturers, including a large share of bakeries, confectionery makers and soft-drinks plants, will not appear. The lists also include sites that are not manufacturers, such as cold stores. Do not treat the list length as a manufacturer count.[3]
Should I target brand owners or the plants that make the product?
For process equipment, packaging lines or other plant-level supplies, target the business that runs the site. A brand owner whose products are made under contract usually does not buy that equipment. Record the product, the process and the site separately, and confirm each from official lists or other public evidence before you count the account.

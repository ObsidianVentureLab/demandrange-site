How to find industrial customers in Spain: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Eurostat counted 164,278 manufacturing enterprises in Spain in 2024 (NACE Section C).[1] By our count of the official 2024 EU member-country cells in that series, Spain ranks sixth, after Italy, France, Poland, Germany and Czechia.[1] Use that headline only to size the market, then build a Spanish account list from national sources the country table does not carry: INE's Central Business Register (DIRCE), the Registro Mercantil, CNAE-2025 activity codes and the Plataforma de Contratación del Sector Público.

For the comparison with other EU countries, see manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country (2024 data). Do not mix that Eurostat stock with INE's figures below; they are different official series, with different sector cuts and reference dates.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat 2024 structural business statistics; INE Central Business Register as at 1 January 2025; CNAE-2025 and the Spanish registers; checked 10 November 2026.

  • Eurostat table sbs_ovw_act counts 164,278 manufacturing enterprises in Spain in 2024 (NACE Section C, indicator "Enterprises - number"). The dataset was last updated on 30 September 2026 and was re-extracted on 10 November 2026. No flag is attached to that cell.[1][2]
  • By our count of the official 2024 EU member-country cells in the same extract, Spain has the sixth-largest manufacturing enterprise count, after Italy (347,845), France (279,540), Poland (241,804), Germany (198,560) and Czechia (193,224).[1]
  • INE's statistical use of the Central Business Register (DIRCE / CBR) counted 3,310,824 economically active enterprises in Spain as at 1 January 2025, 1.7% more than a year earlier, including 172,765 in INE's Industry sector (5.2% of the total). Those results were published on 11 December 2025 and are final.[3][4]
  • By our count of INE's official CNAE-2009 manufacturing divisions (10–33) in DIRCE table 73019, there were 153,167 manufacturing enterprises at 1 January 2025, and 156,267 a year earlier. That cut is narrower than INE's Industry total of 172,765 (sections B–E) and is a different series from Eurostat's 2024 NACE Section C count of 164,278; do not subtract these figures from one another.[15]
  • Of those Industry enterprises, 16,577 had 20 or more employees (9.6% of the Industry total). INE also counted 3,866,958 active premises (local units) in all sectors, of which 214,904 were in Industry.[3][5]
  • INE warns that, after EBS Regulation 2019/2152 and its Implementation Act 2020/1197, these DIRCE figures are not comparable with earlier years. The 1 January 2025 tables still code main activity to CNAE-2009.[3][4][6]
  • CNAE-2025, Spain's version of NACE Rev. 2.1, was approved by Royal Decree 10/2025 of 14 January 2025, published in the official gazette on 15 January 2025, and entered into force on 16 January 2025.[7][8] INE will move its statistics onto CNAE-2025 on a staged calendar; DIRCE results in CNAE-2025 were scheduled for publication in 2026 (reference 2025). On 10 November 2026 the latest DIRCE release on INE's operation and press pages was still the 1 January 2025, CNAE-2009 vintage.[8][6]
02 / how big the spanish industrial base is

How big the Spanish industrial base is

Eurostat's 164,278 figure is the official 2024 headline for Spanish manufacturing enterprises (NACE Section C). Division, size-class, employment and investment rows for Spain sit on the EU manufacturing enterprises by country page; this page does not repeat them.[1]

INE publishes a separate national count from DIRCE, the statistical use of Spain's Central Business Register. The latest figures checked on 10 November 2026 are as at 1 January 2025:[3][4][5]

Spain, INE Central Business Register, 1 January 2025 Count
Economically active enterprises, all sectors 3,310,824
Industry (INE sector label) 172,765
Manufacturing (CNAE-2009 divisions 10–33), by our count 153,167
Industry enterprises with 20 or more employees 16,577
Active premises (local units), all sectors 3,866,958
Industry premises (local units) 214,904

INE's "Industry" label is the official sector total on the 1 January 2025 release. It is a wider cut than manufacturing: it covers CNAE-2009 sections B to E (mining, manufacturing, energy and water and waste). By our count of INE's official 2-digit manufacturing divisions (10–33) in table 73019, there were 153,167 manufacturing enterprises at 1 January 2025, and 156,267 a year earlier.[15] That manufacturing sum is not the same series as Eurostat's 2024 NACE Section C cell of 164,278, and the reference dates also differ. Do not subtract the 153,167, 172,765 and 164,278 figures from one another. An enterprise, in INE's definition, is the smallest combination of legal units that forms an organisational unit producing goods or services, with a degree of autonomy in decision-making; a local unit is an enterprise, or part of one, at a geographically identified place.[6] Treat these official series as market-sizing figures, not as a named company list.

Eurostat permits reuse of its figures with acknowledgement.[2] INE also publishes DIRCE under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence.[3][4]

03 / build the list in layers

Build the list in layers

  1. Companies. Confirm each legal entity in the Registro Mercantil (Mercantile Registry), which the Colegio de Registradores describes as the main legal instrument for giving certainty to commercial dealings. Official online publicity, through the registrars' online services, can include an informative note (general company data, share capital, a list of acts published in the official mercantile gazette, filed accounts and legalised books), certifications, and the annual accounts companies have filed.[9][10] The official mercantile gazette, the Boletín Oficial del Registro Mercantil (BORME), is published by the state official-gazette agency and can be read without a register request; the latest issue on the gazette site on 10 November 2026 was that day's edition.[11]
  2. Activity. Code the list to CNAE. Latest DIRCE tables still use CNAE-2009; register and administrative records have been moving to CNAE-2025, which is aligned with NACE Rev. 2.1. Keep both codes until your sources have moved, and map them with the official correspondence, as described in our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.[7][8][6]
  3. Plants. The register shows the registered entity, not every site. Use local-unit thinking (one enterprise can have several premises) and site-level evidence such as the EU Industrial Emissions Portal. By our count of the EEA map service on 10 November 2026, that portal listed 7,591 Spanish industrial-site rows for reporting year 2024, and 7,502 for 2023; those counts can move.[4][6][16]
  4. Buying signals. Search official Spanish public contracts on the Plataforma de Contratación del Sector Público (PLACSP), and EU-threshold notices on TED with CPV codes.[12][13]

A register match confirms a legal entity. It is not, by itself, a purchasing signal.

04 / know the spanish company landscape

Know the Spanish company landscape

  • Legal forms. INE classifies DIRCE enterprises by legal condition, including public limited companies, limited liability companies and sole proprietors, among other forms.[6] In everyday use those first two are the sociedad anónima (S.A.) and the sociedad de responsabilidad limitada (S.L.). Record the operating entity and, where the group differs, the group.
  • Registered office versus plant. DIRCE assigns an enterprise to the territory of its corporate headquarters, and a local unit to the place where the activity is carried out, which may be different.[6] A Spanish manufacturer can run several plants under one registered company. Find the plants from public site evidence, not from the registered office alone.
  • Language. Register entries, DIRCE tables, tender notices and many technical pages are in Spanish. Plan keyword lists in Spanish, for example bombas, válvulas, tratamiento de aguas and mantenimiento.
  • Activity-code vintage. A CNAE-2025 code taken from a register entry will not always match a CNAE-2009 filter on the latest DIRCE table. Do not merge the two unchecked.
05 / cnae 2009 and cnae 2025 run side by side

CNAE-2009 and CNAE-2025 run side by side

CNAE is Spain's national classification of economic activities. CNAE-2025 was approved by Royal Decree 10/2025 of 14 January 2025 so that the national classification stays consistent with NACE Rev. 2.1 (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/137) and with ISIC Rev. 5. It succeeds CNAE-2009, which had been approved by Royal Decree 475/2007. The 2025 decree was published in the official gazette on 15 January 2025 and entered into force on the following day, 16 January 2025.[7][8]

The decree also updated the activity-code that has to be notified to certain state administrative registers, including Social Security identification records: parties already on those records had to notify their CNAE-2025 code before 30 June 2025, and new parties have to notify it when they are added.[7]

For official statistics, INE's 31 March 2025 note said the regulated implementation date is 1 January 2025, and that statistical operations would start publishing results on CNAE-2025 from 2026. DIRCE was listed for a 2026 publication with reference year 2025. The same note, and INE's methodology for the 1 January 2025 DIRCE release, still describe that release as coded to CNAE-2009. On 10 November 2026, INE's DIRCE latest-data and press pages still presented 1 January 2025 as the latest results, published 11 December 2025.[8][6][3]

For a list, that means:

  • Codes in older datasets, directories and your own records are probably CNAE-2009.
  • Keep both CNAE-2009 and CNAE-2025 on each row until your sources have moved, and map them with the official correspondence described in our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.
  • Do not filter one dataset on CNAE-2009 and another on CNAE-2025 and then merge them unchecked.

The method is the same one used for industrial account research in Germany, where WZ 2025 is that country's NACE Rev. 2.1 implementation.

07 / step by step

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat headline, then define it with CNAE / NACE codes. See our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide for how CNAE-2025 relates to the European classification, and the EU enterprises table for other countries.
  2. Use INE DIRCE only for market sizing. It tells you how large the Industry sector is, and that many sites sit behind a smaller number of enterprises. It does not give you the names.
  3. Resolve each prospect in the Registro Mercantil. Confirm the legal entity, the filed accounts and the identity of the operating company. Read BORME notices for published acts. Do not treat officer names as a contact list; record the function, not a person.
  4. Find the plants from public site evidence, including the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for process industries, because the registered office is not always the plant.
  5. Add a trigger only when you have one: a published BORME act, a filed-accounts change, a PLACSP or TED notice, or a new site record.
  6. Log the evidence for each account: legal entity, CNAE-2009 and CNAE-2025 codes, site evidence and the reason it fits the brief.

Fictional example: A fictional Irish maker of industrial heat exchangers targets Spanish food and drink plants. It filters the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for food and drink installations in Spain and resolves each operator in the Registro Mercantil. It finds that one fictional dairy group runs three plants under one S.L., and that the filed accounts and BORME notices belong to the same company. It records the three plants as separate accounts under one group, each with maintenance as the owning function. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

08 / common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Using only registered-office data. In Spain, as elsewhere in the EU, buying influence often sits at plant level.
  • Treating INE's Industry total as if it were Eurostat's NACE Section C manufacturing count, mixing the 1 January 2025 DIRCE stock with the 2024 Eurostat stock, or treating the DIRCE manufacturing division sum (153,167, by our count) as either of those published headlines.
  • Filtering one source on CNAE-2009 and another on CNAE-2025 and merging them unchecked.
  • Treating a register match or a tender notice as proof that a plant is buying your product.
09 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in Spain?
Eurostat counted 164,278 manufacturing enterprises in Spain in 2024, for NACE Section C.[1] INE's Central Business Register is a different series. By our count of its official CNAE-2009 manufacturing divisions (10–33), there were 153,167 manufacturing enterprises at 1 January 2025. That is narrower than INE's Industry total of 172,765, which covers sections B to E.[3][15]
What is CNAE-2025?
CNAE-2025 is Spain's national classification of economic activities, approved by Royal Decree 10/2025 of 14 January 2025 so that it stays consistent with NACE Rev. 2.1 and ISIC Rev. 5. It succeeds CNAE-2009. The decree was published on 15 January 2025 and entered into force on 16 January 2025. INE publishes the structure and an implementation calendar for official statistics.[7][8]
Should I use CNAE-2009 or CNAE-2025 codes?
Both, for now. CNAE-2025 has been in force since 16 January 2025, and some administrative registers already required CNAE-2025 codes by 30 June 2025, but the latest INE DIRCE tables checked on 10 November 2026 were still coded to CNAE-2009. Keep both codes on each row and map them with the official correspondence until your sources have moved.[7][8][6]
How do I look up a Spanish company in the commercial register?
Use the official Registro Mercantil, through the Colegio de Registradores' online publicity services or its electronic office. Those services can provide an informative note, a certification and filed annual accounts for registered companies. Published mercantile-register acts also appear in BORME, the official gazette, which can be read without a register request. Check the registrars' site for the current access terms.[9][10][11][14]
What is the Plataforma de Contratación del Sector Público?
It is Spain's official public-procurement platform, also called PLACSP. It publishes contract notices, contractor profiles and open data, and it has a public search. Some other official Spanish contracting platforms connect to it. EU-threshold notices also appear on TED. A notice is a public buying signal, not proof that a named plant will buy from you.[12][13]
Why do the INE Industry count and the Eurostat manufacturing count differ?
They are different official series and should not be subtracted from one another. Eurostat's 164,278 figure is the 2024 count of manufacturing enterprises in NACE Section C.[1] INE's 172,765 figure is the wider DIRCE Industry total (sections B to E) as at 1 January 2025. By our count of INE's manufacturing divisions 10–33, that narrower cut is 153,167.[3][15][6]
Can Spanish public sources show who buys at a plant?
No. INE's DIRCE counts Spanish enterprises and their premises, and the Registro Mercantil and BORME identify each registered company and its published acts.[3][9][11] Official PLACSP tenders and BORME acts can give buying signals, but none of these sources shows who buys at a plant.[12][11] Demand Range does not include named contacts in the default account-research scope.

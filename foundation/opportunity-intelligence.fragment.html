What the brief needs to make clear.

  • Company and facility: which entity the source refers to and how it relates to the target account.
  • Observed change: the specific project or development, with source references and dates.
  • Supplier relevance: why the change may matter to the agreed product or service category.
  • Stage and route: what is known about progress and the organisations involved; what is not established.
  • Unknowns and review: specification gaps, package status, timing and the next verification step.

Outputs, evidence supplied and review cadence are agreed for the brief. This is not a continuous live feed or a promise of complete project coverage. Role-level mapping does not include named contacts by default.

A signal is not an open equipment order.

Observed

State exactly what the source supports. For example, a fictional company statement proposes additional production space.

Inferred

The change may be worth reviewing against the supplier’s range. That is a relevance hypothesis—not proof of required equipment.

Unknown

Whether the project will proceed, which specifications apply, how procurement will run and whether any package is available.

This example is invented to explain the method. In real work, planning, construction, tender and award evidence must keep their actual meanings and dates. An award notice cannot support a claim that the same work remains unawarded.

Which package decision matters to your offer?

Before allocating a product specialist or scoping a service, ask which decision matters to your offer, and for which scope. A building-shell handover and a line not yet installed cannot tell you whether components are already ordered or a separate service responsibility has been assigned.

Consider this independently authored fictional illustration: an invented industrial production site in the Republic of Ireland, at one stipulated review point.

  • Building shell: handed over. Process-line operation and utility completion are not established.
  • Process line: the equipment order is placed, including its instrumentation, but installation has not started. No alteration or later supply order is described. The order expressly excludes site-wide commissioning coordination.
  • Utility options: assessment of alternative concepts is authorised. Investment in a selected concept is not approved; the equipment configuration has not been chosen. No decision date, implementation date or purchasing route is supplied.
  • Site-wide coordination: no separate assignment evidence is supplied. That does not establish that nobody has been appointed: responsibility could sit within another scope or a separate assignment.

For an instrumentation-component supplier

The line’s instrumentation is already committed in the placed order, despite installation being ahead. The distinct question is whether the utility scope has an unresolved equipment-selection decision. Permission to assess concepts is not approval to purchase equipment, and instrumentation requirements are not established.

A useful research request would be: “Check the utility-options decision, not a fresh line-instrumentation order. Separate concept assessment, concept selection, investment approval and any defined equipment configuration. Establish, where permitted evidence allows, whether a component-selection decision remains unresolved. If requirements, technical relevance to our components, the purchasing route or dates cannot be resolved, leave them unknown—not a projected buying window.”

For a commissioning-coordination service supplier

The exclusion from the line order defines what that order does not cover; it does not prove that the service is unallocated elsewhere. The immediate research question concerns responsibility across scopes, not whether the equipment has arrived.

A different request would be: “Check whether site-wide commissioning coordination is covered within an existing scope or separately assigned. Distinguish the responsibility’s evidenced coverage from what remains unestablished. If allocation cannot be resolved within the agreed research limits, report it as unknown, not ‘nobody appointed’. Leave service availability and timing unknown unless established. We are not enquiring about the original equipment supply.”

Neither offer has an established available order here. The company could still fit an industry-matched account brief independently of these package questions.

Irish industrial supplier research

Read Irish tenders for supplier relevance

Use this guide to decide which notice deserves further investigation, which needs clarification and which falls outside your supply scope. The unit of review is a specific notice and, where relevant, a lot—not a company labelled as a ready buyer.

Download the blank industrial tender triage worksheet (CSV)

This is an original research workflow, not registration help, bid writing, procurement advice or a list of open packages. It does not establish eligibility, engineering suitability, purchasing authority or buying intent.

Before searching notices, check the portal's purpose

A supplier-facing link is worth classifying before it becomes a route-to-market entry. Start with the task the service is described as performing, not the word “supplier” in its name. For a component seller, the important question is whether the evidence concerns administration of transactions, qualification, an invitation, or the actual scope being purchased.

The European Commission describes electronic invoicing as exchanging an electronic invoice between supplier and buyer, with structured data enabling automatic processing; its explanation was updated on 6 March 2026.[8] That gives a useful concrete example of an administrative function. It does not establish that every supplier portal is an eInvoicing service, or that any particular organisation uses one.

Our research rule is to keep the function you observe separate from the commercial conclusion you want to test. An invoice-processing description can answer “what does this service do?” without answering “can a new component supplier participate in this purchase?” Even where several functions sit in one portal, evidence for one function does not establish the others.

A five-question route-verification card

Question Record what the public evidence establishes Keep separate
What is the portal for? Its stated tasks and intended users, with a source URL Do not infer invitation access from transaction administration
Is supplier qualification described? The scope of any documented qualification process, if present A qualification process is not evidence that this supplier has qualified
Is a procurement action invited? The separately evidenced notice or invitation reference A portal link is not a current invitation or a response deadline
What is the package? The specified supply/service/works scope and any relevant lot Equipment words in a service description do not establish a component-supply package
Who decides? Only the evidenced selection, approval or ordering responsibility for that scope An administrator, invoice recipient or help function is not automatically a component selector

Attach the source publication/update date, retrieval date, page purpose, permitted-use check and next validation to the card. Use not stated for a missing source date, rather than copying today's access date into it. Leave notice, deadline and package fields empty if you have not obtained a notice. No login or registration is needed to perform this public-description check; this guide provides neither.

Fictional stop-card example

An invented procurement page for Fictional Water Organisation E links to a service described as handling invoices and existing transaction records. There is no separately supplied invitation, lot, qualification decision or component specification. The organisation, page and service are wholly fictional, not a description of an actual utility portal.

For a supplier seeking new valve-supply work, record transaction-administration purpose observed; new procurement route unresolved. Reject “the portal gives us access to valve buyers”. Hold the invitation, package and selection-authority fields. Do not infer that E has no procurement opportunities; the available observation simply answers a different question.

The minimal next check is a public, attributable procurement source that identifies a relevant notice or invitation. Set the review boundary in advance. If that check yields only the same transaction-service description, stop and preserve the unresolved route. If a specific notice is found, advance to this guide's notice-purpose and package-fit checks—not directly to a sales-ready label. A positive scope mismatch can exclude that notice from the shortlist without excluding E from a separately agreed account brief.

Turn the check into a useful enquiry

Ask for: “Research [product/service range] routes in [territory], excluding [delivery roles]. Where a supplier portal is mentioned, identify its documented purpose and look separately for [required procurement evidence]. Record unknown qualification, scope and selection responsibility rather than treating portal availability as buyer access.”

Start with the work you can supply

Write a short inclusion brief: product or service range, territory, delivery role and explicit exclusions. Separate component supply from complete-system delivery, installation and ongoing maintenance. A pump distributor and a contractor offering a complete treatment installation should not use the same scope test.

CPV, the Common Procurement Vocabulary, is a classification system describing the subject of procurement contracts.[1] Our recommendation is to pair classification with plain-language scope checks, not use a matching code as an acceptance decision.

  • Try product language such as pumps, valves, dosing equipment or instrumentation.
  • Compare activity language such as servicing, renewal, supply or installation.
  • Record the query and code as searched; check the official vocabulary rather than guessing a code.

These are exploratory prompts, not tested coverage claims. For a broader account brief, see industrial lead research; for application and route questions, see pump and flow-control prospecting.

Do these records describe the same work?

Before moving from a project update to a notice, test the connection between the records. A shared place name or equipment word is a search clue, not a verified link. This original exercise concerns record identity; the notice and package checks below still come afterwards.

Similar name, missing connection
An invented update describes wastewater works at “Demo Quay” under reference DEMO-WW-17. An invented notice mentions pumping works at Demo Quay but gives no project reference. Hold the proposed link. The notice's hypothetical supporting document instead identifies DEMO-DW-17 and expressly describes a different drinking-water scheme. Reject this record pairing, not the whole account. Keep each record separately; do not silently change either identifier to make them match.
Explicit connection, limited conclusion

That link establishes neither a current invitation nor a component-supply route. Stage, lot, obligations and equipment suitability remain separate questions.

Keep account fit separate from the disputed project link: an otherwise relevant organisation need not be discarded because this particular connection fails. Continue with the notice's actual purpose; do not turn a successful identity check into an opportunity claim.

Read the notice’s purpose before its headline

The EU’s eForms guidance distinguishes planning forms, competition forms and result forms: they concern preparation, calls for competition and procurement outcomes respectively.[2] It also includes prior information notices used as calls for competition, so “PIN” alone is not enough to classify the opportunity.[2]

Our triage rule is to retain the full notice type and check what action is actually invited. Do not turn a planning reference into a current bid invitation, or an award record into unawarded work.

Check the notice identifier, version, lot, published date, response deadline and stated time zone. Keep publication, retrieval and deadline dates separate. The EU guidance describes change notices that update published information.[2] Recheck changes before relying on an earlier deadline; record uncertainty rather than inventing one.

Test the package, not just the product word

Compare the stated deliverable with your inclusion brief. Use the following questions as research prompts, not technical acceptance criteria:

  • Is the subject equipment supply, spare parts, maintenance, installation or a combined works package?
  • Which lot contains the relevant scope? Do its obligations match the supply role you want to investigate?
  • Is a component mentioned as an item being purchased, an installed asset to service or background context?
  • Does the record establish a direct supply route, or are you assuming a contractor will buy from you?
  • Which document would resolve the remaining scope question, and can it be accessed and used for the intended purpose?

Keep duty conditions, materials, interfaces, standards and compatibility in the unknowns field until appropriately assessed. A category match is not an engineering conclusion. Do not assume subcontract access from the existence of a larger contract.

Choose investigate, hold or out of scope

These are our research labels, not official procurement statuses or bid/no-bid approval:

Investigate
The described deliverable has a defensible connection to the brief and a specific next check. Advance that check—not a claim that the package is open or your offer qualifies.
Hold
A necessary fact is missing, contradictory or inaccessible, or intended source reuse is unresolved. Name the question and evidence needed to release the hold. Missing evidence is not a positive mismatch.
Out of scope
Positive evidence contradicts a stated inclusion condition, or the record serves a different research purpose. Write the reason against this notice or lot, not against the entire organisation.

Make the decision relative to the next action. Unknown timing may permit an initial scope investigation but must block describing a notice as currently open. Keep source-use decisions separate: permission to inspect information is not automatically permission to redistribute it.

EU pump and valve notice cases: read the fields, not just the match

For pump, valve and wastewater suppliers comparing notices beyond Ireland, the first three real records show three different traps: a supply classification beside repair wording, repeated descriptions across separate lots, and values with different scopes. Each example ends with a specific document question rather than a buyer label.

Dated reading snapshots, checked 22 October 2026—not an open-opportunity list. The first three records below are competition notices published on 5, 13 and 15 October 2026 respectively, each displaying version 01.[9][10][11] We inspected these public notice versions, not the linked procurement documents or a complete subsequent-notice history. Their printed deadlines are source observations, not confirmation that a procedure remains open. Before any procurement action, verify the latest notice, changes and authoritative documents.

Netherlands: a pump category does not resolve supply versus repair

TED 683590-2026, published 5 October 2026, describes supply, repair and overhaul of pumps, submersible pumps and drive equipment; its main CPV is 42122000, Pumps.[9] Within that procedure, LOT-0001 describes supply of sewage submersible pumps and associated impellers and bottom plates, whereas LOT-0005, whose title begins “Perceel 5A”, describes repair and overhaul of sewage submersible pumps.[9] Despite that repair wording, the latter lot's structured main-nature field says Supplies.[9]

For an equipment-only brief, this is a reason to read the deliverable, not automatically accept every pump-coded lot. Preserve LOT-0005 and “Perceel 5A” as separate identifiers; do not rename it lot 5A in your record key. Keep the classification and the repair description together rather than silently correcting either. Investigate LOT-0001's specification; hold the direct component-supply interpretation of LOT-0005 until the repair obligations and parts responsibility are resolved.

Next document question: does the selected lot purchase equipment, a repair outcome, or both, and who supplies the parts? The notice lists Dutch as the submission language and prints 20/11/2026 11:00:00 (UTC+00:00) as the tender deadline for these lots.[9] Retain that source timing and the original Dutch scope alongside your working translation. An English interface is not evidence that an English submission is accepted.

Malta: repeated scope text is not a lot-level item schedule

TED 706732-2026, published 13 October 2026, concerns supply and delivery of valves, couplings and flange adaptors for the Fiddien trunk-mains upgrade.[10] It gives LOT-0001 the title “Lot 1 - Valves” and LOT-0002 “Lot 2 - Couplings and Flange Adaptors”, while both lot descriptions repeat the wider supply-and-delivery wording.[10]

A valve supplier can identify the first lot as the more specific starting point without concluding that every item mentioned in its repeated description belongs there. Keep the lot title, description and detailed schedule as distinct evidence layers. Do not allocate quantities, materials, pressure ratings or interfaces from the shared wording, and do not relabel this supply-and-delivery description as responsibility for the whole upgrade.

Next document question: which line items and technical obligations belong to the chosen lot? Check the schedule before deciding whether your range covers the package. The notice lists English for documents and submissions and prints 11/11/2026 09:30:00 (UTC+02:00) for receipt of tenders.[10] Preserve that printed offset; do not replace it with an assumed local-clock conversion. If timing matters to an action, resolve it against the authoritative procurement source rather than a research spreadsheet.

Portugal: preserve value labels before estimating relevance

TED 713010-2026, published 15 October 2026, classifies its mechanical-material procurement under 42124000, parts of pumps, compressors, engines or motors.[11] Its procedure-level estimated value before value-added tax is 0,00 EUR; LOT-0001's estimated value before value-added tax is 10 000,00 EUR; that lot also displays 182 500,00 EUR as the maximum value of the framework agreement.[11]

These are not interchangeable budget fields. Record each amount with its label and procedure-or-lot scope. Do not interpret the zero as no purchasing requirement, repair it with your own total, or present the framework maximum as equipment revenue available to one supplier. A broad parts classification also does not establish compatibility with your products.

Next document question: what does each value cover, and what item schedule and ordering terms define the selected lot? Keep the value interpretation unresolved until the documents explain it. The notice lists Portuguese for documents and submissions and prints 12/11/2026 17:00:59 (UTC+00:00) as the deadline.[11] Preserve the seconds as published; a tidied-up timestamp is not the source record.

Denmark: a changed deadline does not settle the delivery destination

TED 644250-2026, published 18 September 2026, displays version 01 and a competition change notice for monoflange valves. Its change section identifies previous notice 570328-2026 and says the buyer extended the tender deadline.[12] The printed deadline is 25/09/2026 12:00:00 (UTC+02:00).[12] That date precedes this 22 October reading: use this as a historical scope-and-change example, not an invitation to submit now. We have not established the subsequent outcome or latest procedure status.

The useful delivery question is inside LOT-0000. Its title names Køge, Herning and Frøslev, but its description says “Køge, Hjørring Nørskov og Frøslev” and refers to Appendix 3 Schedule of Prices Køge, Frøslev, Herning, document 26/12038-19.[12] The lot's place-of-performance field separately gives ML Energy, Tømmervej 6-8, Brørup, 6650.[12] These are distinct source fields, not interchangeable delivery instructions.

For a valve supplier assessing logistics, do not turn the project names into an assumed multi-site delivery obligation, replace the stated address with the buyer's office, or silently reconcile Herning with Hjørring Nørskov. Preserve the conflicting wording. A record announcing a deadline change does not, by itself, explain those destination differences. This case merits a hold on the delivery interpretation, rather than a confident freight allowance or a claim that the notice is unusable.

Next document question: does document 26/12038-19, read with the contract's delivery terms and relevant clarifications, allocate items to named installations while requiring delivery to Brørup, or specify another arrangement? Those documents were not inspected here. Resolve quantities, unloading responsibility and delivery points before pricing logistics; separately establish present procurement status before considering any response.

Germany: a reference-project threshold is not the pump duty

TED 208240-2026, published 26 March 2026, displays version 01 and a competition notice for the Rathenaustraße pumping-station plant package in Hamburg.[13] LOT-0001 is classified as Works: its German description covers pumps, pipework and valves, heating, air conditioning and ventilation, and connection to existing district-heating lines. Pumps, 42122000, is an additional CPV classification, not the whole package.[13]

The selection-criteria field asks for evidence of at least three comparable plant-engineering projects within the last ten years. Its examples of comparable projects include pipework of >=DN500 or a pumping station with capacity of at least 500 m³/h; it points to Anlage 00a Eignungskriterien – TL4.[13] Do not transfer those reference-project examples into the proposed station's equipment specification. They describe the experience evidence being requested, not a verified operating point for a pump you could supply.

For an equipment-only brief, separate two decisions: the integrated works package is outside a component-only delivery role; the pump duty remains unestablished by those reference criteria. Do not invent a component lot or assume the eventual contractor will buy from you. A business offering the wider works scope would instead need to examine the qualification evidence and delivery responsibilities together.

Next document question: which requirements in the technical specification define the actual equipment duty, and which requirements in Anlage 00a concern the applicant's past projects? The procedure is negotiated with prior publication; the printed request-to-participate deadline is 28/04/2026 12:00:00 (UTC+02:00), not a component quotation deadline.[13] This is another historical reading snapshot, not a current access route. German scope and selection-criterion summaries above are our working translations; no technical specification or subsequent result has been verified.

Return a lot decision, not a new-buyer label

In the existing triage worksheet, keep one notice/lot review per copy. Attach the exact notice version, field-level observation, original-language wording where needed, relevance reasoning and next document question. Keep document-access and reuse checks separate from whether a portal URL appears. These examples establish neither eligibility nor component suitability, purchasing authority, country coverage or access to a buyer.

Four fictional notice-triage scenarios

All scenarios and evidence descriptions below are invented teaching examples, not real notices, anonymised prospects or quotations. Notice URLs, publication dates, retrieval dates and deadlines are deliberately left blank; no current availability is established.

Scenario A: a defined equipment-supply lot

The invented scope expressly asks for valve supply, without installation. A component-only supplier’s brief includes that delivery role. Investigate: establish the lot’s detailed requirements and current status. Specifications, eligibility and timing remain unresolved; this is not permission to bid.

Scenario B: maintenance with unclear parts responsibility

The invented description concerns pump maintenance but does not say whether spare components are purchased separately or provided within the service. Hold: clarify the parts-supply boundary before treating it as component demand. The word “pump” does not resolve that question.

Scenario C: complete installation outside the brief

The invented package explicitly requires design, installation and commissioning as one delivery scope. The supplier’s brief explicitly excludes that role. Out of scope for direct pursuit under this brief. Do not invent a separate component lot or an accessible subcontracting route.

Scenario D: a result mistaken for a fresh invitation

The invented record expressly reports an award, while the research brief seeks current invitations. Out of scope for that shortlist. It could prompt a separately scoped background question, but it supplies no evidence of another available package.

Use the blank worksheet without manufacturing certainty

The CSV is a single-record field,value template. Make a separate copy for each notice/lot review. Every value starts empty, including the decision. Enter dates and URLs only when verified; leave unknowns blank and explain the gap in the relevant unknowns field.

  1. Define the brief, supply role and exclusions before recording a match.
  2. Keep the source observation, your relevance reasoning and unresolved questions distinct.
  3. Record the applicable source/item terms and permitted use before sharing source-derived material.
  4. Assign a next verification step and a reviewer role; choose a review date yourself.

The file contains no formulas or automation. Import values as text; do not enable formula interpretation for pasted source content. It does not schedule checks or collect named contacts.

How fast manufacturing enterprise lists change, by EU country (2024 and 2023 data)

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

In 2024 the EU-27 manufacturing official churn rate was 15.25% (provisional), from a birth rate of 7.54% and a provisional death rate of 7.71%.[1] Ireland's official 2024 rates were a 9.43% birth rate, a 14.34% death rate (provisional, not final) and an official churn rate of 23.78% (provisional).[1] In 2023, the latest year whose EU-27 death and official churn rates are not flagged provisional, the EU-27 official churn rate was 14.44%, from a 7.65% birth rate and a 6.78% death rate. Ireland's official 2023 churn rate was 19.62% (provisional).[1]

These figures come from Eurostat's business demography dataset bd_size, for NACE Rev. 2 Section C (manufacturing), size class TOTAL and age TOTAL. Eurostat last updated the dataset on 23 October 2026. The tables and CSV were extracted from the live Eurostat API and checked against the Research Analyst country table on 10 November 2026. This page is a companion to the 2024 stock of manufacturing enterprises by country, which uses a different official series; do not mix the two stocks.

Key facts (Eurostat business demography, 2024 and 2023 data, dataset updated 23 October 2026, extracted 10 November 2026)

  • In 2024 the EU-27 manufacturing birth rate was 7.54%, the death rate was 7.71% (provisional) and the official business churn rate was 15.25% (provisional). 2024 is the latest year with a complete EU country set. Every 2024 death rate and official churn rate in the country table is flagged provisional (p), estimated and provisional (ep), or break and provisional (bp).[1]
  • Ireland's 2024 official rates were a 9.43% birth rate, a 14.34% death rate (provisional) and an official churn rate of 23.78% (provisional). The 14.34% death rate is not a final figure. The Eurostat active-enterprise stock for Ireland was 19,617 in 2024, compared with 19,416 in 2023, so that provisional death rate is not a measure of how the stock changed.[1]
  • In 2023, the latest year whose EU-27 death and official churn rates are unflagged, the EU-27 birth rate was 7.65%, the death rate was 6.78% and official churn was 14.44%. Ireland's 2023 official rates were 10.08%, 9.54% (provisional) and 19.62% (provisional).[1]
  • In 2022 the EU-27 official rates were 7.67%, 6.62% and 14.29%, and Ireland's were 11.11%, 7.85% and 18.96%, all unflagged.[1]
  • By our count of the official 2024 EU member-country cells, Lithuania had the highest official churn rate, at 25.47% (estimated and provisional), then Estonia at 24.67% (provisional) and Ireland at 23.78% (provisional). Cyprus had the lowest, at 7.59% (provisional).[1]
  • Official churn is Eurostat's published ENT_BRTHR_DTHR_PC cell, not a Demand Range sum of the rounded birth and death rates. For the EU-27 in 2023, 7.65 plus 6.78 is 14.43 by our count of those rounded rates, while the official cell is 14.44%.[1][5]
  • The UK is not in the dataset. The active-enterprise counts in the tables are the bd_size stock and will not match the 2024 structural business statistics stock on the enterprises-by-country page.[1]

03 / what the rates mean and what they do not

What the rates mean, and what they do not

Eurostat's birth rate is enterprise births divided by active enterprises, as a percentage. The death rate is enterprise deaths divided by active enterprises. Business churn is the official indicator Eurostat labels "birth and death rate" for that same population. It is a published official cell (ENT_BRTHR_DTHR_PC), not a Demand Range sum of the rounded rates.[1][5] The 2025 business-demography manual defines that rate as enterprise births and enterprise deaths in the year, divided by active enterprises in the year. Because the published birth and death rates are rounded, adding them can differ from the official cell by 0.01 percentage points. Use the official cell.[5]

Eurostat treats a death as confirmed only if the enterprise is not reactivated within two years, so for the latest year only preliminary death data are normally available. That is why every 2024 death rate and official churn rate in the table carries a provisional flag, sometimes with an estimate or a break, and why Ireland's 14.34% death rate must not be read as a final figure.[4]

An enterprise is the statistical unit in the table: broadly, a business with some autonomy over its decisions. It is not a plant. A manufacturer with several factories in one country still counts as one enterprise there. A birth is an enterprise entering the active population, and a death is an enterprise leaving it. Neither fact, on its own, tells you that a factory opened or closed, or that a named site still operates. Births and deaths also exclude mergers, break-ups and other non-birth or non-death entries and exits, so the change in the active stock is not births minus deaths.[4][5]

For a supplier who keeps a list of manufacturing accounts, the practical reading is:

  • Official churn describes the population, not your rows. A 23.78% (provisional) official churn rate in Ireland in 2024 does not mean that 23.78% of the companies on a particular list changed, and it is not a measure of how fast contact details go out of date.
  • A provisional death rate is not a final fact, and it is not the stock change. Ireland's 14.34% (provisional) 2024 death rate sits beside an official active-enterprise count that rose from 19,416 in 2023 to 19,617 in 2024.[1]
  • Country rates differ enough to matter. An EU-27 official churn rate of 15.25% (provisional) in 2024, or 14.44% in 2023, sits well below some countries and well above others. Plan refresh work country by country, and read the flags before you treat a gap as a real difference.
  • The stock on this page is a different official count from the 2024 enterprise totals on our manufacturing enterprises by country page. Use this page for flow (births, deaths and official churn) and that page for the structural business statistics stock, size class and local-unit figures.

For how often to refresh a list you already hold, see industrial prospect-list maintenance. That guide is about cadence and method. This page supplies the official enterprise-population rates that guide can now point to.

04 / ireland the uk and the two official irish series

Ireland, the UK and the two official Irish series

The Irish cells in the tables are Eurostat's. Ireland's 2024 death rate of 14.34% and official churn rate of 23.78% are provisional Eurostat figures, not final, and they are not CSO cells. Ireland's Central Statistics Office publishes its own manufacturing birth and death counts as a national official series. Those CSO counts are not copied into the Eurostat columns here. For how to research Irish manufacturers, including CSO business-demography context, see our Irish manufacturing account-research guide. If you quote an Irish figure, name the source and the year; do not place a CSO cell in a Eurostat column.

The UK is not in the dataset. Eurostat's EU-27 business demography table used here does not publish the United Kingdom. For UK manufacturers, start from national official sources; our UK manufacturing account-research guide covers how to build a UK list from those sources. Demand Range researches accounts across Ireland, the UK and the EU; this particular table covers the EU-27 only.

05 / how to use the table

How to use the table

  • ☐ Use 2024 as the latest year, and keep every provisional, estimated or break flag with the number. Do not present a 2024 death rate or official churn rate, including Ireland's 14.34%, as final.
  • ☐ Use 2023 to compare, especially where you need the latest unflagged EU-27 death and official churn rates.
  • ☐ Compare countries on the same official indicator and year. Do not add a birth rate to a death rate yourself when Eurostat already publishes the official churn cell, and do not mix these stocks with the 2024 structural business statistics counts.
  • ☐ Treat high official churn as a reason to refresh a country list more often, not as evidence about a named plant or a named buyer, and not as a contact-data ageing rate.
  • ☐ For Ireland, keep Eurostat and CSO figures in separately labelled places.
  • ☐ Quote the source, the dataset (bd_size), the year, the filters and the extraction date of 10 November 2026.
  • ☐ Turn a shortlist of countries into a site-level brief. Our markets page shows where Demand Range researches accounts, and the industrial lead research page explains the method.
06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How fast do manufacturing enterprise lists change in the EU?

In 2024 the EU-27 official churn rate was 15.25% (provisional), from a birth rate of 7.54% and a provisional death rate of 7.71%.[1] Those 2024 death and churn rates are not final.[1] In 2023, the latest year whose EU-27 death and official churn rates are unflagged, official churn was 14.44%, from a 7.65% birth rate and a 6.78% death rate.[1]

What is the manufacturing enterprise churn rate in Ireland?

Eurostat's 2024 official churn rate for Ireland is 23.78% (provisional), from a 9.43% birth rate and a provisional death rate of 14.34%.[1] That 14.34% is provisional, not final.[1] In 2023 official churn was 19.62% (provisional).[1] Ireland's Central Statistics Office publishes a separate national birth and death series, which is not mixed into this table. See our Irish manufacturing account-research guide.

Which EU country has the highest manufacturing enterprise churn?

By our count of the official 2024 EU member-country cells, Lithuania has the highest official churn rate, at 25.47% (estimated and provisional), ahead of Estonia at 24.67% (provisional) and Ireland at 23.78% (provisional).[1] Cyprus has the lowest, at 7.59% (provisional).[1] Every 2024 country death rate and official churn rate is flagged provisional, or estimated or break and provisional.[1]

What do birth, death and churn rates mean?

Eurostat's birth rate is enterprise births divided by active enterprises, and the death rate is enterprise deaths divided by that stock.[1] Official churn is the published cell for that population, not a sum of the rounded rates.[1][5] An enterprise is a statistical unit, not a plant, and the rates do not name the firms that entered or left.

Why do these figures differ from the manufacturing enterprises by country page?

The two pages use different official Eurostat datasets. This page uses business demography (bd_size) for 2024 and 2023 birth, death and official churn rates. The manufacturing enterprises by country page uses structural business statistics for the 2024 stock of enterprises. The EU-27 stock figures in the two series are not the same and should not be substituted for one another.[1]

Is the UK in this table?

No. The UK is not published in Eurostat's EU-27 manufacturing business demography table used here.[1] For UK manufacturers, use national official sources and our UK manufacturing account-research guide. Demand Range researches accounts across Ireland, the UK and the EU; this dataset covers the EU-27 only.

How to research medical-device manufacturers in Ireland, the UK and the EU

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

If you are building a list of medical-device manufacturing accounts, start from the official actor and facility registers, then confirm the plant. A public EUDAMED registration, or a national device registration, confirms a legal manufacturer or another economic operator. It does not, by itself, confirm an operating site, a production line or a purchase. Cross-check the legal entity against a facility register, an environmental licence and an official enterprise count before you treat the name as a plant to call on.

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns medical-device manufacturer and facility research from official sources. Medicines stay on our Irish pharmaceutical manufacturing account-research guide, which uses HPRA manufacturing authorisations and EudraGMDP. Device NACE classes, and the move to NACE Rev. 2.1, stay on our NACE Rev. 2.1 industrial account lists page. This page names only legal entities as they appear in public official sources. It does not collect or publish personal contact data.

01 / key facts

Key facts

EUDAMED, HPRA, MHRA and Eurostat; checked 10 November 2026.

  • The European Commission's actor-registration page states that Commission Decision (EU) 2025/2371 declared the EUDAMED Actor module functional and that, "as from 28 May 2026 the Actor module is mandatory to use." The same page says the module "is used voluntarily since December 2020" and that "The first four modules of EUDAMED became mandatory since 28 May 2026".[1]
  • "The Actor ID/SRN is a EU-wide unique identification for economic operators in the medical devices sector." "Every economic operator (EU and non-EU manufacturers, authorised representatives, system/procedure pack producers and importers) must register as an actor in EUDAMED before placing devices or Systems/Procedure Packs in the EU market." Non-EU manufacturers "must have an active authorised representative".[1]
  • "Once an Economic Operator has been validated by the relevant Competent Authority and its Actor ID / SRN is issued, its details (except those only for the Competent Authorities) become visible to all users in EUDAMED and will be publicly available on the EUDAMED public website." The public search shows limited information compared with a logged-in view. We have not published an own-search manufacturer total, because the public site does not provide an official aggregate count by country.[2][3]
  • The HPRA, Ireland's competent authority for devices, publishes separate guidance for manufacturer registration (manufacturers established in Ireland, non-EU manufacturers, and custom-made device manufacturers) and for manufacturing facilities. "Manufacturing facilities established in Ireland must register their organisation details and device types nationally with us using our online registration form." The HPRA also validates EUDAMED registration applications for Irish-based authorised representatives and their non-EU manufacturers, and for system and procedure pack producers.[4][5][6]
  • A manufacturing facility, in the HPRA's wording, "is a place where an entity, which does not place devices on the market under its own name or under its own trademark, manufactures a device, manufactures one or more critical components of a device to a set of specifications, conducts packaging activities or labelling activities in relation to a device." That is a site role, not the same thing as a legal manufacturer.[6]
  • For Great Britain, the MHRA states that "All medical devices, including in vitro diagnostic devices (IVDs), custom-made devices and systems or procedure packs, must be registered with the MHRA before they can be placed on the market in Great Britain (England, Wales and Scotland)." "Registration of your devices with the MHRA (the UK Competent Authority) does not represent any form of accreditation, certification, approval or endorsement by the MHRA." "Registration requirements differ for Northern Ireland." A manufacturer based outside the UK "must appoint a single UK Responsible Person".[7][8]
  • Eurostat structural business statistics, dataset sbs_ovw_act, updated 30 September 2026 at 11:00 UTC+2 and extracted on 10 November 2026, publish enterprise counts for the two NACE Rev. 2 device classes. For class 26.60, "Manufacture of irradiation, electromedical and electrotherapeutic equipment", the EU-27 had 1,791 enterprises in 2023 and 1,771 in 2024, and Ireland had 23 in 2024 (the 2023 Irish cell is flagged confidential in this extract). For class 32.50, "Manufacture of medical and dental instruments and supplies", the EU-27 had 63,227 enterprises in 2023 and 62,000 in 2024 (Eurostat estimated), and Ireland had 377 in 2023 (the 2024 Irish cell is flagged confidential in this extract). These are enterprise counts, not plant counts, and they are not a named list.[9]
  • CSO Business Demography table BRA31 (updated 6 October 2026; years 2019 to 2024) publishes Irish manufacturing at Section C, at C26, and at a combined C31 and C32 row. It does not publish classes 26.60 or 32.50 separately, so it cannot be used as a class-level Irish device count.[10]
02 / start with the official actor register then find the site

Start with the official actor register, then find the site

  1. Search the public EUDAMED economic-operator screen by name, country or Actor ID/SRN, and by the actor role you actually need (manufacturer, authorised representative, importer, or system/procedure pack producer). Record the SRN, the role, the country and the date you searched. The Commission help page also describes a bulk XML download for logged-in users; the public information is limited.[2][3]
  2. Treat the SRN as evidence of a validated legal actor, not of a factory. A non-EU manufacturer will often appear with an EU authorised representative. That representative is not the plant.
  3. For Ireland, use the HPRA manufacturer-registration guidance for legal manufacturers and the HPRA manufacturing-facility guidance for Irish sites that manufacture, pack or label without placing the device on the market under their own name. The two registers answer different questions and both may be needed for one account.[4][5][6]
  4. For Great Britain, use MHRA device registration as evidence that a device may be placed on that market, and note the UK Responsible Person where the manufacturer is based outside the UK. For Northern Ireland, follow the MHRA's separate Northern Ireland rules; do not assume the Great Britain registration is enough.[7][8]
  5. Confirm the operating site from a second official source. In Ireland, that can be the HPRA facility registration, an EPA licence record, or both. An EPA licence confirms a regulated activity at a named site; it does not confirm that the site makes medical devices unless the licence or decision document says so. The method for Irish environmental registers is on our environmental-register guide.[6][11]
  6. Use the Eurostat class counts, and the CSO section or division counts, only to size the pool. They will not name the companies. For the class list and the NACE Rev. 2.1 mapping, use NACE Rev. 2.1 industrial account lists. For the Irish manufacturing method more generally, see Irish manufacturing account research and Irish manufacturing NACE codes. For UK codes, see UK SIC codes for manufacturing (SIC 2007 classes 26.60 and 32.50).

Do not take personal names or personal contact details from these sources, and do not treat a registration as permission to contact anyone.

These two official NACE Rev. 2 classes do not include medical or dental practice (NACE Q86.2), wholesale or retail of devices, or repair. A company counted here is an enterprise classified to that class, not necessarily a plant that would hold an HPRA facility registration or an EPA licence. Ireland's 2024 count of 23 enterprises in 26.60 and its 2023 count of 377 in 32.50 are scale guides only.

CSO BRA31 can be used as a wider Irish manufacturing context, but not as a substitute for those class cells. We have not taken a combined C31 and C32 figure as a medical-device total.[10]

04 / what these sources do not show

What these sources do not show

An EUDAMED or MHRA registration is not a site list, a capacity figure, a product-by-product plant map, or a buying signal. The HPRA facility definition is limited to entities that do not place devices on the market under their own name or trademark; a legal manufacturer may still operate a plant that is evidenced somewhere else, including in an EPA licence. Eurostat and CSO counts are classified enterprises, often at a registered address, and they can include very small businesses.

Medicines manufacturing is a different legal track. If your research is about HPRA medicine authorisations or EudraGMDP, use the Irish pharmaceutical manufacturing account-research guide and do not mix those totals with the device figures on this page.

05 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Does an EUDAMED registration show where a device is manufactured?
No. An Actor ID, or SRN, identifies a validated economic operator, such as a manufacturer or an authorised representative, after the relevant competent authority has issued it. It is evidence of a legal actor, not of an operating plant. For an Irish site, cross-check against the HPRA manufacturing-facility register and, where relevant, an EPA licence.
How do I search EUDAMED for manufacturers by country?
Open the public EUDAMED economic-operator search, and filter by country and by the actor role you need, for example manufacturer. Record the SRN, the role and the date of the search. The public view is limited, and the Commission does not publish an official running total of manufacturers by country on that screen. We have not invented one.
Which NACE classes cover medical-device manufacturing?
The two official NACE Rev. 2 manufacturing classes used on this page are 26.60, manufacture of irradiation, electromedical and electrotherapeutic equipment, and 32.50, manufacture of medical and dental instruments and supplies. Eurostat published 1,771 EU-27 enterprises in 26.60 in 2024, and 63,227 in 32.50 in 2023. Map those classes to NACE Rev. 2.1 on our NACE page.
How are medical devices registered for the Great Britain market?
The MHRA requires all medical devices, including IVDs, custom-made devices and systems or procedure packs, to be registered with it before they are placed on the market in Great Britain. That registration is not accreditation or approval. The rules differ for Northern Ireland, and a manufacturer based outside the UK must appoint a single UK Responsible Person.
Do Irish medical-device manufacturing facilities have a separate register?
Yes. The HPRA requires manufacturing facilities established in Ireland to register their organisation details and device types nationally. A manufacturing facility is a place where an entity that does not place devices on the market under its own name or trademark manufactures, packs or labels a device or a critical component. That is separate from manufacturer registration in EUDAMED.
Can I use a CSO or Eurostat count as a list of plants?
No. Eurostat's class counts, and CSO's section or division counts, measure enterprises, not named sites. CSO BRA31 does not even publish classes 26.60 or 32.50 separately. Use the counts only to size the pool, then build the named list from the actor, facility and licence registers described above.

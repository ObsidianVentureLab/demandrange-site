Industrial account prioritisation: score fit, evidence and timing

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

Once accounts have passed your fit screen, you still have to choose which to work first. A short weighted score for fit, evidence and timing makes that choice visible and repeatable. The score orders accepted and held accounts. It never turns an excluded account into a target, and it does not measure willingness to buy.

This guide applies to account lists for Ireland, the UK and the EU. It starts after the industrial ICP screen, where each account has a fit decision. The weights and the worked example are illustrative and fictional. They are not a tested model, a Demand Range standard or observed results.

01 / the scoring table fit evidence and timing

The scoring table: fit, evidence and timing

Agree the weights before anyone scores an account. The table below is an illustrative starting point for a process-equipment supplier. Change the weights to suit your offer, then keep them fixed for the whole list.

Factor Weight Score 2 Score 1 Score 0
Gate: fit decision Gate Accepted or held: score the account — Excluded or rejected: stop, do not score
Fit 50 ICP points 90 to 100 ICP points 75 to 89 Held (ICP points 50 to 74)
Evidence 30 Every scored fit field has a named source checked in the last 12 months Every field has a source, but one is older than 12 months or only covers the group At least one field is not checked, or two sources conflict
Timing 20 A dated, site-level public signal relevant to the brief, published in the last 12 months or scheduled in the next 12 A signal that is undated or only at group level No signal found in the bounded review

Priority points are the weight multiplied by the score, divided by 2, added across the three factors. The maximum is 100. An illustrative decision rule:

  • 70 or more: work first.
  • 40 to 69: next pass, after the weakest factor is checked.
  • Under 40: park the account and fix its weakest field before it is scored again.

The fit bands use the points from the ICP weight table, so the two pages work together. Fit carries the most weight on purpose: a strong timing signal on a weak-fit account should not jump the queue. Record "not checked" separately from a score of 0, as on the ICP page.

02 / what counts as a timing signal

What counts as a timing signal

A timing signal is a dated public record that ties a relevant change to a site. Examples:

  • an energy-audit Action Plan item published in an annual report (see energy-audit signals for industrial suppliers)
  • a planning application or grant of permission for the site
  • a licence review or a new licence application on an environmental register (see Irish environmental register evidence)
  • a public tender notice from the account (see Irish eTenders for industrial suppliers)

Record the date, the site and the stage the source supports. A sustainability target, a press interview or an undated plan scores 1 at most. A score of 0 means no signal was found in the review you did. It does not mean no project exists. Timing is never evidence of a purchase. See Opportunity Intelligence when the project itself is the question.

03 / worked example five fictional accounts scored

Worked example: five fictional accounts scored

Fictional example. A fictional maker of hygienic valves wants food-and-beverage manufacturers with production sites in the Republic of Ireland. It uses the ICP weights and the table above. All five companies, their scores and their sources are invented. None is a real account.

Fictional company Fit decision Fit Evidence Timing Points Decision
Company P-A: dairy ingredients maker Accepted, ICP 100 2 (50) 2 (30) 0 (0) 80 Work first
Company P-B: brewery Accepted, ICP 85 1 (25) 2 (30) 2 (20) 75 Work first
Company P-C: ready-meals maker Accepted, ICP 90 2 (50) 1 (15) 0 (0) 65 Next pass
Company P-D: chilled-food distributor Held, ICP 55 0 (0) 0 (0) 2 (20) 20 Park
Company P-E: infant-formula plant Excluded — — — — Not scored

How to read it:

  • P-A ranks first with no timing signal at all. Strong, current fit evidence is enough. A project is not required for account research.
  • P-B has a fictional planning application for a new brewhouse at the site, dated three months ago. That earns timing 2, but its weaker fit keeps it below P-A.
  • P-C fits well, but its site-activity source is 14 months old. The next action is to refresh that source, not to contact anyone.
  • P-D has a dated tender notice, but it is held: its site activity was never checked. Timing cannot rescue it. Check the activity first.
  • P-E would score highly, but the brief excludes the existing customer's group companies, so it is never scored.

These companies and figures are fictional and only illustrate the method.

04 / turn the score into research actions

Turn the score into research actions

The score says which accounts matter most. A short queue says what to check next with the time you have. In the fictional pass there are 60 analyst minutes, with 10 reserved for review and recording, leaving 50 for actions. Each estimate includes writing the evidence note.

Action Account (points) Next research action Estimate State
Q01 P-B (75) Confirm the planning application names this company's site, not a group company 20 minutes Selected
Q02 P-C (65) Find a current source for the site activity, then rescore evidence 15 minutes Selected
Q03 P-D (20) Check whether production happens at the site at all 25 minutes Deferred: does not fit the 15 minutes left
Q04 P-A (80) None needed this pass: the account is ready for the account plan — No action

Q01 and Q02 use 35 of the 50 action minutes. Q03 is deferred for capacity and kept for the next pass. There is no need to fill the spare 15 minutes. A high score does not always need research: P-A's best next step is to hand it on, not to keep checking it.

05 / rescore when evidence arrives

Rescore when evidence arrives

Suppose Q02 finds a current, fictional EPA licence record that confirms P-C's production activity. Evidence moves to 2 and P-C rises to 80: work first.

Suppose Q01 finds the fictional planning application was made by P-B's parent company for a different site. It is a group-level signal, so timing drops to 1 and P-B falls to 65: next pass. Record "not attributable to this site in the checked source". That does not mean P-B has no project.

If a new source contradicts a fit field, reopen the fit decision on the ICP page before you rescore. New evidence changes the claim first, then the score. Keep each queue version, so a later reorder does not hide the earlier reason. If a changed fact reopens many accounts, use the maintenance guide.

06 / what the score is for and what it is not

What the score is for, and what it is not

  • It orders work. It does not approve an account, and it cannot supply a missing mandatory fact or reverse an exclusion.
  • It is explainable. Every point traces to a weight, a band and a dated source. Do not add decimal scores, revenue guesses or equipment-fit claims to break a tie. Break a genuine tie openly, for example by account ID, and revisit it next pass.
  • It is local. Agree the weights and bands with the person who owns the brief. If they change their priorities, record the new version and rescore. Do not present a changed preference as new company evidence.
  • It is not a readiness label. "Work first" is a research and planning decision. It does not mean the company is buying.
07 / use the blank research action queue

Use the blank research-action queue

The header-only CSV download contains zero data rows: no fictional cases, formulas or automatic approvals. Add one row per proposed action per queue version. Reuse an account ID across its actions; action rows are not company counts. The file has no score columns.

Record the three factor scores and the total in allocation_reason, or add your own columns. Import it as UTF-8 comma-separated text, keep identifiers as text, use internal role labels rather than personal contact details, and keep confidential exclusions and restricted source content out of the file.

Column guide

Group Columns What to record
Queue identity queue_version, action_id, account_id, brief_version The queue snapshot, a stable action ID, the existing account record and the fit rules in force
Prior screen fit_decision, fit_decision_ref, mandatory_gap The recorded ICP decision and its evidence, and every open mandatory issue, or "none". A queue selection never changes these fields
Claim and uncertainty claim_question, claim_evidence_refs, uncertainty_note The exact claim being checked, the evidence behind it, and whether information is missing, conflicting or out of scope
Importance buyer_importance_reason, buyer_instruction_ref Which decision the action serves and the instruction behind it. If unknown, say so
Action boundary next_research_action, action_authorisation_ref, dependency_or_blocker, stop_condition, owner_role What will be done, who allowed it, what must happen first, when to stop and which internal role owns it
Capacity effort_minutes_estimate, estimate_basis, pass_action_capacity_minutes A scoped estimate, or empty with a reason. Unknown effort is not zero. The capacity is the same on every row of a queue version
Allocation queue_state, allocation_reason, revisit_trigger selected, deferred, blocked or completed; the scores and the reason; what would reopen the action
After the check result_evidence_refs, outcome_and_remaining_gaps, reviewed_at The outcome evidence, what is still open, and the actual review date. Leave reviewed_at empty for a proposed action

Before using a completed queue, check that selected actions are authorised and fit the capacity, and explain any ties and spare time. A tidy file cannot replace reading the evidence.

08 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What should industrial account prioritisation be based on?

On fit first, then the quality of the evidence, then timing. In this method, fit carries 50 of the 100 points, evidence 30 and timing 20. Those weights are an illustrative choice. Agree your own before you score.

Should a held account be scored?

Yes, but it scores 0 for fit, so it can reach at most 50 points and never the work-first band. Its next action is to check its weakest field. Excluded and rejected accounts are never scored.

Does a high timing score mean the company is about to buy?

No. Timing records a dated public signal at the site. It says nothing about budget, specification or supplier choice.

How often should accounts be rescored?

Whenever evidence changes a factor, and at the start of each research pass. Keep the old version so the reason for any reorder stays visible.

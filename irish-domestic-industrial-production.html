What does Irish industry make at home?

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

The Central Statistics Office (CSO) publishes an experimental domestic industrial production index that measures the volume of goods produced in Ireland and excludes contract manufacturing and outsourcing done abroad. In Q2 2026, domestic manufacturing production was 3.9% lower than in Q2 2025, after a 31.8% rise in 2025 as a whole. It was 54.7% higher than in Q1 2026. These are unadjusted volume figures, base year 2021=100, from a CSO Frontier Series release.[1][2]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one thing: CSO's experimental domestic industrial production index, compared with the headline Industrial Production Index, which includes contract manufacturing abroad. For Eurostat's industrial production index by EU country, which is a different series, see our EU industrial production index by country page. For company-level research in Ireland, see Irish manufacturing account research.

01 / key facts

Key facts

CSO Domestic Industrial Production, Quarter 2 2026 Frontier Series, and CSO tables DIPQ01, DIPM01 and MIM05; checked 24 November 2026.

  • The domestic index is experimental (CSO Frontier Series). It is an unadjusted volume measure, 2021=100, for manufacturing (NACE 10–33). It excludes outsourcing and contract manufacturing done abroad.[1][3]
  • Domestic manufacturing production rose 31.8% in 2025, to 136.2, after a 5.8% rise in 2024 and a 3.3% fall in 2023.[1]
  • By our count, from CSO's unadjusted headline manufacturing production index (MIM05), the 12-month average rose 18.6% in 2025, from 103.8 to 123.0. Domestic manufacturing therefore grew faster than the headline that year.[1][5]
  • In Q2 2026, the domestic manufacturing index was 137.5: 54.7% higher than in Q1 2026 (88.9) and 3.9% lower than in Q2 2025 (143.1, the peak quarter in the published series, by our count).[1][2]
  • In June 2026, domestic manufacturing production was 4.0% higher than in May and 17.3% higher than in June 2025. The June 2026 index was 158.5; the monthly peak in the published series is 172.0 in May 2025, by our count.[1][2]
  • As of 24 November 2026, the latest domestic release is Q2 2026 (published 7 October 2026). CSO had not published a Q3 2026 domestic release. The headline Industrial Production Index has later months, through September 2026; those later months are not part of this comparison.[1][5]
02 / what the domestic production index measures

What the domestic production index measures

The domestic series is a CSO Frontier Series Output. Particular care is needed: it may use new methods that are still being developed, or data sources that may be incomplete.[1][3]

CSO's background notes describe it as tracing "the quantitative volume of production (i.e. with the effect of price changes excluded) each month of goods produced in the state only, and purposely excludes all outsourcing and contract manufacturing production occurring abroad."[3]

The headline Industrial Production and Turnover index follows international recommendations that include outsourced production in an enterprise's Irish production, even when the goods were made abroad. That activity has been a large part of Irish industrial production since 2015, and it can make the headline index move sharply from one period to the next. The domestic index was developed to show production carried out in Ireland only. CSO launched it on 8 April 2025 and now publishes it each quarter. It should be read alongside the monthly Industrial Production and Turnover release, not instead of it.[1][3][4]

Both series on this page are unadjusted volume indices, 2021=100, for manufacturing (NACE 10–33). Growth rates can be compared. The index levels are not a share of the same total, and they are not a measure of how much Irish production is done abroad. CSO does not publish that share, and this page does not estimate one.[1][2][5]

The survey covers the larger local units, in general those with 20 or more persons engaged in NACE divisions 5 to 35 in the 2021 base year. Sub-indices are combined using a proportion of 2021 gross value added, and the series is deflated with the Wholesale Price Index (2021=100). The published domestic tables give only manufacturing (NACE 10–33) and all industries (NACE 05–35). There is no sector split and no seasonally adjusted domestic series.[1][3]

In September 2025, CSO updated the base weights and revised earlier figures in this series. The figures below are from that revised index.[6]

Q1 2026 was the weak quarter: the domestic index fell 32.5% from Q4 2025 and was 33.3% lower than in Q1 2025. Q2 2026 then rose 54.7% on the quarter, but remained 3.9% lower than the series peak quarter of Q2 2025 (143.1, by our count).[1][2]

By our count, from the official quarterly cells, the first-half 2026 average of the domestic manufacturing index was 18.1% lower than in the first half of 2025 (113.2 against 138.2). From CSO table MIM05, last updated 9 November 2026, the first-half 2026 average of the headline manufacturing index was 11.0% lower than a year earlier (104.2 against 117.1), by our count. Both measures fell; the domestic fall was steeper, mostly because of Q1.[1][2][5]

05 / the latest months april to june 2026

The latest months: April to June 2026

CSO's Key Findings for the Q2 2026 release are the monthly as well as the quarterly rates. Domestic manufacturing production increased 4.0% in the month to June 2026 and was 17.3% higher than in June 2025. The official June 2026 index is 158.5, after 152.4 in May 2026 and 101.6 in April 2026. The monthly peak in the published domestic series is 172.0 in May 2025, by our count.[1][2]

Table MIM05, last updated 9 November 2026, also has later months through September 2026. This page does not add July to September 2026 to the domestic comparison, which ends in June 2026.[5]

06 / how to read the two series

How to read the two series

A rise in the domestic index means the volume of manufacturing production carried out in Ireland is higher than in the comparison period. A rise in the headline index can also reflect contract manufacturing or outsourcing attributed to Ireland but carried out abroad. When the two growth rates diverge, the headline series is not a clean guide to activity in Ireland.[1][3]

The figures still have limits for anyone selling to manufacturers:

  • They are national totals for manufacturing as a whole, with no sector split in the domestic tables.[2]
  • They are unadjusted, so a sharp quarter-to-quarter change is not, on its own, a seasonally adjusted turning point.[1]
  • They do not identify individual manufacturers, sites or buying needs, and they do not give a euro value of output or of purchases.[1][3]
  • CSO itself asks for particular care, because this is an experimental Frontier Series.[1]

For the Eurostat headline index by country, including Ireland, use the EU industrial production index by country page. That series is not the CSO domestic index, and the two should not be mixed in one comparison.

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What is the Irish domestic industrial production index?
The Irish domestic industrial production index is an experimental CSO Frontier Series. It measures the volume of manufacturing production carried out in Ireland, with price changes removed, as an unadjusted index, 2021=100. It excludes outsourcing and contract manufacturing done abroad. CSO publishes it quarterly and says particular care is needed, because the methods may still be developing.
How does it differ from the headline industrial production index?
The headline Industrial Production Index includes contract manufacturing and outsourcing attributed to Ireland, even when the goods were made abroad. The domestic index keeps only production carried out in Ireland. Both are unadjusted volume indices, 2021=100, for manufacturing (NACE 10–33). Compare growth rates; the index levels are not a share of one total.
Did Irish domestic manufacturing production rise in 2025?
Yes. CSO's experimental domestic manufacturing index rose 31.8% in 2025, to 136.2 (2021=100). That followed a 5.8% rise in 2024 and a 3.3% fall in 2023. The headline manufacturing index rose 18.6% in 2025, by our count from CSO monthly cells. The two levels are not a split of the same total.
What happened to domestic production in the first half of 2026?
It was lower than a year earlier, then rebounded within the year. In Q1 2026 the domestic manufacturing index was 88.9, 33.3% below Q1 2025. In Q2 2026 it rose to 137.5, 54.7% higher than Q1 but 3.9% lower than Q2 2025. By our count, the first-half average was 18.1% below the first half of 2025.
Does this index show how much Irish production is done abroad?
No. The CSO does not publish a share of Irish industrial production that is contract manufacturing or outsourcing abroad. The domestic and headline series are separate indices with their own weights. Their growth rates can be compared. The gap between two index levels, such as 137.5 and 108.8 in Q2 2026, is not that share.
Can this index tell a supplier which Irish manufacturers will buy?
No. It is a national volume index for manufacturing as a whole. The published tables have no sector split, so they do not show whether food or machinery is rising. The index can show whether production in Ireland is up or down, and how that compares with the headline series. Finding manufacturers still takes company-level account research.

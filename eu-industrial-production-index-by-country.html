Which EU countries and manufacturing sectors are producing more?

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Eurostat's industrial production index shows whether the volume of factory and other industrial output is rising or falling, country by country and by NACE division. The annual series is an index, 2021 = 100; this page uses the calendar-adjusted version. In 2025 the EU-27 manufacturing index was 101.3. The latest month is different: in September 2026, EU industrial production was 0.8% lower than a year earlier, according to first estimates.

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns only the volume index from Eurostat datasets sts_inpr_a and sts_inpr_m. It does not cover sold production by product, gross value added, or turnover in euro.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat sts_inpr_a and sts_inpr_m, data last updated 19 November 2026; euro indicators release 16 November 2026; ONS Index of Production, 12 November 2026; checked 24 November 2026.

  • The EU-27 manufacturing index was 101.3 in 2025, 2021 = 100, calendar adjusted. That is 1.8% above the 2024 figure of 99.5, by our count.[1]
  • By our count, manufacturing volume rose in 17 EU countries in 2025, fell in nine, and was unchanged in the Netherlands. Ireland had the largest rise, +18.5%. Bulgaria had the largest fall, −3.9%.[1]
  • At EU level, pharmaceuticals (C21) was the strongest manufacturing division in 2025, +15.6% on 2024 by our count. Leather and related products (C15) had the largest fall, −6.2%, also by our count.[1]
  • In September 2026, seasonally adjusted industrial production in the EU rose 0.5% on August and was 0.8% lower than in September 2025 (calendar adjusted), according to first estimates published on 16 November 2026. Ireland had the largest annual fall, −19.1%.[2][3]
  • Eurostat's index has no UK figures after 2020. On the ONS Index of Production — a different series — UK manufacturing was 4.4% higher in September 2026 than a year earlier, by our count. Compare directions only; do not rank the UK against EU countries.[4][5]

04 / the latest month september 2026

The latest month: September 2026

Eurostat's 16 November 2026 euro indicators release gives first estimates for September 2026. Month-on-month rates are calendar and seasonally adjusted. Year-on-year rates are calendar adjusted. In this release, "industrial production" and "total industry" cover NACE Rev. 2 sections B to D, which is broader than manufacturing alone. The next release is on 15 December 2026. Missing recent observations from member states are estimated when Eurostat calculates the EU and euro-area totals, and the EU and euro-area cells for September 2026 are flagged as imputed.[2][3]

In September 2026, compared with August 2026, seasonally adjusted industrial production increased by 0.5% in the EU and by 0.2% in the euro area. In August 2026, it had fallen by 0.3% in the EU and by 0.5% in the euro area. Compared with September 2025, industrial production decreased by 0.8% in the EU and by 1.5% in the euro area.[2]

By main industrial grouping, EU production of capital goods was unchanged month on month and 1.0% higher year on year. Intermediate goods rose 0.8% month on month and 1.1% year on year. Durable consumer goods rose 1.2% month on month and 1.5% year on year. Non-durable consumer goods rose 0.3% month on month but were 6.1% lower than in September 2025, the only main industrial grouping lower than a year earlier. Energy output fell 1.1% month on month and was 0.8% higher year on year.[2]

Among the member states with published data, the largest monthly rises were in Denmark (+3.7%), Poland (+2.9%), and Czechia and Slovakia (both +2.7%). The largest monthly falls were in Ireland (−5.5%), Luxembourg (−3.8%) and Romania (−2.6%). The largest annual falls were in Ireland (−19.1%), Bulgaria (−8.9%) and Romania (−6.3%). The highest annual rises were in the Netherlands (+8.8%), Hungary (+8.2%), and Lithuania and Malta (both +7.1%). Belgium had no published September figure in the release.[2]

Manufacturing, as a separate series in sts_inpr_m, tells a similar EU-level story but not an identical one. In September 2026 the EU-27 manufacturing index was 0.7% higher than in August, seasonally adjusted, and 0.8% lower than in September 2025, calendar adjusted. Both EU figures are imputed first estimates. Electrical equipment (C27) was 5.8% higher than a year earlier and machinery and equipment (C28) was 3.9% higher. Pharmaceuticals (C21), the strongest division in the 2025 annual figures, was 13.5% lower than in September 2025. Computer, electronic and optical products (C26) were 4.8% lower year on year and 7.5% lower month on month. All of those EU division rates for September 2026 are also flagged as imputed.[3]

05 / ireland and the uk reading different series

Ireland and the UK: reading different series

Ireland is in Eurostat's index; the UK has no figures there after 2020. The two countries cannot be placed in the same ranked table.

For Ireland, the 2025 annual manufacturing index was 123.0, up 18.5% on 2024, by our count — the largest country rise in that year.[1] The latest month points the other way. In September 2026, Ireland's industrial production (sections B to D) was 5.5% lower than in August, seasonally adjusted, and 19.1% lower than in September 2025, calendar adjusted, the largest annual fall among the member states with published data. Both Irish September figures are provisional. Irish manufacturing (section C) was similar: −5.6% month on month and −18.9% year on year, also provisional.[2][3] Read the latest month beside the annual index. A volume series for Ireland can move sharply from one period to the next; this page does not assign a cause.

The UK figures come from the Office for National Statistics (ONS) Index of Production, released on 12 November 2026, with the next release on 11 December 2026. That series is a chained volume measure, seasonally adjusted. The 2024 annual value is 100.0 for both production and manufacturing, so we read the reference year as 2024. Eurostat uses 2021 = 100 and, for the annual table, calendar adjustment rather than seasonal adjustment. Coverage differs too: ONS production covers sections B to E, including water supply and sewerage; Eurostat's total industry is sections B to D. Do not compare index levels across the two sources, and do not rank the UK against EU countries.[4][5]

On that ONS series, monthly production output was estimated to have increased by 0.3% in September 2026, after a 0.7% rise in August and a 0.1% fall in July. Manufacturing rose 0.7% in September. In the third quarter of 2026, production output was 0.1% higher than in the second quarter, after a 0.1% fall in the second quarter. Manufacturing rose 0.9% in the third quarter, with 8 of the 13 manufacturing subsectors up. The largest positive contribution was computer, electronic and optical products, up 4.6%; the largest negative contribution was rubber and plastics products, down 2.0%.[4]

The year-on-year rates below are by our count, from the official seasonally adjusted index cells. In September 2026 the production index (K222) was 101.4, against 98.4 in September 2025, a 3.0% rise. The manufacturing index (K22A) was 102.4, against 98.1, a 4.4% rise. For the calendar year 2025 compared with 2024, production was 0.4% higher (100.4 against 100.0) and manufacturing was 0.6% lower (99.4 against 100.0).[5]

In September 2026, UK manufacturing (section C) was up 4.4% year on year by our count (ONS K22A, seasonally adjusted). The same month, EU manufacturing was 0.8% lower and Irish manufacturing 18.9% lower than a year earlier (Eurostat sts_inpr_m, section C, calendar adjusted; the EU figure is an imputed first estimate and the Irish figure is provisional). The series differ in adjustment and base year, so this is a difference of direction, not a ranking of the UK against EU countries.[3][5]

For UK plant and company research, see UK manufacturing account research.

06 / what a volume index does not tell a supplier

What a volume index does not tell a supplier

The production index is a trend in volume, not a market-sizing table and not a prospect list.

  • It is not a count of enterprises or plants. For how many manufacturers each country has, and how many are larger employers, use industrial enterprises by EU country.
  • It is not sold production by product. The index does not say which goods were made, or in what quantity or euro value. For that, use Prodcom: what EU plants make, product by product.
  • It is not gross value added. Volume can rise while value added moves differently. Country shares of manufacturing value added are on manufacturing GVA by EU country.
  • It is not turnover or a price. The index removes the price effect as far as the method allows. A country can show rising volume and still have weaker sales in euro, or the reverse.
  • It is not a buying signal from a named plant. A national or sector index does not identify who will order plant, parts or services, or when. Use the trend to see which countries and NACE divisions are making more, then move to registers and site evidence, as in industrial account prioritisation.
  • The latest month can be revised. September 2026 EU and euro-area figures are first estimates, with imputed aggregates. Annual cells marked provisional can also change.
  • The index is not a size ranking. Each country's series is relative to its own 2021 level. Denmark's high 2025 index does not mean it has the largest manufacturing sector in the EU.
07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What is the EU industrial production index?
The industrial production index tracks the volume of output in industry, excluding construction, relative to a base year. Eurostat's series uses 2021 = 100. The annual country figures on this page are calendar adjusted, not seasonally adjusted. Total industry covers NACE Rev. 2 sections B to D; manufacturing is section C only.[1][2][7]
Which EU countries' manufacturing output rose in 2025?
By our count of Eurostat's calendar-adjusted manufacturing index (2021 = 100), volume rose in 17 EU countries in 2025, fell in nine, and was unchanged in the Netherlands. Ireland had the largest rise, 18.5%. Bulgaria had the largest fall, 3.9%. The EU-27 index was 101.3, up 1.8% on 2024.[1]
Which EU manufacturing sectors are growing?
In 2025 the strongest EU-27 manufacturing division was pharmaceuticals (C21), up 15.6% on 2024 by our count. Other transport equipment (C30) rose 5.8% and other manufacturing (C32) 5.6%. Leather (C15) fell 6.2% and chemicals (C20) 3.2%. A 2025 rise can reverse in the latest month: pharmaceuticals were 13.5% lower in September 2026 than a year earlier, as a first estimate.[1][3]
What does a manufacturing index of 101.3 mean?
It means EU-27 manufacturing volume in 2025 was 1.3% above its 2021 average, on Eurostat's calendar-adjusted series, 2021 = 100. It is not a measure of how large manufacturing is, and it is not a sales figure in euro. The same index was 99.5 in 2024, so 2025 was 1.8% higher than 2024, by our count.[1]
Why is the UK not ranked with EU countries?
Eurostat's index has no UK figures after 2020. The ONS Index of Production is a different series: chained volume, seasonally adjusted, whose 2024 annual value is 100.0. It also covers sections B to E, including water and waste, whereas Eurostat's total industry is B to D. Compare directions, not index levels.[2][4][5]
Can a rising production index tell a supplier who will buy?
No. A volume index shows whether output in a country or NACE division is rising or falling. It does not name plants, buyers or budgets, and it is not a sales or investment figure. Use it to see which sectors and countries are making more, then turn to enterprise counts, product statistics and site evidence to build an account list.[1]

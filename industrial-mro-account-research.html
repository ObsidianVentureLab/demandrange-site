Industrial MRO account research: follow the service scope, not the label

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

Industrial MRO account research works when you follow what crosses the service boundary, not the word "maintenance" on a website. A parts supplier needs to know who inspects, who repairs, who supplies the parts and who restocks them. Those can be four different businesses. This guide shows where MRO-heavy sites are in Ireland and how to record each role.

Key facts (Eurostat and CSO, 2024 data; checked 28 October 2026)

  • Eurostat table sbs_r_nuts2021 counts 19,618 manufacturing sites (local units) in Ireland in 2024, including 2,134 making food products (C10) and 2,028 repairing and installing machinery and equipment (C33), from data updated on 30 September 2026.[3]
  • The CSO counted 2,028 active repair and installation enterprises (C33) in Ireland in 2024, 1,907 of them with fewer than 10 persons engaged, in table BRA31.[4]
  • In 2024 the CSO counted 55 Irish food, drink and tobacco enterprises (C10 to C12) with 250 or more persons engaged, 33 in pharmaceuticals (C21) and 11 in chemicals (C20).[4]

01 / where mro heavy sites are in ireland

Where MRO-heavy sites are in Ireland

Process plants that run continuously, such as food, drink, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, paper, cement and metals, tend to buy maintenance work and spare parts all year. This is our own sector selection, not an official grouping. Eurostat's regional business statistics count these Irish manufacturing sites (local units) for 2024:[3]

NACE division Activity Sites in Ireland, 2024
C10 Food products 2,134
C11 Beverages 274
C17 Paper and paper products 208
C20 Chemicals and chemical products 494
C21 Pharmaceutical products 256
C23 Other non-metallic mineral products 1,405
C24 Basic metals 487
C33 Repair and installation of machinery and equipment 2,028
C All manufacturing 19,618

Division C33 matters twice. Its 2,028 sites are the repair and installation businesses that may buy your parts on behalf of plants.[3] The CSO counts the same number of active enterprises in C33 for 2024, and 1,907 of them have fewer than 10 persons engaged.[4]

Size points you to the bigger maintenance budgets. In 2024 the CSO counted 55 food, drink and tobacco enterprises (C10 to C12) with 250 or more persons engaged, 33 in pharmaceuticals and 11 in chemicals.[4] Across all manufacturing, the CSO count of 19,617 active enterprises is almost the same as Eurostat's 19,618 sites. For the table's divisions that the CSO publishes on their own (C20, C21, C23 and C33), its 2024 enterprise counts equal Eurostat's site counts exactly. So read the site table as close to a company count, and check multi-site groups yourself.[3][4]

02 / define the service output

Define the service output

Template — replace the marked fields with your own details. Start with a brief: "Research businesses doing the specific work on your equipment family in your territory, where the parts responsibility is required." Use the ICP guide for identity, territory and exclusions.

For each service description, ask three questions: what arrives, what work is done, and what leaves. Then record whether the text describes a general capability, an agreed scope or a completed job. Keep these roles apart:

  • Equipment operation: running the equipment. It does not mean inspecting it or buying its parts.
  • Inspection: examining and reporting within a stated scope. A report is not a repair order.
  • Repair or overhaul: record the work actually described. "Repair" does not mean a full overhaul or new parts.
  • Provision of spares: supplying a part or assembly to fit in place of another. Fitting it, approving it and deciding to change it are separate questions.
  • Stocking and restocking: holding stock and being responsible for refilling it are two facts. Neither follows from a single repair.

Where a description is broad, write a narrower question instead of guessing at a service package.

03 / fictional example one plant four service roles

Fictional example: one plant, four service roles

Fictional example. All organisations and notes are invented and describe no real business.

A fictional seals and bearings distributor wants accounts where it can supply parts for repairs. It starts with one fictional plant on a fictional handling line:

Operator O runs the line

A note gives O operation of the line. It says nothing about who owns the equipment or buys maintenance. Keep O as the operator; do not name it as the parts buyer.

Inspector I reports on it

I inspects the line. Its scope excludes repair and parts. For a parts brief, I is outside this route.

Workshop R repairs with parts supplied by the client

R repairs a customer-owned assembly using a kit the client supplies. R is a repair account, but not a parts buyer for this arrangement. The note does not say who bought the kit, so leave that blank.

Service provider S repairs and supplies the parts

S's scope includes repair labour and the parts it fits. That supports a parts route for S's arrangement. It does not show an installed fleet or a new order.

A second invented note says the client approves the part model and S places the order. Ordering is now supported for S. Supplier choice is still open: the note does not say whether S picks the seller or follows a nominated one. That is the next question.

A third note says S offers spare assemblies. It says nothing about stock on hand or who restocks. Keep stock as unknown.

What changes the route: if S's note only said "repair support", its parts role would become unknown, not excluded. R's client-supplied kit rule stands until evidence about the same arrangement says otherwise.

The distributor's handover for S reads: "S repairs and fits parts for this line and places the order for an approved model. Find out whether S chooses the seller. Stock holding and current need are unknown."

04 / keep six conclusions separate

Keep six conclusions separate

  1. Account fit: does the company meet the brief's activity, territory and exclusion rules?
  2. Current maintenance need: is a specific present task evidenced?
  3. Installed base: which assets are actually linked to the site and period?
  4. Parts compatibility: this method does not assess it.
  5. Purchasing authority: model approval, supplier selection and order placement are three separate facts.
  6. Availability: stock, service capacity and an open buying route are separate again.

Do not derive equipment life, maintenance intervals or failure timing from service text.

05 / use the blank scope worksheet

Use the blank scope worksheet

The header-only CSV in the resource library has no data rows, contacts or formulas. Import it as UTF-8 text and switch off formula interpretation for pasted content.

One row is one fact or open question about one service boundary, not one company. The fields:

  • record_id, company_id, scope_id: row, company and service-arrangement identifiers. Repeated IDs do not add accounts.
  • brief_version, record_context: the brief, and whether the row is working research or labelled fictional practice. service_scope: operation, equipment family, location and period.
  • scope_basis: capability, agreed scope, completed job or unknown. input_boundary and output_boundary: what is received and what is returned or supplied.
  • fact_name: the single assertion tested, such as inspection scope, repair work, parts provision, stockholding, restocking, model approval or order placement.
  • fact_status: supported, explicit_negative, unknown, conflict or not_assessed. The next column holds the narrow supported fact, in your words.
  • evidence_reference, source_stated_date, checked_date, source_use_status: where the fact comes from, the source's own date, your check date and any reuse limit.
  • responsibility_limit, research_implication, next_question, stopping_rule: exclusions, your reading, the missing link and where the check stops.

Each positive or negative finding needs its own reference. Never paste confidential manuals, customer asset registers, work orders or personal details.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What counts as an MRO account?

Any business that inspects, repairs, overhauls or stocks parts for industrial equipment, plus the plants that buy that work. The role decides the route, so record which role each account plays.

Which Irish sectors have the most MRO-heavy sites?

By site count in 2024, food (2,134), other non-metallic minerals (1,405), chemicals (494) and basic metals (487) lead among process sectors.[3] Pharmaceuticals has fewer sites (256) but 33 enterprises with 250 or more persons engaged.[3][4]

Are repair and installation businesses worth targeting?

Often, yes. Ireland had 2,028 active enterprises in repair and installation of machinery and equipment (C33) in 2024, most of them small.[4] Check whether each one supplies parts or uses kits from its clients.

Does a "spares" page mean a company holds stock?

No. An offer to supply spares says nothing about stock on hand or who restocks. Record stock as unknown until a source says so.

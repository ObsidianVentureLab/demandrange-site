Irish food and beverage company research: which plants to target and where to find them

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

Ireland had 2,434 active food, beverage and tobacco manufacturing enterprises in 2024, but only 226 had 50 or more persons engaged. For an industrial supplier, the useful targets are the plants whose process your product serves. This guide shows how to pick them and which public lists confirm what each site does.

01 / how many food and drink manufacturers ireland has

How many food and drink manufacturers Ireland has

The CSO's business demography table counts active enterprises in NACE divisions C10 to C12 (food products, beverages and tobacco) together.[1]

Persons engaged, 2024 Active enterprises
Under 10 1,904
10 to 19 157
20 to 49 147
50 to 249 171
250 or more 55
All sizes 2,434

So 530 enterprises had 10 or more persons engaged and 226 had 50 or more. Together they engaged 60,134 persons.[1] These are counts of enterprises, not plants, and the group includes tobacco, so treat them as the size of the universe, not as a list. Our Irish manufacturing account research guide gives the same table for other manufacturing divisions.

02 / which plants to target

Which plants to target

Start from your product, not from the food category. A supplier of clean-in-place (CIP) systems, a supplier of refrigeration and a supplier of packaging lines may all sell to "dairy", but they need different plants.

If you supply… Target plants that… Usually not a fit
Process equipment, pumps, valves, CIP Receive raw materials and process them on site: dairies, meat plants, breweries, distilleries, ingredient plants Brand owners whose products are made under contract elsewhere
Refrigeration and cold-chain equipment Process or store chilled and frozen products Ambient packers
Packaging and end-of-line equipment Pack finished product at volume Bulk ingredient plants that ship in tankers
Water and wastewater treatment Have a licensed discharge or high water use Small sites connected to municipal sewers with low loads

Separate three kinds of business that look alike in search results: manufacturers that run their own plants, brand owners that use contract manufacturers, and distributors or packers. Record which one each company is before you count it.

03 / where to find them

Where to find them

No single list covers every plant. Use each register for the question it can answer.

  • EPA industrial emissions licences (class 7, food and drink): the First Schedule to the EPA Act sets the activities and thresholds that need a licence. Class 7 covers, among others, milk processing where more than 200 tonnes of milk are received per day, brewing above 25 million litres a year, distilling above 1,500 tonnes of pure alcohol a year, slaughterhouses above 50 tonnes of carcass a day, and processing of animal raw materials above 75 tonnes of finished product a day.[2] Licences and licence documents are on the EPA's LEAP portal.[3] A licence is strong evidence of a large processing site and its activity. Read more in our guide to Irish environmental register evidence.
  • DAFM approved establishments: the Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine publishes lists of meat establishments (including fish, egg and dairy establishments) approved or registered under S.I. 22 of 2020, and a separate list of milk and dairy establishments.[4] Approval lists name the site, which is what you need.
  • FSAI approved food premises: the FSAI links to the approved-establishment lists for each Irish competent authority: DAFM, the HSE for low-throughput meat processors, and the Sea-Fisheries Protection Authority for seafood processors and factory vessels.[5] It also links to the lists for other EU member states.
  • Bord Bia Origin Green members directory: the directory lists Origin Green members by industry (manufacturers, foodservice and retail) and by sector, such as dairy, meat, beverages, prepared food and seafood.[6] Membership is a company-level sustainability commitment, not a site register, so use it to identify active manufacturers, then confirm their plants in the regulatory lists.

For plants elsewhere in the EU, the same approach uses national approval lists and the EU Industrial Emissions Portal. See EU food and beverage plant research.

04 / map each company by product work and place

Map each company by product, work and place

For each company, record three things separately: the product (what it makes or sells), the work (what process happens) and the place (which site does that work). A company that sells cheese may make it at its own plant, buy it in and pack it, or have it made under contract. Only the first runs a cheese-making process.

Use the segmentation worksheet to record the three answers and the source for each, and keep "not found" separate from "checked, does not apply". The fictional practice cases show how the same companies sort differently for two different briefs.

05 / worked example a fictional dairy cip supplier

Worked example: a fictional dairy CIP supplier

Fictional example. A fictional supplier of CIP systems for dairy plants wants Irish sites that process milk on site and clean tanks, lines and evaporators between runs.

  1. Universe: the CSO table suggests that 226 enterprises in C10 to C12 have 50 or more persons engaged. Dairy is a small part of that.
  2. Plants: the supplier lists dairy sites from the DAFM milk and dairy establishment list and the EPA licences for milk processing under class 7.
  3. Fit: it sorts three fictional companies. Company A runs a licensed milk-powder plant: target. Company B is a cheese brand whose product is made under contract by Company A: not a separate plant, so it is recorded under Company A's site. Company C runs a small approved farmhouse cheese site with no licence: a possible target for a smaller CIP skid, held for a separate brief.
  4. Context: Company A is also an Origin Green member, which confirms it is an active manufacturer but adds nothing about its plant.

The companies and decisions are fictional and only illustrate the method.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many food and beverage manufacturers are there in Ireland?

The CSO counted 2,434 active enterprises in food products, beverages and tobacco (C10 to C12) in 2024. Of these, 530 had 10 or more persons engaged and 226 had 50 or more.[1]

Where can I find a list of Irish food processing plants?

Use the DAFM approved-establishment lists and the FSAI's links to other approval lists for named sites, and the EPA licence register for the largest processing sites.[3][4][5]

Which food plants need an EPA licence?

Those carrying out a class 7 activity above the thresholds in the First Schedule to the EPA Act, such as milk processing above 200 tonnes of milk received per day.[2]

Is the Origin Green directory a list of food factories?

No. It lists member companies by industry and sector. Use it to identify active manufacturers, then confirm their plants in the regulatory lists.[6]

Should I target brand owners or manufacturers?

For process equipment, target the business that runs the plant. A brand owner whose products are made under contract usually does not buy process equipment for that product.

How to find industrial customers in France: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

France had 279,540 manufacturing enterprises in 2024, the second-highest count in the EU, so finding industrial customers in France is a filtering job, not a search for names.[1] Build the list in layers from free public sources: the Sirene register, activity codes, plant permits and public tenders. Then check each account against your brief.

01 / key facts

Key facts

  • Eurostat counted 279,540 manufacturing enterprises in France in 2024, up from 266,065 in 2023, and 3,265,897 persons employed.[1]
  • 1,535 French manufacturing enterprises employed 250 or more people in 2024, and 4,636 employed 50–249; by our count that is 6,171 with 50 or more, 2.2% of the total.[2]
  • Eurostat counted 328,137 manufacturing local units (sites) in France in 2024, about 1.2 per enterprise by our count.[1][3]
  • NAF 2025, France's version of NACE Rev. 2.1, becomes the reference for APE activity codes on 1 January 2027.[4]
02 / how big the french manufacturing base is

How big the French manufacturing base is

Eurostat's structural business statistics give these figures for French manufacturing (NACE Rev. 2 Section C) in 2024:[1][2][3]

France, manufacturing, 2024 Count
Enterprises 279,540
Enterprises with 50–249 persons employed 4,636
Enterprises with 250 or more persons employed 1,535
Persons employed 3,265,897
Local units (sites) 328,137

Source: Eurostat sbs_ovw_act, sbs_sc_ovw and sbs_r_nuts2021, datasets updated 30 September 2026.[1][2][3]

INSEE's own figure is close but not the same measure. Its Ésane statistics count 266,068 manufacturing enterprises in France in 2023: 2,004 large and intermediate-sized enterprises (GE and ETI), 23,203 SMEs excluding microenterprises, and 240,861 microenterprises.[12] It differs from Eurostat's 279,540 in three ways. It is a year older (Eurostat's 2023 count was 266,065). INSEE counts the enterprise in its economic sense, which can group several legal units. And its size categories are not Eurostat's persons-employed bands.[1][12] Quote each source with its own year and definition, and don't mix them in one table.

Most French manufacturers are very small: 251,498, about 90% by our count, employ fewer than 10 people.[2] Employment sits in the large firms. By our count, enterprises with 250 or more persons employed had 60.9% of French manufacturing employment in 2024 (1,987,615 of 3,265,897).[2] For most suppliers of industrial equipment and services, the 6,171 enterprises with 50 or more people are closer to the realistic account universe than the headline count. To compare France with the other EU countries, use our EU manufacturing enterprises by country table.

The local-unit count comes from a separate regional dataset, so treat the 1.2 sites per enterprise ratio as indicative.[3] If you sell per plant, the site count is the better starting point.

03 / build the list in layers

Build the list in layers

  • Companies. The Sirene register lists every legal unit (SIREN) and establishment (SIRET), as open data.[6]
  • Activity. APE codes in NAF rév. 2 today, NAF 2025 from January 2027.[4][5]
  • Plants. Installations classées (ICPE) records on Géorisques, plus the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for large installations.[10][11]
  • Buying signals. Public tenders on BOAMP and PLACE.[8][9]
04 / sirene and the annuaire des entreprises

Sirene and the Annuaire des Entreprises

INSEE's Sirene base is published on the State open-data portal under the Licence Ouverte 2.0. Monthly stock files cover legal units and establishments, active and ceased.[6] That gives you two levels a supplier needs: the SIREN legal unit you invoice and the SIRET establishments where plants actually run. INSEE refreshed the headcount bands to 31 December 2024 values in October 2026.[6]

For one company at a time, the free Annuaire des Entreprises looks up SIRET numbers from the INSEE base.[6][7] Its search shows each company's current activity code and its future NAF 2025 code side by side.[7]

Record the operating entity, each relevant establishment and the group. A French group can run several plants as separate establishments of one legal unit, or as separate subsidiaries.

05 / naf rev 2 and naf 2025

NAF rév. 2 and NAF 2025

NAF is France's activity classification, built on the same structure as NACE.[5] NAF rév. 2 stays in force until the end of 2026. NAF 2025, which nests inside NACE Rev. 2.1, was adopted by INSEE in December 2023 and approved by Eurostat in May 2024.[4]

From 1 January 2027 every active legal unit in Sirene, and its establishments, gets a modified APE code.[4] Since 16 December 2025 Sirene files have published the future NAF 2025 code for information next to the NAF rév. 2 code.[6] INSEE publishes a NAF rév. 2 to NAF 2025 correspondence table.[4]

For a list, that means:

  • Keep both codes on each row until all your sources have moved.
  • Don't filter one file on NAF rév. 2 and another on NAF 2025 and merge them unchecked.
  • Map to NACE Rev. 2.1 with our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.
06 / public tenders boamp and place

Public tenders: BOAMP and PLACE

BOAMP, the Bulletin officiel des annonces des marchés publics, has a public tender search, alerts and an open-data API.[9] PLACE is the State's procurement platform, where firms can read and answer consultations from central and deconcentrated State services, State public bodies and independent authorities, with daily or weekly alerts.[8] Contracts above EU thresholds also appear on TED; see our TED tenders guide and CPV codes for the search terms.

A tender is a buying signal at one public buyer. It tells you nothing about private plants.

07 / plant evidence icpe on georisques

Plant evidence: ICPE on Géorisques

Any industrial or agricultural operation that can create risks, pollution or nuisance is an installation classée pour la protection de l'environnement (ICPE).[10] There are four regimes: déclaration (D), déclaration with periodic checks (DC), enregistrement (E) and autorisation (A).[10] Each rubrique in the ICPE nomenclature has a 4-digit number: 1xxx for substances, 2xxx for activities, 3xxx for Industrial Emissions Directive activities and 4xxx for Seveso substances.[10] Géorisques publishes the installations data.[11] It lists every site under autorisation and enregistrement; déclaration and below-threshold sites appear only where an inspection is on record.[11]

An ICPE record puts a named operator at a site, which a register code can't. For the largest installations, cross-check with the EU Industrial Emissions Portal.

08 / step by step

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat figures, then define it with NAF and NACE codes.[1][4]
  2. Pull establishments from the Sirene stock files by APE code and headcount band.[6]
  3. Add plant evidence from ICPE records for process industries.[10][11]
  4. Resolve each one in the Annuaire des Entreprises: legal unit, establishments and group.[7]
  5. Note the plant-level functions that own the decision, such as directeur d'usine (plant manager), responsable maintenance and acheteur technique. Record the function, not a person.
  6. Add a trigger: a new ICPE authorisation, a BOAMP or PLACE tender, or a published expansion.[8][9][10]

Fictional example: a fictional Irish maker of industrial pumps targets French chemical plants. It filters ICPE records for chemical installations under the autorisation regime, then resolves each operator in the Annuaire des Entreprises. It finds that one fictional group runs two plants as two SIRET establishments of a single SIREN, and records them as two accounts under one group. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

09 / common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Counting legal units when you sell to sites. Use SIRET establishments and the local-unit count.
  • Mixing NAF rév. 2 and NAF 2025 codes in one filter during the switch.
  • Reading the headline count as the market. Nine in ten French manufacturers have fewer than 10 people, by our count.[2]
  • Treating a tender as a signal for the whole sector.

For the same method in Germany, see our Germany industrial account research guide.

10 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in France?
Eurostat counted 279,540 manufacturing enterprises in France in 2024, up from 266,065 in 2023. Of these, 1,535 employed 250 or more people and 4,636 employed 50 to 249. By our count that makes 6,171 with 50 or more persons employed, which is 2.2% of the total.[1][2]
Is the French company register free to use?
Yes. INSEE publishes the Sirene base of legal units and establishments as open data under the Licence Ouverte 2.0, with monthly stock files. The free Annuaire des Entreprises lets you look up one company at a time, including its SIRET numbers and its activity code under both NAF versions.[6][7]
What is the difference between SIREN and SIRET?
SIREN identifies the legal unit and SIRET identifies each establishment of it. For plant-level selling, the establishment matters more, because one legal unit can run several sites. Sirene publishes separate stock files for legal units and establishments, active and ceased, so you can work at either level.[6]
Should I use NAF rév. 2 or NAF 2025 codes?
Both, for now. NAF rév. 2 stays in force until the end of 2026, and NAF 2025 becomes the reference for APE codes from 1 January 2027. Sirene already publishes the future NAF 2025 code alongside the current one, and INSEE publishes a correspondence table between the two versions.[4][6]
Where can I find French industrial sites?
Start with installations classées (ICPE) records on Géorisques. Any industrial operation that can cause risk, pollution or nuisance is classified under one of four regimes, from déclaration to autorisation. For the largest installations, cross-check with the EU Industrial Emissions Portal, then match each operator to its Sirene establishment.[10][11]

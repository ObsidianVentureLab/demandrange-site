How to find manufacturing companies in Poland: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Poland had 241,804 manufacturing enterprises in 2024, the third-highest count in the EU after Italy and France.[1] Nearly nine in ten employ fewer than 10 people, so finding manufacturing companies in Poland is a filtering job. Build the list in layers from free public sources: the GUS REGON register, KRS and CEIDG, PKD codes, plant evidence and public tenders. Then check each account against your brief.

01 / key facts

Key facts

  • Eurostat counted 241,804 manufacturing enterprises in Poland in 2024, up from 241,254 in 2023, and 2,829,435 persons employed.[1]
  • 1,565 Polish manufacturing enterprises employed 250 or more people in 2024, and 5,575 employed 50–249; by our count that is 7,140 with 50 or more, 3.0% of the total.[2]
  • The GUS REGON register held 411,279 manufacturing entries at the end of 2025, more than Eurostat's count, because a register is not a count of active enterprises.[7]
  • PKD 2025, Poland's activity classification aligned with NACE Rev. 2.1, applies from 1 January 2025; PKD 2007 codes can be used until 31 December 2026.[4][5]
02 / how big the polish manufacturing base is

How big the Polish manufacturing base is

Eurostat's structural business statistics give these figures for Polish manufacturing (NACE Rev. 2 Section C) in 2024:[1][2][3]

Poland, manufacturing, 2024 Count
Enterprises 241,804
Enterprises with 50–249 persons employed 5,575
Enterprises with 250 or more persons employed 1,565
Persons employed 2,829,435
Local units (sites) 246,236

Source: Eurostat sbs_ovw_act, sbs_sc_ovw and sbs_r_nuts2021, datasets updated 30 September 2026.[1][2][3]

Most Polish manufacturers are very small: 213,956, about 88.5% by our count, employ fewer than 10 people.[2] By our count, enterprises with 250 or more persons employed had 49.1% of Polish manufacturing employment in 2024 (1,389,690 of 2,829,435).[2] Value added per person employed was about EUR 43,500.[1] For most suppliers of industrial equipment and services, the 7,140 enterprises with 50 or more people are closer to the realistic account universe than the headline count. To compare Poland with the other EU countries, use our EU manufacturing enterprises by country table.

The local-unit count comes from a separate regional dataset, so treat the ratio of about 1.02 sites per enterprise, by our count, as indicative.[3] If you sell per plant, the site count is the better starting point.

03 / register entries are not enterprises

Register entries are not enterprises

GUS, Statistics Poland, publishes its own count from the REGON register: 411,279 manufacturing entries (PKD 2007 Section C) at 31 December 2025, of which 65,448 had suspended activity.[7] REGON counts registered entities by expected employment. Eurostat counts active enterprises by persons employed. The two figures measure different things, so don't subtract one from the other or mix their size bands.

Use Eurostat to size the segment. Use REGON to find and identify the businesses in it.

04 / build the list in layers

Build the list in layers

  • Companies. KRS for companies and other registered entities; CEIDG for sole traders.[11][12]
  • Identifiers. REGON numbers for every entity and each of its local units.[8][9]
  • Activity. PKD 2025 codes, with PKD 2007 still on many entries until the end of 2026.[4][10]
  • Plants. Local units in REGON, plus the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for large installations.[8][16]
  • Buying signals. Public tenders on e-Zamówienia.[14][15]
05 / regon krs and ceidg

REGON, KRS and CEIDG

Poland splits company records by legal form. KRS, the National Court Register, holds companies and other registered entities. Anyone can search it and see information equal to a current or full extract, including deleted entries, without an account.[11] CEIDG holds only sole traders and partners in civil-law partnerships of natural persons, with their PKD codes and a predominant activity code.[12] Its search of firms is free.[13]

That split matters for manufacturing. By our count, about three in four REGON manufacturing entries at the end of 2025 were natural persons conducting a business, which means CEIDG entries, not KRS companies.[7][12] By our count, 97.7% of those natural persons (299,263 of 306,402) expected to employ fewer than 10 people.[7] A list that searches only KRS misses them, but few of them run plants of the size most industrial suppliers sell to.

REGON, run by GUS, ties the two together. It covers legal persons, organisational units without legal personality, natural persons conducting a business, and their local units.[8] An entity has a 9-digit REGON number. A local unit has a 14-digit number: the parent's nine digits, a four-digit sequence number and a check digit.[8] GUS's free REGON search finds an entity by REGON number, tax identification number (NIP) or KRS number, by address, or by activity, with an option to match the predominant PKD code only.[9]

For a supplier, the 14-digit local units are the site layer. They let you record each plant under the right legal entity.

06 / pkd 2007 and pkd 2025

PKD 2007 and PKD 2025

PKD is Poland's activity classification. PKD 2025 was introduced on 1 January 2025 by a regulation of the Council of Ministers, following the EU's NACE revision, and REGON, KRS and CEIDG all had to adapt to it.[4][5] PKD 2007 codes recorded before then can be used until 31 December 2026.[5]

From 1 January 2027, entries that haven't been updated are reclassified automatically, using GUS's transition keys from PKD 2007 to PKD 2025.[4][6] Some codes map one to one, some merge and some split; for a split, GUS picks the single most suitable PKD 2025 code.[6] Until then, a REGON entry has one list of codes, which can be PKD 2007, PKD 2025 or a mix, and REGON data now say which version applies.[10]

For a list, that means:

  • Keep both codes on each row until all your sources have moved.
  • Don't filter one file on PKD 2007 and another on PKD 2025 and merge them unchecked.
  • Map to NACE Rev. 2.1 with our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.
07 / public tenders e zamowienia

Public tenders: e-Zamówienia

Since 1 January 2022 the Biuletyn Zamówień Publicznych (BZP), Poland's public procurement bulletin, has been part of the e-Zamówienia platform.[14] Its notice list can be filtered by phrase, procedure (national or EU), notice type, publication date and deadline, and saved as PDF, CSV or XML.[15] Contracts above EU thresholds also appear on TED; see our TED tenders guide and CPV codes for the search terms.

A tender is a buying signal at one public buyer. It tells you nothing about private plants.

08 / step by step

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat figures, then define it with PKD 2025 and NACE codes.[1][4]
  2. Pull candidates from the REGON search by predominant PKD code.[9]
  3. Resolve each one in KRS or CEIDG, depending on its legal form.[11][12]
  4. Record the plants as REGON local units under the parent entity, and cross-check the largest installations in the EU Industrial Emissions Portal.[8][16]
  5. Note the plant-level functions that own the decision, such as dyrektor zakładu (plant manager), kierownik utrzymania ruchu (maintenance manager) and dział zakupów (purchasing). Record the function, not a person.
  6. Add a trigger: an e-Zamówienia tender, a new local unit or a published expansion.[8][15]

Fictional example: a fictional Irish maker of industrial filtration systems targets Polish food plants. It pulls food manufacturers from the REGON search by predominant PKD code, keeps those registered in KRS and resolves each one there. It finds that one fictional dairy company has three local units under one 9-digit REGON number, and records them as three accounts under one company. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

09 / common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Reading the REGON count as the number of active manufacturers.
  • Counting entities when you sell to sites. Use REGON local units and the local-unit count.
  • Mixing PKD 2007 and PKD 2025 codes in one filter during the switch.
  • Treating CEIDG sole traders as plant accounts. By our count, nearly all expect fewer than 10 people.[7]

For the same method in Germany, see our Germany industrial account research guide.

10 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in Poland?
Eurostat counted 241,804 manufacturing enterprises in Poland in 2024, the third-highest count in the EU. Of these, 1,565 employed 250 or more people and 5,575 employed 50 to 249. By our count that makes 7,140 with 50 or more persons employed, which is 3.0% of the total.[1][2]
Why does GUS show more manufacturers than Eurostat?
Because they count different things. The GUS REGON register held 411,279 manufacturing entries at the end of 2025, including 65,448 with suspended activity. Eurostat counted 241,804 active manufacturing enterprises in 2024. Use Eurostat to size the market and REGON to find and identify the businesses, and don't mix their size bands.[1][7]
What is the difference between KRS and CEIDG?
KRS is the court register for companies and other registered entities, while CEIDG holds only sole traders and partners in civil-law partnerships of natural persons. Both have free searches. KRS shows information equal to a current or full extract without an account. GUS's REGON register covers entities from both, plus their local units.[8][11][12][13]
Should I use PKD 2007 or PKD 2025 codes?
Both, for now. PKD 2025 has applied since 1 January 2025, but PKD 2007 codes recorded earlier can be used until 31 December 2026. From 1 January 2027, entries not updated are reclassified automatically with GUS's transition keys. A REGON entry can carry PKD 2007 codes, PKD 2025 codes or both.[5][6][10]
Where can I find Polish industrial sites?
Start with REGON local units. Each site an entity registers gets a 14-digit number that begins with the parent's 9-digit REGON, so you can group plants under the right company. For the largest installations, cross-check with the EU Industrial Emissions Portal, then confirm the operator in KRS.[8][11][16]

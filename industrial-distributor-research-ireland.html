Irish industrial distributor research: building a channel shortlist

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

A company that sells relevant equipment is not automatically a suitable distributor for your range. Before building a shortlist, decide what the channel would need to do: resell components, hold stock, support installation, service equipment or develop an application market. Each is a different research question.

This guide covers distributors operating in the Republic of Ireland. If your brief also covers Northern Ireland or Great Britain, scope those separately with their own registers. It is a company-level research method, not a directory of available partners. The worked cases are wholly fictional, and no company is represented as willing or able to take on a new range.

01 / start with the channel job not a broad company label

Start with the channel job, not a broad company label

Write a short brief that another researcher could apply without asking you what "a good distributor" means:

  • Product boundary: the component or equipment family being considered. Do not treat all pumps, valves or controls as interchangeable.
  • Application boundary: the uses and customer environments you want to investigate. Sector marketing is not evidence of technical compatibility.
  • Territory rule: whether you require an Irish operating location, service coverage in Ireland, or another defined relationship. Those are separate conditions.
  • Channel job: for example, resale and stockholding, or resale with a separately evidenced repair capability. Record which requirements are mandatory.
  • Exclusions: existing partners, protected client accounts, retail-only operations or unsuitable application categories. Apply only the exclusions actually agreed.
  • Unknowns to keep: appetite for a new principal, competing agreements, financial capacity, commercial terms and technical suitability, unless separately established.

Do not add an active-project requirement unless the brief needs one. A company can match an account-research brief without a current purchasing event.

02 / where to find irish distributors

Where to find Irish distributors

No single public list covers industrial distributors in Ireland. Build the long list from several source types, and record which one each candidate came from.

Source What it tells you What it does not tell you
CRO company search[3] Registered name, company number, status and registered address What the company sells, where it operates or whether it holds stock
CRO Open Data, Company Records[4] A daily snapshot of registered and dissolved companies, as CSV or API Product range, branches or channel role
CSO business demography, table BRA31[5] How many wholesale enterprises are active, by size band Which enterprises they are or what they distribute
Hardware Association Ireland[6] A member body for hardware and builders merchants, and for distributors and manufacturers of hardware, building materials or allied commodities, with a member directory[7] Industrial product coverage, or whether a member is open to a new range
Electrical Manufacturers and Distributors Association (EMDA)[8] The representative body for manufacturers and distributors of electrical equipment in the Republic of Ireland Membership of any particular company, unless the source shows it
Manufacturers' "where to buy" or distributor-locator pages Which companies a manufacturer currently lists for a territory and range Exclusivity, current stock or appetite for a competing range
The candidate's own website Product, service and branch claims in its own words Independent confirmation of those claims

How big is the pool? The CSO counted 14,564 active enterprises in wholesale trade (NACE G46, excluding motor vehicles) in 2024, with 127,149 persons engaged.

12,386 of those enterprises had fewer than 10 persons engaged, and 402 had 50 or more (354 with 50 to 249, and 48 with 250 or more).[5] These counts cover all wholesale trade, including food, consumer goods and fuel, so the industrial share is much smaller. BRA31 does not split G46 further.

Use each source for what it shows. Hardware Association Ireland says it represents over 100 distributors and suppliers in the hardware industry.[6] Trade-association membership shows that a company is in the trade, not that it carries your product category.

A manufacturer's locator page shows a current relationship for that manufacturer's range. That is a reason to look closer, not proof of exclusivity. Use the CRO to confirm the legal entity and status of each candidate, and match it to the trading name you found elsewhere.

03 / test the product application and territory separately

Test the product, application and territory separately

Use a candidate's own product and service information to write a narrow inclusion reason. Where available, compare it with a manufacturer's current distributor information. Record what each source establishes, its date when stated, and when you checked it. Respect each source's terms of use: this method is not permission to copy directories or republish their records.

A reference to a product family does not establish coverage of every model, material, pressure range or application. A service described on a group website does not show that an Irish branch performs it. If a source does not answer your question, leave the answer unresolved rather than widening the claim.

For a candidate serving several markets, keep the activity relevant to the brief. Do not count the same business twice because it also installs or repairs equipment. Record extra roles against the same company identity.

04 / separate an existing relationship from an available partnership

Separate an existing relationship from an available partnership

A publicly described relationship with a manufacturer is evidence to investigate, not a verdict on your own route to market. Keep these questions separate:

  1. Which relationship is described, for which product range and territory?
  2. How current is the source, and does another source disagree?
  3. Is any exclusivity or restriction stated for the relevant scope?
  4. Is there evidence of willingness to discuss your range? If not, leave willingness unknown.

Do not infer exclusivity from a logo. Equally, the absence of a competing brand on a website does not prove the company is free to represent you. A contractual assessment is outside this research.

Use a conflict flag to prompt review, not to reach a legal conclusion. Where an existing client or supplier relationship creates a handling restriction, apply it before sharing or acting on the shortlist.

05 / three fictional screening decisions

Three fictional screening decisions

Fictional example. The brief, the companies and every record below are invented. No real distributor, manufacturer or association member is represented.

The invented brief seeks businesses with an Irish operation that resell industrial isolation valves and provide documented workshop repair. Stockholding is desirable but not mandatory. Retail-only businesses are excluded. New-partner availability is not assumed.

Fictional Channel A: relevant range, incomplete capability evidence

Channel A came from a fictional trade-association directory. The CRO record shows an active company, and its website describes an Irish operation and resale of industrial isolation valves. It also describes on-site installation, but says nothing about workshop repair.

Decision: hold for the missing repair evidence. Installation does not substitute for the mandatory capability. Record the range and location evidence without promoting the candidate to a complete match. Stockholding and willingness to discuss a new range remain unknown.

Fictional Channel B: brief match, commercial questions still open

Channel B came from a fictional manufacturer's distributor-locator page for Ireland. Its website describes an Irish operation, the relevant valve resale and workshop repair. The locator page shows a relationship with that other manufacturer, but there is no exclusivity wording, stockholding evidence or invitation to new principals.

Decision: retain as a brief-matched research candidate, with the relationship flagged for review. Do not mark it an available partner, approved distributor or exclusive agent. Its commercial suitability needs separate work.

Fictional Channel C: relevant product word, wrong route

Channel C came from a keyword search for "valves". Its website describes a retail-only domestic plumbing shop. It uses the word "valves", but shows no industrial isolation-valve resale or workshop repair.

Decision: exclude under the agreed retail-only rule. The shared product word does not override the route and application exclusions. This is a decision about the invented brief, not about the quality of any real business.

06 / produce a shortlist someone can challenge

Produce a shortlist someone can challenge

For each candidate, record the company identity, where you found it, the relevant operation, product and application evidence, mandatory capability evidence, relationship or conflict flag, decision, reason and next question. Keep each source reference beside the fact it supports, not one generic homepage link for the whole record.

The worksheet uses three decisions: retain_for_review, hold_for_evidence and exclude. "Retain" means the account criteria are met. It does not mean partnership availability has been verified. Mandatory unknowns trigger a hold. An explicit exclusion triggers an exclusion. Optional unknowns stay visible rather than being scored as present or absent.

Review the shortlist against the original brief before any next step. Remove duplicate companies, check unresolved scope conflicts, and ask whether every inclusion reason would survive a challenge from your commercial team. To decide which retained candidates to research first, use the account prioritisation scoring table. Named-person research and outreach need a separately agreed, lawful process; this guide does not authorise either.

07 / use the blank and fictional worksheets separately

Use the blank and fictional worksheets separately

Open the channel-screening downloads in the resource library. The blank CSV contains headers only. The separate fictional CSV contains the three cases above. Neither file supplies researched companies or personal contacts.

The worksheet is written for the invented isolation-valve and workshop-repair brief. It is not a universal distributor score. Before using the blank file for another brief, rename the product, territory and capability fields to your criteria. Keep mandatory and optional tests separate. One row is one company; record extra roles without counting the company again.

Decision rules. Use yes, no or unknown for the capability and retail-only tests:

  • An explicit retail-only exclusion takes precedence.
  • A no on a mandatory criterion excludes.
  • An unknown mandatory criterion holds.
  • Only all required yes answers retain for review.
  • Optional stockholding does not change the decision.

The file stores your decision. It contains no formulas and does no automatic validation.

Field names. In the blank file, company_name and record_context take the place of the fictional file's fictional_company and example_status. Keep the fictional records marked wholly_fictional, and do not import them as prospects.

Evidence references. Each tested fact has its own _evidence_ref field. Put the exact source location and the scope it supports there, not just a homepage. An unknown fact can leave its evidence reference blank; record the missing check in the reason. The source_reference field gives overall record context. It is not evidence for every cell.

Dates. Record the date you checked in checked_date. If facts were checked on different dates, put each date in that fact's evidence reference rather than implying one date covers them all. The teaching cases have no external source and no checked date. Keep relationship scope, exclusivity and willingness separate, and use the reason field for the missing evidence or next question.

08 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Is there an official list of industrial distributors in Ireland?

No. The CRO registers companies, and the CSO counts wholesale enterprises, but neither says what a company distributes.[3][5] Build the list from trade associations, manufacturer pages and company websites, then confirm each legal entity with the CRO.

Does membership of a trade association mean a company is a distributor for my range?

No. Membership shows the company is part of that trade. Product coverage, capability and willingness to take on a new range each need their own evidence.

Can I use CRO Open Data to build a long list?

You can reuse it under its CC BY 4.0 licence with the attribution statement CRO asks for.[4] It identifies companies, not distributors, so you still need product and capability evidence for each one.

What if a distributor already carries a competing brand?

Record the relationship and flag it for review. Do not assume exclusivity, and do not assume the company is free to represent you.

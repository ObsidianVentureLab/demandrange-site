What EU manufacturers spend on goods and services, by country

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

EU-27 manufacturers bought €7,330,954.6 million of goods and services in 2024, according to Eurostat's structural business statistics. That is the closest official measure of what plants buy from suppliers: goods and services bought for use in production or for resale, excluding capital goods. This page gives the dated official figures by EU country and by NACE division, with every confidential cell flagged rather than filled.

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns only the purchases measure. For how many manufacturing enterprises each country has, use our EU manufacturing enterprises by country page. This page does not reprint enterprise counts, turnover, value added or investment.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat sbs_ovw_act, extracted 10 November 2026; dataset last updated 30 September 2026.

  • EU-27 manufacturing (NACE Section C) purchases of goods and services were €7,330,954.6 million in 2024, after €7,502,067.42 million in 2023 and €7,720,730.5 million in 2022.[1][2]
  • By our count, that is a fall of 2.3% from 2023 to 2024, in current euro, not adjusted for prices.[2]
  • Germany's manufacturers bought the most in 2024, at €2,030,471.93 million, 27.7% of the EU-27 total by our count. Italy (€937,013.4 million) and France (€936,904.04 million) were next.[2]
  • By our count, motor vehicles (C29, €1,105,014.07 million) and food products (C10, €1,097,322.31 million) together made up 30.0% of EU-27 manufacturing purchases in 2024.[1]
  • Ireland's manufacturers bought €263,116.98 million of goods and services in 2024. Eurostat marks 11 of Ireland's 24 manufacturing division cells confidential for that year.[1][2]
  • In the UK, the Office for National Statistics records £467,832 million of total purchases of goods, materials and services by manufacturing in 2024, on a different survey and in sterling.[6][7]
02 / what purchases of goods and services means

What "purchases of goods and services" means

Eurostat's glossary defines the measure: "Total purchases of goods and services include the value of all goods and services purchased during the accounting period for resale or consumption in the production process, excluding capital goods (the consumption of which is registered as consumption of fixed capital)."[5]

For a supplier, that has three consequences:

  • It covers what enterprises buy to use up in production or to resell. That makes it a better base than an enterprise count for suppliers of materials, consumables, MRO items, components and contract services. Eurostat also publishes purchases of energy products as a separate variable.[2][3]
  • It excludes capital goods. Machinery and plant bought as fixed assets are counted as investment, a separate SBS measure that this page does not show.
  • It includes goods bought for resale, so a sector with a large trading arm can look bigger than its production inputs alone.

The figures are in million euro at current prices. They are enterprise-level totals by the enterprise's main activity, not plant-level or site-level spend. Eurostat's structural business statistics metadata lists purchases of goods and services among the "input related" variables, beside labour and capital input.[4]

By our count, the five largest countries (Germany, Italy, France, Spain and Poland) accounted for 66.7% of EU-27 manufacturing purchases in 2024. By our count, 70 of the 648 country-division cells for 2024 (27 countries × 24 divisions) are confidential, about 10.8%. Luxembourg (13), Ireland (11) and Malta (6) have the most. In Germany, France, Italy, Spain and Belgium, every 2024 division cell is published.[1]

Eurostat explains why: "a cell is confidential if it is primary or secondary confidential". A cell is primary confidential when too few enterprises contribute or one dominates, and secondary confidential "if it can be used with marginal totals to disclose primary confidential cells".[4] A confidential cell is not a zero. Do not estimate it by subtracting the published divisions from the total.

07 / the uk a different survey in sterling

The UK: a different survey, in sterling

Eurostat's SBS tables in this extract do not include the UK. The closest official UK measure is the Office for National Statistics Annual Business Survey (ABS). Its Section C table records "Total purchases of goods, materials and services" of £467,832 million in 2024 and £464,563 million in 2023, an increase of 0.7% by our count.[6][7]

The ABS dataset was released on 26 May 2026. It covers the UK non-financial business economy on SIC 2007, not Eurostat's EU framework. Do not add the UK figure to the EU-27 total or rank it in the EU table: the survey, currency and definitions differ. For the UK classification, see our UK SIC codes for manufacturing page, and for the UK method, UK manufacturing account research.[6][7]

08 / how to use purchases figures in a supplier search

How to use purchases figures in a supplier search

A purchases total tells you where the spend pool is, not who will buy from you. Use it in three steps:

  1. Rank sectors and countries by spend, not by enterprise count. A country with fewer, larger plants can buy more than one with many small workshops. Our industrial account prioritisation method shows how to weight that spend against fit and evidence.
  2. Match the division to what you sell. NACE divisions are coarse. Drill down to the class codes that match your range with our NACE Rev. 2.1 industrial account lists guide. These 2024 SBS figures still use NACE Rev. 2.
  3. Move from the total to named sites. A large division total does not identify a plant. Use site registers, such as those in our EU industrial emissions portal research guide, and energy signals from our energy audit supplier signals page, to find the plants behind the figure.

Purchases are in current prices. A rise can mean higher input prices, more volume or both. Eurostat says countries may revise their figures between releases, while EU aggregates are recalculated only at the next scheduled EU release.[4]

09 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How much do EU manufacturers spend on goods and services?
EU-27 manufacturers bought €7,330,954.6 million of goods and services in 2024, after €7,502,067.42 million in 2023, according to Eurostat dataset sbs_ovw_act.[2] By our count, that is a 2.3% fall in current euro. The measure covers goods and services bought for use in production or for resale, and excludes capital goods.[5]
Which EU country's manufacturers buy the most?
Germany's manufacturers bought the most in 2024: €2,030,471.93 million, or 27.7% of the EU-27 total by our count. Italy and France followed, at about €937,000 million each. By our count, Germany, Italy, France, Spain and Poland together accounted for 66.7% of EU-27 manufacturing purchases.[2]
Which manufacturing industries buy the most in the EU?
Motor vehicles (C29) and food products (C10) are the two largest EU-27 divisions, at €1,105,014.07 million and €1,097,322.31 million in 2024. By our count, together they made up 30.0% of manufacturing purchases. Computers and electronics (C26) and other manufacturing (C32) are confidential, so they cannot be ranked.[1]
Why are some cells marked confidential?
Eurostat does not publish a cell when too few enterprises contribute, when one dominates, or when the cell could reveal another confidential cell. By our count, 70 of 648 country-division cells for 2024 are confidential. A confidential cell is not a zero, and we do not estimate it from the published cells.[1][4]
How much do Irish manufacturers spend on goods and services?
Irish manufacturers bought €263,116.98 million of goods and services in 2024, after €279,819.54 million in 2023, according to Eurostat.[2] Eurostat marks 11 of Ireland's 24 division cells confidential in 2024, including pharmaceuticals. By our count, those confidential divisions hold 81.2% of the Irish total.[1]
Is there an equivalent figure for UK manufacturers?
Yes, but from a different survey. The Office for National Statistics Annual Business Survey records £467,832 million of total purchases of goods, materials and services by UK manufacturing in 2024.[7] It uses SIC 2007 and sterling, so do not add it to the EU-27 total or rank it against EU countries.
Does a purchases figure show what a plant will buy from me?
No. A purchases figure is an enterprise-level total by country and division, not a plant budget or a buying signal. It shows where the spend pool is. To find named sites and evidence of need, combine it with registers, tenders and site-level research, then rank accounts with our prioritisation method.

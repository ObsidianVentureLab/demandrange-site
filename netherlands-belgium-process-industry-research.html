Process industry account research in the Netherlands and Belgium

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Process-industry account research in the Netherlands and Belgium is mostly about matching plants to legal entities, especially in the main port-side chemical clusters, not about finding names. Build the list in layers from free public sources: national counts, the KvK and KBO registers, SBI and NACE-BEL codes, site evidence and tenders. Then check each account against your brief.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat 2024 data, CBS StatLine, KvK, FPS Economy and Statbel; checked 10 November 2026.

  • Eurostat counted 1,421 chemical manufacturing enterprises (NACE Rev. 2 division C20) in the Netherlands and 634 in Belgium in 2024, 2,055 together by our count.[1]
  • Eurostat counted 92,488 manufacturing enterprises (Section C) in the Netherlands and 37,583 in Belgium in 2024.[1]
  • Statbel counted 779,949 enterprises in Belgium's non-financial market economy in 2024, across industry, construction, trade and services; industry was 5% of them.[18]
  • CBS counted 1,525 businesses in the Dutch chemical industry (SBI 2008 division 20) on 1 October 2026, a provisional figure; 105 of them had 100 or more working persons.[2]
  • KvK has registered every Business Register entry with an SBI 2025 code since the weekend of 6 and 7 September 2025; CBS is moving its statistics to SBI 2025 in phases from 2026 to 2029.[3]
  • NACE-BEL 2025, Belgium's version of NACE Rev. 2.1, has applied since 1 January 2025, and the KBO converted existing activity codes automatically.[6]
02 / how big the dutch and belgian base is

How big the Dutch and Belgian base is

Eurostat's structural business statistics give the headline counts for both countries, on NACE Rev. 2, for 2024 (data updated 30 September 2026):[1]

2024, enterprises Netherlands Belgium
Manufacturing (Section C) 92,488 37,583
Chemicals (C20) 1,421 634

These are enterprise counts, not sites, and they are for market sizing, not a list. They also move between years: Belgium's manufacturing count was 42,355 in 2023 and the Dutch chemicals count was 1,288, so check whether a change reflects the business population or the statistics before you read it as growth or decline.[1] For every other EU country, and the size bands, see our EU manufacturing enterprises by country comparison. For the EU-wide C20 method, see our European chemical manufacturer research guide.

"Process industry" is wider than C20. Depending on your brief it can also cover refined petroleum (C19), pharmaceuticals (C21), food and drink (C10–C11), paper (C17) and basic metals (C24). Define the divisions first, then count.

03 / the national statistics offices add a second view

The national statistics offices add a second view

Netherlands (CBS). CBS StatLine table 81589NED counts businesses by SBI 2008 activity, size and legal form, as a snapshot on the first day of each quarter. On 1 October 2026 (provisional) it counted 88,100 businesses in manufacturing (Industrie) and 1,525 in the chemical industry. Of the chemical businesses, 910 had one working person and 105 had 100 or more.[2]

The CBS and Eurostat numbers are not meant to match. They use different units, dates and code versions, so don't add one to the other or treat the gap as an error. Use CBS for a recent Dutch snapshot and Eurostat when you compare countries.

Belgium (Statbel). Statbel owns the NACE-BEL classification. Its rollout plan for NACE Rev. 2.1 puts structural business statistics for reference year 2025 at 31 October 2026 (preliminary) and 30 June 2027 (final).[5] Until those land, Eurostat's 2024 figures above are on NACE Rev. 2. Statbel's structural business statistics counted 779,949 enterprises in Belgium's non-financial market economy in 2024, with 3,105,845 persons employed. Industry was the smallest of the sectors in that release, at 5% of those enterprises, and employed 13.8 persons per enterprise on average. Those figures cover industry, construction, trade and services, not manufacturing (Section C) alone, so they are a second Belgian view, not a substitute for Eurostat's 37,583 manufacturing enterprises.[18]

04 / build the list in layers

Build the list in layers

  1. Size. Eurostat for the comparison, CBS for a recent Dutch snapshot.[1][2]
  2. Companies. The KvK Business Register (Netherlands) and the KBO, the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises (Belgium).[4][7]
  3. Activity. SBI 2025 and NACE-BEL 2025 codes, mapped to NACE Rev. 2.1.[3][5][6]
  4. Plants. The European Industrial Emissions Portal for the larger installations.[10]
  5. Buying signals. TenderNed, Belgium's e-Procurement platform and TED.[8][9][11]
05 / the two business registers

The two business registers

KvK (Netherlands). The KvK Business Register (Handelsregister) holds information on all businesses and organisations in the Netherlands. Anyone can view the public data, part of it for a fee.[4] The search on kvk.nl is free and shows the KVK number, branch (vestiging) numbers and legal form, but returns at most 50 results per search; KvK points to its API for more.[12] Officers, signing authority and filed accounts sit in paid products: on the KvK tariff list from 1 January 2026, viewing an extract online costs €2.95.[13]

KBO / BCE (Belgium). The KBO is run by FPS Economy and gives each entity one enterprise number and each establishment unit its own establishment unit number. Public Search lets anyone consult the public data on registered entities and their establishment units free of charge. You can search by number, name, address, activity or authorisation, with up to 10 NACE-BEL codes per activity search and at most 200 results.[7]

Two rules matter for list building. Public Search data can only be consulted entity by entity, and reuse or systematic downloading of it is prohibited; FPS Economy has a separate route for public data available for reuse.[7] Record the enterprise number and the establishment unit number on each row, because one Belgian entity can run several sites.

06 / sbi and nace bel the national code systems

SBI and NACE-BEL: the national code systems

SBI (Netherlands). SBI is the Dutch Standard Industrial Classification, maintained by CBS, with KvK assigning the code to each business. In SBI 2025 the first four levels are derived from NACE Rev. 2.1 and, with a few exceptions, match it; the fifth level is a Dutch breakdown.[3][14] Four-level codes get a fifth digit of zero in SBI 2025.[15]

NACE-BEL (Belgium). NACE-BEL 2025 came into force on 1 January 2025, after NACE Rev. 2.1 was adopted, and Eurostat approved its new structure and titles.[5][6] It uses five-digit codes, and FPS Economy also publishes seven-digit activity codes and a 2008–2025 conversion table.[6] The KBO converted active 2008 codes automatically, but not always one to one: where a 2008 code had several 2025 matches, only one 2025 code was entered, so a company's recorded activity can be wrong or incomplete.[6]

For a list, that means:

  • KvK register codes are SBI 2025, while CBS StatLine tables may still be on SBI 2008 during the switch.[2][3]
  • Belgian codes in the KBO are NACE-BEL 2025 for activities from 1 January 2025, but older directories and your own CRM may hold 2008 codes.[6]
  • Keep both versions on each row and map them through NACE Rev. 2.1 with the correspondence tables described in our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.
  • Don't drop a company because its single converted code looks wrong. Check its establishment units and website.
07 / site evidence in the port side chemical clusters

Site evidence in the port-side chemical clusters

Registers show the legal entity and its registered seat, not always where the process plant is. For the larger installations, use the European Environment Agency's European Industrial Emissions Portal. It brings together data on emissions from major industrial sites in Europe and on major accident hazards.[10] Filter by country and activity, then resolve each operator in the KvK or KBO register.

In the main port-side chemical clusters, check whether one address or site holds more than one operator before you merge rows. Keep one row per installation, linked to its operating entity and group. Our EU Industrial Emissions Portal guide covers the download, filters and limits.

Industry bodies help you understand a sector, but membership is not a buying signal. essenscia describes itself as the federation of the chemical industry and life sciences sector in Belgium; VNCI is the Royal Association of the Dutch Chemical Industry.[16][17] We make no claims about their members.

08 / tender portals

Tender portals

  • TenderNed (Netherlands). It carries all public notices of Dutch tenders, both above the EU thresholds and national contracts that authorities tender openly, with more than 20,000 notices a year. You can filter by CPV code, contracting authority, place of performance and closing date, and save searches in an interest profile. TenderNed removes tenders that were completed more than five years ago.[8]
  • e-Procurement (Belgium). FPS BOSA's platform lets companies find and bid for public contracts. You search the Tender Bulletin, or set search profiles that send an email when a matching contract is published.[9]
  • TED. Contracts above EU thresholds also appear on TED; see our TED guide and use CPV codes to filter.[11]
09 / step by step

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat counts, then define it with NACE Rev. 2.1 divisions and their SBI 2025 and NACE-BEL 2025 equivalents.
  2. Pull plants from the Emissions Portal for the larger process sites.
  3. Resolve each operator in the KvK or KBO register. Record the KVK number and branch number, or the enterprise number and establishment unit number.
  4. Check the activity codes against the plant. Converted Belgian codes and older SBI 2008 codes need a second look.
  5. Note the plant-level functions that own the decision, such as plant manager, maintenance, technical purchasing and process engineering. Record the function, not a person.
  6. Add a trigger: a TenderNed or e-Procurement notice, a published expansion, or a new permit.
  7. Log everything in a single sheet with the source and check date for each field.

Fictional example: A fictional Irish maker of industrial valves targets chemical plants in the Netherlands and Belgium. It filters the Emissions Portal for chemical installations in both countries, then resolves each operator. In the KBO it finds that one fictional Belgian group runs two establishment units under one enterprise number, and that one unit still shows a single converted NACE-BEL code that does not fit the plant. It records both sites as separate accounts under one group and flags the code for checking. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

10 / common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Treating CBS and Eurostat counts as the same measure.
  • Reusing or bulk-downloading KBO Public Search data, which FPS Economy prohibits.[7]
  • Filtering on one code version and merging with a list built on another.
  • Counting enterprises when you sell to sites. Many process plants sit behind one entity.
11 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many chemical companies are there in the Netherlands and Belgium?
Eurostat counted 1,421 chemical manufacturing enterprises (NACE C20) in the Netherlands and 634 in Belgium in 2024, or 2,055 together by our count. CBS counted 1,525 Dutch chemical businesses on 1 October 2026, a provisional figure on SBI 2008. These are enterprise counts, not sites, so plants need separate evidence.
Is the Belgian KBO public search free?
Yes. FPS Economy's Public Search lets anyone consult public data on registered entities and their establishment units free of charge. It works entity by entity, returns at most 200 results per search, and prohibits reuse or systematic downloading. For bulk use, FPS Economy points to its separate page on public data available for reuse.
Can I search the Dutch KvK register for free?
Yes, for basic details. The search on kvk.nl shows names, KVK numbers, branch numbers and legal forms, with at most 50 results per search. Officers, signing authority and filed accounts sit in paid products; viewing an extract online costs €2.95 on KvK's January 2026 tariff list. Bulk lookups go through the KVK API.
Should I use SBI 2008 or SBI 2025 codes?
Use both for now. KvK moved every Business Register entry to SBI 2025 in September 2025, but CBS is switching its statistics in phases from 2026 to 2029, so StatLine tables may still use SBI 2008. Keep both codes on each row, and map them through NACE Rev. 2.1 correspondence tables.
What is NACE-BEL 2025?
NACE-BEL 2025 is Belgium's national version of NACE Rev. 2.1, with five-digit codes, in force since 1 January 2025. The KBO converted existing activity codes automatically, but not always one to one: where several 2025 codes matched, only one was entered. Check the codes on each establishment unit before you filter.
Where do I find process plants in the main port-side chemical clusters?
Start with the European Industrial Emissions Portal from the European Environment Agency. It covers emissions from major industrial sites, plus major accident hazard data. Filter by country and activity, then resolve each operator in the KvK or KBO register to confirm the legal entity, establishment unit and activity code.

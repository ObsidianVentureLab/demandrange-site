How to research Irish plastics and packaging manufacturers

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 25 November 2026

Ireland had 484 rubber and plastics manufacturers (NACE C22) in 2024, with 10,569 persons employed and net turnover of €2,878.38 million. That division is not a packaging-only total, and it is not the same as manufacture of articles of paper and paperboard (NACE 17.2), which had 183 enterprises in 2024. By our count, the two groups together come to 667 enterprises. A narrower packaging-manufacturing count, also by our count, is 100 enterprises in 2024: plastic packing goods, corrugated paper and paperboard and containers, and light metal packaging.[1]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns the official size of those Irish manufacturer groups, and the public registers that can name plants. It is not a named-account list. For the wider Irish manufacturing method, see Irish manufacturing account research.

Key facts

  • Irish rubber and plastics manufacturers (NACE C22) in 2024: 484 enterprises, 10,569 persons employed, 10,451 employees, net turnover €2,878.38 million and value added €1,092.8 million. Persons employed were 3.8% of Irish manufacturing employment; net turnover was 0.68% of manufacturing net turnover. No flags. Irish manufacturing as a whole had 19,617 enterprises, 278,377 persons employed and net turnover €423,431.95 million in 2024.[1][2]
  • C22 enterprise counts were 497 in 2021, 481 in 2022, 481 in 2023 and 484 in 2024. Persons employed were 9,645 in 2021, 10,195 in 2022, 10,310 in 2023 and 10,569 in 2024. Net turnover was €2,296.29 million in 2021, €2,728.32 million in 2022, €2,849.35 million in 2023 and €2,878.38 million in 2024.[1]
  • Of the 484 C22 enterprises in 2024, 396 were in plastics products (C222) and 88 were in rubber products (C221). Most of the division is plastics products, not rubber.[1]
  • Plastic packing goods (C2222), the packaging core of C22, had 49 enterprises, 1,660 persons employed, 1,659 employees, net turnover €457.66 million and value added €76.76 million in 2024, or 0.6% of manufacturing employment. In 2023 there were 52 enterprises, 1,802 persons employed and €488.59 million net turnover.[1]
  • Manufacture of articles of paper and paperboard (C172 / NACE 17.2) had 183 enterprises in 2024. Employment, turnover and value added for that group are not published for 2023 or 2024. The last full official year is 2022: 170 enterprises, 3,736 persons employed, net turnover €820.25 million and value added €437.32 million.[1]
  • Of those 183 enterprises in 2024, 44 were in corrugated paper and paperboard and containers (C1721), the same official enterprise count as in 2023. Employment and turnover for C1721 were last published in 2022: 2,224 persons employed and €436.58 million net turnover. The rest of C172 in 2024, by enterprise count only, was paper stationery 76, other paper articles 48, household and sanitary goods 11 and wallpaper 4. These are not all packaging manufacturers.[1]
  • C22 plus C172 enterprises in 2024 come to 667, by our count (484 + 183). That is a "plastics and paper-articles manufacturers" total if defined that way. It is not an official packaging-sector total.[1]
  • A narrower packaging-manufacturing count in 2024, by our count, is 100 enterprises: C1721 (44) + C2222 (49) + light metal packaging, C2592 (7). C2592 is a related official class in fabricated metals, not in NACE 22 or 17.2. Only the enterprise count is published for C2592 in 2024; its last full year is 2023, with 7 enterprises, 311 persons employed and €62.56 million net turnover. Do not treat 100 as a published official aggregate.[1]
  • Of the 484 C22 enterprises in 2024, 95 had zero employees, 194 had 1–4, 52 had 5–9, and 143 had 10 or more. The 143 firms with 10 or more persons employed accounted for 9,781 of the 10,569 persons employed in C22, 92.5% by our count. There were 29 enterprise births in C22 in 2024.[2]
  • As a historical official check only, CSO business demography (BRA16) for 2020 recorded 473 active enterprises in rubber and plastic products (NACE 22), with 9,058 persons engaged and 8,958 employees. Irish manufacturing in 2020 had 16,894 active enterprises and 236,067 persons engaged. CSO "persons engaged" is not the same series as Eurostat "persons employed". CSO has no 17.2 cell in BRA16. Those NACE-detailed CSO tables still end in 2020.[3]
  • Packaging activities (NACE N8292), a business-support service rather than manufacturing, had 132 enterprises in 2024. CSO recorded 129 active enterprises in this class in 2020. Do not add this count to the manufacturer totals.[1][3]
02 / what the codes cover and what they do not

What the codes cover, and what they do not

The useful official groups for this brief are NACE Rev. 2 22 (manufacture of rubber and plastic products) and 17.2 (manufacture of articles of paper and paperboard). They are not the same thing, and neither is a packaging-only total.[1]

  • C22 includes rubber products (C221) and plastics products (C222). Plastic packing goods (C2222) sits inside C222.
  • C172 includes corrugated paper and paperboard and containers (C1721), and also paper stationery, wallpaper, household and sanitary goods and other paper articles.
  • C2592, light metal packaging, is a related official class in fabricated metals. It is not part of NACE 22 or 17.2. It is included only in the narrower packaging-manufacturing count below, and only with that labelled as by our count.
  • N8292, packaging activities, is a business-support service. It is not manufacturing and is kept off the manufacturer headlines.

These figures count enterprises and, where published, persons employed, employees, net turnover and value added. They are national totals for Ireland, not a list of named plants, and not a buying signal.[1][2]

05 / a narrower packaging manufacturing count by our count

A narrower packaging-manufacturing count, by our count

If the brief is packaging manufacture rather than all of C22 and C172, add only the official packaging classes: corrugated paper and paperboard and containers (C1721, 44 enterprises in 2024), plastic packing goods (C2222, 49) and light metal packaging (C2592, 7). That sum is 100 enterprises in 2024, by our count. It is not a published official aggregate, and C2592 sits in fabricated metals, not in NACE 22 or 17.2.[1]

Light metal packaging has only an enterprise count in 2024. Its last full official year is 2023: 7 enterprises, 311 persons employed and €62.56 million net turnover. Do not fill the missing 2024 employment or turnover cells from the 2023 figures.[1]

By our count, C22 plus C172 came to 667 enterprises in 2024 (484 + 183). Use that only as a "plastics and paper-articles manufacturers" total, and only with the by-our-count label. It is wider than packaging manufacture.[1]

Packaging activities (N8292) had 132 enterprises in 2024, and 129 active enterprises in 2020 in the CSO table. That class is a business-support service. Do not add it to either manufacturer total.[1][3]

06 / size classes for account research c22 2024

Size classes for account research (C22, 2024)

Most C22 employment sits in a minority of enterprises. Of 484 enterprises in 2024, 95 had zero employees, 194 had 1–4, 52 had 5–9, and 143 had 10 or more. The 143 firms with 10 or more persons employed accounted for 9,781 of the 10,569 persons employed in C22 — 92.5%, by our count.[2]

There were 29 enterprise births in C22 in 2024. Births are a flow, not a substitute for the stock of 484 enterprises, and they do not name the firms.[2]

07 / official sources that can name plants

Official sources that can name plants

Eurostat and CSO give national totals. They do not publish a public named list of Irish plastics or packaging manufacturers.[1][3]

The Environmental Protection Agency grants and enforces Industrial Emissions (IE) and Integrated Pollution Control (IPC) licences for specified activities listed in the First Schedule to the Environmental Protection Agency Act 1992, as amended. The public licence search is the official starting point, with the Agency's Industrial Emissions and Integrated Pollution Control explainer pages for the two licence types.[4][5][6]

This page does not give a national count of plastics or packaging licences. No official national count was verified for those activities on the check date. A licence search can name a site; a sector total cannot. Do not treat a licence on its own as evidence that a plant will buy. For the method of using environmental-register evidence, see environmental-register evidence for industrial accounts.[4]

The Companies Registration Office is a public source of legal names. This page does not publish registration numbers or contact details, and it does not list sites.

08 / a historical cso check 2020 only

A historical CSO check, 2020 only

CSO table BRA16, and the related NACE-detailed demography tables, still end in 2020 (table updated 22 January 2025). Use those cells only as a historical official figure, with the year shown, not as the current size of the sector.[3]

In 2020, CSO recorded 473 active enterprises in rubber and plastic products (NACE 22), with 9,058 persons engaged and 8,958 employees. Manufacturing as a whole had 16,894 active enterprises and 236,067 persons engaged in 2020. CSO "persons engaged" is the official CSO wording and is not the Eurostat "persons employed" series. CSO does not publish a 17.2 cell in BRA16; paper appears only as the mixed group of paper and paper products, printing and reproduction of recorded media (17, 18), which is not used here as a 17.2 figure.[3]

For 2021 to 2024, including C172, C1721 and C2222, this page uses Eurostat.[1]

09 / how to use these figures in a supplier search

How to use these figures in a supplier search

  1. Define the brief against official classes, not a loose "plastics and packaging" label. Decide whether you need all of C22 and C172, only the packaging classes (C2222, C1721 and, if relevant, C2592), or a single class such as plastic packing goods.
  2. Size the market from the latest official cells. Use 2024 enterprise counts where they are published. For C172 employment and turnover, and for C1721 and C2592 employment and turnover, use the last full official year and keep that year beside the figure.
  3. Use size classes to scope the work. In C22, most employment is in the 143 enterprises with 10 or more persons employed. That is a research-scope signal, not a named-account claim.[2]
  4. Move from totals to names with public registers. Search EPA licences for in-scope activities and confirm legal names in the Companies Registration Office. Keep evidence types apart: a sector total, a licence and a legal name answer different questions.
  5. Do not add packaging-service firms to a manufacturer list. N8292 is a service class.

To go from a sector definition to an account list, use Irish manufacturing account research and our industrial ideal customer profile method.

10 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many Irish plastics and packaging manufacturers are there?
There is no single official total. In 2024 Ireland had 484 rubber and plastics manufacturers (NACE C22) and 183 manufacturers of paper and paperboard articles (NACE 17.2), or 667 enterprises by our count. By our count, a narrower packaging-manufacturing group is 100 enterprises: plastic packing goods, corrugated containers, and light metal packaging.[1]
Is NACE 22 the same as packaging manufacturing?
No. NACE C22 is the manufacture of rubber and plastic products, not packaging only. In 2024 it had 484 enterprises: 396 in plastics products and 88 in rubber products. The packaging core of that division is plastic packing goods (C2222), with 49 enterprises, 1,660 persons employed and €457.66 million net turnover in 2024.[1]
What does NACE 17.2 cover?
It covers manufacture of articles of paper and paperboard, not packaging only. Ireland had 183 such enterprises in 2024, including 44 in corrugated paper and paperboard and containers. The rest were paper stationery (76), other paper articles (48), household and sanitary goods (11) and wallpaper (4). Employment and turnover for the group were last published in full for 2022.[1]
Where can I find named Irish plants?
Use the Environmental Protection Agency's public licence search for Industrial Emissions and Integrated Pollution Control licences, and the Companies Registration Office for legal names. This page does not publish a national count of plastics or packaging licences, and it does not list sites. A sector total is not a named-account list. See our guide to environmental-register evidence for the method.[4][5][6]
Does CSO still publish a recent NACE-detailed count?
No. CSO's NACE-detailed business demography tables, including BRA16, still end in 2020. In 2020, CSO recorded 473 active enterprises in rubber and plastic products (NACE 22), with 9,058 persons engaged. That table has no 17.2 cell. For 2021 to 2024, this page uses Eurostat Structural Business Statistics, which do publish the recent C22 and C172 enterprise counts.[1][3]
Should packaging-service firms be added to the manufacturer count?
No. NACE N8292, packaging activities, is a business-support service, not manufacturing. Eurostat recorded 132 such enterprises in Ireland in 2024. CSO recorded 129 active enterprises in this class in 2020. Keep that count separate from the manufacturer totals and from the narrower packaging-manufacturing count of 100 enterprises, by our count.[1][3]

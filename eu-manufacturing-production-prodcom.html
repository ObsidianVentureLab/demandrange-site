Prodcom: how to see what EU plants make, product by product

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Prodcom is Eurostat's free manufacturing database of sold production by product. For each EU country it gives the value in euro, and usually the volume, of about 4,000 manufactured products, each with an 8-digit code.[1][2][3] An enterprise count tells you how many manufacturers a country has. Prodcom tells you what its plants actually make, and how much of it.

02 / what prodcom measures and what it doesn t

What Prodcom measures, and what it doesn't

Prodcom is a product statistic, not an activity statistic. National statistical institutes survey enterprises in NACE sections B and C, plus a materials-recovery class in section E, and report, for each product on the Prodcom List, how much was made in the country during the year.[3] Eurostat then adds the national figures into EU totals.[3]

The main variables are:[2][3]

  • Sold production: production carried out at any time and sold (invoiced) during the year, given as a value in euro (collected in thousands of national currency and converted) and as a volume in a unit set for each product, such as kilograms, square metres or number of items.
  • Production under sub-contracted operations: made by a subcontractor and sold to the principal. Since reference year 2021 this is collected separately from sold production.
  • Actual production: all production, including output used inside the same enterprise. It is only collected for some products and sits in a separate dataset (DS-059368).

Because it counts products, Prodcom is not strictly comparable with structural business statistics, which classify a whole enterprise by its main activity.[3] A plant classed in one NACE class can still report products from several others. That is why Prodcom answers a different question from our EU industrial enterprises by country table: not how many manufacturers there are, but what their plants produce.

03 / how a prodcom code rolls up to nace

How a Prodcom code rolls up to NACE

Every 8-digit Prodcom code carries its classification in its digits:[2][3]

Digits Classification Example: 28133100, parts of pumps for liquids
First 4 NACE Rev. 2.1 class 28.13, manufacture of other pumps and compressors
First 6 CPA 2.2 product 28.13.31
All 8 Prodcom heading 28133100

From reference year 2025, Prodcom codes are based on NACE Rev. 2.1 and CPA 2.2.[2][3] So a supplier can start from a product it sells into, find its Prodcom heading, and read off the NACE class whose companies are most likely to make it. Our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide for industrial account lists covers how to use those classes to build and filter a list.

One caution on history. The 2025 data use the new codes. Eurostat says the years 2008 to 2024 converted to NACE Rev. 2.1 will follow in mid-July 2027, and the conversion is incomplete where old and new codes don't match directly.[3] Until then, the older series sit in separate NACE Rev. 2 datasets (DS-059358 and DS-059359), so check codes before you compare years.[3]

Flags, as Eurostat defines them:[4]

  • (R) rounded. Eurostat applies controlled rounding to EU totals that contain confidential national cells. The true value lies within plus or minus the published rounding base: €2 million for processed cheese, €40 million for corrugated board, €700 million for ready-mixed concrete and €30 million for pump parts.[3][4][5]
  • (E) a reliable estimate, accurate enough to publish at national level. An EU total that includes a low-reliability national estimate is also flagged E, which fits the polyethylene sacks row.[4]
  • (U) a low-reliability estimate, suppressed from the national data but used in the EU total.[4]
  • (C) confidential, not published.[4]

The same query returns volumes. For Ireland in 2025, Eurostat shows sold production of 8,040,724,440 kg of ready-mixed concrete, 75,334,655 kg of polyethylene sacks and bags, 34,044,280 kg of corrugated paper and paperboard and 21,249,805 kg of processed cheese.[5] Pump parts have no volume: the quantity flag is "not applicable", so only a value is collected.[4][5]

What a supplier can read from it. Ireland's ready-mixed concrete works out at about €65 per tonne of sold production in 2025, by our count (€526.6 million over about 8.04 million tonnes), against about €45 per tonne for Germany (€3,274.7 million over about 72.7 million tonnes).[5] Eurostat itself compares unit values across countries as a quality check, but treats differences as something to explain, not as an error by default.[3] For account research, a sold production value shows whether a country makes a product at scale. It doesn't tell you which plants make it.

05 / how to pull prodcom data for your product

How to pull Prodcom data for your product

  1. Find your heading. Search the Prodcom List 2025 (published in EU Vocabularies) for the product your customers make, or the product your equipment goes into. Note the 8-digit code and its unit.[3]
  2. Open the dataset. From the Prodcom database page, open DS-059367 (sold production, exports and imports by Prodcom List, CPA 2.2) in Eurostat's data browser.[1][4]
  3. Set the dimensions. Choose reporter (country or EU27_2020), product, the indicators PRODVAL (value, euro) and PRODQNT (volume), plus the flag fields PVALFLAG and PQNTFLAG, and the year.[4]
  4. Read the flags before the numbers. A blank cell is not a zero. Check for C, E, U and R, and read the rounding base on any EU total.[4]
  5. Add trade if you need it. The same dataset carries export and import values and quantities from external trade statistics, because most Prodcom codes link to Combined Nomenclature headings.[2][4]
  6. Map back to NACE. Take the first 4 digits and use the NACE class to find the companies likely to make the product, then confirm each company and plant from registers and site evidence.[2][3]
  7. Record the date. Note the dataset code and the update date with every figure. Eurostat republishes revised data most months.[3]

The API returns the same cells in JSON, which is useful if you track several products. The query behind our table is listed in the sources.[5]

06 / prodcom s confidentiality gaps

Prodcom's confidentiality gaps

Prodcom has more holes than most Eurostat business tables, because a product is often made by only a few plants in a country:

  • Country cells go dark. National institutes mark some cells confidential and Eurostat must suppress them.[3] In the table above, Poland's polyethylene sacks and pump parts values are confidential. For Ireland in 2025, by our count, 1,927 of the product codes in the dataset carry a confidential value flag, 791 show a published value above zero and 1,419 show zero.[6] A smaller economy with fewer producers per product hides more.
  • EU totals are rounded, not hidden. Eurostat can publish an EU total that contains confidential cells if it is safe to do so, and now uses controlled rounding instead of suppressing unsafe totals.[3] Treat an R value as a range.
  • Low-reliability estimates vanish nationally. A U-flagged country figure is removed from the national data but kept inside the EU total.[4]
  • Coverage is a cut-off sample. Most countries survey enterprises above a threshold, with sampling of small enterprises mainly where needed to reach 90% of national production in a NACE class. No country can prove it has found every producer of a product.[3]
  • The volume unit varies. Some products have no quantity at all, and units differ by product, so check the unit before you compare.[3][4]

When a cell is confidential, don't guess it from the EU total. Use the neighbouring 6-digit CPA or 4-digit NACE level, another year, or the national institute's own release.

07 / the uk is no longer in eurostat s prodcom

The UK is no longer in Eurostat's Prodcom

Eurostat's Prodcom reference area is the EU, Norway, Iceland and the candidate countries, so the UK is not covered.[3] The UK runs its own ProdCom survey through the Office for National Statistics (ONS). The latest release, UK manufacturers' sales by product: 2025, came out on 24 July 2026, and ONS says it suppresses some product figures to avoid disclosing data on individual businesses.[7] Our query for the UK on ready-mixed concrete in DS-059367 returned no 2025 value.[5] For UK product data, use the ONS release; for the UK activity codes, see our UK SIC codes for manufacturing guide.

08 / how prodcom fits account research

How Prodcom fits account research

Prodcom is a sizing and targeting tool, not a list. Use it to:

  • Pick countries. See which EU countries make the products your offer serves, by value and volume.
  • Pick NACE classes. Turn product headings into classes, then into companies.
  • Check a story. If a market brief says a country is big in a product, Prodcom shows whether sold production backs it up.

Then build the account list from registers, permits and plant evidence. Our guides to Irish manufacturing account research and German industrial account research show that step country by country.

09 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What is the Prodcom database?
Prodcom is Eurostat's database of sold production by product for EU countries. National statistical institutes survey manufacturers each year and report, for about 4,000 products with 8-digit codes, the value in euro and usually the volume sold. Eurostat publishes the country figures and EU totals free, together with related export and import data.
How is Prodcom different from an enterprise count?
An enterprise count classifies each business by its main activity and tells you how many there are. Prodcom counts products instead: what was made and sold, in value and volume, whichever enterprise made it. So Prodcom shows what a country's plants produce, while an enterprise count shows how many manufacturers a country has.
Why are some Prodcom figures marked confidential?
Prodcom figures are marked confidential when publishing them could reveal data about individual producers, often because few plants in a country make that product. National institutes flag those cells, and Eurostat must suppress them. EU totals containing confidential cells can still appear, but with controlled rounding, so treat them as a range.
How do Prodcom codes relate to NACE codes?
The first 4 digits of an 8-digit Prodcom code are the NACE Rev. 2.1 class, and the first 6 digits are the CPA 2.2 product. The last 2 digits are specific to Prodcom. From reference year 2025, Prodcom uses NACE Rev. 2.1, so you can roll any product up to its industry class.
When is Prodcom data updated?
Prodcom data are first released every July for the previous year, so 2025 data came out in July 2026. Countries can send revisions at any time, and Eurostat republishes in most months, sometimes for earlier years too. Always record the dataset code and its update date beside any figure you use.
Does Prodcom include the UK?
No. Eurostat's Prodcom covers the EU, Norway, Iceland and candidate countries, not the UK. The UK publishes its own ProdCom survey through the Office for National Statistics, as UK manufacturers' sales by product. The 2025 edition was released on 24 July 2026. Like Eurostat, ONS suppresses some product figures to protect individual businesses.

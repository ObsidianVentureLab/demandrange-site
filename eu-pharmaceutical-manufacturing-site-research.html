EU pharmaceutical manufacturing site research: authorisations and GMP certificates by country

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

A useful pharmaceutical account is an authorised, inspected operation, not a company name beside a medicine. Across the EU, two public record types in one official database get you closest to that: manufacturing and importation authorisations (MIAs) and good manufacturing practice (GMP) certificates, both held in EudraGMDP, the European Medicines Agency's database.[1]

This guide shows how to search both by EU country, gives a dated count table you can reproduce, and keeps Eurostat's enterprise counts as context only. For the Irish view, with the national regulator and environmental licences, see Irish pharmaceutical manufacturing account research. This page does not repeat that method.

01 / key facts

Key facts

EMA EudraGMDP and Eurostat; checked 10 November 2026.

  • EMA describes EudraGMDP as the EU database on manufacturing, import and wholesale-distribution authorisations and on GMP and good-distribution-practice (GDP) certificates. A public version has been available since 2011, showing information that is not commercially or personally confidential.[1]
  • By our count of 27 country searches in the public EudraGMDP MIA module, searched 10 November 2026, the EU countries had 7,292 MIA documents listed. Germany had the most, with 1,642.[3][4]
  • By our count of the same 27 country searches in the GMP certificate module, with non-compliance reports left out, EudraGMDP listed 13,981 GMP certificate documents for sites in EU countries, searched 10 November 2026. Germany again had the most, with 2,249.[6][7]
  • Eurostat estimates 4,000 enterprises in the EU in 2024 whose main activity is the manufacture of basic pharmaceutical products and pharmaceutical preparations (NACE Rev. 2 division C21). The figure carries Eurostat's "estimated" flag.[8]
  • The content of EudraGMDP is provided by the national competent authorities of the EEA, and EMA accepts no liability for it, so counts reflect each authority's documents, not a census of plants.[4]
02 / what eudragmdp holds and what it does not

What EudraGMDP holds, and what it does not

EudraGMDP's public portal has separate modules for MIAs, GMP certificates, active-substance registrations (API REG), wholesale-distribution authorisations (WDA) and GDP certificates.[2] This guide uses only the first two, because they are the records tied to manufacturing and importation.

  • MIAs. EudraGMDP states that manufacturing and importation authorisations are to be entered in the database under article 40(4) of Directive 2001/83/EC and article 88(4) of Regulation (EU) 2019/6, using a Union format document.[3] The search form lets you filter by five legal bases: Art. 40 of Directive 2001/83/EC, Art. 44 of Directive 2001/82/EC, Art. 13 of Directive 2001/20/EC, Art. 88 of Regulation (EU) 2019/6 and Art. 61 of Regulation (EU) No 536/2014.[4]
  • GMP certificates. EudraGMDP states that GMP certificates are to be entered under article 111(6) of Directive 2001/83/EC and article 91(3) of Regulation (EU) 2019/6.[6] Each result row shows a certificate number, document type, MIA number, site name and address, country and inspection end date.[7]
  • What it leaves out. Internet users do not see confidential information or commercially sensitive data.[5] Neither module lists equipment, suppliers, headcount, contacts or buying plans.

A missing record is not proof that a site is unauthorised. Some content is hidden from public users, and the data depend on what each national authority has entered.[4][5]

Filters used, so you can reproduce the counts:

  • MIA: open the public portal, choose MIA, then Search MIA.[3] Set Country to the EU country. Leave every other field empty, leave all five legal-basis boxes unticked and leave Enable Operations Search unticked. Click Search and read the "of N" total.[4]
  • GMP certificates: choose GMP, then GMP Certificates.[6] Set Country, leave every other field empty, leave Enable Scope Search, Enable Inspection Search and Enable Operations Search unticked, and untick Include Non-Compliance Report, which is ticked by default. Click Search and read the total.[7]
  • We re-ran four MIA searches (Germany, Czechia, Ireland and Spain) and two GMP searches (Germany and Ireland) a few minutes later on the same day, and got the same totals. France's MIA total was re-searched later the same day and returned 528 on two searches.

How to read the table:

  • A count is of documents, not sites or companies. The EU-27 sums add country searches; they are not figures EudraGMDP publishes.
  • EudraGMDP's own help says withdrawn and suspended MIAs are included only when you tick options to include them.[5] The public form we used on 10 November 2026 showed no such options, so treat the MIA totals as what the default public search returns.
  • The GMP results include certificates with older inspection end dates. Check each certificate's date before you treat it as current evidence.[7]
  • Do not rank countries as markets from these counts. Each national authority enters its own documents, and how it issues and splits them can differ.[4] Compare with Eurostat's C21 enterprise count and with the EU manufacturing enterprises by country page before you plan territory.
  • The EEA list in EudraGMDP also includes non-EU EEA members. This table covers the 27 EU countries only.
04 / turn a document into a site record

Turn a document into a site record

  1. Pick the operation first. Decide which authorised operations matter for your offer. The MIA form's operations search lists manufacturing, batch-certification, primary-packaging and importation operations, such as sterile products and aseptic preparation.[4]
  2. Run one search per operation if you need a clean count. EudraGMDP's help says selected operations combine with OR, so more ticks return more MIAs.[5]
  3. Open each document and record the MIA number, the authorisation holder, the site details and the legal basis exactly as shown.[4]
  4. Add the GMP certificate for the same site. The GMP form accepts an MIA number, so you can link the two records and read the latest inspection end date.[7]
  5. Resolve the company. Match the holder to a legal entity in the national business register, and record the group separately.
  6. Keep the gaps. Equipment, decision-makers and timing stay "unknown" until you have separate evidence.

Pharmaceutical manufacturing (NACE C21) is a separate division from chemicals (C20).[8] For chemical producers, formulators and active-ingredient chemistry upstream of a pharmaceutical site, use European chemical manufacturer research.

05 / worked example fictional

Worked example (fictional)

This example is fictional. The supplier, the companies and the records are invented.

A fictional supplier of isolators and cleanroom services wants EU sites that make sterile products. Its researcher runs the MIA search for three EU countries with Enable Operations Search ticked and only the aseptically prepared operations selected, and logs each country's total with the date.

  • Record A lists aseptic preparation and batch certification, and a GMP certificate for the same MIA number has an inspection end date in 2026. Include for further research.
  • Record B lists only secondary packaging and importation. Exclude from this brief, but keep it for a packaging brief.
  • Record C has an MIA but its latest GMP certificate dates from several years ago. Hold until a newer certificate or another source confirms the operation is active.

The companies and decisions are fictional and only illustrate the method.

Checklist before you use an EudraGMDP list

  • The country, filters and search date are recorded for every count
  • Counts are labelled as documents, not sites or companies
  • Each MIA is linked to a GMP certificate by MIA number where one exists
  • Every GMP certificate's inspection end date is checked
  • The holder is resolved to a legal entity and, where possible, a group
  • Missing equipment, size and contact data is marked unknown, not guessed
06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many pharmaceutical manufacturing authorisations are there in the EU?
By our count, 7,292, searched 10 November 2026. That is the sum of 27 public EudraGMDP MIA searches, one per EU country, with only the country set. Germany had the most, with 1,642. The figure counts documents, not sites or companies, and is not a total EudraGMDP publishes itself.[4]
What is the difference between an MIA and a GMP certificate?
An MIA authorises manufacturing or importation, and EudraGMDP records it under article 40(4) of Directive 2001/83/EC and article 88(4) of Regulation (EU) 2019/6. A GMP certificate records compliance with good manufacturing practice, and each result shows an inspection end date. Link the two by MIA number to see what a site may do and when it was inspected.[3][6][7]
How do I search EudraGMDP by country?
Open the public EudraGMDP portal, choose MIA and then Search MIA, set Country and click Search. Country is the only field you must fill in, and an otherwise empty search returns every document for that country. Repeat in the GMP module with Include Non-Compliance Report unticked to list certificates.[4][5][7]
Why do the counts differ so much between EU countries?
Mostly because they count documents, not plants. Each national competent authority enters its own records, and issuing and splitting practices can differ. Do not treat the table as a market ranking. Compare it with Eurostat's C21 enterprise counts, then open individual documents to confirm what each site is authorised to do.[4][8]
How many pharmaceutical manufacturers are there in the EU?
Eurostat estimates 4,000 enterprises in the EU in 2024 whose main activity is NACE division C21, the manufacture of basic pharmaceutical products and pharmaceutical preparations. That is an enterprise count by main activity, not a site count, so it will not match the number of EudraGMDP documents in any country.[8]
Does EudraGMDP show UK pharmaceutical sites?
Mostly not as current records. EudraGMDP says documents issued by UK authorities up to 31 December 2020 remain available for consultation, but they are no longer included or updated from 1 January 2021, "with the exception of the documents pertaining to sites located in Northern Ireland". Use the UK regulator's own records for other UK sites.[4]

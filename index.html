01 / Research classes

Two research classes. One standard of relevance.

Industry-matched leads

Also called Look Alike leads: companies that match your agreed industries, territory and company criteria. An active or upcoming project is not required. Look Alike means a match to the written brief, not a prediction of buying intent.

Opportunity-signal leads

Relevant companies with an identified, evidenced project or development. The record explains what changed, why it may matter to your category and what remains unknown.

Target mix: we aim for 90% industry-matched leads and 10% opportunity-signal leads. This is not a guaranteed split. The final mix, counts and deliverables are agreed in writing. Both classes require fit checks and source traceability.

02 / The brief

Start with what makes an account relevant.

Tell us what you sell, which industries and countries matter, and which companies should be excluded. Your target countries define the research territory, not where your sales team is based. We use that brief to explain why a business belongs in your prospect base—not just to attach an industry label.

A company-level record brings together the business or agreed site, source-supported fit, research class, review dates and unresolved questions. Named contacts, email addresses and direct dials are not included by default.

04 / Written scope

A written scope, not a stock package.

Agree the target profile, company or site unit, sources, fields, class treatment and acceptance criteria before research begins. Volume, timing, price and any replacement terms belong in that written scope.

Opportunity Intelligence remains part of the service for work that needs project context. A project signal does not establish buying intent, an accessible buyer or an open equipment package. Research does not include outreach by default.

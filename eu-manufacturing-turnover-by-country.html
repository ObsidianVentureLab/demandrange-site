Are EU manufacturers selling more? Turnover growth by country

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Yes, since March 2026: EU-27 manufacturing turnover was 5.9% higher in August 2026 than a year earlier, after a 1.5% rise for 2025 as a whole and a 2.3% fall in 2024. Those are Eurostat's short-term business statistics, calendar adjusted, in current prices.[1][4]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one thing: how fast manufacturers' sales are growing or shrinking, by EU country, for all manufacturing and for two supplier-heavy divisions, electrical equipment (NACE C27) and machinery (C28), split into home-market and non-domestic sales. For how many manufacturing enterprises each country has and their annual turnover levels, see our EU manufacturing enterprises by country page. For what manufacturers spend with suppliers, see EU manufacturing purchases by country.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat sts_intv_a, sts_intvd_a, sts_intvnd_a and sts_intv_m; extracted 24 November 2026.

  • EU-27 manufacturing turnover fell 2.3% in 2024 and rose 1.5% in 2025, calendar adjusted, on the previous year.[1]
  • In 2025, EU-27 manufacturers' home-market turnover rose 1.2% and their non-domestic turnover rose 1.8%.[2][3]
  • 17 of the 25 EU countries with a published 2025 figure grew manufacturing turnover, by our count. Cyprus (+8.8%), Croatia (+8.0%) and Slovakia (+6.5%) grew fastest; Hungary (−2.8%) fell furthest.[1]
  • In August 2026, EU-27 manufacturing turnover was 5.9% above August 2025. Electrical equipment was up 8.0% and machinery up 4.9%.[4]
  • All 25 EU countries with a published August 2026 figure were above their August 2025 level, by our count.[4]
  • Eurostat publishes no all-manufacturing or machinery turnover figure for Ireland in these tables. Irish electrical equipment turnover rose 56.2% in 2025.[1][4]

By our count, 2025 was a turn: 16 of the 25 countries shrank in 2024, while 17 grew in 2025, 1 was flat and 7 fell. Germany was still slightly down in 2025 (−0.3%) after −3.7% in 2024. For how to work in that market, see our guide to finding industrial customers in Germany.[1]

03 / home market and export market sales

Home market and export market sales

Eurostat splits turnover by the first buyer's residence. Home-market (domestic) turnover is sold to buyers resident in the same country. Non-domestic turnover is sold to buyers outside it, so for an EU country it includes sales to other EU member states, not only exports outside the EU.[5]

For the EU-27 aggregate, non-domestic turnover grew faster than home-market turnover in 2025: +1.8% against +1.2%. In 2024 it fell less: −1.7% against −2.8%.[2][3] Eurostat also splits non-domestic sales into the euro area (+1.1% in 2025) and outside the euro area (+2.7% in 2025).[3]

By our count, 23 EU countries publish both the home and non-domestic figures for 2025, and 11 of them saw non-domestic sales grow faster than home sales. They include Germany, France, Austria and the Netherlands. Slovakia's home market (+11.8%) and Luxembourg's (+13.0%) grew much faster than their non-domestic sales.[2][3]

For a supplier, the split hints at where a customer's growth is coming from. A manufacturer whose home market is flat but whose non-domestic sales are growing is often exposed to other countries' demand, not its own.

In 2025, the EU-27 home-market turnover of electrical equipment makers rose 1.8% and their non-domestic turnover 3.0%. For machinery, home-market turnover rose 2.0% and non-domestic 1.6%.[2][3]

Ireland. Of the three activities on this page, Eurostat publishes Irish turnover for electrical equipment only; Irish all-manufacturing and machinery cells are confidential. 06 / turnover is value not volume

Turnover is value, not volume

Eurostat defines net turnover as the totals invoiced to third parties in the period, including charges passed on such as transport and packaging, and excluding taxes on the goods or services invoiced. The index shows the development of the market for goods and services in value terms.[5]

That matters when you read the growth rates:

  • Turnover is measured in current prices. A rise can come from higher prices, more units sold, or both, and these tables cannot separate them. Eurostat's production index, a volume measure, is a separate series.[5]
  • These are growth rates on an index, not euro levels. They do not tell you which country's manufacturing sector is largest; for that, use the annual levels on our EU manufacturing enterprises by country page.
  • Eurostat compiles these figures from kind-of-activity units, grouped by activity and by country, not by site.[5] A national rate is a backdrop for account research, not a buying signal for any plant.

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Is European manufacturing demand recovering in 2026?
Yes, on turnover. EU-27 manufacturing turnover was 5.9% above its year-earlier level in August 2026, the sixth month in a row above the year before by our count, after falls in January and February. Turnover is in current prices, so part of the rise may be price rather than volume. The figures are calendar adjusted.[4]
Which EU country's manufacturing turnover grew fastest in 2025?
Cyprus grew fastest in 2025, at +8.8% on 2024, followed by Croatia at +8.0% and Slovakia at +6.5%, by our ranking of Eurostat's calendar-adjusted figures. Hungary fell furthest, at −2.8%. Ireland and Portugal are confidential, so they cannot be ranked. Seventeen of the 25 published countries grew, by our count.[1]
Did EU manufacturers sell more at home or abroad in 2025?
Abroad grew a little faster. EU-27 non-domestic turnover rose 1.8% in 2025, against 1.2% in the home market. Non-domestic means buyers outside each manufacturer's own country, so it includes sales to other EU member states. Sales outside the euro area grew 2.7%, and sales within it 1.1%.[2][3]
How is electrical equipment manufacturing doing in the EU?
It is growing faster than manufacturing overall. EU-27 electrical equipment turnover rose 2.4% in 2025 after a 6.3% fall in 2024, and was 8.0% above its year-earlier level in August 2026. Machinery grew 1.9% in 2025 and was up 4.9% in August 2026.[1][4]
Is there a manufacturing turnover figure for Ireland?
Not for all manufacturing. Eurostat marks Ireland's all-manufacturing and machinery turnover confidential in these tables, so no Irish manufacturing-wide growth rate is published there. Electrical equipment is published: Irish turnover in that division rose 56.2% in 2025. Its monthly figures swing sharply, so read them with care.[1][4]
Is turnover growth the same as production growth?
No. Turnover is the value invoiced to customers at current prices, so it rises with higher prices as well as with more output. Production is a volume measure that strips out price changes. Eurostat publishes them as separate series, and the two can move differently, especially when prices are changing fast.[5]

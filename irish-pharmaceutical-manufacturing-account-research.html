Irish pharmaceutical manufacturing companies: how to research them as accounts

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

For an industrial supplier, a useful pharmaceutical account is a named site with an evidenced activity, not a company name beside a medicine. Two public registers show which Irish pharmaceutical manufacturing companies operate which sites: HPRA manufacturing authorisations and EPA licences for chemical and pharmaceutical production sites. This guide shows how to use both.

Key facts (HPRA Annual Report 2025, EudraGMDP and EPA LEAP; checked 28 October 2026)

  • The HPRA's Annual Report 2025 counts 156 manufacturers of human medicines in Ireland in 2025, against 161 in 2024, plus 88 manufacturers of investigational medicinal products (a scale guide; the report does not say whether it counts holders, authorisations or sites).[6]
  • The same report records 108 good manufacturing practice (GMP) inspections in 2025 at sites producing human medicines or active substances.[6]
  • Our own EudraGMDP search on 28 October 2026 (Search MIA, Country set to Ireland, withdrawn and suspended authorisations excluded) listed 307 Irish manufacturing authorisations (MIAs); an MIA is an authorisation, not a site or a company.[5]
  • Our own LEAP search on 28 October 2026 (EPA licence register filtered to Type Industrial Emissions Licence, Sector Chemicals, Status Licensed) returned 71 licences; the site details of 41 of them carry NACE code 2110 or 2120 (pharmaceuticals).[8]

The territory here is the Republic of Ireland. If your brief also covers Northern Ireland, say so and scope it separately.

01 / how many authorised manufacturers ireland has

How many authorised manufacturers Ireland has

The HPRA's 2025 annual report gives these key figures for human medicines:[6]

HPRA key figure (human medicines) 2023 2024 2025
Manufacturers 151 161 156
Manufacturers of investigational medicinal products 82 89 88
Active substance registrations: manufacturers 22 29 30
Active substance registrations: importers 75 74 80

The same report counts 34 manufacturers of veterinary medicines in 2025 and 108 GMP inspections at sites producing human medicines or active substances that year.[6] The report does not say whether these figures count authorisation holders, authorisations or sites, or whether they are year-end totals, so treat 156 as a scale guide, not a target-list size. For a current list, use EudraGMDP. Its results are authorisations (MIAs), not sites or companies: one authorisation can cover several sites, and one company can hold several. In our own search on 28 October 2026, EudraGMDP listed 307 Irish MIAs, excluding withdrawn and suspended ones (human 236, veterinary 34, investigational 86; one MIA can cover more than one type).[5]

02 / find authorised sites in eudragmdp

Find authorised sites in EudraGMDP

The HPRA does not routinely issue paper manufacturing authorisations. It publishes them in EudraGMDP, the EU database of manufacturing, import and wholesale-distribution authorisations and GMP certificates.[2][3] There are three Irish types: a manufacturer's authorisation for human medicines, a veterinary one, and one for investigational medicinal products.[3]

  1. Open the public EudraGMDP site and click Search MIA in the MIA menu.[5]
  2. Set Country to Ireland. It is the only mandatory field, and an empty search returns every document for that country.[5]
  3. Leave withdrawn and suspended authorisations out unless you are checking history.[5]
  4. Use Legal Basis of Authorisation to narrow the results.[5]
  5. Tick Enable Operations Search to filter by authorised operation. Ticked operations combine with OR, so run one search per operation if you need a clean count.[5]
  6. Sort the results and export them to Excel, then open each blue authorisation number to read the site address and authorised operations.[5]

Read the operations closely. HPRA lists processing a dosage form or intermediate, manufacturing a biological active substance through to the dosage form, primary and secondary packaging (including relabelling and serialisation), importing from outside the EU/EEA, and batch certification as manufacturing activities.[4] A packaging-only authorisation is a different account for most suppliers than a sterile production site. Contract test labs may appear on the contract giver's authorisation rather than their own.[4]

Confidential authorisations are shaded dark grey in the results.[5] A search that returns nothing does not prove a site is unauthorised.

03 / add epa licences for active substance sites

Add EPA licences for active-substance sites

Sites carrying out the chemical and pharmaceutical activities in the First Schedule need an EPA licence. The EPA Act's First Schedule puts them in class 5 (chemicals). Class 5.16 covers industrial-scale production of pharmaceutical products, including intermediates, by chemical or biological processing. Class 5.6 covers other manufacture of pharmaceutical or veterinary products and their intermediates.[7] Classes 5.12 to 5.17 are industrial emissions activities.[7]

In LEAP, the EPA's licence search, filter Type to Industrial Emissions Licence, Sector to Chemicals and Status to Licensed.[8] On 28 October 2026 that returned 71 licences. In the site details of those 71 records, 41 carry NACE code 2110 or 2120 (basic pharmaceutical products or pharmaceutical preparations).[8] Class 5.6 is not an industrial emissions activity, so class 5.6 sites can hold Integrated Pollution Control licences, and filtering on industrial emissions licences alone misses them.[7] Repeat the search with Type set to Integrated Pollution Control Licence. On the same date that gave 12 more Chemicals licences, one of them with NACE 2110.[8] Each licence in LEAP links to its latest decision document. That document sets out the licensed activity classes and can describe plant on site, such as wastewater treatment or solvent recovery.[8]

An EPA licence tells you about the process, the emissions and the site's location. Neither register lists equipment, suppliers or buying plans.

04 / a medicine approval is not a facility description

A medicine approval is not a facility description

EMA defines marketing authorisation as approval to market a medicine in one, several or all EU Member States.[1] That is a product-market record. An address attached to a medicine or its marketing-authorisation holder does not show where anything is made. Record the exact entity and site named by each source, and use the company and facility mapping guide to link company, group and site records.

05 / worked example a fictional clean utilities service supplier

Worked example: a fictional clean-utilities service supplier

Fictional example. A fictional supplier services clean-steam and purified-water systems and wants Irish sites that make sterile or active-substance products on site.

  • Site A holds a human-medicines authorisation in EudraGMDP that lists sterile processing and batch certification, plus an EPA industrial emissions licence under class 5.16. Both records name the same site. Include for further research.
  • Site B holds an authorisation listing secondary packaging and import only, and has no EPA licence. Exclude from this brief, but keep it for a packaging-equipment brief.
  • Site C has an EPA licence in the Chemicals sector, but its site NACE code is not pharmaceutical and no authorisation was found. Hold until its decision document confirms the activity.
  • Site D is an engineering contractor whose case study mentions a pharmaceutical client. Route separately as a service account, not as a manufacturing site.

The companies and decisions are fictional and only illustrate the method.

06 / keep engineering service relevance on a separate route

Keep engineering-service relevance on a separate route

For an operator account, look for evidence of the relevant activity at the site. For an engineering or maintenance business, look for evidence of the service it performs. A contractor's case study proves only what it describes, and may be historical. It does not show a current appointment or who chooses suppliers.

An operator can be the end user while another firm specifies or orders a component. Keep "operator", "specifier", "installer" and "purchaser" as open questions. The pump and flow-control prospecting guide covers that customer-route distinction.

07 / a facility fit note you can copy

A facility-fit note you can copy

Account and location: legal entity, group if relevant, and the operating site. Keep a registered or marketing address separate.

Observed activity: the authorised operations or licensed class, quoted from the record. Allow more than one activity when evidenced.

Evidence: source URL, record reference (authorisation number or EPA licence number), document date and retrieval date.

Supplier-relevance question: the link to your range, without answering an engineering question you have not checked. For example: "Does this site's licensed activity fall within our service brief?"

Gaps: operating status, technical requirements, service responsibility and purchasing route. Unknown is an acceptable answer.

Decision: include, hold for a named check, or exclude from this brief.

08 / before accepting a pharmaceutical account list

Before accepting a pharmaceutical account list

  • ☐ Each record has an evidenced site activity, not just a medicine, sector label or marketing address.
  • ☐ Sites and companies are distinguishable; several sites are not counted as several companies.
  • ☐ Authorised operations and EPA classes are recorded as stated, not flattened into "production plant".
  • ☐ Packaging, import, testing and service businesses are judged against the actual brief.
  • ☐ Withdrawn or suspended authorisations and surrendered licences are excluded or flagged.
  • ☐ Source dates, conflicts and missing checks stay visible.
09 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Is there an official list of pharmaceutical manufacturers in Ireland?

Yes. The HPRA publishes manufacturing authorisations in EudraGMDP, which you can search by country and export.[3][5] The HPRA annual report gives yearly totals.[6]

Does every pharmaceutical site need an EPA licence?

No. EPA licensing applies to the activities and scales in the First Schedule, such as industrial-scale production under class 5.16.[7] Check both registers, because a site can appear in one and not the other.

Does a marketing authorisation show where a medicine is made?

No. It is approval to market the medicine.[1] Use EudraGMDP and EPA records for site evidence.

Do these registers show equipment or purchasing plans?

No. They show authorised operations and licensed activities. Equipment, service contracts and buying plans need separate evidence.

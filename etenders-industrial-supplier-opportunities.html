Irish industrial supplier research

Irish eTenders for industrial suppliers

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

eTenders is where Irish public bodies advertise contracts: goods and services from €50,000 and works from €200,000, both excluding value added tax. Set CPV alerts for what you sell, then triage each notice by scope, stage and your supply role before you spend time on it.

This guide covers how eTenders works for industrial suppliers and a method for reading notices. Demand Range does company-level account research; named contacts and outreach are separate scopes, not included by default. It is not registration help, bid writing or procurement advice, and a relevant notice does not establish eligibility, engineering suitability or buying intent.

01 / etenders basics

How Irish eTenders works for industrial suppliers

What must be advertised. Under Circular 05/2023, contracting authorities must advertise all goods and services contracts of €50,000 and upwards on eTenders, and works contracts with an estimated value of €200,000 or more should be advertised there as part of a formal tendering process, both excluding value added tax.[9] Works-related services stay at €50,000, and authorities must publish contract award information on eTenders for all procurements over €25,000.[10] Award notices are where you find the contractor who will buy your equipment.

Above EU thresholds, check TED too. From 1 January 2026, advertising in the Official Journal of the EU is obligatory above €140,000 for goods and services bought by government departments, €216,000 for other public bodies, €432,000 for utilities and €5,404,000 for works.[9] Those notices appear on TED; see TED EU tenders for industrial suppliers.

Set CPV alerts when you register. eTenders' registration guide asks you to select every CPV code relevant to your business.[13] You can add more later with the "Edit CPV Codes" button in your profile, and the platform emails you newly published tenders matching your codes. You only get award alerts for competitions where you expressed an interest.[11]

Know how matching works. Since a July 2024 release, alerts follow the CPV tree: the same code matches, and a top-level code matches sub-codes in its tree. Two different sub-codes in the same tree do not match; eTenders' example is a notice coded 15131130 and a supplier holding 15131134.[12] List every exact code you sell. Our CPV codes for pumps, valves and equipment gives a starter set with parent codes and a free CSV.

Selling into Northern Ireland or Great Britain? Those contracts are on separate portals; see UK public tenders for industrial suppliers.

02 / portal purpose check

Before searching notices, check the portal's purpose

A supplier-facing link is worth classifying before it becomes a route-to-market entry. Start with the task the service is described as performing, not the word “supplier” in its name. For a component seller, the important question is whether the evidence concerns administration of transactions, qualification, an invitation, or the actual scope being purchased.

The European Commission describes electronic invoicing as exchanging an electronic invoice between supplier and buyer, with structured data enabling automatic processing; its explanation was updated on 6 March 2026.[8] That gives a useful concrete example of an administrative function. It does not establish that every supplier portal is an eInvoicing service, or that any particular organisation uses one.

Our research rule is to keep the function you observe separate from the commercial conclusion you want to test. An invoice-processing description can answer “what does this service do?” without answering “can a new component supplier participate in this purchase?” Even where several functions sit in one portal, evidence for one function does not establish the others.

A five-question route-verification card

Question Record what the public evidence establishes Keep separate
What is the portal for? Its stated tasks and intended users, with a source URL Do not infer invitation access from transaction administration
Is supplier qualification described? The scope of any documented qualification process, if present A qualification process is not evidence that this supplier has qualified
Is a procurement action invited? The separately evidenced notice or invitation reference A portal link is not a current invitation or a response deadline
What is the package? The specified supply/service/works scope and any relevant lot Equipment words in a service description do not establish a component-supply package
Who decides? Only the evidenced selection, approval or ordering responsibility for that scope An administrator, invoice recipient or help function is not automatically a component selector

Attach the source publication/update date, retrieval date, page purpose, permitted-use check and next validation to the card. Use not stated for a missing source date, rather than copying today's access date into it. Leave notice, deadline and package fields empty if you have not obtained a notice. No login or registration is needed to perform this public-description check; this guide provides neither.

Fictional stop-card example

An invented procurement page for Fictional Water Organisation E links to a service described as handling invoices and existing transaction records. There is no separately supplied invitation, lot, qualification decision or component specification. The organisation, page and service are wholly fictional, not a description of an actual utility portal.

For a supplier seeking new valve-supply work, record transaction-administration purpose observed; new procurement route unresolved. Reject “the portal gives us access to valve buyers”. Hold the invitation, package and selection-authority fields. Do not infer that E has no procurement opportunities; the available observation simply answers a different question.

The minimal next check is a public, attributable procurement source that identifies a relevant notice or invitation. Set the review boundary in advance. If that check yields only the same transaction-service description, stop and preserve the unresolved route. If a specific notice is found, advance to this guide's notice-purpose and package-fit checks—not directly to a sales-ready label. A positive scope mismatch can exclude that notice from the shortlist without excluding E from a separately agreed account brief.

Turn the check into a useful enquiry

Ask for: “Research your product or service range routes in your territory, excluding delivery roles you do not offer. Where a supplier portal is mentioned, identify its documented purpose and look separately for the procurement evidence you need. Record unknown qualification, scope and selection responsibility rather than treating portal availability as buyer access.” Replace each marked field with your own details.

03 / search for scope

Start with the work you can supply

Write a short inclusion brief: product or service range, territory, delivery role and explicit exclusions. Separate component supply from complete-system delivery, installation and ongoing maintenance. A pump distributor and a contractor offering a complete treatment installation should not use the same scope test.

CPV, the Common Procurement Vocabulary, is a classification system describing the subject of procurement contracts.[1] Our recommendation is to pair classification with plain-language scope checks, not use a matching code as an acceptance decision.

For a starting set of codes with their official wording, see CPV codes for pumps, valves and industrial equipment. Check each code against the official vocabulary before relying on it.

  • Try product language such as pumps, valves, dosing equipment or instrumentation.
  • Compare activity language such as servicing, renewal, supply or installation.
  • Record the query and code as searched; check the official vocabulary rather than guessing a code.

These are exploratory prompts, not tested coverage claims. For a broader account brief, see industrial lead research; for application and route questions, see pump and flow-control prospecting.

04 / record link test

Do these records describe the same work?

Before moving from a project update to a notice, test the connection between the records. A shared place name or equipment word is a search clue, not a verified link. This original exercise concerns record identity; the notice and package checks below still come afterwards.

Entirely fictional exercise: all identifiers, places, documents and observations below are invented. No real project, notice, environmental finding or purchasing requirement is represented.

Similar name, missing connection
An invented update describes wastewater works at “Demo Quay” under reference DEMO-WW-17. An invented notice mentions pumping works at Demo Quay but gives no project reference. Hold the proposed link. Record both identifiers exactly as supplied, including the absent reference, and identify the document needed to establish which works the notice concerns. Do not fill the gap with the shared place name.
Contradictory identity
The notice's hypothetical supporting document instead identifies DEMO-DW-17 and expressly describes a different drinking-water scheme. Reject this record pairing, not the whole account. Keep each record separately; do not silently change either identifier to make them match.
Explicit connection, limited conclusion
In an alternative version of the exercise, a hypothetical project document explicitly links the update and notice to the same DEMO-WW-17 works. Advance to notice-level review. That link establishes neither a current invitation nor a component-supply route. Stage, lot, obligations and equipment suitability remain separate questions.

Keep account fit separate from the disputed project link: an otherwise relevant organisation need not be discarded because this particular connection fails. Continue with the notice's actual purpose; do not turn a successful identity check into an opportunity claim.

05 / read notice purpose

Read the notice’s purpose before its headline

The EU’s eForms guidance distinguishes planning forms, competition forms and result forms: they concern preparation, calls for competition and procurement outcomes respectively.[2] It also includes prior information notices used as calls for competition, so “PIN” alone is not enough to classify the opportunity.[2]

Our triage rule is to retain the full notice type and check what action is actually invited. Do not turn a planning reference into a current bid invitation, or an award record into unawarded work.

Check the notice identifier, version, lot, published date, response deadline and stated time zone. Keep publication, retrieval and deadline dates separate. The EU guidance describes change notices that update published information.[2] Recheck changes before relying on an earlier deadline; record uncertainty rather than inventing one.

06 / test package fit

Test the package, not just the product word

Compare the stated deliverable with your inclusion brief. Use the following questions as research prompts, not technical acceptance criteria:

  • Is the subject equipment supply, spare parts, maintenance, installation or a combined works package?
  • Which lot contains the relevant scope? Do its obligations match the supply role you want to investigate?
  • Is a component mentioned as an item being purchased, an installed asset to service or background context?
  • Does the record establish a direct supply route, or are you assuming a contractor will buy from you?
  • Which document would resolve the remaining scope question, and can it be accessed and used for the intended purpose?

Keep duty conditions, materials, interfaces, standards and compatibility in the unknowns field until appropriately assessed. A category match is not an engineering conclusion. Do not assume subcontract access from the existence of a larger contract.

07 / triage decisions

Choose investigate, hold or out of scope

These are our research labels, not official procurement statuses or bid/no-bid approval:

Investigate
The described deliverable has a defensible connection to the brief and a specific next check. Advance that check—not a claim that the package is open or your offer qualifies.
Hold
A necessary fact is missing, contradictory or inaccessible, or intended source reuse is unresolved. Name the question and evidence needed to release the hold. Missing evidence is not a positive mismatch.
Out of scope
Positive evidence contradicts a stated inclusion condition, or the record serves a different research purpose. Write the reason against this notice or lot, not against the entire organisation.

Make the decision relative to the next action. Unknown timing may permit an initial scope investigation but must block describing a notice as currently open. Keep source-use decisions separate: permission to inspect information is not automatically permission to redistribute it.

09 / fictional triage cases

Four fictional notice-triage scenarios

All scenarios and evidence descriptions below are invented teaching examples, not real notices, anonymised prospects or quotations. Notice URLs, publication dates, retrieval dates and deadlines are deliberately left blank; no current availability is established.

Scenario A: a defined equipment-supply lot

The invented scope expressly asks for valve supply, without installation. A component-only supplier’s brief includes that delivery role. Investigate: establish the lot’s detailed requirements and current status. Specifications, eligibility and timing remain unresolved; this is not permission to bid.

Scenario B: maintenance with unclear parts responsibility

The invented description concerns pump maintenance but does not say whether spare components are purchased separately or provided within the service. Hold: clarify the parts-supply boundary before treating it as component demand. The word “pump” does not resolve that question.

Scenario C: complete installation outside the brief

The invented package explicitly requires design, installation and commissioning as one delivery scope. The supplier’s brief explicitly excludes that role. Out of scope for direct pursuit under this brief. Do not invent a separate component lot or an accessible subcontracting route.

Scenario D: a result mistaken for a fresh invitation

The invented record expressly reports an award, while the research brief seeks current invitations. Out of scope for that shortlist. It could prompt a separately scoped background question, but it supplies no evidence of another available package.

10 / use triage worksheet

Use the blank worksheet without manufacturing certainty

The CSV is a single-record field,value template. Make a separate copy for each notice/lot review. Every value starts empty, including the decision. Enter dates and URLs only when verified; leave unknowns blank and explain the gap in the relevant unknowns field.

  1. Define the brief, supply role and exclusions before recording a match.
  2. Keep the source observation, your relevance reasoning and unresolved questions distinct.
  3. Record the applicable source/item terms and permitted use before sharing source-derived material.
  4. Assign a next verification step and a reviewer role; choose a review date yourself.

The file contains no formulas or automation. Import values as text; do not enable formula interpretation for pasted source content. It does not schedule checks or collect named contacts.

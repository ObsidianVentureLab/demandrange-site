Irish public tenders for industrial equipment: what the eTenders open data shows

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

The Office of Government Procurement recorded 8,004 contracts advertised on eTenders in 2025, with a median of 3 bids per tender. This page is a dated reading of that official headline and of the open contract-notices dataset on data.gov.ie, so a supplier of industrial equipment can see how much of the pipeline is supplies and how often notices use CPV codes beginning with 42. It is not registration help, bid writing or procurement advice. For advertising thresholds, CPV alerts and how to triage a notice, use our eTenders guide for industrial suppliers.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Office of Government Procurement and data.gov.ie; checked 10 November 2026.

  • The Office of Government Procurement's Public Procurement at a Glance fact sheet, published and last updated on 5 August 2026, records 8,004 contracts advertised in 2025 and a median of 3 bids per tender. All figures on that sheet relate to 2025.[1]
  • The same official sheet says 60% of contracts are below the EU threshold, 88% of contracts are awarded to SMEs, 1,948 buyers published a tender, 10,147 suppliers responded to a tender, and 2,262 individual suppliers won a contract.[1]
  • By our count, from the official eTenders contract-notices file, supplies were 18.3% of published competitions in 2025 (1,465 of 8,020).[2][3]
  • By our count, 153 of those 2025 competitions had a main CPV code beginning with 42, up from 99 in 2024 and 67 in 2023.[3]
  • By our count, in 2025 the median number of bids on the 59 awarded competitions with a main CPV 42 code was 2, and 15 of those 59 (25%) received a single bid.[3]
02 / the official 2025 headline

The official 2025 headline

Start with the Office of Government Procurement's own totals, not with a recount of the spreadsheet. The fact sheet "Procuring for our Future – Public Procurement at a Glance", published and last updated on 5 August 2026, is a plain-text version of the fact sheet for the National Public Procurement Strategy 2026–2030. It states: "Please note all figures relate to the year 2025."[1]

The headline volume is "8,004 number of contracts advertised". Beside it sits "3 median number of bids per tender". The sheet also records that "60% of contracts are below the EU threshold", that "1,948 buyers published a tender", that "10,147 suppliers responded to a tender", and that "2,262 individual suppliers won a contract". It adds that "88% of contracts are awarded to SMEs" and that "8,826 SMEs responded to a tender". The largest tender categories named are "Professional services, Fleet and plant, and Minor building works and civils".[1]

The same sheet gives "€29.5 billion estimated value of contracts awarded". Use that figure only with the Office of Government Procurement's own caveat, which appears on the same page: "This figure represents the estimated value of all contracts awarded. It does not indicate the actual spend arising from those contracts. The figure is based on information entered by public bodies at the contract award stage."[1]

Those official lines answer "how many tenders are published on eTenders each year?" for 2025. They do not say how many notices were for supplies, or how often a notice used an industrial-equipment CPV code. For those shares, we use the open data, labelled as our own counts.

03 / what the open data can add

What the open data can add

The Office of Government Procurement also publishes "Contract Notices Published on eTenders" on data.gov.ie, licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. The dataset page, checked on 10 November 2026, states: "This dataset covers all published competitions from 01/01/2013 till 30/09/2026."[2] Use the file as historical context, not as a list of competitions that are still open. Re-check the catalogue and the CSV before this page is shipped, because a later quarterly update will change the coverage end-date and any 2026 year-to-date counts.

Our share and CPV figures below are Demand Range counts from that official file. They use only rows whose Source field is "OpenData-Publications", take one row per Tender ID, and date each competition by "Notice Published Date / Contract Created Date". Cite 8,004 when you quote a headline total. Use our counts only for shares and CPV breakdowns, and only with the words "by our count".[1][2][3]

04 / supplies are under a fifth of competitions

Supplies are under a fifth of competitions

By our count, from the official eTenders contract-notices file, supplies were 18.3% of published competitions in 2025 (1,465 of 8,020). The same count breaks down as follows:[3]

Contract type (as recorded) Published competitions, 2025 (by our count) Share of 8,020 (by our count)
Services 4,396 54.8%
Works 1,669 20.8%
Supplies 1,465 18.3%
Work Related Services 490 6.1%

Supplies, the contract type that usually covers equipment, were under a fifth of competitions. Services were more than half. That is a reason to search by CPV code as well as by contract type: a relevant pump or valve package can sit inside a works or services notice. For the official codes themselves, use our CPV codes for pumps, valves and industrial equipment list.

By our count, 4,842 of those 8,020 competitions (60.4%) were recorded as national threshold and 3,178 as OJEU. The 60.4% share is consistent with the official "60% of contracts are below the EU threshold". Quote the official 60% as the headline and our 60.4% only as a cross-check on the same year's open data, labelled "by our count".[1][3]

Notices that also have to appear in the Official Journal of the EU are covered in our TED guide for industrial suppliers. This page does not recount TED or other EU above-threshold totals.

05 / industrial machinery codes are a small growing slice

Industrial machinery codes are a small, growing slice

Most industrial equipment codes sit in CPV division 42 (industrial machinery). By our count, only 153 published competitions in 2025 had a main CPV code beginning with 42. That was up from 99 in 2024 and 67 in 2023, still by our count. Many more notices carry a 42 code only as an additional code: by our count, 337 in 2025, 247 in 2024 and 310 in 2023, when we count a notice if the main code or any additional code begins with 42.[3]

Of the 153 notices with a main 42 code in 2025, by our count, 119 were supplies, 20 were works, 12 were services and 2 were work-related services. By threshold, again by our count, 104 were national and 49 were OJEU.[3]

Pump and valve packages rarely appear as the main code. By our count, only 12 published competitions in 2025 had a main CPV code beginning with 4212 or 4213 (10 and 2 respectively). Notices with a 4212 or 4213 code as a main or additional code numbered 34 in 2025, by our count. That is why a supplier who only alerts on a narrow main code will miss most of the relevant pipeline, and why our eTenders guide tells you to list every exact code you sell.[3][4]

The figures in the table below are by our count:

Year Main CPV beginning 42 Any 42 code, main or additional Main CPV beginning 4212 or 4213
2023 67 310 6
2024 99 247 8
2025 153 337 12

A CPV code on a notice is a classification of the subject of the contract, not evidence that a named product will be bought, that a particular supplier is eligible, or that a package is still open.

06 / how many bids machinery notices attracted

How many bids machinery notices attracted

The official median for 2025 is 3 bids per tender.[1] By our count, 3,751 published competitions with a 2025 notice date later had an award published and at least one recorded bid; the median in that set was also 3, and 768 (20.5%) had a single bid.[3]

The machinery slice is smaller and should not be read as a rate. By our count, in 2025 the median number of bids on the 59 awarded competitions with a main CPV 42 code was 2, and 15 of those 59 (25%) received a single bid. That is a 2025 count on a small set, not a typical win chance and not an official Office of Government Procurement rate.[3]

07 / some contracting authorities have company names

Some contracting authorities have company names

eTenders is the Irish public-procurement platform, but not every buyer name in the open data is that of a government department or local authority. By our count, 29 of the 153 notices with a main CPV code beginning with 42 in 2025 came from contracting authorities whose names include Ltd, Limited, DAC or Teoranta.[3] The file does not say why. Treat 29 as a name-pattern count, not as a qualified account list.

08 / 2026 so far to 30 september

2026 so far, to 30 September

By our count, the current file contains 6,164 published competitions dated January to September 2026, against 6,036 in the same months of 2025, a rise of 2.1%. Because the published coverage ends on 30 September 2026, that is not a full-year figure and will move when the Office of Government Procurement next updates the CSV. Do not compare it with the official 8,004, which is a 2025 total.[2][3]

09 / how to use these figures without inventing demand

How to use these figures without inventing demand

A yearly total, a CPV share or a bid median is programme-level context. It does not identify a buyer, a site, or a package you can bid. Once you have a specific notice, use the triage method on our eTenders guide and the code list on our CPV codes page. For above-threshold notices that also appear at EU level, see our TED guide. Contracts in Great Britain and Northern Ireland are advertised on different portals; see our UK public tenders guide. Water-sector programmes in Ireland are a separate evidence problem, covered in Irish water investment research for suppliers.

Demand Range can turn notice and award records into company-level account research against a written brief. A relevant notice still leaves eligibility, engineering fit and present availability to be checked.

10 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many tenders are published on eTenders each year?
The Office of Government Procurement recorded 8,004 contracts advertised in 2025, in its fact sheet published on 5 August 2026; all figures on that sheet relate to 2025.[1] That official total is the figure to cite. The same sheet gives a median of 3 bids per tender and says 60% of contracts are below the EU threshold.[1]
How many bids does a typical Irish public tender get?
The same official fact sheet gives a median of 3 bids per tender in 2025.[1] By our count, in 2025 the median number of bids on the 59 awarded competitions with a main CPV 42 code was 2, and 15 of those 59 (25%) received a single bid.[3] That is a small-set count, not an official rate.
What share of eTenders notices are for supplies or industrial equipment?
By our count, from the official eTenders contract-notices file, supplies were 18.3% of published competitions in 2025 (1,465 of 8,020).[3] In the same year, by our count, 153 notices had a main CPV code beginning with 42, and 337 carried a 42 code as a main or additional code.[3] Quote the official 8,004 as the headline total.
How often do pump and valve CPV codes appear as the main code?
Rarely. By our count, only 12 published competitions in 2025 had a main CPV code beginning with 4212 or 4213 (10 and 2). Notices with one of those codes as a main or additional code numbered 34, again by our count. List every exact code you sell, as our eTenders guide explains, or most related notices will be missed.[3][4]
Do company-named buyers use eTenders?
Sometimes. By our count, 29 of the 153 notices with a main CPV code beginning with 42 in 2025 came from contracting authorities whose names include Ltd, Limited, DAC or Teoranta.[3] The file does not say why. Treat 29 as a name-pattern count, not as a list of qualified accounts.
How does this page differ from the eTenders supplier guide?
This page is a dated analysis of official 2025 volumes and of contract-type and CPV shares in the open data. Our eTenders guide covers thresholds, CPV alerts and notice triage. Above-threshold notices also appear on TED. Great Britain and Northern Ireland use the portals in our UK public tenders guide.

Manufacturing investment by EU country, 2025

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 25 November 2026

Eurostat has not published an EU-27 total for manufacturing gross fixed capital formation in 2025. Among the 26 countries with a published cell, Germany had the highest total, at 157,036.0 million euro (P), current prices. Ireland recorded 46,359.0 million euro, 5th of those 26 countries by our count, and 24.5% above its 2024 total of 37,225.7 million euro, by our count.[1][2] The figures are national accounts, not project announcements: gross fixed capital formation (P51G) in manufacturing (NACE Rev. 2 Section C), total fixed assets (N11G), in current prices, million euro. Total fixed assets include buildings, machinery and intellectual property products, so Ireland's total should not be read as spending on plant and machinery. A rise in a current-price total can reflect prices rather than more capital goods. The dataset, "Capital formation by industry (NACE Rev.2) and detailed asset type" (nama_10_a64_p5), was last updated on 21 November 2026, 10:00 Irish time, UTC+0, and was checked on 24 November 2026.[1][2]

03 / what the figures measure

What the figures measure

The series is gross fixed capital formation (P51G) in manufacturing, for total fixed assets gross (N11G), in current prices, million euro. Those filters matter. Fetching the dataset on manufacturing alone can mix in other national-accounts items and other asset types. The unit is current prices, not a volume index, so a higher total can reflect prices rather than more capital goods.[1][2]

N11G is total fixed assets, including buildings, machinery and intellectual property products. A machinery-and-equipment-only series exists under a different asset code; this page uses the total. Ireland's manufacturing total can be dominated by intangible assets, so the 2025 Irish figure should not be treated as spending on plant and machinery.[1][2]

These national-accounts totals are not Industrial Development Agency project announcements, and they are not Irish capital-goods import values. Those are covered on separate Demand Range pages: IDA investment account signals and Irish capital-goods imports.[1][2]

05 / how to use the figures

How to use the figures

Use the published country totals to see where manufacturing fixed capital formation is largest, and use the Irish series to see how Ireland's current-price total has moved. Do not treat a high total as a list of factory projects, buying contacts, or confirmed equipment demand. Allow for provisional flags, for the missing 2025 EU-27 cell, and for the fact that Ireland's total can be dominated by intangible assets. Demand Range can help turn the official totals into a named account list for a chosen country or sector.[1][2]

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Is there a published EU total for manufacturing investment in 2025?
No. Eurostat has not published an EU-27 manufacturing cell for gross fixed capital formation in 2025 in this series (P51G, N11G, current prices). A missing cell is not zero. The 26 published country totals should not be added together to stand in for the bloc, because coverage is incomplete. The latest published EU-27 total is 591,843.2 million euro in 2024.[1][2]
How large was Irish manufacturing fixed capital formation in 2025?
Ireland's manufacturing gross fixed capital formation was 46,359.0 million euro in 2025, with no Eurostat flag. That is 5th of the 26 countries with a published 2025 cell, by our count, and 24.5% above the 2024 total of 37,225.7 million euro, by our count. The figure covers total fixed assets, including intellectual property products, not only plant and machinery.[1][2]
Which country had the highest manufacturing investment in 2025?
Germany, at 157,036.0 million euro (P) in 2025, current prices, the highest published country total. Italy followed at 88,019.1 million euro, then France at 67,740.3 million euro (P) and Spain at 49,701.5 million euro (P). By our count, Germany's total was 0.1% lower than in 2024, while Italy, France and Spain were 4.2%, 3.8% and 4.6% higher.[1][2]
Does Ireland's total measure spending on plant and machinery?
No. The series used here is total fixed assets (N11G), which includes buildings, machinery and intellectual property products. Ireland's manufacturing total can be dominated by intangible assets, so the 2025 figure of 46,359.0 million euro should not be read as spending on plant and machinery. The figures are also current-price values, not a volume measure.[1][2]
Are these the same as IDA announcements or capital-goods imports?
No. These are national-accounts figures for gross fixed capital formation in manufacturing. They are not Industrial Development Agency project announcements, and they are not Irish capital-goods import values. A separate Demand Range page covers IDA investment account signals, and another covers Irish capital-goods imports. Use this page for the official country totals.[1][2]
How should industrial suppliers use these figures?
Use them to see which countries recorded the largest manufacturing fixed capital formation, not as a list of projects or buying contacts. The 2025 figures are current-price values, so a rise can reflect prices rather than more capital goods. There is no published EU-27 total for 2025. Demand Range can help turn the official totals into a named account list.[1][2]

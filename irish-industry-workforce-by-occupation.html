Who works in Irish industry, by occupation

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

If you sell equipment, consumables or services into Irish plants, the people who run, maintain and specify that equipment are a measurable group. The CSO Labour Force Survey counts people in employment by occupation and by economic sector each quarter, so you can see who works in Irish industry before you plan who to contact.

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns the occupation counts for Irish industry from CSO table QES27. It does not cover NACE code lists, plant-level headcounts or named contacts.

01 / key facts

Key facts

CSO Labour Force Survey, table QES27, Q3 2026; checked 24 November 2026.

  • 361,700 people aged 15 and over were in employment in Industry (NACE B to E) in Q3 2026, out of 2,884,100 in employment across all sectors. That is about 12.5% of all employment, by our count.[1][2]
  • Process, plant and machine operatives were the largest occupational group in Industry (B to E), at 100,500, about 27.8% of the sector, by our count. Professionals were next, at 84,600 (about 23.4%), then skilled trades, at 51,300 (about 14.2%).[1]
  • 201,900 process, plant and machine operatives were in employment across all sectors. About half of them, 49.8% by our count, worked in Industry (B to E).[1]
  • "Industry" in this table is NACE sections B to E. It includes mining and quarrying, electricity and gas supply, and water supply, sewerage and waste, not only manufacturing.[1][3]

We have not compared occupational groups year on year: the CSO revised its occupation estimates for Q3 and Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 on 25 September 2026 after a coding issue.[1]

03 / operatives professionals and skilled trades

Operatives, professionals and skilled trades

Three groups make up about 65.4% of Industry (B to E) employment, by our count. They are also the three groups an industrial supplier meets most often on site.[1]

  • Process, plant and machine operatives: 100,500. The people who run production lines, process plant and machinery. Industry (B to E) holds about half of all operatives in employment in Ireland (100,500 of 201,900, by our count). They are the daily users of equipment, consumables and safety products.[1]
  • Professionals: 84,600. This is SOC 2010 major group 2. Industry (B to E) employs about 11.0% of all professionals in employment, by our count, so in industry they are a smaller part of a much larger national group.[1][4]
  • Skilled trades: 51,300. Industry (B to E) employs about 15.4% of all people in skilled trades, by our count. Construction (F) employs more: 107,100 in Q3 2026 in the same table.[1]

Associate professional and technical occupations (43,100) and managers, directors and senior officials (41,200) are the next two groups. Together with professionals, they hold most of the roles that write specifications, approve budgets and manage sites.[1]

04 / what industry covers in the labour force survey

What "industry" covers in the Labour Force Survey

  • Sections B to E, not only manufacturing. In QES27, "Industry (B-E)" covers mining and quarrying (B), manufacturing (C), electricity, gas, steam and air-conditioning supply (D), and water supply, sewerage, waste management and remediation (E). Do not read the 361,700 figure as manufacturing employment alone.[1][3]
  • Construction is separate. Construction is section F. The CSO also publishes "Industry and Construction (B-F)", at 562,000 in Q3 2026.[1]
  • NACE Rev. 2.1. From Q1 2026 the CSO publishes the Labour Force Survey on NACE Rev. 2.1. It backcast the new classification to 2019, so the Industry (B to E) series in QES27 is on one basis.[1][3] For how the classification change affects account lists, see NACE Rev. 2.1 industrial account lists.
  • Occupation classification. The CSO codes occupations to UK SOC 2010 and derives ISCO-08 from it. The major groups in the table are SOC 2010 major groups.[4]
  • A survey of people, not of plants. The Labour Force Survey is a household survey. It counts people by the sector they work in. It does not give plant-level headcounts, job titles at named companies, or vacancies.[4]
05 / using occupation data to plan who to contact

Using occupation data to plan who to contact

  1. Start from the users. If your product is run or handled on the line, operatives are the largest group in Irish industry.[1] Plan training, safety and consumables messaging around them, then find the role that buys for them.
  2. Find the specifiers. Professionals and associate professional and technical staff together number 127,700 in Industry (B to E), by our count.[1] That is where engineering, quality and process roles sit. Our industrial ideal customer profile guide shows how to turn roles into an account profile.
  3. Plan maintenance and repair separately. Skilled trades in industry (51,300)[1] are the group to check first if your sale is led by maintenance or repair. For spares and maintenance supply, see MRO account research.
  4. Narrow the sector before you narrow the role. B to E mixes manufacturing, energy, water and mining. Use Irish manufacturing NACE codes to choose the activities that fit your offer before you build a list.
  5. Keep the evidence types apart. A national occupation count sizes the audience. It does not name a company, a site or a person.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many people work in Irish industry?
361,700 people were in employment in Industry (NACE B to E) in Q3 2026, according to CSO table QES27. That is about 12.5% of the 2,884,100 people in employment in Ireland, by our count. Industry here includes mining, energy and water, not only manufacturing, and excludes construction.[1][2]
What is the largest occupation in Irish industry?
Process, plant and machine operatives are the largest group. In Q3 2026, 100,500 of the 361,700 people in Industry (NACE B to E) were operatives, about 27.8% by our count. Professionals were second, at 84,600, and skilled trades third, at 51,300, in CSO table QES27.[1]
How many people work in manufacturing in Ireland?
This table does not give manufacturing alone. CSO table QES27 reports 361,700 people in Industry (NACE B to E) in Q3 2026, which combines manufacturing with mining, energy supply and water and waste. Use a manufacturing-only CSO series if you need section C by itself.[1][3]
What share of Ireland's machine operatives work in industry?
About half. In Q3 2026, 100,500 of the 201,900 process, plant and machine operatives in employment in Ireland worked in Industry (NACE B to E), 49.8% by our count from CSO table QES27. Transportation and storage had the next largest published count, at 53,200.[1]
Can I use these figures to find contacts at a company?
No. The Labour Force Survey is a household survey that counts people by occupation and sector. It gives national totals, not names, job titles at a company or site headcounts. Use it to size each audience and plan messaging, then use company-level research to find the accounts and roles.[4]

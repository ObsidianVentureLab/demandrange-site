Nordic process industry suppliers: account research in Sweden, Denmark, Finland and Norway

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Eurostat counted 45,810 manufacturing enterprises in Sweden, 15,256 in Denmark and 29,230 in Finland in 2024. Statistics Norway counted 18,259 in Norway.[1][3] Those are market-sizing figures, not a list of names. This guide shows how to turn them into Nordic process-industry accounts, such as pulp and paper mills, metal works and chemical plants, using each country's statistics office, business register, activity codes and public tender portal.

Sweden, Denmark and Finland are EU members. Norway is in the European Economic Area (EEA), not the EU, so its figures come from Statistics Norway and are labelled separately throughout.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat structural business statistics and the four national statistics offices; checked 10 November 2026.

  • Eurostat's sbs_ovw_act dataset counts 45,810 manufacturing enterprises in Sweden, 15,256 in Denmark and 29,230 in Finland in 2024 (NACE Rev. 2 Section C). The dataset was last updated on 30 September 2026.[1]
  • Statistics Norway counts 18,259 manufacturing enterprises and 20,603 manufacturing establishments in Norway at 31 December 2024 (table 12910, updated 7 May 2026). By our count, that is about 1.13 establishments per enterprise.[3]
  • Statistics Sweden's Structural Business Statistics (45,810) and Statistics Denmark's general enterprise statistics (15,256) give the same 2024 counts as Eurostat.[4][5]
  • All of these 2024 counts still use the older classifications: NACE Rev. 2, SNI 2007 in Sweden, DB07 in Denmark, TOL 2008 in Finland and SN2007 in Norway. The 2025 national codes, which follow NACE Rev. 2.1, are what you will meet in the registers.[1][3][13][14][15][16]

To compare these with other EU countries, use our manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country table. This page does not repeat it.

Why the national figures can differ

  • Sweden. Statistics Sweden's Structural Business Statistics match Eurostat at 45,810.[5] Its Business Register (FDB) shows a larger stock, 54,493 manufacturing enterprises in November 2024 and 55,623 in November 2025, but Statistics Sweden marks that table as not official statistics, and it covers a wider population of registered legal and natural persons. Do not swap it for 45,810 or add the two together.[6]
  • Denmark. Statistics Denmark's table GF11 also shows 15,256 for 2024, the same official cell as Eurostat.[4]
  • Finland. Statistics Finland's own enterprise-unit statistics give 29,227 manufacturing enterprises for 2024 under TOL 2008, three fewer than the Eurostat cell by our count, and 29,581 for 2024 under TOL 2025. These are three separate figures from different tables and classifications, so label each one with its source.[1][7][8]
  • Norway. Eurostat also publishes a 2024 Norway cell of 18,260, one above Statistics Norway's 18,259 by our count. This page uses the Statistics Norway figure.[1][3]

By our count, enterprises with fewer than 10 persons employed were about 76.6% of manufacturing enterprises in Denmark, 87.9% in Finland and 89.4% in Sweden in 2024.[2] For most equipment and site-services suppliers, the bands from 50 persons upward are closer to the real account universe than the headline count.

03 / the process industry divisions

The process-industry divisions

Process plants sit across several NACE divisions. Pulp is a class inside C17 paper and paper products, not a division of its own, and C16 is wood products, not pulp. Do not add these divisions together and call the total "the process industry": other process plants sit outside them.[1]

For Sweden, Denmark and Finland, the 2024 enterprise counts for these divisions (C16 wood products, C17 paper and paper products, C20 chemicals, C23 other non-metallic mineral products and C24 basic metals) are in our manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country table.[1]

Norway (EEA), units by industry at 31 December 2024 (Statistics Norway, SN2007):[3]

Industry Enterprises Establishments

The Norway rows are a separate national source and are not part of any EU total. Enterprise counts tell you how many companies are active in a division, not where the plants are. For named process sites, use the EU Industrial Emissions Portal method, and for chemicals see our European chemical manufacturer research guide.

04 / build the list in layers

Build the list in layers

  1. Size the segment with the counts above, then define it with activity codes.[1][3]
  2. Companies. Resolve each legal entity in the national register.[9][10][11][12]
  3. Activity. Check the entity's activity code in its 2025 national version.[13][14][15][16]
  4. Plants. Confirm the site from public site evidence, such as the emissions portal. A register entry or tender notice is not proof of a plant.
  5. Buying signals. Add public tenders from the national notice services and TED.[17][18][19][20]
05 / national business registers

National business registers

Country Register What it gives you
Sweden Bolagsverket (Swedish Companies Registration Office), "Find company information" The latest company information, documents and certificates; some information is free and some documents are for sale[9]
Denmark Central Business Register (CVR), via CVR.dk and Virk The authoritative register of basic data on registered Danish businesses, current and historical, including their linked production units[10]
Finland Business Information System (YTJ) and the Finnish Trade Register (PRH) A public search returning the Business ID, company form and main line of business; PRH holds the filed Trade Register record[11]
Norway (EEA) The Register of Legal Entities (Enhetsregisteret), Norway's official business register A public search of registered businesses, with the activity code recorded for each unit[12][16]

Two points matter for plant-level selling:

  • Denmark's CVR records production units as well as legal units, so you can see where a Danish business operates, not just where it is registered.[10]
  • A register count is not a statistic. Search results in Bolagsverket, CVR, YTJ or the Norwegian business register are not the Eurostat or national statistics counts above, and the registers hold every kind of business, not only manufacturers.[9][10][11][12]

Record the legal entity, its activity code and each site you can confirm. Do not copy personal data from a register into your list.

06 / activity codes and nace rev 2 1

Activity codes and NACE Rev. 2.1

Each country has its own activity classification built on NACE. All four now follow NACE Rev. 2.1 on the first four levels, with a national fifth or sixth level:

Country Current code system Earlier version (used by the 2024 counts) Note
Sweden SNI 2025 SNI 2007 Identical to NACE Rev. 2.1 on the first four levels; 835 codes at the finest level against NACE Rev. 2.1's 651[13]
Denmark DB25 (Dansk Branchekode 2025) DB07 A six-digit Danish breakdown of NACE Rev. 2.1, valid from 1 January 2025[14]
Finland TOL 2025 TOL 2008 Valid from 1 January 2025, following NACE Rev. 2.1 on the 1- to 4-digit levels[15]
Norway (EEA) SN2025 SN2007 Taken into the Enhetsregisteret from 1 September 2025; 736 activity codes against SN2007's 820[16]

NACE Rev. 2.1 entered into force on 1 January 2025 in all EU member states, under Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/137.[14] Norway applies it through the EEA route, and Statistics Norway expects its official statistics to move to SN2025 at different times during 2026–2029.[16] Statistics Sweden says its own move to SNI 2025 will take several years, and Statistics Denmark says the move from DB07 to DB25 happens in different years across statistical domains.[13][14]

For a list, that means:

  • Keep the old and new code on each row until all your sources have moved.
  • Don't filter one source on SNI 2007 and another on SNI 2025 and merge them unchecked. The same applies to DB07 and DB25, TOL 2008 and TOL 2025, and SN2007 and SN2025.
  • If you need Finnish counts already published on TOL 2025, Statistics Finland's table 15jn has them. Keep them separate from the 2024 Eurostat headline.[8][15]
  • Map each national code to NACE Rev. 2.1 with our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.
07 / public tenders

Public tenders

Country Where official notices appear
Sweden A notice database registered under the Procurement Statistics Act (2019:668), plus TED for contracts above the EU thresholds, as described by the National Agency for Public Procurement (Upphandlingsmyndigheten)[17]
Denmark udbud.dk, the official public-procurement notice site, plus TED for EU-level notices[18]
Finland Hilma (hankintailmoitukset.fi), the official public-procurement notice service, plus TED for EU-level notices[19]
Norway (EEA) Doffin, the official database for public procurement[20]

Upphandlingsmyndigheten explains where Swedish notices appear; it is not itself the notice database.[17] Norway is an EEA neighbour, so the tender rules are shared, but a Doffin notice is not an EU-member award.[20] For EU-level searches, see our TED tenders guide and CPV codes for industrial equipment.

A tender is a buying signal at one public buyer. It tells you nothing about private process plants.

08 / step by step

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat counts for Sweden, Denmark and Finland, and Statistics Norway for Norway.[1][3]
  2. Pick the divisions that match your product, such as C17 for paper and C24 for basic metals, then find their codes in SNI 2025, DB25, TOL 2025 or SN2025.[13][14][15][16]
  3. Find named process sites from public site evidence, starting with the emissions portal.
  4. Resolve each operator in the national register: legal entity, activity code and, in Denmark, production units.[9][10][11][12]
  5. Note the plant-level functions that own the decision, such as the mill manager, maintenance lead and technical buyer. Record the function, not a person.
  6. Add a trigger, such as a public tender or a published expansion.[17][18][19][20]

Fictional example: a fictional Irish maker of industrial valves targets Nordic paper mills. It filters emissions-portal records for pulp and paper installations in Sweden and Finland, resolves each operator in Bolagsverket or YTJ, and checks the main line of business against SNI 2025 and TOL 2025. It finds that one fictional group runs two mills through two separate companies, and records them as two accounts under one group. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

09 / common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Using Sweden's Business Register stock (54,493 in November 2024) as the manufacturing count. It is not official statistics.[6]
  • Treating Statistics Finland's 29,227 or 29,581 as the Eurostat 29,230 cell. Each is a different table or classification.[1][7][8]
  • Counting Norway as an EU member, or adding its figures into an EU total.[3]
  • Presenting the 2024 counts as SNI 2025, DB25, TOL 2025 or SN2025 figures. They still use the older codes.[13][14][15][16]
  • Treating C17 as pulp mills only. It is paper and paper products, with pulp as one class inside it.[1]
  • Reading a register hit or a tender notice as proof of a plant.

For the same method in other countries, see our Germany industrial account research guide.

10 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in the Nordic countries?
Eurostat counted 45,810 manufacturing enterprises in Sweden, 15,256 in Denmark and 29,230 in Finland in 2024. Statistics Norway counted 18,259 manufacturing enterprises and 20,603 establishments in Norway at 31 December 2024. Norway's figure comes from a different publisher, because Norway is in the EEA, not the EU.[1][3]
Is Norway covered the same way as Sweden, Denmark and Finland?
Not quite. Norway is an EEA neighbour, not an EU member, so this page uses Statistics Norway's counts rather than an EU headline. It applies NACE Rev. 2.1 through the EEA route as SN2025, and publishes public tenders on Doffin. Keep Norway's figures out of any EU total.[3][16][20]
Why does Statistics Finland give a different count from Eurostat?
Because the tables differ. Eurostat shows 29,230 Finnish manufacturing enterprises in 2024. Statistics Finland's own enterprise-unit statistics give 29,227 under TOL 2008 and 29,581 under TOL 2025 for the same year. Label each figure with its source and classification, and never treat them as interchangeable.[1][7][8]
Which activity codes should I use for Nordic manufacturers?
Use the 2025 national codes in the registers: SNI 2025 in Sweden, DB25 in Denmark, TOL 2025 in Finland and SN2025 in Norway. All follow NACE Rev. 2.1 on the first four levels. The 2024 statistics on this page still use the older versions, so keep both codes during the switch.[13][14][15][16]
Where can I find Nordic public tenders?
Search each country's official notice service: a registered notice database in Sweden, udbud.dk in Denmark, Hilma in Finland and Doffin in Norway. Contracts above the EU thresholds in Sweden also appear in TED. A tender is a signal at one public buyer, so pair it with site evidence before acting.[17][18][19][20]

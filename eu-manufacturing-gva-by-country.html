Manufacturing share of gross value added by EU country, 2025

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Manufacturing produced 15.79% of the EU-27's gross value added (GVA) in 2025, by our count, from Eurostat's national accounts.[1][2] Ireland had the highest share of any member state at 30.84%. Luxembourg had the lowest at 4.57%. The full 27-country table is below, with 2024 for comparison.

If you sell equipment, components, consumables or services to manufacturers, this tells you how much of each economy rests on the plants you sell to. It is a different measure from how many manufacturing enterprises a country has. For counts of firms, see industrial enterprises by EU country.

01 / key facts

Key facts

  • EU-27: manufacturing was 15.79% of total GVA in 2025 and 16.05% in 2024, by our count (Eurostat nama_10_a10, current prices).[1]
  • Ireland: 30.84% in 2025 (€176,834.7 million of €573,442.9 million), by our count, the highest in the EU.
  • Germany: 19.42% in 2025 (provisional), by our count. Germany alone produced about 29.8% of EU-27 manufacturing GVA, by our count.
  • Four member states were at or above 20% in 2025: Ireland, Slovenia, Czechia and Denmark, by our count.
  • Eleven of 27 member states were above the EU-27 share in 2025, by our count.
  • UK: not in the Eurostat table for these years. See the UK note below.
02 / what the figure measures

What the figure measures

Gross value added is the value of output less the value of goods and services used up in producing it.[3] Eurostat publishes it by industry under the NACE Rev. 2 classification. Section C is manufacturing.

We divided each country's manufacturing GVA (section C) by its total GVA across all activities, using the same year and current prices in euro. Eurostat publishes the two cells; the percentages and ranking are our arithmetic, so we label them "by our count". Cells Eurostat flags as provisional are marked (p).

04 / ireland a high share with a caveat

Ireland: a high share with a caveat

Ireland's manufacturing share, 30.84% in 2025 by our count, is about 1.95 times the EU-27 share. It rose slightly from 30.67% in 2024 while most member states fell.

Ireland's national accounts are strongly shaped by foreign-owned multinational groups. In its Annual National Accounts 2025, the CSO reports that foreign-owned multinational-dominated sectors accounted for 50.4% of total value added in the Irish economy, and that the industry sectors it treats as dominated by those groups — including pharmaceuticals, computer, electronic and optical products, and medical and dental instruments — made up 28.1% of 2025 gross value added at constant basic prices.[4][5] The CSO also publishes modified Gross National Income (GNI), the indicator designed to exclude globalisation effects disproportionately impacting Irish economic results.[4] GNI does not replace the Eurostat manufacturing share in the table above. For account research, treat the share as a signal of where value is recorded, not a count of buyers. Our Irish manufacturing account research and Irish pharmaceutical manufacturing research pages cover the plant level.

05 / germany and central europe

Germany and central Europe

Germany is the largest manufacturing economy in absolute terms: €797,190.0 million of manufacturing GVA in 2025 (provisional), about 29.8% of the EU-27 total by our count. Its share of its own GVA, 19.42%, is lower than Ireland's but sits on a far bigger base.

Slovenia (21.10%), Czechia (20.77%), Slovakia (17.46%), Hungary (17.09%, provisional) and Poland (16.86%) all sat above the EU-27 share in 2025, by our count. These are supplier-dense economies tied into German automotive and machinery chains. See Germany industrial account research.

06 / where the share is falling

Where the share is falling

Between 2024 and 2025, the EU-27 share fell from 16.05% to 15.79%, by our count. Czechia, Hungary, Bulgaria and Slovakia each fell by more than 0.8 percentage points, by our count. A falling share does not always mean falling output. In Czechia and Bulgaria, manufacturing GVA in euro still rose; in Hungary and Slovakia it fell by less than 1% while total GVA grew, by our count.

07 / uk note

UK note

The UK is not in Eurostat's nama_10_a10 table for 2024 or 2025, so it is not ranked here. UK national accounts by industry are published by the Office for National Statistics (ONS) in sterling. We have not added a UK figure to this page until it can be checked against a matching ONS series. For UK plant-level work, see UK manufacturing account research and UK SIC codes for manufacturing.

08 / what this means for industrial b2b sellers

What this means for industrial B2B sellers

  • Size the market before the list. A high manufacturing share points to where plant spend is concentrated. Pair it with enterprise counts to see whether that spend sits in many sites or a few large ones.
  • Separate value from volume. Ireland's share is high, but a small number of large sites carry much of it. Account selection matters more than list length.
  • Watch the direction. A falling share in a supplier-heavy economy can mean tighter budgets and longer buying cycles for some categories.
  • Use NACE codes to go from country to account. The share is a section C total. Our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide shows how to break it into the divisions you sell to.

09 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What share of EU gross value added comes from manufacturing?
Manufacturing produced 15.79% of EU-27 gross value added in 2025, by our count, down from 16.05% in 2024. We divided Eurostat's published manufacturing GVA by total GVA, both at current prices in euro, from the nama_10_a10 dataset updated on 5 November 2026.[1][2]
Which EU country has the highest manufacturing share of GVA?
Ireland has the highest share: manufacturing was 30.84% of its gross value added in 2025, by our count. Slovenia, Czechia and Denmark followed, each at or just above 20%. Luxembourg had the lowest share in the EU at 4.57%, followed by Cyprus and Malta.
Why is Ireland's manufacturing share so high?
Foreign-owned multinationals carry a large weight in Ireland's economy, including in manufacturing. The CSO's Annual National Accounts 2025 show multinational-dominated sectors made up 50.4% of Irish value added in 2025, including pharmaceuticals, computer, electronic and optical products, and medical and dental instruments.[4][5] The CSO's GNI* adjusts for globalisation effects.[4] It does not replace the Eurostat share above.
Is the UK included in the table?
No. Eurostat's nama_10_a10 table does not include the UK for 2024 or 2025, so the UK is not ranked here. UK national accounts by industry come from the Office for National Statistics in sterling. We will only add a UK figure once it is checked against a matching ONS series.
How is the manufacturing share of GVA calculated?
Divide a country's manufacturing gross value added, NACE section C, by its total gross value added for the same year and price basis. We used Eurostat's current-price figures in million euro. Eurostat publishes both cells; the percentages and ranking on this page are our own arithmetic.

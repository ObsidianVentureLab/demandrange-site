Irish manufacturing exporters by firm size

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Ireland had 2,742 manufacturing enterprises that exported goods in 2024, and 190 enterprises with 250 or more employees accounted for 90.7% of the export value, by our count. Those large enterprises were only 6.9% of the exporter list. Together, Irish manufacturing enterprises exported €186.3 billion of goods, according to Eurostat table ext_tec01.[1]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns the official counts and values for Irish manufacturing exporters by firm size, and the same figures for Germany, France, Spain, Poland and the Netherlands. It does not name companies, sites or products, and it is not a substitute for product-based trade statistics.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat ext_tec01, manufacturing (NACE Rev. 2 section C), exports to all countries of the world, 2024; checked 24 November 2026.

  • Ireland had 2,742 manufacturing enterprises that exported goods in 2024, down from 2,818 in 2023, a fall of 2.7% by our count.[1]
  • Their export value was €186.3 billion in 2024, up from €160.6 billion in 2023, a rise of 16.0% by our count.[1]
  • Enterprises with 250 or more employees numbered 190. They were 6.9% of manufacturing exporters but 90.7% of export value, by our count (€168,981.7 million of €186,310.2 million).[1]
  • Enterprises with fewer than 250 employees numbered 2,552 and accounted for 9.3% of export value, by our count.[1]
  • Manufacturing enterprises were 24.1% of all Irish exporting enterprises (2,742 of 11,398) but 85.3% of all Irish enterprise export value (€186.3 billion of €218.4 billion), by our count.[1]
  • The official EU aggregate in the same table (geo code EU) is 368,937 manufacturing exporters and €3,764.7 billion of export value in 2024. Ireland was 0.74% of those exporters and 4.9% of that value, by our count.[1]
02 / irish manufacturing exporters by size 2024

Irish manufacturing exporters by size, 2024

The figures come from Eurostat dataset ext_tec01, "Trade by NACE Rev. 2 activity and enterprise size class", in the trade-in-goods statistics by enterprise characteristics (TEC). An exporter here is an enterprise that exported goods during at least part of the year, classified by the main activity of the enterprise, not by the product shipped. Manufacturing is NACE Rev. 2 section C. Export values are recorded at the national frontier on a free-on-board (FOB) basis, in current prices. The partner is "all countries of the world". Eurostat last updated the dataset on 3 November 2026 at 22:00 Irish time, UTC+0. The latest reference year is 2024.[1][2][3]

Eurostat's published size-class labels are in employees. From the 2022 reference year, the size class is the average number of employees and self-employed persons in the year; until 2021 it was employees only.[3]

The statistical unit is the enterprise. Where a country has not yet implemented that concept, it uses the legal unit as an approximation. The dataset covers traders successfully matched to a statistical business register. Trade by non-resident traders and by private individuals is out of scope, so these figures are not a complete total of goods trade and should not be mixed with product-based trade statistics.[3]

In 2023 there were 2,818 Irish manufacturing exporters and export value was €160.6 billion. Enterprises with 250 or more employees numbered 185 and accounted for 89.1% of that value, by our count.[1]

03 / how concentrated export value is

How concentrated export value is

The headline is the split between a short list of large firms and everyone else. In 2024, 190 enterprises carried €168,981.7 million of the €186,310.2 million total, 90.7% by our count. The other 2,552 manufacturing exporters, 93.1% of the list by our count, accounted for 9.3% of the value.[1]

That concentration was already high in 2023, when large firms held 89.1% of value, by our count, and was slightly higher in 2024.[1]

The overall average export value, €67.9 million per manufacturing exporter in 2024 by our count, is not a typical firm. It is pulled up by the large-firm average of about €889.4 million. Enterprises with fewer than 10 employees averaged about €3.2 million, those with 10 to 49 employees about €3.0 million, and those with 50 to 249 employees about €21.1 million, by our count.[1]

04 / ireland against other eu countries

Ireland against other EU countries

Among the EU countries compared here, Ireland has the smallest number of manufacturing exporters and the highest large-firm share of export value, by our count. Germany has the most manufacturing exporters, at 63,124. Ireland's large-firm share of value, 90.7%, sits just above Germany (88.6%) and France (86.2%), and well above Poland (76.2%), Spain (70.4%) and the Netherlands (69.7%), by our count of the same official cells for 2024.[1]

Poland's total includes a small unknown size class (292 enterprises), which is in the total but not in the 250-or-more row; that class had a very small export value. The other countries in the table had no unknown-size exporters. Average value per exporter is a mean pulled up by large firms in every country, and should not be read as a typical exporter.[1]

Manufacturing also accounts for a larger share of all enterprise export value in Ireland than in the other countries in the table, by our count: 85.3% in Ireland, 78.7% in Germany, 74.7% in Poland, 68.8% in France, 65.2% in Spain and 32.6% in the Netherlands. The enterprise share is much lower. Manufacturing was 24.1% of Irish exporting enterprises (2,742 of 11,398), by our count. Most Irish exporting enterprises are not manufacturers, but manufacturers carry most of the value.[1]

Eurostat also publishes an official EU aggregate in this table, under the geo code EU. In 2024 the EU had 368,937 manufacturing exporters and €3,764.7 billion of manufacturing export value. Both of those EU totals are unflagged official cells. Ireland accounted for 0.74% of those exporters and 4.9% of that value, by our count.[1]

Eurostat also publishes an EU size-class breakdown. The EU cells for enterprises with 250 or more employees, and several other EU size-class value cells, are flagged estimated; the unknown-class value cell is flagged low reliability. The published classes sum to 364,544 enterprises and €3,758.3 billion, which is 4,393 enterprises and €6.5 billion short of the official totals, by our count. A large-enterprise share of EU manufacturing export value can be calculated as 78.0%, by our count, but the 250-or-more value cell in that ratio is flagged estimated, so this page does not use that breakdown as a like-for-like comparator with the unflagged country figures in the table.[1]

05 / what this means for sme focused account lists

What this means for SME-focused account lists

  1. The manufacturing exporter list is short. 2,742 manufacturing enterprises exported goods in 2024. That is a countable universe for account research, not an unbounded one. Our Irish manufacturing account research page sets out the other official sources that sit around that list.
  2. Export activity is a weak filter if you sell to smaller manufacturers. "Exporter" alone will pull in the 190 large firms that hold 90.7% of the value, by our count, and a long tail of 2,552 smaller exporters that together hold 9.3%. If your offer fits mid-size or smaller plants, you need size and site evidence as well as export activity.
  3. Do not treat the average as a typical order book. The €67.9 million average is a mean, not a midpoint. Most manufacturing exporters are in the two smallest size classes, whose average value is about €3 million, by our count.
  4. Not every exporter is a manufacturer. Only 24.1% of Irish exporting enterprises were in manufacturing in 2024, by our count, even though they accounted for 85.3% of enterprise export value. Filter by activity first, then by size.
  5. These tables do not name accounts. ext_tec01 is a national breakdown, not a company list. To move from a size class to named sites, use company-level research such as lookalike account research. For how enterprise counts compare across the EU, see industrial enterprises by EU country.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturers in Ireland export?
Ireland had 2,742 manufacturing enterprises that exported goods in 2024, according to Eurostat table ext_tec01, down from 2,818 in 2023. These are enterprises whose main activity is manufacturing (NACE Rev. 2 section C). The figure is a count of those enterprises, not a count of plants or products.[1]
How concentrated is Irish manufacturing export value?
Very concentrated. In 2024, 190 enterprises with 250 or more employees accounted for 90.7% of Irish manufacturing export value, by our count, but were only 6.9% of manufacturing exporters. All enterprises with fewer than 250 employees together accounted for the remaining 9.3% of value. The source is Eurostat ext_tec01.[1]
How does Ireland compare with Germany, France, Spain and Poland?
Ireland's manufacturing export value is more concentrated in large firms than in Germany, France, Spain or Poland, by our count of Eurostat ext_tec01 for 2024. Enterprises with 250 or more employees accounted for 90.7% of value in Ireland, 88.6% in Germany, 86.2% in France, 76.2% in Poland and 70.4% in Spain.[1]
Which of these countries has the most manufacturing exporters?
Germany had the most in 2024, at 63,124, then Poland (41,576), Spain (36,633) and France (22,355), according to Eurostat ext_tec01. Ireland had 2,742. The official EU aggregate in the same table is 368,937 manufacturing exporters and €3,764.7 billion of export value. Ireland was 0.74% of those exporters and 4.9% of that value, by our count.[1]
Can I use these figures as a list of target accounts?
No. ext_tec01 gives national counts and values by size class and activity, not company names, sites or contacts. It can tell you how short the manufacturing exporter list is and how much value sits with large firms. Use company-level research to build an account list, then apply size and site evidence.[1]

Which Irish manufacturers are investing in decarbonisation?

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

Public SEAI and Enterprise Ireland programme data can show that Irish firms are being funded to plan or invest in decarbonisation, but neither body publishes a complete public list of grant recipients. What is public are the scheme rules, official totals, selected case studies and, sometimes, a firm’s own announcement. Those sources are useful account signals if they are read as what they are, not as a supplier shortlist.

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page owns one thing: how to use public Irish decarbonisation grant and programme data, mainly from the Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland (SEAI) and Enterprise Ireland, as account signals. For who must have an energy audit, published Action Plans and SEAI’s Large Industry Energy Network, see energy-audit signals for industrial suppliers.

01 / key facts

Key facts

SEAI and Enterprise Ireland programme publications; extracted 24 November 2026.

  • SEAI’s Excellence in Energy Efficient Design (EXEED) grant can provide up to €3 million per project for energy-efficiency and renewable-energy investments on non-domestic buildings and processes, including new and upgraded manufacturing processes. The 2026 scheme is open year-round.[1][2][3]
  • 2024 approvals, from SEAI’s Annual Report 2024: SEAI approved EXEED grant support to 44 companies, totalling €7.2 million.[4]
  • 2024 and 2025 outturn, from SEAI’s Programme Impact Report 2025, Table 3: 12 EXEED grants supported in 2025, with €4.5 million of SEAI capital spend and an estimated €15 million of private spend leveraged. The same table’s 2024 outturn was 14 grants supported and €1.8 million of capital spend. That outturn series is not the same as the 2024 approvals figure.[5]
  • Payments by year, from a Dáil written answer of 18 September 2025: 61 businesses were paid under EXEED in 2023, 49 in 2024, and 36 in 2025 to the end of August.[6]
  • Enterprise Ireland’s Green Transition Fund was announced in June 2022 as a €55 million fund for 2022 to 2026, with a Climate Planning Fund for Business (€25 million) and an Enterprise Emissions Reduction Investment Fund (€30 million) aimed at manufacturing investment of up to €1 million per project.[7][13]
  • On 12 November 2024, Enterprise Ireland said it had approved over €55 million, under the Environmental Aid scheme and the Green Transition Fund, to more than 400 Irish companies since June 2022, with an estimated 130,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide abated.[11]
02 / what the public record shows and what it does not

What the public record shows — and what it does not

What is published, and is usable with care:

  • Scheme rules and award ranges. SEAI and Enterprise Ireland set out who can apply, what costs are eligible and the maximum grant for each support.[1][2][3][8][9][10][12]
  • Official totals. Annual reports, impact reports, department announcements and Dáil answers publish counts and euro amounts. They rarely name the firms behind the totals.[4][5][6][11][13]
  • Selected case studies and company announcements. SEAI publishes chosen EXEED case studies, and some firms describe a grant in their own news or reports. Those are examples, not a register, and they are not reproduced here.
  • Larger aid awards. Some larger awards may also appear in official state-aid transparency sources. That is a separate record.

A figure about “companies supported” tells you that funded work exists in the country, and at what scale. It does not identify the firms.

03 / seai s exeed grant and related business supports

SEAI’s EXEED grant and related business supports

EXEED is SEAI’s grant for more complex, high-impact energy projects that need a bespoke evaluation. It sits beside other SEAI business supports for more common upgrades, such as solar PV, insulation and heat pumps. It is aimed at large energy users (typically more than 100,000 kWh a year) but is open to all non-domestic organisations: businesses, public bodies and not-for-profits.[1][2][3]

Eligible projects include new and upgraded manufacturing processes, major renovations and energy upgrades of existing buildings, and new buildings. Applicants can apply for Stage 1, Stage 2, or both, independently.[1][3]

Stage What it supports Maximum grant rate in the 2026 guidelines
1 Pre-investment work, including energy-efficient-design studies, feasibility, design and professional services Up to 50% for EXEED projects; up to 30% for impactful projects
2 Investment in energy efficiency and renewable energy Up to 30% for most measures; up to 40% for heat pumps

Source: SEAI, EXEED Grant Scheme Guidelines, July 2026. Support of up to €3 million is available per project across both stages. Higher amounts may be considered with pre-approval from the Department of Climate, Energy and the Environment. Projects must meet a minimum score of 65 out of 100 on impact, value for money, and the project team and processes. Typical project timelines are up to 24 months. SEAI expects evaluation, from a complete submission, within about 3 weeks for Stage 1 and 8 to 12 weeks for Stage 2, plus about 4 weeks for grant requests above €1 million.[3]

Three official series describe EXEED activity. They should not be mixed, because they measure different things:

Official series 2023 2024 2025
2024 approvals (SEAI Annual Report 2024) — 44 companies, €7.2 million approved —
2024 and 2025 outturn (SEAI Programme Impact Report 2025, Table 3) — 14 grants; €1.8 million SEAI capital spend; an estimated €21 million private spend 12 grants; €4.5 million SEAI capital spend; an estimated €15 million private spend
Payments by year (Dáil written answer, 18 September 2025) 61 49 36 (to end August)

Sources: SEAI Annual Report 2024; SEAI Programme Impact Report 2025, Table 3 (2024 figures in brackets in the source); Dáil written answer of 18 September 2025. The 2023 payments figure is from that 2025 answer. The impact report also estimates 20 GWh of new annual final energy savings and 4.3 kt of new annual carbon savings from EXEED in 2025.[4][5][6]

The 2024 approvals total (€7.2 million to 44 companies) is much larger than the 2024 outturn capital spend (€1.8 million on 14 grants supported). That is expected if approvals, payments and completed, impact-assessed projects land in different years. Use each series only for what it measures.

SEAI also runs other business supports that can sit beside EXEED, including energy-audit support for smaller users, renewable heat and non-domestic solar PV. The audit supports and the legal audit obligation are covered on our energy-audit signals page, not here.[1][6]

04 / enterprise ireland climate and emissions reduction supports

Enterprise Ireland climate and emissions-reduction supports

In June 2022 the Department of Enterprise announced a €55 million Green Transition Fund, to run from 2022 to 2026, administered with Enterprise Ireland and funded through Ireland’s National Recovery and Resilience Plan. It had two streams: a Climate Planning Fund for Business (€25 million) and an Enterprise Emissions Reduction Investment Fund (€30 million) for manufacturing businesses investing in lower-carbon processes, energy monitoring and related research.[7][13]

The planning stream, as published in 2022 and still described on Enterprise Ireland’s support pages on 24 November 2026, includes:[7][8][9][10]

Support What it is for Published award
Climate Action Voucher An initial sustainability, decarbonisation or circular-economy plan, with an independent adviser or trainer Maximum award of €1,800
GreenStart A short in-company assignment with a sustainability consultant Up to 80% support, to a maximum grant of €5,000
GreenPlus A longer assignment with an external environmental expert, including eligible training A grant of up to €50,000; the 2022 announcement set this at up to 50% of eligible costs

The 2022 announcement also listed a strategic consultancy support, at up to €35,000, or €7,200 on a shorter programme.[7]

The investment stream was aimed at manufacturing. The 2022 announcement set capital investment for decarbonisation technologies at a minimum of €20,000 and a maximum of €1 million, at grant rates of 30% for a large enterprise, 40% for a medium enterprise and 50% for a small enterprise, for Enterprise Ireland, Údarás na Gaeltachta and IDA Ireland manufacturing clients. A separate energy-metering support was set at €5,000 to €50,000, at up to 50% of eligible costs.[7] The National Enterprise Hub, on 24 November 2026, still describes a capital grant of between €20,000 and €1 million per project for technologies including industrial heat pumps, electric steam boilers, heat recovery, mechanical vapour recompression evaporators and biomass boilers.[12]

Because the Green Transition Fund was designed to run to 2026, treat those pages as the live check on what is still open. This page does not say whether any stream is open or closed on a given day.

Take-up has been published only as totals. Up to the end of September 2023, a Dáil answer reported 185 approved Climate Planning Fund projects worth €1.4 million, and 33 approved Enterprise Emissions Reduction Investment Fund projects worth €5.5 million, and roughly €7 million across both streams in 2023, in the wording of that answer.[13] On 12 November 2024, Enterprise Ireland said it had approved over €55 million under the Environmental Aid scheme and the Green Transition Fund to more than 400 Irish companies since June 2022, reducing carbon dioxide emissions by an estimated 130,000 tonnes. The same announcement said the Department of Enterprise had allocated a further €300 million in June 2024 under the Environmental Aid scheme.[11] That later €55 million figure combines two schemes, so it should not be read as Green Transition Fund spend alone.

05 / how to read grant data as an account signal

How to read grant data as an account signal

In our view, a published decarbonisation grant is evidence that a supported project was planned or funded. It is not evidence that a particular supplier will be appointed, that a specified piece of equipment will be bought, or that work is still underway.

How suppliers can use the public record:

  • Separate planning support from capital support. A voucher, GreenStart, GreenPlus or energy-audit grant is a capability or intent signal. An EXEED Stage 2 award or a capital investment grant is a stronger investment signal, but it still does not identify the equipment, the site or the buying route.[3][7][12]
  • Keep official series apart. An approvals total, a payments total and an impact-report “grants supported” total can all be correct and still disagree. Quote the series you mean, with its year.[4][5][6]
  • Treat case studies as examples. A published case study names one organisation and one project. It does not represent all recipients, and it may omit later changes to scope or timing.
  • Confirm the account, not just the programme. Whether one named manufacturer received a grant, which site it covers, and whether any supplier decision remains open, has to be checked from a source that actually names that firm and that project. To rank accounts once that is checked, see industrial account prioritisation.

06 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Is there a public list of Irish manufacturers that received decarbonisation grants?
No. Neither SEAI nor Enterprise Ireland publishes a complete public list of recipients for these programmes. What is public are the scheme rules, official totals, selected case studies and, in some cases, a company’s own announcement. A named firm’s award has to be confirmed from a source that actually names that firm.
What is the SEAI EXEED grant?
It is SEAI’s grant for more complex energy-efficiency and renewable-energy projects on non-domestic buildings and processes, including new or upgraded manufacturing processes. Support of up to €3 million per project is available in two stages: pre-investment work and capital investment. The 2026 scheme is open year-round, and applicants can apply for either stage independently.
What Enterprise Ireland decarbonisation supports should suppliers know about?
The published supports fall into planning and capital investment. Planning supports include a Climate Action Voucher of up to €1,800, GreenStart support of up to €5,000, and GreenPlus support of up to €50,000. For manufacturing processes, capital investment support was published at between €20,000 and €1 million per project. Check the live Enterprise Ireland pages, because funds have end dates.
How should suppliers use grant data as an account signal?
Treat a published award or case study as evidence that a supported project existed, not as a supplier shortlist. Planning grants are a capability or intent signal. A capital grant is an investment signal, but it does not identify the equipment, the site, the buying route or whether work is finished. Confirm those points account by account.
How is this different from energy-audit signals?
This page covers public grant and programme data for decarbonisation investment. The energy-audit signals page covers energy-audit evidence and how far it can be taken in account research. An audit is not the same as a grant, and a grant is not the same as a supplier opportunity.

EU manufacturing capacity utilisation, Q4 2026

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

EU-27 manufacturing plants reported capacity utilisation of 78.4% in the fourth quarter of 2026, seasonally adjusted, 0.5 percentage points higher than in the third quarter of 2026 and than in the fourth quarter of 2025, by our count.[1][2] Ireland stood at 76.4%, 2.0 percentage points below the EU-27, and ranked 16th of the 25 member countries with a published figure, by our count.[1][2] The figures come from the European Commission's harmonised industry survey of manufacturing, indicator "Current level of capacity utilization (%)" (BS-ICU-PC), disseminated by Eurostat in dataset ei_bsin_q_r2. The series has no NACE industry split. The dataset was last updated on 29 October 2026, 11:00 (UTC+1), and was checked on 24 November 2026.[1][2][3] The Q4 2026 figures are from the October 2026 industry survey, which Eurostat labels as the fourth quarter. Fieldwork is in the first two to three weeks of the first month of each quarter (January, April, July and October), so the Q4 2026 reading is not a measure of the completed quarter.[1][3]

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat ei_bsin_q_r2, indicator BS-ICU-PC, seasonally adjusted; data updated 29 October 2026, 11:00 (UTC+1); checked 24 November 2026.

  • EU-27 manufacturing capacity utilisation, Q4 2026: 78.4%, up 0.5 percentage points from 77.9% in both Q3 2026 and Q4 2025, by our count. No observation flag on those cells.[1][2]
  • The Q4 2026 EU-27 rate is 4.3 percentage points below the 82.7% recorded in Q1 2022, and 3.7 percentage points below the 2015–2019 average of 82.1% (the mean of 20 quarterly observations, by our count).[1][2]
  • Austria and Sweden were joint highest among the published EU countries in Q4 2026, both at 84.8%, by our count. Cyprus was the lowest published rate, at 62.2%.[1][2]
  • Ireland: 76.4% in Q4 2026, 16th of the 25 published EU countries and 2.0 percentage points below the EU-27, by our count. That is down 1.5 percentage points from 77.9% in Q3 2026 and down 1.1 percentage points from 77.5% in Q4 2025, by our count.[1][2]
  • Estonia and Malta have no published figure for Q3 2026 or Q4 2026. Their last published seasonally adjusted rates were 71.1% and 43.8% respectively, both in Q2 2026. The United Kingdom is not in the dataset.[1][2][3]

Three points stand out:

  • Spare capacity is still wider than in 2022. The EU-27 rate of 78.4% in Q4 2026 remains 4.3 percentage points below the 82.7% recorded in Q1 2022, by our count, and 3.7 percentage points below the 2015–2019 average of 82.1%, by our count. After easing from that 2022 peak to 77.4% in Q1 2025, the EU rate has only edged up.[1][2]
  • The Q4 2026 change was uneven. Of the 25 published countries, 10 rose, 11 fell and 4 were unchanged from Q3 2026, by our count. The largest quarterly falls were Hungary (−3.8 percentage points), Greece (−3.2) and Czechia (−2.7). The largest rises were Sweden and Slovakia (both +2.5) and Croatia (+2.2), by our count. Belgium had the largest year-on-year fall, at −5.0 percentage points, from 78.7% in Q4 2025 to 73.7%.[1][2]
  • Ireland sat below the EU-27 and in the middle of the published ranks. At 76.4%, Ireland was 2.0 percentage points under the EU-27 rate and 16th of 25, by our count, just below Luxembourg (76.5%) and just above Croatia (76.0%). Ten published countries were above the EU-27 rate, Poland matched it at 78.4%, and 14 were below, by our count.[1][2]
03 / ireland against the eu

Ireland against the EU

Irish manufacturing capacity utilisation has run below the EU-27 in most quarters since the first quarter of 2022, by our count. The gap closed in Q4 2024, when Ireland (78.6%) was above the EU-27 (77.5%), and again in Q3 2026, when both were 77.9%. In Q4 2026 Ireland dropped back to 76.4%, 2.0 percentage points below the EU-27, by our count. Ireland's 74.5% reading in Q1 2026 was its lowest since 73.9% in Q2 2021, by our count.[1][2]

By our count, the average of the 20 seasonally adjusted quarterly observations from 2015-Q1 to 2019-Q4 was 82.1% for the EU-27 and 77.9% for Ireland. Ireland's Q4 2026 rate of 76.4% is 1.5 percentage points below that Irish average, by our count.[1][2]

04 / what the figures say about equipment and capex demand

What the figures say about equipment and capex demand

Capacity utilisation is a survey measure of how fully manufacturers say they are using their current capacity. A high rate means less spare capacity, which can precede orders for equipment, tooling and plant expansion. A lower rate means more spare capacity, so a rise in output can often be met without new kit. The figures are a timing signal for suppliers, not a list of plants that will buy.[1][2][3]

On that reading, the EU-27 is not as tight as it was in 2022. Utilisation in Q4 2026 was 78.4%, still below both the Q1 2022 peak of 82.7% and the 2015–2019 average of 82.1%, by our count. Some member countries are tighter: Austria and Sweden, both at 84.8%, and Slovenia, at 83.1%, sit well above the EU-27. Others, including Cyprus (62.2%), Romania (65.6%) and Denmark (66.0%), report much more spare capacity. Those gaps are large enough to matter when you choose a sales territory, but this series cannot tell you which industry inside a country is busy, because it has no NACE split.[1][2]

For Ireland, pair the survey rate with actual capital-goods trade in Irish capital goods imports. Use both, with production and investment data, when you prioritise industrial accounts. Demand Range can turn that country-level signal into a named list of plants and operating companies, with the public evidence for each row. Request a written scope if you want that work done on a territory.

Use the figures with five caveats. First, these are survey estimates from the Commission's harmonised industry survey of manufacturing, not a census of plants, and some country series move sharply from one quarter to the next. Second, the dataset has no NACE dimension, so there is no sector split. Third, Estonia and Malta are not published for Q3 2026 or Q4 2026, and there is no UK series. Fourth, the EU-27 figure is a published aggregate; do not average the country rates to reconstruct it. Fifth, the Q4 2026 figure is the October 2026 survey reading, taken in the first month of the quarter, not an average for a completed quarter.[1][2][3]

05 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What is the Q4 2026 EU manufacturing capacity utilisation figure?
Seasonally adjusted capacity utilisation in EU-27 manufacturing was 78.4% in the fourth quarter of 2026. That figure is from the October 2026 survey, which Eurostat labels Q4 2026; it is not a completed-quarter average. That is 0.5 percentage points higher than in both the third quarter of 2026 and the fourth quarter of 2025, by our count.[1][2]
What is Ireland's Q4 2026 manufacturing capacity utilisation figure?
Seasonally adjusted capacity utilisation in Irish manufacturing was 76.4% in the fourth quarter of 2026. That figure is from the October 2026 survey, which Eurostat labels Q4 2026; it is not a completed-quarter average. It is 2.0 percentage points below the EU-27 rate of 78.4%, and 16th of the 25 EU countries with a published figure, by our count.[1][2]
Which EU countries had the highest manufacturing capacity utilisation?
Austria and Sweden were joint highest among the published EU countries in the fourth quarter of 2026, both at 84.8%, by our count. Slovenia followed at 83.1%, then Czechia and France, both at 82.1%. Cyprus had the lowest published rate, at 62.2%, with Romania at 65.6% and Denmark at 66.0%.[1][2]
Does the survey break capacity utilisation down by industry?
No. Eurostat's quarterly industry survey series for this indicator has no NACE activity dimension, so the figures are for manufacturing as a whole. You cannot read a food, pharmaceutical or machinery utilisation rate from this dataset. Use other official sources when a sector split is needed.[1][3]
What does capacity utilisation say about equipment and capex demand?
A high rate means plants report less spare capacity, which can precede equipment demand. The EU-27 rate of 78.4% in the fourth quarter of 2026 was below the 82.7% recorded in the first quarter of 2022, and 3.7 percentage points below the 2015–2019 average of 82.1%, by our count. It is a timing signal, not a list of buying plants.[1][2]
Is there a UK figure in this dataset?
No. The Eurostat industry survey dataset used on this page does not publish a United Kingdom series. The published geographies are the EU-27 aggregate, the euro area, the 27 EU member countries and a small group of candidate countries. This page therefore has no UK manufacturing capacity utilisation figure.[1][3]

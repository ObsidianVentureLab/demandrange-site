Innovative manufacturers in Ireland and the EU: what the innovation survey shows

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

In 2022 to 2024, 52.2% of Irish industry enterprises with 10 or more persons engaged were innovation-active, up from 50.3% in 2020 to 2022, according to the CSO. Across all surveyed enterprises the share fell, from 47.3% to 44.9%. Industry here means NACE sections B to E: mining, manufacturing, energy, and water and waste.[1][3]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page covers one thing: the official innovation survey shares for Irish industry and EU manufacturers, by firm size and ownership, and how a supplier can read them. It covers which firms innovate, not how much they spend on R&D.

01 / key facts

Key facts

CSO Innovation in Irish Enterprises 2024, released 22 July 2026; Eurostat inn_cis13_bas, CIS2022, updated 29 April 2026; checked 24 November 2026.

  • Irish industry: 52.2% innovation-active in 2022 to 2024, against 42.0% in selected services and 44.9% for all surveyed enterprises.[1][3]
  • Irish enterprises of 250 or more persons engaged: 73.8% innovation-active in 2022 to 2024, against 40.9% of those with 10 to 49. Figures cover industry and selected services together. In industry alone the shares were 84.9% and 45.5% in 2022 to 2024.[1][4][8]
  • Foreign-owned enterprises in Ireland: 50.5% innovation-active in 2022 to 2024, against 42.9% of Irish-owned enterprises, across industry and selected services together. In industry alone the shares were 65.6% and 48.4%.[5][9]
  • Irish manufacturing (NACE C) in Eurostat's CIS2022: 58.7% innovation-active in 2020 to 2022, 10th of the 26 EU countries with a published value, by our count. The EU-27 manufacturing total is confidential.[6]
  • The EU-27 industry share (NACE B to E) was 53.3% in 2020 to 2022, against 56.1% for Ireland.[6]
02 / what innovation active means in official statistics

What "innovation-active" means in official statistics

Both the CSO and Eurostat take their figures from the Community Innovation Survey (CIS), which follows the OECD and Eurostat guidelines for innovation data (2018 edition). Those guidelines define an innovation as a new or improved product or process, or a combination of both, that differs from the firm's earlier products or processes and has been made available to users or brought into use.[1][2]

An enterprise counts as innovation-active if, during the three-year survey period, it did at least one of these:[1]

  • introduced a product innovation (a new or improved good or service on the market);
  • introduced a process innovation (a new or improved business process brought into use);
  • carried out in-house or external R&D;
  • had innovation activity that did not lead to a product or process innovation, whether ongoing, abandoned or completed.

So innovation-active is broader than "launched a new product". A firm that brought in a new production method, or ran R&D that has not yet paid off, also counts.[1]

The survey covers enterprises with 10 or more persons engaged in industry and selected services. In Ireland, industry is NACE sections B to E and selected services are NACE divisions 46, 49 to 53, 58 to 63, 64 to 66 and 71 to 73. The CSO issued about 4,450 forms for the 2022 to 2024 survey, with a response rate of 53.2%.[2]

  • Size matters more than ownership. In 2022 to 2024, the innovation-active share of large enterprises was 32.9 percentage points above that of small ones across all surveyed enterprises, and 39.4 points above in industry, by our count.[4][8]
  • Ownership gap. Across all surveyed enterprises, the gap between foreign-owned and Irish-owned enterprises was 7.6 points in 2022 to 2024, down from 11.0 points in 2020 to 2022. In industry it was wider, at 17.2 points, up from 16.3 in 2020 to 2022, by our count.[5][9]
  • Spending is concentrated. Foreign-owned enterprises accounted for 75.5% of innovation spending in Ireland in 2024, and Irish-owned enterprises 24.5%.[1]

06 / innovation as an account fit signal

Innovation as an account-fit signal

The survey shares are not a list of firms. The CSO treats CIS answers as strictly confidential and uses them only for statistical purposes, so the survey names no firms.[2] What the shares give a supplier is a prior: how likely a typical account in a segment is to be buying or changing something.

  • Weight by size first. Large enterprises are far more likely to be innovation-active than small ones: 84.9% against 45.5% in Irish industry in 2022 to 2024, and 82.1% against 48.0% in EU-27 industry in 2020 to 2022.[6][8] If your product needs a firm that is changing its processes, start with medium and large accounts. Our industrial ideal customer profile guide shows how to add a size band.
  • Innovation-active firms hold most of the activity. In 2020 to 2022, innovation-active manufacturers in Ireland accounted for 93.1% of Irish manufacturing net turnover and 79.1% of persons employed, among enterprises with 10 or more employees.[6]
  • Process change is the common form. Irish industry enterprises were more likely to bring in a process innovation (38.2%) than a product one (23.4%) in 2022 to 2024. Suppliers of equipment, automation and software sell into process change.[1]
  • Energy and materials. 14.2% of Irish industry enterprises introduced an innovation that significantly reduced their own energy use or CO2 footprint in 2022 to 2024.[1]
  • Add an account-level trigger. A segment share tells you where to look, not which firm is ready. Pair it with dated, firm-level evidence, such as IDA-announced investments, and rank accounts with our industrial account prioritisation method.
  • Size the segment. For how many manufacturers each EU country has, by size class, see EU industrial enterprises by country.

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What does innovation-active mean?
An innovation-active enterprise introduced a new or improved product or process, did in-house or external R&D, or had innovation activity that was ongoing, abandoned or completed during the three-year survey period. The definition follows the OECD and Eurostat innovation guidelines, which the CSO and Eurostat both use for the Community Innovation Survey.[1][2]
What share of Irish industry is innovation-active?
52.2% of Irish industry enterprises with 10 or more persons engaged were innovation-active in 2022 to 2024, up from 50.3% in 2020 to 2022, according to the CSO. Industry covers mining, manufacturing, energy, and water and waste. Across all surveyed enterprises the share fell from 47.3% to 44.9%.[3]
How do Irish manufacturers compare with the rest of the EU?
In 2020 to 2022, 58.7% of Irish manufacturers were innovation-active, according to Eurostat. That placed Ireland 10th of the 26 EU countries with a published value, by our count, above the median of 57.2%. Belgium was highest at 76.0%. The EU-27 manufacturing total is confidential.[6]
Are larger firms more likely to innovate?
Yes. In 2022 to 2024, 84.9% of Irish industry enterprises with 250 or more persons engaged were innovation-active, against 60.2% of those with 50 to 249 and 45.5% of those with 10 to 49, according to the CSO. Across industry and selected services together, the shares were 73.8%, 52.7% and 40.9%.[4][8]
Why do the CSO and Eurostat give different Irish figures?
One difference is timing: the CSO revised its weighting on 22 July 2026, after Eurostat's last update on 29 April 2026. For 2020 to 2022, the CSO's revised all-enterprise figure is 47.3%, while Eurostat still shows 51.4%. Use the CSO for Irish trends and Eurostat for comparing EU countries.[1][6]
Does the data cover the UK?
No. The CSO survey covers enterprises in Ireland, and Eurostat's CIS2022 table has no UK row. The UK publishes its own innovation survey, so check its definitions and reference period before comparing UK shares with the CSO or Eurostat figures on this page.[2][6]

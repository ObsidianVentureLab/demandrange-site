Industrial lead generation approaches compared

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Industrial suppliers in Ireland, the UK and the EU find new accounts through agencies, contact-data providers, paid project-lead databases, public tenders, official registers and researched account lists. Intent data is a timing signal, not a seventh full method. This page compares what each approach finds, what it misses and what proof it gives, without ranking them.

This is an informational comparison, not a service pitch. Demand Range also publishes a commercial service page for industrial lead generation in Ireland and the UK. The deeper comparisons sit on data providers versus researched lists and project-lead databases. The definition of industrial account research is the counterpart to this overview.

01 / key facts

Key facts

  • TED, the online version of the Supplement to the Official Journal of the EU, is dedicated to European public procurement and is free to use. Its About page also states that, every day from Monday to Friday, over 3,000 public procurement notices are published on TED, as seen on 10 November 2026. That weekday figure has no "last updated" date on the page.[1][2]
  • TED's European public procurement page states that each year services, supply and public works contracts worth about EUR 815 billion are published by public authorities in the EU, as seen on 10 November 2026. That annual figure is also undated on the page.[2]
  • Find a Tender is where UK public sector buyers publish notices about procurement opportunities and contracts. An enhanced service was launched on 24 February 2025. A GOV.UK factsheet updated on 21 October 2026 says "Find a Tender is free to use for everyone."[3][4]
  • eTenders "connects public sector buyers with suppliers who want to sell to Government." The official open-data dataset of contract notices covers published competitions from 1 January 2013 to 30 September 2026.[5][6]
  • Companies House makes the public data it holds on companies available free of charge, and you do not need to register to view it. It also says the service is not intended to be comprehensive.[7]
  • The European Industrial Emissions Portal, modified 20 August 2026, presents information on the largest industrial complexes covered by the Industrial Emissions Directive and on E-PRTR releases and transfers, and it includes large-combustion-plant data for EU Member States and the United Kingdom.[8][9]
  • Vendor-sourced, not official: Forrester's 21 January 2026 press release says "the typical buying decision now includes 13 internal stakeholders and nine external influencers," and its blog of the same day says the report is based on a survey of nearly 18,000 global business buyers. This is a cross-industry analyst figure, not an industrial-only measurement.[10][11]

03 / agencies

Agencies

Agencies in this market usually sell activity: meetings, introductions or campaign responses. The proof is whatever the contract measures. That proof shows that a conversation happened or that someone replied. It does not, by itself, show that a site runs a relevant process or that a project is public, unless the same engagement also includes that research.

Agency models differ, and this page does not rate them. For Demand Range's own scoped research offer, see industrial lead generation for Ireland and the UK.

04 / contact data providers

Contact-data providers

Contact-data providers sell breadth and speed: filter a large database of companies and people, then export or load the records into a CRM. That is useful when a market is well described by industry codes, size and location, and when the records will be checked before use.

Industrial briefs often depend on facts a filter field may not hold, such as the process at a named site. This overview does not repeat the vendor-page descriptions or the test already set out on data providers versus researched lists.

05 / paid project lead databases

Paid project-lead databases

Paid project-lead databases find planned or active works: a plant to be built, extended or upgraded. That is a different unit from an account. A project record is time-limited, and many equipment purchases at running plants never appear as a construction or capex lead.

Providers should be described only from their own pages on the day they are checked, with no "better than" claims. That comparison is the job of industrial project leads in Ireland and the UK, which this page does not repeat.

06 / public tenders

Public tenders

Public tenders answer a precise question: what is a public buyer, or another body the rules cover, purchasing or awarding now? They do not map the installed industrial base.

TED (Tenders Electronic Daily) is the online version of the Supplement to the Official Journal of the EU, dedicated to European public procurement. Access is free of charge. The most widely used procedures involve a competition notice and a contract award notice; planning notices and contract modifications also appear. TED's own pages state that over 3,000 notices are published each weekday and that about EUR 815 billion of services, supply and public works contracts are published each year; both figures were visible on 10 November 2026 and are undated on those pages.[1][2]

Since 1 January 2021, UK contracting authorities and contracting entities can no longer publish procurement notices on TED, except for later notices on procedures already published by 31 December 2020.[2] New UK notices are published on Find a Tender. From 24 February 2025, that service carries above- and below-threshold notices for new UK procurements, except below-threshold notices in Scotland, and GOV.UK says it is free to use.[3][4]

In Ireland, eTenders connects public sector buyers with suppliers who want to sell to government. An official open-data file of contract notices covers published competitions from 1 January 2013 to 30 September 2026.[5][6]

How to search those portals, including CPV codes and notice types, is already documented on the TED, eTenders and UK public-tenders guides. This page does not repeat those walk-throughs.

07 / public registers

Public registers

Official registers answer a different question: which legal unit or site is this, and what does the state already publish about it?

Companies House makes its public company data available free of charge, without registration to view, including basic company information, the nature of business (SIC), status, officers, charges and insolvency information. It also warns that the service is not intended to be comprehensive, and that many details which may be relevant to particular circumstances have been omitted.[7] A registered office is an official address, not proof of a plant.

The European Industrial Emissions Portal is a single entry point for data on industrial emissions and safety. Its Industrial Emissions section, modified 20 August 2026, presents information on the largest industrial complexes in Europe covered by the Industrial Emissions Directive, and on pollutant releases and transfers under the E-PRTR Regulation, and it includes large-combustion-plant data for EU Member States and the United Kingdom.[8][9] That is evidence about in-scope sites. It does not list every manufacturer, and it does not show who buys equipment.

National environmental licences and company registers in Ireland, the UK and the EU work the same way: they prove what they publish, on the date you retrieved them. They do not, on their own, prove demand.

08 / researched account lists

Researched account lists

A researched account list starts from a written brief and records the companies or sites that fit it, with dated public sources and a note of what remains unknown. The unit is the company or the named site, not a meeting and not a contact record. The proof is the reason for inclusion.

That work is slower to build than a database export, and it does not include named contacts unless that is a separate written scope. The method is defined on what industrial account research is. The commercial scope for Ireland and the UK is on the service page.

09 / intent data

Intent data

Intent data appears here as one row, not as a page of its own.

On 10 November 2026, a 6sense guide published on 31 July 2024 described intent data as "info about what your potential clients are thinking when they're making decisions," gathered from "topics being researched, research intensity, and product pages browsed," and tied "back to individual companies that are performing research."[12] Those are vendor words, not official statistics.

A vendor score can be a timing signal. It is not a licence, a tender notice, a filed account or a site-level process proof. Forrester, also vendor-sourced, describes large cross-industry buying groups — 13 internal stakeholders and nine external influencers in its January 2026 release — which is a reason to treat a single behavioural flag with care, not a reason to prefer one approach over another.[10][11]

10 / choosing an approach

Choosing an approach

Match the approach to the question you need answered:

  • If you need meetings or campaign activity, an agency contract defines that work.
  • If industry, size and location are enough, and you will check the records, a contact-data list may be enough; test it as set out on the data-provider page.
  • If you need to know which plants are being built or upgraded, start with project-lead databases and with public tenders, then confirm the account.
  • If you need legal identity or site-level official evidence, start with the registers.
  • If you need a list of companies or sites that fit a brief, with reasons, dates and open questions, that is account research.

These approaches are often used together. A tender or a project record can sit on an account that was first found in a register. Intent data can time an approach to an account you already have evidence for. Combining them does not make one source "better than" another.

11 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What are the main industrial lead-generation approaches?
There are six common approaches, plus intent data as a timing signal. Agencies deliver campaign activity or meetings. Contact-data providers sell filterable company and contact records. Paid project-lead databases list planned or active works. Public tenders and official registers publish notices and site evidence. A researched account list records why each company or site fits a written brief.
Do public tenders show private industrial plant buying?
No. TED, Find a Tender and eTenders publish notices from public buyers, and from other bodies the procurement rules cover. A private plant's maintenance or upgrade spend often never appears as a public notice. Use a tender as proof of a published public purchase, not as a complete picture of industrial demand in Ireland, the UK and the EU.
Is intent data enough to choose industrial accounts?
No. One vendor describes intent data as information about topics being researched, research intensity and product pages browsed, tied back to companies. That can be a timing signal. It does not, on its own, prove that a site runs a process, appears on an official register, or fits a product brief. Check public evidence as well.
What proof does each industrial lead-generation approach give?
Each approach proves something different. An agency records activity. A data provider shows the fields that matched a filter. A project database gives a project record, dated as far as its provider states. A tender is a published notice, and a register is an official record. A researched list gives a written, sourced reason for each account.
Can industrial lead-generation approaches be combined?
Yes. A tender notice or a project record can sit on an account first found in an official register, and intent data can time an approach to an account you already have evidence for. Combining sources adds evidence, but no single source answers every question, so record which source supports each claim and the date you checked it.

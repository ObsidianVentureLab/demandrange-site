EPC and EPCM projects: who controls your equipment package?

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

For EPC and EPCM equipment supplier research, the contractor's name is only the start: find which package it controls, in which capacity and at what stage. The firms that specify, enquire and sign the supply contract can differ. An award notice tells you who won; it does not tell you who buys your equipment.

The useful output is a package-responsibility note: the supported roles, the supply boundary, the procurement stage and the open question that changes your route. Start with one package, not a list of project participants. The public notices below are dated examples; the worked maps are fictional.

01 / use epc and epcm as starting questions not buyer labels

Use EPC and EPCM as starting questions, not buyer labels

EPC refers here to engineering, procurement and construction; EPCM refers to engineering, procurement and construction management.[1][3] The distinction worth researching is integrated delivery responsibility versus a management or consultancy appointment. These are not interchangeable routes, and the acronym alone cannot assign a particular equipment purchase.

A German battery-storage notice illustrates an integrated EPC scope. TED 512696-2026, published 24 July 2026, describes the proposed Mosbach project as turnkey EPC, including engineering, procurement, construction, installation, the grid-connection interface, testing, commissioning and handover.[1] Its preliminary scope says final technical requirements, interfaces, contractual conditions and detailed scope would be specified in the subsequent RFP phase.[1]

By contrast, Finnish notice 343546-2025, published 28 May 2025, concerns an EPCM consultancy service for Kajaani's district-heating renewal. Its description expands EPCM as Engineering, Procurement and Construction Management and assigns the consultant design, procurement and construction-management tasks; this is our working translation of the Finnish description.[3] That is a service-scope description, not evidence that the consultant contracts every equipment supply in its own name.

Use the following conditional research branches:

  • If the relevant package sits inside an integrated EPC delivery scope, investigate the appointed contractor's package-procurement responsibility. Do not assume the project-wide title includes your component, removes owner approvals or creates an accessible subcontract.
  • If the appointment is EPCM services, separate the consultant's design and procurement tasks from the entity entering the equipment contract. Test whether the owner contracts directly, whether the consultant acts on the owner's behalf, or whether a package contractor has the relevant supply responsibility. Leave that field unresolved until package-specific evidence answers it.
  • If the wording is only “engineering partner”, “project delivery” or a corporate capability statement, do not promote it to either model. Find the scope and appointment evidence first.

These branches are our research method, not contractual rules.

02 / read the action behind the appointment

Read the action behind the appointment

A particularly useful EPCM example is TED 511070-2026, published 23 July 2026, titled Pre-Market Consultation – 48-inch Valves for Offshore Hydrogen Pipeline. Its buyer wording names AquaDuctus Pipeline GmbH with support from Worley Projects as appointed EPCM; the description says Worley Projects GmbH acts for and on behalf of the client.[2] The same description explicitly says the exercise is non-binding, is not a request for quotation and does not launch a procurement procedure.[2]

There is a source distinction to preserve: TED labels the record Direct award preannouncement, with notice type Voluntary ex-ante transparency notice, while the title and description concern that pre-market consultation.[2] Record both layers. Do not silently “correct” the notice type, infer an award from the display category, or describe the consultation as an equipment enquiry ready for pricing.

The Finnish record makes a different point about appointments. It is a contract modification notice, names Sweco Finland Oy as winner for LOT-0000, and records 13 March 2024 as the contract-conclusion date.[3] It supports a dated consultancy-contract finding. It does not establish which equipment packages were unawarded then, or are available now.

Likewise, the Mosbach page points readers to a later notice, 558900-2026, published 12 August 2026 for the same procedure.[1][5] It is a buyer's correction: the procurement documents are now officially available in German only, and tenders in the second phase may be submitted only in German.[5] We have not reviewed the current outcome, so re-check status before any real action.

The practical rule is to keep two timelines: appointment of the project participant and procurement of your equipment package. An established consultant can be involved in preparatory work; an EPC award does not date every supply order. For an actual notice or lot assessment, continue with the existing tender-triage guide, rather than treating this article as a notice-status service.

03 / define the package before assigning responsibility

Define the package before assigning responsibility

Write a boundary sentence: “We are investigating your equipment or component family within the named package, at the project or site, during the project phase.” Record the source's package identifier verbatim. If the package is only your hypothesis, mark it proposed research scope—not evidenced procurement.

Check what crosses that boundary. Is the deliverable a complete skid, the components incorporated into it, installation of supplied equipment, commissioning support, or spares? Do not use the same record for a complete-system sale and a component sale into that system. Each asks a different responsibility question.

For this research method, use these working distinctions—not legal definitions:

  • Included equipment: the scope expressly assigns provision of the equipment to the delivery package. Check selection constraints separately; provision is not necessarily free choice of supplier.
  • Nominated equipment or supplier: a source identifies a specified make, model or seller, or an approval restriction. Record exactly what is nominated. A fixed model does not automatically identify the seller, and an approved vendor list does not prove a new vendor can join it.
  • Free-issued or owner-supplied equipment: the scope describes equipment supplied to the package contractor by another party. Investigate the upstream supply arrangement separately. Receiving, installing or connecting that equipment does not establish that the recipient purchases it.
  • Unresolved boundary: an overall project description mentions the equipment but does not allocate its provision. Keep the component route on hold; do not invent a separate lot or supplier need.

If a company builds the supplied system rather than managing the project, use the OEM component-route guide for the build-operation question. If the issue is later servicing and parts provision, use the MRO scope guide. Neither route inherits procurement authority from the EPC/EPCM label.

04 / separate the roles that change the supplier route

Separate the roles that change the supplier route

For each role, record the organisation, the exact task, the package and phase, and its evidence. A single organisation may fill several roles, but do not copy it across the fields merely to complete the note.

Specification author
Who develops the relevant requirement or datasheet? Distinguish the author's task from the owner setting standards and the party approving the design. A technical contribution is not authority to accept an offer.
Technical approver
Who can approve the equipment, an alternative model or a deviation? Ask whether approval is for a product, a manufacturer or a particular supplier. Do not treat engineering approval as a commercial order.
Enquiry issuer
Who sends the request, and in whose stated capacity? An EPCM team can appear in correspondence on behalf of a client, as the consultation example illustrates; that does not by itself establish the eventual contracting entity.
Commercial evaluator and selector
Who compares offers, recommends a supplier and makes the decision? Keep recommendation and approval separate where the evidence does. Do not infer an unrestricted choice from the ability to collect quotations.
Contracting entity
Which legal entity would enter the supply agreement or issue the order, and for which deliverable? Identify whether an issuer acts in its own name or on behalf of another party. A logo, email domain or project-directory entry cannot settle this field.
Installer and interface owner
Who receives, connects or commissions the equipment, and who manages the adjacent scope? Keep these delivery tasks separate from purchasing. Record excluded equipment and unresolved interfaces without inventing a technical solution.

The point is not to create six sales contacts. It is to identify the responsibility that a permitted next check must resolve. Names of people and contact sourcing are separate scopes, not default outputs of this map.

05 / work one fictional package through the decision

Work one fictional package through the decision

Fictional example. Every organisation, package and statement below is invented and describes no real project.

The invented supplier sells valve components. Its research question concerns Package FIC-P17, a utility skid at a fictional EU industrial site. The first invented announcement says Fictional Owner O has appointed Fictional Consultant M for EPCM services. At that point, the equipment boundary, specification responsibility and contracting entity are all unknown. “Approach M as the valve buyer” is not a supported handover.

Card A: an owner-side package contract

An invented package schedule assigns M preparation of the skid specification and administration of enquiries on behalf of O. It assigns technical approval and the skid supply contract to O. Fictional Skid Builder B supplies the complete skid and purchases its incorporated valves; O free-issues a separate drive unit to B.

The note now separates two purchases. For the complete skid, O is the stipulated contracting entity and M administers the enquiry. For the incorporated valves, B has the stipulated purchasing responsibility. The free-issued drive is outside B's purchasing scope in this example. None of those findings establishes an open order, a suitable valve or a willingness to consider this supplier.

Card B: ordering without unrestricted selection

A second invented schedule requires B to use a valve model approved by O and seek O's approval for alternatives. It does not say whether B may choose among sellers of the approved model or whether a seller is nominated.

For the component supplier, the next question is seller choice and the applicable approval route, not who physically installs the skid. B's ordering responsibility is supported in the fiction; freedom to select this supplier is unknown. M's specification work does not make M the valve-contract counterparty.

A bounded handover would read: “Investigate B's incorporated-valve supply arrangement for FIC-P17. Retain O's model-approval restriction and M's package-enquiry role. Establish whether seller choice remains with B or is nominated by O. Package status and acceptance of alternatives are unknown; no approach is authorised by this note.”

Card C: change the contract boundary, not just the acronym

Consider a separate counterfactual—not a later event in the same project. An invented EPC scope assigns Fictional Contractor E the complete utility-skid delivery and incorporated-equipment procurement, but expressly excludes the drive supplied by O. It still requires O's approval of valve alternatives.

The route to investigate changes to E's procurement of the incorporated valves. Owner approval and the excluded drive remain separate. If the scope instead said E only installs a free-issued complete skid, that would not support valve purchasing by E. The scope change changes the research route; changing the heading to “EPC” does not.

06 / fictional example from a ted award to an epc package

Fictional example: from a TED award to an EPC package

Fictional example. The notice, organisations and packages are invented; the notice number is not a real TED record.

A fictional pump and valve supplier reads an invented contract award notice on TED, FIC-000001-2026. It names Fictional Water Utility W as buyer and Fictional Contractor K as winner of Lot 1, the EPC delivery of a treatment-plant extension. The supplier traces it in four steps:

  1. Read the award, not just the winner. The notice type is a contract award notice, so a contract exists for Lot 1. The lot description assigns K design, procurement, construction and commissioning. That supports an integrated EPC scope for K. It does not say which equipment K buys itself.
  2. Find the package. An invented scope summary in the tender documents splits the works into packages. Package P-04, chemical dosing skids, is inside K's procurement scope. Package P-07, the main pumps, is owner-supplied: W buys them under a separate framework and free-issues them to K.
  3. Pick the right route. For the dosing skids, the route runs to K, or to a skid builder K appoints. For the main pumps, the route runs to W's framework, not to K. Writing "K is the pump buyer" would be wrong.
  4. Check timing and limits. The award date is not the order date for P-04. A further invented note says W must approve skid valve makes. So K orders, but within W's approved list.

The handover reads: "K holds the EPC contract for Lot 1 (award FIC-000001-2026). P-04 dosing skids sit in K's procurement scope, subject to W's approved valve makes. P-07 pumps are free-issued by W. Find out whether K has placed P-04 and whether new makes can be added to W's list." The same steps apply to a real award: read the TED notice types first, then the lot, then the package documents.

07 / verify these points before any approach

Verify these points before any approach

Use the map to decide the next research action, not to grant commercial access. Before a separate, authorised approach, check:

  1. Identity and capacity: do the source and package documents identify the same entity, project and appointment? Is an organisation acting for itself or for a client?
  2. Exact supply boundary: are you offering the package deliverable, a component within it, or work explicitly excluded? Are there nominated or free-issued items?
  3. Decision rights: which roles are supported, which are recommendations only, and which approvals remain? Who would enter this equipment contract?
  4. Current procurement evidence: is this a capability, appointment, consultation, invitation, award or modification? Check the relevant version and subsequent changes; do not invent an open/unawarded status from missing evidence.
  5. Permitted route: do current instructions actually describe how the relevant action may be taken? A registration page, administrative contact or past supplier relationship is not proof of access. Do not bypass stated procurement channels.
  6. Technical and commercial limits: leave duty, materials, interfaces, compatibility, qualification and buying intent unresolved unless separately assessed. A research match supplies none of these conclusions.

If the unknown is the contracting entity, hold the buyer claim. If the evidence expressly excludes component procurement from a contractor's scope, exclude that route for that package, not the company from every account brief. If current status cannot be established within the agreed review, return that gap; do not fill it with a sales deadline.

08 / commission a package responsibility note not a contact list

Commission a package-responsibility note, not a contact list

Template — replace the bracketed fields. A useful brief is: “For the project reference in the EU country, investigate your equipment or package boundary. Establish the appointment scope, specification and approval roles, enquiry issuer and contracting entity. Identify nominations and free-issued items. Return the source-backed finding, unresolved procurement status and next verification question; do not assume access or identify contacts by default.” Replace each marked field with your own details.

Ask for one evidence entry per responsibility: package and phase; organisation and capacity; observed task; exact source/section; source date or not stated; actual checked date; intended source use; limitation; next check. Keep contradictory findings separate. A source that resolves specification authorship need not resolve ordering authority.

Share non-sensitive product and research questions—not confidential tender documents, confidential correspondence or customer records.

10 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between EPC and EPCM for an equipment supplier?

Under an integrated EPC scope, the contractor usually takes on engineering, procurement and construction, as in the Mosbach notice.[1] Under EPCM, a consultant manages design, procurement and construction, and may act on the client's behalf, as in the hydrogen valve consultation.[2] Check which party signs your supply contract in each case.

Does a contract award notice mean the contractor is buying equipment now?

No. An award shows that a contract for that lot exists. Package orders can come much later, and some items may be bought by the owner and free-issued.

Where do I find EPC awards for EU projects?

TED publishes EU contract notices and award notices. Our TED guide explains the notice types and how to search them.

Research guide · United Kingdom

How to build a list of UK manufacturers that fits your brief

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

A UK manufacturing account list is only as useful as the evidence behind each line: a Companies House identity, an operating site confirmed from a second source, and an include, hold or reject decision with its reason. Companies House shows a company's registered office and status free of charge, but a company can use a service provider's address, such as an accountant's or solicitor's, as its registered office, so that address does not show where production happens.[1][2]

The useful output is not a row count: every candidate is checked against a written brief, and the reason and source are recorded. Worked companies on this page are fictional.

Key facts (Companies House, Environment Agency and ONS; checked 9 November 2026)

  • Companies House makes all the public data it holds on companies available free of charge, including the registered office address and company status.[1]
  • A UK company can use a service provider's address, for example an accountant's or solicitor's, as its registered office, and that address is shown on the public register.[2]
  • The Environment Agency's public registers include environmental permits for industrial installations such as refineries and food and drink factories, searchable by business name, permit number, local authority or address.[3]
  • Market context only: the ONS counted about 128,000 UK manufacturing businesses in March 2026; it counts businesses, not sites.[8]

01 / define the uk brief before searching

Define the UK brief before searching

Write down four things before you open a register:

Activity
What the customer must do for your product to matter: “pasteurises liquid milk”, not “food”. An activity can be tested against a source; a sector label cannot.
Territory
England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are covered by one company register but by different environmental regulators.[1] Say which nations are in scope and whether the test is the registered office or the operating site.
Counting unit
Company or site. A group with three plants can be one account, three sites or four records; decide before counting.
Exclusions
Existing customers, distributors without production, service-only firms, dormant companies or anything else your team will reject on sight.

The ideal customer profile guide has a blank worksheet for this step.

02 / use companies house for identity not location

Use Companies House for identity, not location

Companies House gives each candidate a register identity: its registered office address, company status and nature of business code are free to view.[1] Which manufacturing codes to search, and how many active companies use each, is covered in our reference on UK manufacturing SIC codes and company counts.

Two limits matter when you scope the list. First, the nature of business code is the company's own choice when it files, so a manufacturer can carry a holding-company or wholesale code and a trader can carry a manufacturing one. Second, the address on the register is the registered office, which can be a service provider's address, such as an accountant's or solicitor's, rather than the plant.[2] Treat this step as a candidate pool, not a list.

03 / confirm the operating site and activity

Confirm the operating site and activity

For each candidate, look for a second, independent source that ties the company to a site and an activity:

The company's own site pages
Named plant addresses, process descriptions and capability statements. Record the date you saw them.
England: Environment Agency
Public registers including environmental permits for industrial installations, searchable by name, address and permit.[3]
Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
SEPA, Natural Resources Wales and DAERA (for NIEA permits) publish their own registers and data.[4][5][6]
Major-hazard sites in Great Britain
HSE's public information search covers establishments subject to COMAH 2015.[7] Northern Ireland is regulated separately.

A permit shows that a regulated activity is authorised at a site. It does not show what equipment is installed, whether the activity is running today or who buys for it. Record what the source says and keep your inference in a separate field.

04 / include hold or reject every candidate

Include, hold or reject every candidate

Include
Identity is confirmed on the register, the company is active, and a dated source ties the stated activity to an in-scope site.
Hold
The company looks relevant but the site or activity is unconfirmed, for example a holding company whose subsidiaries may operate the plant. Write the next check.
Reject
A source states something that fails the brief: dissolved, dormant, resale only, out-of-scope nation. Record that reason, not “not a fit”.

A hold is not a reject. Rejecting for missing evidence silently shrinks your market; holding keeps the question visible. The same method applied to Ireland is in the Irish manufacturing account-research guide.

05 / a worked example fictional

A worked example (fictional)

Brief (fictional): a supplier of hygienic valves wants dairy processors with a production site in Scotland or Northern Ireland. Exclude wholesalers and existing customers. Count each site once.

Company A — SIC 10511, registered office in Glasgow
Website names a creamery in Ayrshire and describes pasteurising and bottling. Include the Ayrshire site, citing the website and its review date.
Company B — SIC 70100 (activities of head offices), registered office in Belfast
Group pages mention two dairy plants operated by subsidiaries. Hold: map the subsidiaries and their sites before counting.
Company C — SIC 46330 (wholesale of dairy products)
Website describes distribution only, with no processing site. Reject for the stated reason: resale only.
Company D — SIC 10512, status dormant
Reject: dormant on the register. Note it in case it becomes active again.

Companies A to D are invented for illustration and are not real companies, clients or prospects.

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Is a Companies House registered office the manufacturing site?

Not necessarily. A UK company can use a service provider's address, such as an accountant's or solicitor's, as its registered office, and that is the address the public register shows.[2] We treat it as identity evidence only, then confirm the operating site and activity from a second, dated source.

How does an Environment Agency permit help confirm a UK site?

In England, the Environment Agency's public registers include environmental permits for industrial installations, searchable by business name, permit number or address.[3] A permit shows that a regulated activity is authorised at a site. It does not show installed equipment, current output or who buys, so we record it as dated evidence.

What should a UK written scope state before research starts?

Four things: the activity your product depends on, the nations in scope and whether the test is the registered office or the operating site, whether you count companies or sites, and your exclusions. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own environmental regulators, so the territory choice changes which permit sources apply.[4][5][6]

Why is a UK candidate held rather than rejected?

A hold means the company looks relevant but its site or activity is unconfirmed, such as a holding company whose subsidiaries may run the plant. Rejecting for missing evidence shrinks the market. A hold keeps the question open with a next check; a reject needs a stated reason, such as dissolved or resale only.

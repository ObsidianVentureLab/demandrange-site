Who we help

Built for suppliers selling into Ireland, the UK and the EU.

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 28 October 2026

Demand Range helps suppliers of industrial equipment, technology and services define and research relevant businesses in their target countries across Ireland, the UK and the EU. The research territory follows the markets you want to investigate, not your company's home country; every brief needs its own country list, account criteria and exclusions.

A company can fit your market without a new project. Project and development signals add context where evidenced; they do not establish equipment demand.

01 / sectors

Use the sector as a starting point, not the acceptance test.

These are scoping questions, not a claim of complete coverage or technical suitability.

Food and beverage

Which production businesses and activities belong in the brief? Distinguish manufacturers from distributors, retailers and hospitality businesses.

Pharma and life sciences

Which facility types and company activities matter to your range? State the technical requirements that need specialist confirmation.

Water, energy and utilities

Is the intended account an operator, an engineering business or a contractor? Keep account relevance separate from any project or procurement status. For England and Wales, see the guide to UK water (AMP8) buyers.

Chemicals and process industries

Which process activities fit—and which applications or materials must be excluded or left for technical review?

Manufacturing and engineering

Do you sell to end users, machine builders or both? Set the company characteristics and site-level detail needed.

Construction and project delivery

Which contractors, integrators or engineering businesses are relevant routes? A project appointment alone does not establish control of an equipment package.

02 / routes

Specify the account route—not a promised contact.

For a product-specific brief, see prospect research for pump and flow-control suppliers.

  • Operators: businesses operating the facilities or assets covered by the brief.
  • OEMs: manufacturers whose product or equipment scope may be relevant.
  • Integrators: businesses whose work may connect systems or equipment.
  • Contractors and engineering businesses: potential routes where their actual role is evidenced.

The same company can have more than one role. Confirm its activity and relationship to the relevant site or project rather than assuming purchasing authority. Role-level context is not a promise of named contacts, direct dials or buyer access.

03 / eu sales route

Before researching another EU country, decide what you can offer there.

A company can fit your target market while your own route to serving it remains unresolved. Before commissioning the same account list across several countries, write down what the customer would receive and which parts your business could deliver. Shipping equipment, setting it up and supporting it are separate questions; a relevant prospect cannot answer them for you.

One offer, two country decisions

Wholly fictional illustration: an invented industrial monitoring-equipment supplier offers equipment, on-site setup and first-line support as one bundle. In unnamed EU Country A, its own internal delivery plan covers those tasks. In unnamed EU Country B, it has planned shipment only; setup and support arrangements are unresolved. These are invented business-planning assumptions, not facts about any country, technical recommendations or Demand Range delivery capabilities.

Country A: research customers for the defined offer.
The supplier can brief research on operators performing the relevant industrial activity, with the required operation and location stated. Its internal delivery plan does not establish any operator's equipment needs, technical fit or willingness to buy.
Country B: settle the route question before copying that brief.
More operator names would not resolve who performs setup and support. Keep the potential customer profile, but do not describe it as a ready-to-serve sales list. The supplier needs to choose what decision the next research assignment should support.

Choose the next assignment, not a country winner

  1. Keep the bundled offer: investigate potential support businesses against the specific tasks and applications needed. This is a partner-capability brief, separate from the operator-customer brief. Resale activity alone does not establish setup or support capability; capability alone does not establish willingness, capacity or an agreed partnership.
  2. Consider a narrower offer: the supplier first decides whether an equipment-only offer is workable and who would take responsibility for the omitted tasks. If it approves that model, write a new account brief for it. A researcher must not silently remove the support requirement to make Country B look easier.
  3. Defer that country: if neither route is resolved, keep the decision open. Requesting a larger customer list is not a substitute for an offer the supplier can deliver.

Bring the unresolved dependency to the brief

Template: replace the bracketed fields. For the partner route, a useful request is: “Investigate businesses performing [setup/support tasks] for [equipment/application scope] in [country]. Keep capability evidence separate from willingness and any proposed arrangement. We will decide whether the resulting delivery model supports a later operator-account brief.”

Keep account fit and your own delivery readiness separate in the handover. Neither creates an opportunity signal: industry-matched research need not require a project, while an opportunity-signal record needs separate project evidence. Neither establishes an available order.

Country starting points

Counts are manufacturing enterprises (NACE Rev. 2 section C) in 2024, from Eurostat. They size the starting pool only; they are not a count of accounts that fit your brief.

  • Ireland: 19,617 manufacturing enterprises (Eurostat, 2024). See Irish manufacturing account research and industrial lead generation for Ireland and the UK.
  • United Kingdom: the Eurostat dataset has no 2024 UK figure, so no count is given here. See UK manufacturing account research for building a UK list from Companies House.
  • Germany: 198,560 manufacturing enterprises (Eurostat, 2024). See Germany industrial account research for the public registers and activity codes to start from.
  • EU (27 countries): 2,180,638 manufacturing enterprises in total (Eurostat, 2024). Compare EU industrial enterprise counts by country (Eurostat, 2024), by NACE division and size class.

Use the country-specific research brief for source and language scoping. Share only a non-sensitive outline of your offer and missing delivery responsibility; country scope and deliverables still need assessment.

04 / fit

Write down what should be excluded.

A useful scope can exclude residential-only activity, irrelevant distribution models, territories outside your remit, existing accounts or channel conflicts. These are brief-specific choices, not universal judgments about those businesses.

05 / Written scope

Scope your market before counting accounts.

Bring your product or service range, target activities, country list and account exclusions. Country-specific sources, language requirements and deliverables are assessed before a written scope is agreed. Demand Range does company-level account research; named contacts and outreach are separate scopes, not included by default. Each country is scoped on its own. Anything outside Ireland, the UK and the EU is outside standard coverage.

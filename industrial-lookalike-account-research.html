Industrial sales research · Seed-to-company comparison

Industrial lookalike account research

In seed-based industrial lookalike research, compare companies with the relevant characteristics of an authorised seed profile—not simply its name, product vocabulary or industry label. Start with why that profile fits your offer, then test other companies against explicit rules.

A live customer seed is optional: you can start with an agreed target profile instead. This original guide and blank worksheet explore the seed-based route and help industrial suppliers compare company accounts. An industry-matched account, also called Look Alike, needs no active or upcoming project. Account relevance does not establish equipment suitability, current demand or permission to contact anyone.

01 / safe seed

1. Use an authorised profile, not a customer-list upload

Work from a profile you are authorised to use for this purpose. Keep the underlying customer record in its controlled environment. Describe the relevant activity, customer role and operating territory without circulating the customer's identity, order history or commercial arrangements.

Removing a name is not enough: a distinctive site, process combination, contract value or project detail can still expose a customer or confidential relationship. Generalise or omit those details; if the useful profile cannot be shared safely, stop and resolve that boundary first. Do not put live customer lists, personal data or client secrets into this worksheet or a quote enquiry.

Ask why this seed is worth repeating. A successful sale based on a personal introduction, exceptional discount or one-off emergency is not automatically a repeatable company characteristic. Record the hypothesis, not a claim that similar companies will buy.

02 / matching rules

2. Decide which similarities matter

Wholly fictional seed: Fictional Seed Finisher operates abrasive-blasting preparation and powder-coating services for external industrial customers at a production facility in the Republic of Ireland. An invented dust-extraction supplier wants to research companies with that operating profile. Nothing here comes from a client record.

Required characteristics
For this exercise: both stated finishing activities, an external-customer contract-finishing role, and relevant production in the Republic of Ireland. Count companies, with their supporting sites recorded separately.
Useful only with a stated reason
Operating scale or service area may help order otherwise suitable candidates if the supplier can explain the relevance. Do not impose an employee threshold merely because the seed happens to have that size.
Not transferable evidence
A similar business name, shared catalogue terms, attractive website or the seed's installed equipment cannot establish another company's activity or equipment requirements.
Exclusions and unknowns
Check existing-account and channel exclusions within the authorised client workflow. Keep installed equipment, specifications, budget and purchasing responsibility unknown unless separately evidenced.

Fix the rules and brief version before comparing candidates. Treat one seed as a starting hypothesis, not proof of the whole market. If a different activity or customer role looks promising, record a separate proposed brief rather than quietly widening this one.

03 / comparison method

3. Compare evidence, not a similarity score

  1. Search for the required activity and role. A suggested, untested query is “contract powder coating abrasive blasting Republic of Ireland”. Product-supplier searches answer a different question. Use the manufacturing account-discovery guide for discovery and identity checks.
  2. Attribute each observation. Does a permitted source describe this company, a group or one site? Record the actual activity and location; do not transfer a parent's capabilities to every subsidiary.
  3. Preserve the evidence trail. Link each criterion to its source reference, publication date if known and actual check date. Separate the permitted observation from your interpretation. Check access and intended-use terms before retaining source content; public visibility does not clear reuse.
  4. Record both gaps and contradictions. Use supported, contradicted, unknown or conflicting for each criterion. Missing information is not a positive failure. A score cannot compensate for a failed requirement or an unresolved material fact.
  5. Make the bounded decision. Shortlist only when the required fit, identity, exclusions and source-use checks are resolved. Hold material uncertainty with a next question. Reject a supported non-match, recording the failed rule; no need to investigate unrelated optional details to justify that rejection.

Search results are discovery pointers, not complete market coverage or acceptance evidence. Resolve duplicate company references before counting; different sites do not automatically mean different accounts.

04 / fictional comparisons

4. Test four contrasting fictional candidates

All candidate facts and decisions below are invented teaching assumptions, not researched companies, anonymised customers or delivered results. No live sources or review dates exist for them.

A · Relevant operations, different name

Fictional Industrial Treatments A performs both required finishing activities for external customers at its production facility in the Republic of Ireland. Identity, exclusions and intended-use checks are stipulated as resolved. Shortlist for this fictional brief. Its different name and smaller operation do not defeat the required match. Project research was not required; technical suitability remains unknown.

B · Familiar product words, wrong activity

Fictional Blast and Coat Equipment B sells blasting cabinets and powder-coating equipment but explicitly operates no finishing service. Reject for this brief. Its name and catalogue look relevant, but selling equipment is not the required customer activity. Do not convert product-word overlap into an operator account.

C · Group-level claims, unresolved company fit

Fictional Finishing Group C describes both processes across its group and lists a sales office in the Republic of Ireland. The invented material does not identify a company operating relevant production there. Hold. Ask which entity operates the required processes and where. Neither accept the office as production nor infer that the group has none.

D · Same processes, different customer role

Fictional Precision Parts Maker D performs both processes in the Republic of Ireland exclusively on its own products, with no external finishing service. Reject under the current contract-finisher rule. It might warrant a separate in-house-manufacturer brief, but that hypothesis cannot change this decision. Similar processes do not establish identical purchasing arrangements.

05 / comparison worksheet

5. Keep the worksheet useful and blank until checked

The CSV has a seed-profile column and four blank candidate columns. Enter generalised rules in the seed column, not a customer identity. Compare facts against those rules, reference the evidence, and leave unsupported values blank with the gap explained. Candidate slots are not a promised account count.

Open as UTF-8. Copy the evidence block for additional sources and repeat the worksheet for more candidates under the same brief version. Source publication and actual check dates are separate; never fill either with a planned date. Keep one stable company reference when several sites support it.

The final rows separate client-brief fit, project evidence, technical suitability and outreach clearance. “Not assessed—not required” for project research is not “no project exists”. A shortlisted company is not cleared for outreach or delivery. See the fictional account-record sample for the underlying record detail.

06 / lookalike next step

6. Scope the comparison before asking for volume

A useful handoff explains the transferable rules, shortlisted companies, held questions and rejected similarities. Preserve the brief version and comparison reasons; do not relax requirements to fill a quota.

Explore industrial company-level research or request a written scope. Describe the activity, customer role, territory and exclusions at a non-confidential level. Evidence fields, coverage and deliverables need agreement; this educational worksheet is not an acceptance specification.

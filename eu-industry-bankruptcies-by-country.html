EU industry bankruptcies by country

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 25 November 2026

The EU-27 industry bankruptcy index stood at 167.9 in the third quarter of 2026 (2021=100, seasonally and calendar adjusted), 4.0% higher than a year earlier and 3.0% higher than the previous quarter, by our count.[1][2] That is a change in an index, not a count of firms, and it covers industry except construction (NACE Rev. 2 sections B to E: mining, manufacturing, energy and water), not manufacturing alone. A higher country index means a larger rise from 2021 in that country; it does not mean more bankruptcy declarations than another country. The figures come from Eurostat table sts_rb_q. Eurostat last updated the dataset on 16 November 2026 at 10:00 Irish time, UTC+0. Ireland has no quarterly bankruptcy index in this table. The official Irish series is an annual count: 1,359 bankruptcy declarations in 2024, covering all activities, not industry alone.[1][2][5]

Demand Range researches industrial accounts in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page is for account-risk checks before outreach: how the industry bankruptcy index has moved in each EU country, what the index does and does not measure, and Ireland's annual counts. It does not list companies. For annual enterprise births and deaths, see EU manufacturing enterprise churn. Registrations in this table cannot be compared directly with those births.[4]

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat sts_rb_q, seasonally and calendar adjusted index, 2021=100, industry except construction unless noted; annual counts from Eurostat Table 1; checked 24 November 2026.

  • EU-27 industry bankruptcy index: 167.9 in Q3 2026, up from 163.0 in Q2 2026 and 161.5 in Q3 2025, a 3.0% rise on the quarter and a 4.0% rise on the year, by our count. Those three EU cells have no status flag.[1][2]
  • The wider business-economy bankruptcy index (industry, construction and market services) eased to 193.7 in Q3 2026 from 195.2 in Q2 2026, a 0.8% fall, by our count. Q2 2026 is the high of the 2015–Q3 2026 series, by our count.[1][2]
  • EU-27 industry business registrations were 100.0 in Q3 2026, 1.5% higher than Q2 2026 and 1.5% lower than Q3 2025, by our count.[1][2]
  • Of the 27 EU countries, 25 had a published Q3 2026 industry bankruptcy index, by our count. Germany and Ireland had no published cell for that quarter. A missing cell is not zero.[1][3]
  • The highest published Q3 2026 industry indices, which measure change from 2021, not levels, were Greece 463.6 (I), the Netherlands 317.3 (P) and France 249.4 (I).[1][3]
  • Ireland recorded 1,359 bankruptcy declarations in 2024, down 9.9% from 1,509 in 2023 and up from 1,078 in 2022, by our count. The annual totals cover all activities and have no industry split.[5]
02 / what the bankruptcy index measures

What the bankruptcy index measures

Eurostat's quarterly series is the "Business registration and bankruptcy index by NACE Rev. 2 activity". The cells used on this page are seasonally and calendar adjusted and set to 100 in 2021. They are not counts of bankruptcy declarations, not percentages of firms, and not a ranking of how many businesses failed in each country.[1][2]

Industry here means NACE Rev. 2 sections B to E (industry except construction). That is wider than manufacturing (section C). This dataset has no published EU-27 manufacturing-only bankruptcy index for Q3 2026, so the country table uses the industry series.[1][3]

The EU aggregate is the EU-27 (geo code EU27_2020). Most country cells in Q3 2026 carry a status flag: (I) means the value was imputed by Eurostat or another receiving agency, and (P) means provisional. Slovenia's Q3 2026 cell has no flag. The EU-27 industry totals quoted above also have no flag.[3][6]

Eurostat presents the quarterly figures as an index because national administrative rules differ. Bankruptcy declarations in the annual table are absolute counts, but only the all-activity total is published for each country, with no NACE breakdown.[4][5]

Three points stand out, by our count:

  1. The index compares each country with its own 2021 baseline. Greece, the Netherlands and France have the highest Q3 2026 readings, but that is not a ranking of how many industrial firms went bankrupt. France's index was 4.8% lower than a year earlier, even though it remains well above 100.

  3. A missing cell is not zero. Germany has no published Q3 2026 industry bankruptcy index. Its latest published cell in this dataset is 201.9 (I) in Q2 2026. Ireland has no quarterly bankruptcy index at all in this dataset.
04 / ireland s annual bankruptcy declarations

Ireland's annual bankruptcy declarations

Because there is no quarterly Irish bankruptcy index in sts_rb_q, the official Irish figures on this page are Eurostat's annual counts. Table 1 of the Statistics Explained annual bankruptcies file records 1,078 declarations in 2022, 1,509 in 2023 and 1,359 in 2024. That is a 40.0% rise in 2023 and a 9.9% fall in 2024, by our count. Ireland's cells for 2015 to 2021 are marked as not available.[5]

These are all-activity totals. The methodological sheet says only the total number of bankruptcies is published per country, with no NACE breakdown, so 1,359 is not an Irish industry or manufacturing count. For scale, the same 2024 table shows 10,956 declarations in Belgium, 6,085 in Denmark and 4,208 in the Netherlands, also with no industry split.[5]

For Ireland, the same sheet defines a bankruptcy as a company in liquidation, in administration or in examinership, compiled from Companies Registration Office data. National legal definitions differ, which is one reason the quarterly country series is published as an index rather than as raw counts.[4][5]

Eurostat's Statistics Explained article, which in the edition fetched on 24 November 2026 still covers data up to Q2 2026, notes that quarterly transmissions have been mandatory since the first quarter of 2021, "except the bankruptcies for Ireland (due to a derogation for Q1 to Q4 of 2021)". The quarterly Irish gap in this dataset continues through Q3 2026.[4]

05 / registrations as a contrasting signal

Registrations as a contrasting signal

Registrations are a separate indicator in the same table. Eurostat describes them as "an early indicator to measure business intentions" and warns that "Registrations cannot be directly compared with enterprise births."[4]

In Q3 2026 the EU-27 business-economy registration index was 114.1, 2.4% higher than 111.4 in Q2 2026, by our count, and the high of the 2015–Q3 2026 series, by our count. Industry registrations were flatter: 100.0 in Q3 2026, against 98.5 in Q2 2026 and 101.5 in Q3 2025.[1][2]

Ireland does have a quarterly registration series. The industry registration index was 76.0 in Q3 2026, flagged estimated (E), against 93.3 in Q3 2025, flagged imputed (I), an 18.5% fall on the year, by our count. The all-activity registration index was 129.4 (E) in Q3 2026, against 119.1 (I) in Q3 2025, an 8.6% rise, by our count. Ireland's Q2 2026 registration cells, 53.0 (E) for industry and 87.0 (E) for the whole business economy, are also estimates and look like a dip; this page does not treat the rebound from those cells as a headline change.[1][2]

06 / using these figures in account risk checks

Using these figures in account-risk checks

The country index is a market-level signal, not a substitute for checking whether a named account is still trading. Use it to see whether bankruptcy declarations in a country's industry have risen or fallen relative to 2021 and to the latest quarters, then verify individual companies in official registers before outreach.

For Ireland, start with the annual official count and the legal statuses behind it, not with a quarterly index that is not published. Pair the market figures with a prospect-list audit of company status. Annual births and deaths of manufacturing enterprises are on the enterprise churn page; do not mix those counts with this index or with registration figures.

A written scope can include both the country-level bankruptcy signal and account-level status checks. Request a written scope.

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Are EU industrial bankruptcies rising?
The EU-27 industry bankruptcy index was 4.0% higher in Q3 2026 than in Q3 2025, and 3.0% higher than in Q2 2026, by our count. That is a change in an index set to 100 in 2021, not a count of firms, and it covers industry except construction, not manufacturing alone.[1][2]
What does the industry bankruptcy index measure?
It measures quarterly bankruptcy declarations in industry except construction (NACE B–E), as a seasonally and calendar adjusted index with 2021 equal to 100. Country readings show change from 2021 within that country. They are not a ranking of how many businesses failed, and they are not manufacturing-only figures.[1][3]
Why is there no quarterly bankruptcy figure for Ireland?
Eurostat's quarterly table has no published Irish bankruptcy index in this dataset from 2015 through Q3 2026. A missing cell is not zero. The official Irish series used here is the annual count. Germany also has no published industry bankruptcy index for Q3 2026; its latest published cell in this dataset is 201.9 (I) in Q2 2026.[1][2][3]
How many Irish companies went bankrupt in 2024?
Eurostat's annual table records 1,359 bankruptcy declarations in Ireland in 2024, after 1,509 in 2023 and 1,078 in 2022. The totals cover all activities and have no industry or manufacturing split. Ireland has no quarterly index in Eurostat's table, so these annual all-activity counts are the official Irish series used on this page.[5]
How is this different from enterprise births and deaths?
This page uses quarterly bankruptcy declaration indices and Ireland's annual bankruptcy counts. The enterprise churn page uses annual births and deaths of enterprises. Eurostat also warns that registrations, which appear in the same quarterly table, cannot be compared directly with enterprise births.[4]
How should I use these figures before outreach?
Treat the index as a country-level risk signal, then check official company status for the accounts you plan to contact. A prospect-list audit is the account-level step. The index will not tell you whether a named company has entered liquidation, examinership or a related procedure.

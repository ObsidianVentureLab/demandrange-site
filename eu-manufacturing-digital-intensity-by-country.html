Manufacturing digital intensity by EU country, 2025

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 24 November 2026

In 2025, 82.37% of Irish manufacturers reached at least a basic level of digital intensity, against 71.77% across the EU-27, and 12.16% scored very high, against 7.54%.[1][2] By our count, that puts Ireland 6th of the 26 EU countries with a published 2025 value on both measures. The figures come from Eurostat's Digital Intensity Index, which scores how many of 12 digital technologies an enterprise uses.[9][10] They cover enterprises with 10 or more persons employed in manufacturing (NACE Rev. 2 section C).[2][8]

Key facts

Eurostat isoc_e_diin2 and isoc_cicce_usen2, both data updated 27 February 2026; checked 24 November 2026.

  • At least basic digital intensity, manufacturers, 2025: Ireland 82.37%, EU-27 71.77%.[2]
  • Very high digital intensity, manufacturers, 2025: Ireland 12.16%, EU-27 7.54%. By our count, Denmark leads at 20.01% and Bulgaria is lowest at 0.58%.[2]

02 / digital intensity of manufacturers by eu country 2025

Three points stand out:

  • A long tail. In Bulgaria 68.96% and in Romania 59.98% of manufacturers scored very low, against 6.31% in Denmark.[2]

Ireland against the EU

Irish manufacturers are ahead of the EU-27 in every version-3 survey year:[2]

In 2025 the Irish lead was 10.60 percentage points on at least basic and 4.62 points on very high, by our count.[2] Adding the high and very high bands, 43.96% of Irish manufacturers scored 7 or more, against 34.86% across the EU-27, by our count.[2]

Manufacturers are slightly ahead of all Irish enterprises in the survey's scope. Across all enterprises in scope (excluding the financial sector), 79.92% in Ireland reached at least basic digital intensity in 2025 and 11.82% scored very high. The EU-27 figures were 72.11% and 10.05%.[3]

Read the jump between 2023 and 2025 with care. Eurostat changed one item inside version 3 for the 2025 survey, and says this may have partly raised the 2025 results (see below).[7] Some of the rise in Ireland's very high share, from 4.97% in 2023 to 12.16% in 2025, may come from that change in the index's make-up.[2][7]

04 / paid cloud erp and crm take up

Paid cloud, ERP and CRM take-up

Cloud take-up comes from a separate Eurostat table on cloud computing services, not from the Digital Intensity Index itself.[4][5]

In 2025, 77.20% of Irish manufacturers bought paid cloud computing services over the internet, against 53.96% across the EU-27.[5] By our count, Ireland is 2nd of the 26 EU countries with a published 2025 value, just behind Sweden at 77.79%; Finland's value is flagged low reliability and not published.[5] In 2023 the shares were 70.46% in Ireland and 45.01% in the EU-27.[5]

The gap is narrower for business software bought as a paid cloud service. In 2025, 19.84% of Irish manufacturers used cloud ERP software (EU-27 16.14%), 16.58% used cloud CRM (EU-27 11.88%) and 44.06% used cloud security software (EU-27 35.55%).[5] These shares count software bought as a cloud service only, not every manufacturer that runs ERP or CRM on its own servers.

The CSO's own 2025 release, from the same survey year, gives slightly different Irish figures. It notes that selected services enterprises used cloud computing for CRM (21.7%) more often than manufacturers (16.8%) and construction (6.1%).[11]

05 / why odd and even years are not comparable

Why odd and even years are not comparable

Eurostat publishes two versions of the index, and says its composition "varies between different survey years, depending on the questions included in the survey":[2][9]

  • Version 3 in odd survey years (2021, 2023 and 2025).
  • Version 4 in even survey years (2022 and 2024), with its own set of variables.

The two versions are separate indicators, so a 2024 score and a 2025 score don't form a trend. For example, Eurostat's version-4 figure shows 87.05% of Irish manufacturers with at least basic digital intensity in 2024 (EU-27 72.39%).[2] That is not a higher starting point for the 2025 version-3 figure of 82.37%, and the difference is not a fall.

Version 3 also changed slightly in 2025. Eurostat's information note of 11 December 2025 says the item "using 2 or more social media" was replaced by "having a web site". Website figures are typically higher, so the change "might have partially contributed" to higher 2025 results in the low, high, very high and at least basic bands.[7] Compare 2023 and 2025 version-3 figures with that caveat in view.

06 / using digital intensity in an ideal customer profile

Using digital intensity in an ideal customer profile

A country's digital intensity share is a fit signal for an ideal customer profile, not a score for a named company. It is most relevant to suppliers of software, connected equipment, automation and IT services, whose buyers need some digital base already in place.

  • Rank countries, then sectors. The spread is wide: by our count, very low digital intensity ranges from 6.31% of manufacturers in Denmark to 68.96% in Bulgaria.[2] Pair that with company counts from our EU industrial enterprises by country table to weigh market size against readiness.
  • Use cloud take-up as a second signal. Cloud figures by manufacturing group show where buyers already pay for software as a service, for example 74.41% of Irish food-to-printing manufacturers against 45.41% EU-wide.[6]
  • Know what the tables don't show. For Irish and EU-27 manufacturing, this index is published only for all enterprises with 10 or more persons employed, with no size split.[2] There is no UK value in either table for 2021 to 2025.[2][5]
  • Check every account. A national share doesn't tell you which plants are digital. Use company and site evidence, such as job adverts, websites and supplier announcements, when you prioritise accounts. Our Irish manufacturing account research guide shows that step for Ireland.

The EU has set a 2030 goal that more than 90% of small and medium-sized enterprises reach at least a basic level of digital intensity, and Eurostat calculates the "at least basic" level to monitor it.[9][10]

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

What is the Digital Intensity Index?
The Digital Intensity Index is Eurostat's score of how many of 12 digital technologies an enterprise uses, such as AI or e-sales. Scores of 0 to 3 are very low, 4 to 6 low, 7 to 9 high and 10 to 12 very high. A score of 4 or more means at least a basic level of digital intensity.
How digital are Irish manufacturers compared with the EU?
Irish manufacturers are ahead of the EU average. In 2025, 82.37% of Irish manufacturers with 10 or more persons employed reached at least basic digital intensity, against 71.77% across the EU-27. 12.16% scored very high, against 7.54%. By our count, Ireland ranks 6th of the 26 EU countries with a published value.
Which EU countries have the most digitally intense manufacturers?
Denmark, Belgium, Sweden and the Netherlands lead. In 2025, between 17.57% and 20.01% of their manufacturers scored very high, against 7.54% across the EU-27. By our count, Luxembourg is 5th and Ireland 6th. Bulgaria and Romania have the largest shares scoring very low, by our count, and Finland has no published 2025 value.
Can I compare 2024 and 2025 digital intensity figures?
No. Odd years such as 2025 use version 3 of the index, and even years such as 2024 use version 4, which Eurostat builds from a different mix of survey questions. Even 2023 and 2025 differ slightly: for 2025, Eurostat replaced a social media item with having a website, which may have pushed scores up.
How many Irish manufacturers use paid cloud services?
About three in four. In 2025, 77.20% of Irish manufacturers with 10 or more persons employed bought paid cloud computing services over the internet, against 53.96% across the EU-27. By our count, that puts Ireland 2nd of the 26 EU countries with a published value, just behind Sweden at 77.79%.
How should I use digital intensity in an ideal customer profile?
Use it as a country or sector fit signal, not as a score for a named company. A high national share suggests buyers who already run several digital tools, which suits software, connected equipment and IT services. Then check each target account with company or site evidence before you decide where it ranks.

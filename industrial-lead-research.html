Demand Range researches company-level prospects for suppliers of industrial equipment, technology and services selling into Ireland, the UK and the EU. Start with what you sell, the countries you want to research and the businesses you want to reach. The research explains why an account matches your agreed criteria, what supports that decision and what still needs checking.

01 / record

What should an account record tell your team?

Agree the fields before research begins. A useful company-level record lets a reviewer answer these questions:

Which business are we considering?
Company identity and, where in scope, the relevant operating site. Several sites are not automatically several companies.
Why does it belong in our market?
The activity, territory and customer role that meet your written brief, with a reason for inclusion.
What supports that view?
Source references and the relevant publication and review dates, within the agreed evidence-sharing permissions.
Is there a separate project signal?
The research class and project-research status—not an assumption that every matching company has a project.
What remains unresolved?
Material gaps, conflicting information and the next useful verification question.

Account relevance does not establish installed equipment, technical compatibility, purchasing authority or current demand. Named contacts, email addresses and direct dials are not included by default.

Source access and customer-use permissions determine which evidence can accompany an output. A linked source is not, by itself, permission to redistribute its content.

02 / fit

The same product word can point to the wrong customer.

Fictional demonstration—not a real prospect or customer result.

Suppose your brief seeks food-and-beverage manufacturers operating production facilities in Ireland. An invented beverage manufacturer meets that activity and territory rule. It can be relevant without an assessed project. An invented residential pump retailer does not meet the manufacturing rule, even though its catalogue mentions pumps.

The difference is the written inclusion reason, not a larger spreadsheet or a more persuasive industry label. The full fictional sample shows an accepted account, a rejected candidate and a separate project-signal example, with the limits kept visible.

03 / classes

Two research classes, with the same fit standard.

Industry-matched leads, also called Look Alike: companies matching the agreed industries and fit criteria. They do not need an active or upcoming project. “Project research not assessed—not required” is different from a bounded review that found no qualifying signal; neither means that no project exists.

Opportunity-signal leads: matching companies with an identified, evidenced project or development. The record keeps the observed change, its stage, its relevance to your range and the unanswered questions separate.

We aim for a mix of 90% industry-matched leads and 10% opportunity-signal leads. This is a target, not a guaranteed allocation. Both classes require fit checks and source traceability. These are not marketing qualified leads. Neither class is automatically a high-intent buyer.

05 / eu country brief

Write a country-specific EU research brief.

A useful country brief makes inclusion testable. Use these points to define the territory you want researched, wherever your sales team is based:

Country list and priority
Name the EU countries in scope, rank priorities and state any regional boundaries. “Europe” alone is not an agreed country list.
Operation or incorporation?
Must the company operate a relevant facility in the country, be incorporated there, or meet both rules? A registered address alone does not establish local production.
Companies or sites?
Choose the counting unit. State how several facilities, cross-border operations and group companies should be linked without inflating company totals.
Exclusions
Define out-of-scope activities, territories, existing accounts and channel conflicts. Describe the rules without sending confidential lists in the initial enquiry.
Language and source limits
State the source and output languages needed. Assess source access, interpretation requirements, evidence-sharing rights and unresolved gaps country by country; do not assume identical coverage.
Deliverable and acceptance
Agree fields, evidence references, explicit unknowns and acceptance criteria for each country. Confirm feasible scope before counts or timing; this is not a complete EU database or a promise of every language.
06 / country shortlist coverage

Does a shorter country shortlist mean a smaller market?

Wholly fictional comparison—not researched companies, named-country estimates or customer results. Both imagined briefs seek companies operating complete packaging-machine assembly.

Country A has six discovered company-country records: four reviewed, yielding three matches and one unresolved activity-attribution question; two remain unreviewed. Country B has four discovered records: two reviewed, yielding one match and one unresolved operation-location question; two remain unreviewed.

Those differences do not support a market-size ranking. A’s imagined search used a sector source with broader language coverage; B’s used an English-only route. B’s additional local-language route has not been assessed for access, interpretation or intended-use rights. Neither search establishes the total relevant company population.

Reviewed matches are findings against the fictional brief, not evidence of buying intent, technical suitability or permission to contact anyone. Unresolved cases retain their questions; unreviewed records are neither accepted nor rejected. Undiscovered possibilities sit outside both lists. Even reviewing every discovered B record would not establish coverage of its whole market, and no later review outcomes are assumed here.

Keep both country relationships when the same company qualifies in both, but count it once in a separately defined global unique-company total.

Commission different next steps rather than an automatic ranking. For A, retain the brief, document the search boundary and agree which unresolved and unreviewed cases to investigate. For B, first assess a permitted local-language/source route, then scope bounded discovery against the same criteria. If rights remain unresolved, retain the gap.

More research does not guarantee more matches or establish a larger market; commercial preference remains the buyer’s input. Actual language capability, access, evidence-sharing rights, achievable counts, cost and timing require a written scope.

07 / questions

What happens after an enquiry?

We assess product, account and territory fit, clarify the evidence and delivery assumptions, and—where the brief is a fit—propose a written scope and commercial terms. A discovery call is optional.

The written scope sets the record unit, fields, sources, counts, class treatment, timing, price and acceptance terms. Review cadence, acceptance rules and any correction terms are agreed for the work. There is no standard turnaround or correction window promised on this page. Named-person work and outreach remain separate scopes.

Licensed platform, in-house workflow or analyst brief?

Choose how to obtain and maintain your target-account base, not just who supplies a list. Choosing an approach does not establish a project, a reachable contact or buying intent. Compare them against the same brief:

Scope specificity
Licensed platform: test actual territory and category coverage. In-house: translate your application knowledge into repeatable criteria. Analyst brief: agree the narrow question and exclusions before research starts.
Record granularity
Licensed platform: check whether records represent groups, companies, sites or projects. In-house: choose identifiers and deduplicate consistently. Analyst brief: specify the counting unit, required fields and treatment of multiple sites.
Evidence traceability
Licensed platform: inspect source visibility and dates in sample records. In-house: retain references and separate observations from interpretation. Analyst brief: request source-supported reasons, review dates and explicit unknowns in the deliverable.
Refresh burden
Licensed platform: establish what updates cover and whether exports stay current. In-house: assign an owner and review cadence. Analyst brief: agree a dated handover; later refresh work is a separate scope unless included.
Operator effort
Licensed platform: budget time for filtering, verification and integration. In-house: reserve research and peer-review capacity alongside sales work. Analyst brief: retain time to brief the analyst, challenge findings and resolve open questions.
Permitted use
Licensed platform: check export, sharing and reuse terms. In-house: review permissions for every public or licensed source. Analyst brief: agree customer-use rights and evidence-sharing limits. Buying access or analysis does not automatically permit resale or unrestricted reuse.

Fictional scenario: a narrow Irish supplier brief

A fictional conveyor-component supplier wants to identify Irish businesses that build food-packaging machinery. Exclude distributors without machinery-building activity, service-only firms and existing customers. Count each company once, recording the Irish operating site supporting its relevance. Require sources for identity, Irish operations and machinery-building activity, with review dates; installed components, technical suitability and current demand remain unknown.

For this bounded first review, a scoped analyst brief may fit if the supplier lacks research time but can review findings. An in-house workflow may fit better if a knowledgeable employee can own both the initial work and maintenance. A licensed platform is worth evaluating if sample records demonstrate the niche and record detail needed—not merely a broad industry label.

If the brief changes to ongoing project and plant coverage across several territories, assess licensed access plus internal filtering or a hybrid with an analyst, subject to source-use terms. The changed workload, not a universal winner, drives that choice.

Before choosing, request records selected against an explained coverage frame, coverage gaps, deliverable fields, update responsibilities and permitted uses. Specify the handover before choosing how to research.

A targeting brief says which companies belong. An acceptance specification says what you must be able to inspect, import, challenge and retain. Use the same questions for an internal team, a licensed tool or commissioned research; none is the default winner.

Bring your existing industrial ICP brief and account field template. This module adds delivery and responsibility questions, not another targeting exercise or effort estimate.

Write a testable delivery specification

Acceptance: what exactly will the buyer approve?
Which brief and field-dictionary versions govern the delivery? Who can accept it, and is review of every record or a buyer-agreed sample required? Which missing fields block acceptance? Agree separate treatment for accepted accounts, unresolved candidates, duplicates and rejected rows; do not let a delivered row total stand in for an accepted-company count.
Export: what must survive the handover?
Ask for a non-sensitive test file in the proposed format. Can your intended import preserve company and site identifiers, leading zeros, source references, review dates, explicit unknowns and linked-site relationships? How will you reconcile the received file with the approved version? Specify an encoding, field map and version label rather than assuming a dashboard view and an export contain the same detail.
Evidence: what can the reviewer actually inspect?
For each material field, agree the reference needed to challenge it and the permitted evidence format. Will a reviewer receive a link, an original research note or an authorised extract? What happens if access is unavailable or a link changes? Ask for a permitted verification route; do not require copied source content regardless of rights.
Correction: what happens when the test fails?
Agree how to report a disputed row, who investigates it, how revisions are identified and what constitutes completion. Distinguish a correction against the agreed brief from a new research request or a later change in circumstances. Set any review window and remedies in writing; this checklist supplies no default allowance or deadline.
Responsibility: who owns the next decision?
Name internal roles for acceptance, import, unresolved questions, rights review and later maintenance. If several parties contribute, who resolves a disagreement and records the decision? Operational ownership is not the same as intellectual-property ownership or permission to reuse data.
Rights and exit: what may each party keep and do?
Ask separately about ownership of buyer-supplied inputs, original analysis, the compiled deliverable and third-party material. For each, confirm the intended recipients, CRM use, internal sharing, customer-facing use and any onward distribution. What survives completion or subscription expiry, what must be removed, and who records the applicable agreement and exceptions? Request written confirmation; do not treat payment, download access or rewritten wording as permission.

What did the vendor's sample leave out?

A sample can show how a record is written without testing the work you will receive. Before treating a clean demonstration as a buying decision, ask: who selected these companies, from which dated frame, and what was not eligible for selection?

Original buyer worksheet, not a statistical acceptance plan. Define the coverage frame as the versioned list from which companies could be selected—not the entire addressable market. Record its inclusion rules and known omissions. Count distinct company IDs, not sites, spreadsheet rows or repeated appearances across samples. Unknown frame size stays unknown; it is not zero.

  1. Freeze the comparison. Use the same brief, frame version and evidence requirements. Record who selected each set, the selection rule, any pre-screening, and every substituted row. Keep a removed selection and its reason in the trail rather than silently substituting a cleaner row.
  2. Compare what was selected with what was available. Group the frame by a decision-relevant condition fixed before selection. In this exercise, that condition is evidence complexity. Count frame companies, selected companies and unselected companies in each group. A missing group is a question to investigate, not proof that its companies fail.
  3. Separate three denominators. Selected companies describe the sample; companies actually reviewed describe the observed findings; the separately identified production batch is the subject of a later acceptance decision. Never divide sample passes by production rows or treat unreviewed selections as passes.

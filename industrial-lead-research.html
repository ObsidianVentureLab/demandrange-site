01 / brief

The targeting brief comes first.

Define the rules with the industrial ideal-customer-profile guide and brief worksheet. If starting from an authorised seed profile, use the lookalike account comparison guide.

For a worked discovery-to-decision process, follow the Irish manufacturing account-list guide.

Start with the product or service range and the applications you want to explore. Then agree:

  • Target industries, territory and relevant company characteristics.
  • Whether the research unit is a company or an individual facility.
  • The route to market: operators, OEMs, integrators or contractors, where relevant.
  • Existing accounts, channel conflicts and other exclusions.
  • The evidence needed to accept a record and the questions that can remain open.

Sector membership alone is not evidence of installed equipment, technical suitability or a current requirement.

02 / record

What a company-level record contains.

The field set is agreed for the brief. A useful account record makes the following inspectable:

  • Identity: the company and, if in scope, the specific site.
  • Fit: industry, territory and the reason it matches the written criteria.
  • Evidence: source references and the relevant publication and review dates.
  • Class: industry-matched or opportunity-signal, with project research status kept separate.
  • Limits: exclusions, uncertainty and the next verification question.

Source access and customer-use permissions determine which evidence can accompany an output. A linked source is not, by itself, permission to redistribute its content.

03 / classes

Account fit does not require a project.

Industry-matched leads (Look Alike) meet the agreed profile without requiring an active or upcoming project. The record distinguishes a project search that was not required from a bounded review that found no qualifying signal. Neither means that no project exists.

Opportunity-signal leads meet the fit rules and have a relevant, evidenced project or development. Their brief separates the observed fact, commercial interpretation and unknowns.

We aim for 90% industry-matched and 10% opportunity-signal leads. This is a target, not a guaranteed mix. Exact counts, class treatment and deliverables are agreed in writing.

An inclusion reason should survive a challenge.

In our fictional example, a manufacturer meets a brief for Irish food-and-beverage production businesses. It is accepted for account relevance without a current project assessment. A residential pump retailer is rejected because it does not meet the required business type—even though its catalogue mentions pumps.

Neither decision establishes equipment suitability or demand. The written profile determines the fit decision.

05 / quality

Review the evidence and the boundaries.

The research method checks identity, material fit facts, dates, duplicates and exclusions. Unsupported or conflicting material claims remain open for review rather than being converted into a pass. Opportunity-signal records need separate evidence for the signal and its relevance.

06 / research approach

Licensed platform, in-house workflow or analyst brief?

Choose how to obtain and maintain your target-account base, not just who supplies a list. Choosing an approach does not establish a project, a reachable contact or buying intent. Compare them against the same brief:

Scope specificity
Licensed platform: test actual territory and category coverage. In-house: translate your application knowledge into repeatable criteria. Analyst brief: agree the narrow question and exclusions before research starts.
Record granularity
Licensed platform: check whether records represent groups, companies, sites or projects. In-house: choose identifiers and deduplicate consistently. Analyst brief: specify the counting unit, required fields and treatment of multiple sites.
Evidence traceability
Licensed platform: inspect source visibility and dates in sample records. In-house: retain references and separate observations from interpretation. Analyst brief: request source-supported reasons, review dates and explicit unknowns in the deliverable.
Refresh burden
Licensed platform: establish what updates cover and whether exports stay current. In-house: assign an owner and review cadence. Analyst brief: agree a dated handover; later refresh work is a separate scope unless included.
Operator effort
Licensed platform: budget time for filtering, verification and integration. In-house: reserve research and peer-review capacity alongside sales work. Analyst brief: retain time to brief the analyst, challenge findings and resolve open questions.
Permitted use
Licensed platform: check export, sharing and reuse terms. In-house: review permissions for every public or licensed source. Analyst brief: agree customer-use rights and evidence-sharing limits. Buying access or analysis does not automatically permit resale or unrestricted reuse.

Put your own effort against the brief.

Fictional scenario: a narrow Irish supplier brief

A fictional conveyor-component supplier wants to identify Irish businesses that build food-packaging machinery. Exclude distributors without machinery-building activity, service-only firms and existing customers. Count each company once, recording the Irish operating site supporting its relevance. Require sources for identity, Irish operations and machinery-building activity, with review dates; installed components, technical suitability and current demand remain unknown.

For this bounded first review, a scoped analyst brief may fit if the supplier lacks research time but can review findings. An in-house workflow may fit better if a knowledgeable employee can own both the initial work and maintenance. A licensed platform is worth evaluating if sample records demonstrate the niche and record detail needed—not merely a broad industry label.

If the brief changes to ongoing project and plant coverage across several territories, assess licensed access plus internal filtering or a hybrid with an analyst, subject to source-use terms. The changed workload, not a universal winner, drives that choice.

Before choosing, request representative records, coverage gaps, deliverable fields, update responsibilities and permitted uses. Use our research method, fictional sample and manufacturing guide to frame that review. Request a scoped comparison and quote; coverage and deliverables remain subject to written agreement.

07 / research acceptance specification

Specify the handover before choosing how to research.

A targeting brief says which companies belong. An acceptance specification says what you must be able to inspect, import, challenge and retain. Use the same questions for an internal team, a licensed tool or commissioned research; none is the default winner.

Bring your existing industrial ICP brief and account field template. This module adds delivery and responsibility questions, not another targeting exercise or effort estimate.

Write a testable delivery specification

Acceptance: what exactly will the buyer approve?
Which brief and field-dictionary versions govern the delivery? Who can accept it, and is review of every record or a buyer-agreed sample required? Which missing fields block acceptance? Agree separate treatment for accepted accounts, unresolved candidates, duplicates and rejected rows; do not let a delivered row total stand in for an accepted-company count.
Export: what must survive the handover?
Ask for a non-sensitive test file in the proposed format. Can your intended import preserve company and site identifiers, leading zeros, source references, review dates, explicit unknowns and linked-site relationships? How will you reconcile the received file with the approved version? Specify an encoding, field map and version label rather than assuming a dashboard view and an export contain the same detail.
Evidence: what can the reviewer actually inspect?
For each material field, agree the reference needed to challenge it and the permitted evidence format. Will a reviewer receive a link, an original research note or an authorised extract? What happens if access is unavailable or a link changes? Ask for a permitted verification route; do not require copied source content regardless of rights.
Correction: what happens when the test fails?
Agree how to report a disputed row, who investigates it, how revisions are identified and what constitutes completion. Distinguish a correction against the agreed brief from a new research request or a later change in circumstances. Set any review window and remedies in writing; this checklist supplies no default allowance or deadline.
Responsibility: who owns the next decision?
Name internal roles for acceptance, import, unresolved questions, rights review and later maintenance. If several parties contribute, who resolves a disagreement and records the decision? Operational ownership is not the same as intellectual-property ownership or permission to reuse data.
Rights and exit: what may each party keep and do?
Ask separately about ownership of buyer-supplied inputs, original analysis, the compiled deliverable and third-party material. For each, confirm the intended recipients, CRM use, internal sharing, customer-facing use and any onward distribution. What survives completion or subscription expiry, what must be removed, and who records the applicable agreement and exceptions? Request written confirmation; do not treat payment, download access or rewritten wording as permission.

Compare three fictional working arrangements

Wholly fictional scenarios, not customer cases or vendor evaluations. Each starts with the same imagined brief: company-level research on machinery builders operating in the Republic of Ireland. The targeting rules are already agreed; personal contacts and outreach are outside scope. The question is whether the proposed handover can meet the buyer's written tests.

In-house: an internal analyst hands research to sales operations

The fictional buyer wants a method another employee can maintain. Ask: can a colleague reproduce the acceptance decision using the approved brief and permitted evidence? Who reviews disputed records if the original analyst is unavailable? Which source-use checks apply to the team's own notes and retained extracts? Can the receiving team import a versioned test file without losing the evidence trail?

Decision checkpoint: ask for a documented handover and a second-role review. If these remain unresolved, record the gap rather than assuming either that internal work is inadequate or that being internal clears rights.

Licensed tool: an authorised user prepares an export for an internal CRM

The fictional buyer wants to use a tool within its agreed licence. Ask: which required fields and relationships are available in the actual export, and under which access terms? May the intended internal recipients use the exported material? What can remain usable after access ends? Who carries corrections into the CRM, and what evidence will remain reviewable outside the tool?

Decision checkpoint: inspect an authorised test export and the applicable agreement together. A missing answer calls for confirmation; it is not evidence that every tool has that limitation or that commissioned research avoids it.

Commissioned research: a written brief ends in a dated handover

The fictional buyer wants a bounded assignment it can accept and maintain. Ask: which files, field definitions, unresolved-question log and permitted evidence are included? Who accepts or disputes delivery? Which original outputs may the buyer edit or share, and which source materials remain restricted? What correction work is included, and who owns refresh decisions after completion?

Decision checkpoint: agree a test deliverable, acceptance process and rights schedule before committing. A bespoke brief alone does not settle export usability, ownership, source permissions or future maintenance.

Compare the parsed cells against after-expected by handover_record_id, not visual row position. Check all five rows and all fourteen fields. Reordering rows or using valid alternative CSV quoting is not a semantic defect; a byte checksum can change without a field change. Use file hashes to identify the pristine downloads, not to judge semantically equivalent exports.
  5. Reconcile distinct entities: two company rows (0007 and 0008), three facility rows (0101, 0102, 0103). Facilities 0101 and 0102 link to company 0007. Facility 0103 has an empty parent plus explicit unknown relationship. Company 0008 has no linked site in this fixture; that does not prove it operates no site. Five transported rows are not five companies or five accepted accounts.

08 / questions

Before you request a scope.

Are contacts included?

Not by default. This page describes company-level accounts. Named-person research and contact fields remain unagreed unless separately scoped and authorised with the required source, privacy and data controls. Outreach is a separate scope.

Can an account qualify without a project?

Yes. An industry-matched account can meet the written profile without a project signal. It is not automatically a marketing-qualified lead or a high-intent buyer.

Which territories can I request?

Ireland is the current focus. You can state other preferences in the quote request; coverage is assessed before any commitment.

How are deliverables agreed?

In writing: record unit, fields, sources, counts, class mix, timing, price and acceptance terms. A quote request does not book a package or guarantee coverage.

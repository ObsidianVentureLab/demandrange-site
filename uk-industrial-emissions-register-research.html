UK industrial emissions registers: how to research sites the EU portal does not cover

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

To research industrial sites in Great Britain, use the official UK registers: the UK Pollutant Release and Transfer Register (PRTR) and, for England, the Environment Agency's Pollution Inventory. The EU Industrial Emissions Portal is not a source for those sites. From reporting year 2020, only Northern Ireland reports to the portal, and a register match is coverage and identity evidence, not a purchasing signal.

Demand Range, which publishes this page, provides evidence-backed industrial account research and opportunity intelligence for industrial suppliers in Ireland, the UK and the EU. This page is a how-to for the UK registers. For the EU portal itself, start with using the EU Industrial Emissions Portal to build a list of industrial facilities. The Irish counterpart is how to read Irish environmental registers.

01 / key facts

Key facts

  • EU portal coverage of the UK: the European Environment Agency (EEA) states: "As a result of the Brexit withdrawal agreement, as of reporting year 2020, only Northern Ireland shall report information on industrial sites, facilities, installations, LCPs and waste incinerators."[1]
  • UK rows on the portal map, reporting year 2024: by our count, the EEA map service listed 7 UK sites (countryCode GB) for 2024, and 4 for 2023, when queried on 10 November 2026.[2] That is why the portal cannot be used as a Great Britain site list.
  • UK PRTR: Defra describes it as "the publicly available register that implements the PRTR (Kiev) Protocol and UK PRTR legislation."[3] Its download page offers "all UK Pollutant Release and Transfer Register (PRTR) data for a reporting year as an XML file or CSV file."[4] On 10 November 2026 the page listed yearly datasets for 2007 to 2024. The guidance was last updated on 30 June 2026; the download page on 24 July 2026.[3][4]
  • The UK PRTR is moving: Defra says "The UK PRTR is moving to a new system. During this move, you can download industrial pollutant emissions data in CSV and XML format."[3] Check the official download page when you use it.
  • Pollution Inventory, England: the Environment Agency says the inventory is "a public record of pollution levels in England."[5] data.gov.uk publishes yearly files for 2013 to 2024 (catalogue updated 17 April 2026) under the Environment Agency Conditional Licence, so check the terms before reuse.[6]
  • No stock totals on this page. Official, dated counts from the UK files are in scope only if a later research extract is passed by official QA. Until then, this page does not publish a UK PRTR or Pollution Inventory site total.
  • What a match proves: a register match is coverage and identity evidence, not a purchasing signal.

02 / why the eu portal is not the uk register

Why the EU portal is not the UK register

The EU Industrial Emissions Portal is the right starting point for comparing industrial facilities across EU countries. It is not the register to use for sites in Great Britain.

The EEA's own coverage note is unambiguous. Geographical coverage still names the United Kingdom, but the same About page then limits that coverage: from reporting year 2020, only Northern Ireland shall report.[1] The map-service counts above are the practical consequence of that rule, not a UK industry total.[2]

If a brief covers Great Britain, download the UK PRTR and, where the site is in England, the Pollution Inventory. If the brief also covers Ireland, use the Irish environmental register guide for EPA licence files and Irish PRTR returns. Do not treat a UK row on the EU portal as evidence that the portal still inventories Great Britain.

03 / the uk prtr what it is and how to get the files

The UK PRTR: what it is and how to get the files

Start with Defra's guidance, UK Pollutant Release and Transfer Register (PRTR) data sets, then the publication Download industrial emissions and waste transfer (UK PRTR) data.[3][4]

Defra defines pollutants as substances that may be harmful to the environment or to human health, and describes PRTRs as inventories of pollution from industrial sites and other sources. The UK PRTR is the public register that implements the Kiev Protocol and UK PRTR legislation. Annex I of that legislation specifies which activities need to report; in some cases the activities are subdivided.[3]

Operators report amounts released to air, water and soil, or transferred off site for waste management or wastewater treatment. Defra says the available data includes:[3]

  • the facility, including the facility's parent company where applicable, and its geographical location, including the river basin
  • the activity
  • the pollutant or waste
  • each environmental medium (air, water, land) into which the pollutant is released
  • off-site transfers of waste and their destination, as appropriate
  • off-site transfers of pollutants in wastewater

The download page publishes that yearly data as an XML file or a CSV file.[4] On the day this page was checked, attachments ran from the 2024 dataset back to 2007, in both formats, and the page also listed a file titled "UK Registry data". This page does not describe or count the contents of that registry file.

Defra aims to publish data about one year after the end of the calendar year under report. As an example, it says data for the 2025 report cycle is likely to be available by the end of 2026.[3] As of 10 November 2026, the latest yearly dataset on the download page was 2024, which is consistent with that lag. Because the register is moving to a new system, treat the download location as something to re-check rather than as a permanent path.[3][4]

Neither the guidance nor the download page states a reuse licence in its own body. This page does not infer one from the GOV.UK footer.

04 / what a uk prtr record can and cannot show

What a UK PRTR record can and cannot show

A yearly file is a set of operator-reported releases and transfers for a reporting year, after the regulator's checks. It is not a complete inventory of every industrial site in the UK, and it is not a list of buyers.

Defra is explicit about thresholds. The legislation and the Kiev Protocol specify which industrial activities are included and their associated thresholds. "This means that an installation whose details are not shown on the site have not reached the stated thresholds and therefore do not have to be included. Also, for installations shown but where the threshold for reporting for a pollutant is not reached or where there has been no release, no data for the pollutant will be shown."[3]

So a missing site is first a coverage question: the activity may be out of scope, or the installation may sit below the reporting threshold. A present site with a blank for a given pollutant does not mean the substance was assessed and found irrelevant to a sales brief; it may simply have been below the reporting threshold, or not released.

The figures themselves are calculated (C), estimated (E) or measured (M) by the operator, and the method may also be unspecified (U).[3] Record the method code when you keep the figure. Do not upgrade an estimate into a measurement.

Two further limits matter for account research:

  • Identity is not yet resolved. A facility name and, where it is given, a parent company are starting points. Confirm the legal unit on the company register as a separate step; a registered office is often not the plant. See how to build a list of UK manufacturers that fits your brief.
  • A release is not a project or an order. The record can support a claim that a named facility reported a listed activity and, if a threshold was met, a release or transfer in that year. It does not show which equipment is installed, who authorises purchases, or whether anything is being bought.

Defra also distinguishes point-source PRTR data, which can be tied to a specific facility, from diffuse emissions (for example road transport), which it points to the National Atmospheric Emissions Inventory for.[3] Do not treat the industrial site files as a complete picture of all UK emissions.

05 / england s pollution inventory

England's Pollution Inventory

The Pollution Inventory is a separate official source, with a narrower geography. The Environment Agency collection, Pollution inventory reporting, states: "Information submitted will be held on the pollution inventory, which is a public record of pollution levels in England."[5] That England wording is the basis for the scope on this page. The collection explains that the inventory includes annual emissions of certain substances to air, controlled waters and land, and off-site transfers in wastewater and waste.[5]

The public dataset sits on data.gov.uk as Pollution Inventory, published by the Environment Agency and updated on 17 April 2026.[6] The catalogue describes it as collating annual mass releases of specified substances to air, controlled waters and sewers, and quantities of waste transferred off site, from large industrial sites regulated by the Agency. Reporting covers the whole of the authorisation, licence or permit, including non-point and fugitive emissions as well as specific release points.[6] On 10 November 2026 the catalogue listed yearly files for 2013 to 2024. This page does not publish a file count, because the catalogue summary and the file list do not use the same figure.

Reuse is restricted. The dataset is offered under the Environment Agency Conditional Licence. The catalogue records special conditions, including use for internal or personal purposes and a permitted period of one year.[6] Read the current licence before you store, share or republish the files. Do not assume the same terms apply to the UK PRTR downloads.

Use the inventory beside the UK PRTR, not as a substitute for it. The inventory is an England record of reported releases and transfers from sites the Environment Agency regulates. It does not, on the official pages cited here, stand in for the UK PRTR, and a match still does not establish demand for equipment.

06 / how to use the registers in an account brief

How to use the registers in an account brief

Industrial account research, as set out in what industrial account research is, is the work of deciding which companies and sites fit a written brief, with dated public evidence for each one. The UK registers are one evidence class. They work best when the brief already names the activity, the geography and the unit you are counting (company or site).

A practical sequence:

  1. Write the coverage rule first. If the brief includes sites in Great Britain, name the UK PRTR as a source and, for England, the Pollution Inventory. Do not rely on the EU portal for those sites.[1][2]
  2. Take the official file for the reporting year you need. Use Defra's download page for the UK PRTR (CSV or XML) and, where relevant, the Pollution Inventory file for the same year from data.gov.uk.[4][6] Record the page's update date and the reporting year in the file name, not the date you happened to download it.
  3. Treat each row as a facility-year record. Keep the facility identity, activity, medium, pollutant or waste, and the quantity and method code where they are present. Do not collapse several sites into one company, or several years into one "current" row, unless the written scope says to.
  4. Resolve the operator separately. Match the facility, and any parent name, to a legal unit on the company register, and say when the match is uncertain. The UK manufacturing account-research guide covers that step.
  5. Test gaps against the threshold rule before you reject a site. A known plant that is absent from the UK PRTR may be below the reporting threshold, or in an activity the legislation does not list.[3] Mark it as unverified, not as disproved.
  6. State what the record does not settle. Installed equipment, technical fit, purchasing authority and timing all remain open unless another dated source supports them.
  7. Keep Irish and UK evidence on their own tracks. Ireland has its own EPA licence files and PRTR returns; see the Irish environmental register guide. A UK file cannot evidence an Irish site, and the other way around.

Request a written scope if you want this work done as account records rather than as a method you run yourself.

07 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Which official registers should I use for industrial sites in Great Britain?
Use the UK Pollutant Release and Transfer Register, which Defra publishes as a CSV or XML file for each reporting year, and, for sites in England, the Environment Agency's Pollution Inventory.[4][5] Since reporting year 2020, only Northern Ireland reports to the EU Industrial Emissions Portal, so the portal is not a list of industrial sites in Great Britain.[1]
What is the UK PRTR?
The UK Pollutant Release and Transfer Register is, in Defra's words, "the publicly available register that implements the PRTR (Kiev) Protocol and UK PRTR legislation."[3] It holds operator-reported releases to air, water and land, and off-site transfers of waste and wastewater, for listed activities. Defra publishes each reporting year as a CSV or XML file.[4]
What is the Pollution Inventory, and where does it apply?
It is the Environment Agency's "public record of pollution levels in England."[5] The data.gov.uk dataset collates annual mass releases of specified substances to air, controlled waters and sewers, and waste transferred off site, from large industrial sites the Agency regulates. Yearly files for 2013 to 2024 are published under a conditional licence.[6]
Does a register match mean a site will buy from a supplier?
No. A register match is coverage and identity evidence, not a purchasing signal. It can support a claim that a named facility existed, carried out a listed activity and, if a threshold was met, reported a release or transfer in that year. It does not show which equipment is installed, who buys it, or whether any order will follow.
Why might a plant I already know be missing from the UK PRTR?
The register includes only listed activities, and only when reporting thresholds are met. Defra says an installation whose details are not shown has not reached the stated thresholds, so it does not have to be included.[3] A gap is therefore a coverage question, not proof that the site does not exist or falls outside a brief.
Can I reuse the download files in my own research?
Check the terms on each source before you reuse a file. The Pollution Inventory files on data.gov.uk are published under the Environment Agency Conditional Licence; the catalogue records limits including internal or personal purposes and a one-year period, so read the current licence first.[6] The UK PRTR guidance and download pages do not state a reuse licence in their text.[3][4]

How to find industrial customers in Portugal: a public-data account research guide

By Demand Range research team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026

Eurostat counted 69,876 manufacturing enterprises in Portugal in 2024 (NACE Section C).[1] Use that headline only to size the market. Then build a Portuguese account list from national sources the EU country table does not carry: Statistics Portugal (INE) business counts, the commercial register and its online certificate, CAE-Rev.4 activity codes and the BASE public-contracts portal.

For the comparison with other EU countries, see manufacturing enterprises in the EU by country (2024 data). Do not mix that Eurostat cell with INE's national figures below; they are different official series with their own sources and release dates.

01 / key facts

Key facts

Eurostat 2024 structural business statistics; INE Integrated Business Accounts System 2024; CAE-Rev.4 and the Portuguese registers; checked 10 November 2026.

  • Eurostat table sbs_ovw_act counts 69,876 manufacturing enterprises in Portugal in 2024 (NACE Section C, indicator "Enterprises - number"). The dataset was last updated on 30 September 2026 and was re-extracted on 10 November 2026. No flag is attached to that cell.[1][2]
  • INE's Integrated Business Accounts System (SCIE) counted 69,898 manufacturing enterprises in Portugal in 2024: 41,663 companies and 28,235 individual enterprises. The indicator was last updated on 11 December 2025 and codes activity to CAE Rev. 3.[3]
  • By our count, companies made up about 59.6% of INE's 2024 manufacturing enterprises (41,663 of 69,898).[3]
  • CAE-Rev.4, Portugal's version of NACE Rev. 2.1, was established by Decree-Law no. 9/2025 of 12 February 2025 and takes effect from 1 January 2025. The decree says CAE-Rev.4 and NACE Rev. 2.1 are fully equivalent at every level and in all common codes.[5][6][7]
  • The commercial register's permanent certificate (certidão permanente) gives online access to a registered entity's records. Anyone can request one; it is valid for one, two, three or four years and is read with an access code.[9][10]
  • The Portal BASE, run by the public procurement institute IMPIC, centralises information on public contracts concluded in Portugal.[12]
02 / how big the portuguese industrial base is

How big the Portuguese industrial base is

Eurostat's 69,876 figure is the official 2024 headline for Portuguese manufacturing enterprises (NACE Section C). Division, size-class, employment and investment rows for Portugal sit on the EU manufacturing enterprises by country page; this page does not repeat them.[1]

INE publishes its own national count from the Integrated Business Accounts System (SCIE). The latest manufacturing figures checked on 10 November 2026 are for 2024:[3][4]

Portugal, INE SCIE, manufacturing (CAE Rev. 3 Section C), 2024 Count
Manufacturing enterprises 69,898
of which companies 41,663
of which individual enterprises 28,235

INE's 69,898 and Eurostat's 69,876 are close but not the same cell, so quote each with its own source and date and do not subtract one from the other. INE's results for 2024 are final. SCIE is built mainly from two administrative sources: the simplified business information return (IES), through which companies file their annual results to INE, Banco de Portugal and several ministries at the same time, and the tax returns of individual enterprises.[4] Treat both official series as market-sizing figures, not as a named company list.

Eurostat permits reuse of its figures with acknowledgement.[2]

03 / build the list in layers

Build the list in layers

  1. Companies. Confirm each legal entity in the commercial register. The permanent certificate gives online access to the records of companies, cooperatives and other entities subject to commercial registration, and the published company acts are online on the Ministry of Justice publications site.[9][10][11]
  2. Activity. Code the list to CAE. INE's 2024 SCIE tables use CAE Rev. 3, while CAE-Rev.4 has applied from 1 January 2025. Keep both codes until your sources have moved, and map them with INE's equivalence tables, as described in our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide.[3][5][6]
  3. Plants. The register shows the registered entity, not every site. One company can run several plants, so add site-level evidence such as the EU Industrial Emissions Portal.
  4. Buying signals. Search Portuguese public contracts and notices on the Portal BASE, and EU-threshold notices on TED with CPV codes.[12][13]

A register match confirms a legal entity. It is not, by itself, a purchasing signal.

04 / know the portuguese company landscape

Know the Portuguese company landscape

  • Companies and individual enterprises. INE splits enterprises into companies (sociedades) and individual enterprises. In 2024 manufacturing, companies were the larger group.[3] Record the operating entity and, where the group differs, the group.
  • Registered office versus plant. A commercial-register record describes the legal entity. Find the plants from public site evidence, not from the registered office alone.
  • Language. Register records, INE tables, contract notices and many technical pages are in Portuguese. Plan keyword lists in Portuguese, for example bombas, válvulas, tratamento de águas and manutenção.
  • Activity-code vintage. A CAE-Rev.4 code on a newer record will not always match a CAE Rev. 3 filter on the 2024 INE tables. Do not merge the two unchecked.
05 / cae rev 3 and cae rev 4 run side by side

CAE Rev. 3 and CAE-Rev.4 run side by side

CAE is the Portuguese classification of economic activities. Decree-Law no. 9/2025 of 12 February 2025 established its fourth revision, CAE-Rev.4, to implement Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/137, which created NACE Rev. 2.1. It revoked Decree-Law no. 381/2007, which had set up CAE-Rev.3, and it takes effect from 1 January 2025.[5] INE's classification metadata gives 1 January 2025 as the start of validity for CAE Rev.4.[7]

CAE-Rev.4 has five levels: sections (a letter), divisions (two digits), groups (three digits), classes (four digits) and subclasses (five digits). The subclass is the most detailed level.[5][6] Under the decree, CAE-Rev.4 and NACE Rev. 2.1 are fully equivalent at every level and in all common codes, so no correspondence table is needed between them. INE prepares the equivalence tables between CAE-Rev.3 and CAE-Rev.4, in both directions, and publishes them with the classification.[5][6]

INE says CAE-Rev.4 is mainly statistical but also applies to company registration, studies, tax returns and economic-activity incentives.[6] INE's SCIE indicator for 2024, checked on 10 November 2026, was still coded to CAE Rev. 3.[3]

For a list, that means:

  • Codes in INE's 2024 business tables, older directories and your own records are probably CAE Rev. 3.
  • Keep CAE Rev. 3 and CAE-Rev.4 on each row until your sources have moved, and map them with INE's equivalence tables. Our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide explains how the European codes changed.
  • Do not filter one dataset on CAE Rev. 3 and another on CAE-Rev.4 and then merge them unchecked.

The method is the same one used for industrial account research in Germany, where WZ 2025 is that country's NACE Rev. 2.1 implementation.

07 / step by step

Step by step

  1. Size the segment with the Eurostat headline, then define it with CAE / NACE codes. See our NACE Rev. 2.1 guide for how the European codes changed, and the EU enterprises table for other countries.
  2. Use INE SCIE only for market sizing. It tells you how large manufacturing is and how it splits between companies and individual enterprises. It does not give you the names.
  3. Resolve each prospect in the commercial register. Confirm the legal entity and its registered acts through a permanent certificate, and read the published company acts. Do not treat officer names as a contact list; record the function, not a person.
  4. Find the plants from public site evidence, including the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for process industries, because the registered office is not always the plant.
  5. Add a trigger only when you have one: a published company act, a contract or notice on the Portal BASE, a TED notice, or a new site record.
  6. Log the evidence for each account: legal entity, CAE Rev. 3 and CAE-Rev.4 codes, site evidence and the reason it fits the brief.

Fictional example: A fictional Irish maker of industrial heat exchangers targets Portuguese food and drink plants. It filters the EU Industrial Emissions Portal for food and drink installations in Portugal and resolves each operator through a commercial-register permanent certificate. It finds that one fictional dairy group runs three plants under one company, and that the published company acts belong to the same entity. It records the three plants as separate accounts under one group, each with maintenance as the owning function. This example is fictional and only illustrates the method.

08 / common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Using only registered-office data. In Portugal, as elsewhere in the EU, buying influence often sits at plant level.
  • Treating INE's SCIE count and Eurostat's cell as one figure, or subtracting one from the other.
  • Filtering one source on CAE Rev. 3 and another on CAE-Rev.4 and merging them unchecked.
  • Treating a register match or a contract notice as proof that a plant is buying your product.
09 / frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How many manufacturing companies are there in Portugal?
Eurostat counted 69,876 manufacturing enterprises in Portugal in 2024, for NACE Section C.[1] Statistics Portugal's own business accounts system counted 69,898 for the same year, of which 41,663 were companies and 28,235 individual enterprises.[3] The two are separate official series, so quote each figure with its own source and date rather than combining them.
What is CAE-Rev.4?
CAE-Rev.4 is the fourth revision of Portugal's classification of economic activities, its national version of NACE Rev. 2.1. Decree-Law no. 9/2025 of 12 February 2025 established it, with effect from 1 January 2025. It has five levels, down to five-digit subclasses, and matches NACE Rev. 2.1 fully in all common codes.[5][6][7]
Should I use CAE Rev. 3 or CAE-Rev.4 codes?
Both, for now. CAE-Rev.4 has applied since 1 January 2025, but INE's 2024 business accounts tables checked on 10 November 2026 were still coded to CAE Rev. 3. Keep both codes on each row and map them with INE's official equivalence tables until your sources have moved, then filter on one version only.[3][5][6]
How do I look up a Portuguese company in the commercial register?
Request a permanent certificate of commercial registration online. Anyone can request one; after payment you receive an access code that opens the entity's records online for one to four years. Published company acts are also on the Ministry of Justice publications site. Check the official service pages for current types, languages and terms.[9][10][11]
What is the Portal BASE?
It is Portugal's official public-contracts portal, run by the public procurement institute IMPIC. It centralises information on public contracts concluded in Portugal and lets you search contracts, notices, contracting entities and contract changes, including by CPV code. EU-threshold notices also appear on TED. A contract is a public buying signal, not proof of fit.[12][13]
Should I quote the INE or the Eurostat manufacturing count?
Either, with its own source. Eurostat's 69,876 is its 2024 structural business statistics cell for NACE Section C.[1] INE's 69,898 comes from its 2024 business accounts system, coded to CAE Rev. 3.[3] Both use the classifications in force before 2025, so the small gap is not a CAE-Rev.4 effect. Never combine or subtract them.
Can Portuguese public records show who buys at a plant?
No. A commercial-register certificate shows an entity's registration records, published company acts show its registered changes, and the Portal BASE shows public contracts and the entities that won them.[9][11][12] None of them names the person who decides purchases at a plant.
